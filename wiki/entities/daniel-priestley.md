## His frameworks — attributed to him, fenced from the persona

- ★ **Demand and supply tension.** *"Get rid of this idea of fairness — that if you're doing a good job
  you get paid really well. It's not. **If you're able to create demand and supply tension, you get paid
  really well.**"* Evidence by margin: airlines 3–7%, supermarkets 3–5%, a Rolex 75%. Adam Smith's
  water-and-diamonds paradox as the first principle.
- ★ **"Everything is downstream from lead generation"** — his first mentor's mantra (he was employee
  number three and watched the company go from a kitchen table to 60 people in two years).
- ★★ **Market for signals, not sales.** Glastonbury: **700,000** registrations of interest for **130,000**
  tickets, sold in a **30-minute** annual window, with the line-up unannounced. The arithmetic that makes
  it work: **buying requires ~90% certainty; signalling requires only 10–20%.** So collect far more
  signals than you need, then reveal the imbalance.
- **Transparency is the active ingredient.** Publish the imbalance (capacity 20/year, 200 on the list), or
  make it visible — a group, an event, a queue people can see each other in.
- ★ **The buyer pyramid.** Value-perception is hierarchical; process people one at a time and you default
  to the base of the pyramid and its price. Assemble the pyramid with data first, then talk only to the
  top.
- **Waiting list** (future capacity) vs. **registration of interest** (current capacity, gated).
- ★★ **Assessment-based selling.** Refuse to discuss the solution until an assessment is done — the doctor
  will not cast an arm before the X-ray. 30–40 questions, categorised, with a results page keyed to the
  answers. Best-performing formats: **"Are you ready to ___?"** and **"Which type of ___ are you?"** His
  own Key Person of Influence scorecard has run seven years and ~**120,000** completions.
- **The surplus-demand move**: when it sells out, **do not increase capacity** — offer existing buyers
  **2×** to sell their ticket back, taking no margin.
- ★★ **The four-bucket sales structure** (2024-06-30): organise everything a prospect says into **current
  situation → desired situation → obstacle → criteria** — *"sometimes I'm jumping around, but **I'm always
  organising it in my mind.**"* With ★ **the minimum effective dose**: *"you only need a **minimum effective
  dose** of information."* Sometimes three minutes; sometimes thirty and still digging.
- ★★ **"Take it away from me"** — rather than arguing your own value, hand the case to the prospect:
  *"you're a super smart guy, you've got a 30-year background and amazing people — **why haven't you been
  able to do this on your own?**"* — *"and **they start making the case that actually it's really hard for
  us to do this on our own.** I love to take it away from me."*
- ★ **Say when you're lost**: *"I know this is frustrating, I'm asking all these questions and **I'm still
  not getting it**"* — which is what unlocks the distinction.
- ★ **The expert's blind spot**: *"they've been in their business so long that **they can't empathise with
  people who don't know what it's like to not know**… even when you think you're dumbing it down, you
  don't dumb it down anywhere near enough."*

## What is Chris's, and must stay separated

- ★ **"Sell the assessment" — literally, not just figuratively.** Chris's own split: *"we do this all the
  time. To get an appraisal for complex things we have to pay for it. **The X-ray doesn't come for free,
  the blood work is not for free** — and if it's really good and it gives us peace of mind, then it's
  actually worth something."*
- ★ **The two non-monetary benefits he adds**: an assessment signals *"this person is really not rushing
  into giving me a solution,"* and it **filters out clients who won't sit down and reflect.**
- ★ **The value-even-if-they-never-buy standard**: the taker should walk away better off — *"you just made
  me aware of something I was blind to… **even if we haven't done anything, I know that you're a person of
  real value.**"*
- ★ **Fear and love as the two emotions** to build segmentation questions around — credited by Chris to
  **his wife**, not to Priestley.
- **The honesty constraint on scarcity is Chris's own line**, drawn in
  [[../sources/2024-03-14-yt-0wse5TRJYHE]]: *"this is where it gets into **manipulation marketing, which I
  don't like**… if you say I have two [slots] but then you take on 55 clients, it's not going to work."*
