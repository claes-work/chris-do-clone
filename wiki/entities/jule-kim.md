## Her material — attributed to her, fenced

- ★★ **The SEO tripod**: **content** (*"purely in your control"*), **speed**, **popularity** (backlinks,
  with the nuance that a link's value depends on how freely the linker gives links). Plus **negative SEO**
  — buying shady links pointed at competitors.
- ★★ **The keyword taxonomy**: ~10% **branded**, ~10% **product/service** (the *money keywords*), and
  **~80% topic keywords** — *"the money is in the 20%, **but the real opportunity is in that 80%**, where
  you're probably not generating content that teaches what you know."*
- ★ **Topic clusters, not single keywords**; mining autocomplete and related searches for free.
- ★★ **The brand-strategist mismatch**: *"y'all call yourselves brand strategists, but **the average person
  doesn't know what the hell that means. The average person is like: I don't like how my website looks.**"*
- ★★ **The three-question listening drill**, run live: (1) what was the main takeaway, (2) **which specific
  words or phrases** did they use — *"we don't want you to repeat everything like a parrot… but there does
  need to be some level of you knowing **which pieces to pull out**"* — and (3) **what was the other person
  experiencing while they were talking?**
- ★★ **Comprehension before response.** *"Today is not so much about the quality of your response… **today
  is just: how much are you actually picking up on what he's sharing with you.**"* Most listening training
  conflates the two; separating them is the structural move.
- ★ **The default-to-strength diagnosis**: presented with a need outside their strongest skill, people
  *"didn't even hear you"* and revert to where they feel most confident — sellers get *"wrapped up in their
  skills and what they could deliver."*
- **On her own pivot**: leaving SEO for coaching was *"probably one of the hardest moves I've ever made."*

makes misattribution plausible and hard to detect later. **The listening drill in particular is the
