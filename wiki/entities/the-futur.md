## Business model

Multi-channel education business [press-corroborated: Motionographer 2017; thefutur.com/shop]:

  Chris Do (thefutur.com/shop/business-bootcamp).
- **Coaching/community** — the Futur Pro Group membership with biweekly calls hosted by Chris
  [self-reported: Creator Science #111, 2022-08-02, podcast.creatorscience.com/chris-do/];
  formerly the **Accelerator** program (spun out as Maker Division when Ben Burns left, Nov
  2021 — see [[futur-instructors]]). **The Pro Group's largest growth period came via live

  **40-question** assessment that outputs **one of four result types** telling a person which
  product/service they likely need. Chris **credits COO Ben Burns** for it and calls the

- **Free content engine** — the [[thefutur-youtube|YouTube channel]] (2.81M subs as of

### The business-model pivot: courses → subscription (2024-07-10)

*"I'm an educator at heart… **I want to release it all for free** — **but it's in direct opposition to me as
an entrepreneur** and my ability to support and grow my team and pay them a livable wage."* The stated aim:
*"**we don't have to market and create funnels, we don't have to create courses — we can just teach.** That
is me in my **zone of genius.**"*

- ★★ **The metric he proposes to be judged on**: *"having a lot of subscribers is nice, but that's **a
  vanity metric.** **The real metric that matters is the value per fan.** … **We don't really have two and a
  half million fans — and I want to test if this model is true.**"*
- **YouTube memberships, two tiers**: **$1/month** (a badge) and **$15/month** (unlisted, unreleased and
  unfiltered longer-form content *"that should be courses that we charge money for"*). Premium-destined
  content gets a **48-hour free window** first.
- **Scale at the time**: **2.5M subscribers, growing ~20,000/month**. The arithmetic: *"if we got **less
  than 5% of you** to step up — let's say 100,000 people — I would be able to pay my team a livable wage."*
- ★ **Revenue lines, listed by him**: digital courses and resources; group coaching (**Pro Group** and
  **Accelerator**); public speaking; and **one-on-one consulting and coaching** — the last of which he flags
  as news to most viewers: *"**I'm rather reluctant to talk about it.**"*
- ★ **The self-assessment**: *"**I hate marketing and sales and I'm not very good at it**, to be honest with
  you."*
- **The 2019 studio, and its timing**: he converted the office into *"a dedicated event space where we can
  host workshops for over 100 people"* — and **its first and only event was a Marty Neumeier brand-strategy
  workshop**, with attendees flying in from Australia and China, before the pandemic left it *"gathering
  dust and cobwebs."* The 2024 plan was a **hybrid learning space in Pasadena** — live-audience podcasts,
  prototype workshops, dinners, **apprentices in the studio** — justified editorially: *"**the
  best-performing content on the channel involves real people working in a real space together**… **that
  friction is what creates the magical moments.**"*
- **Team named by role**: **Diego** and **Mo** (content, editing, channels), **Amy** (brand sponsorships),
  **Rich** (podcast booking and strategy).
- ⚠️ **Framing**: the source is an explicit fundraising ask — *"I'm going to do something very rare: I'm
  going to ask you for something"* — not a neutral teach.
([[../sources/2024-07-10-yt-7Gi-lHLgTeI]])

### The European tour (2023) — how it was actually run

Sparked by **[[anneli-hansen]]** asking him to come to Stockholm and chain cities together. **Polled into
existence** rather than booked: a poster asking which of two workshops and which cities, then a shortlist
poll, then details and price — *"**it's like peeling back an onion, one layer at a time**"* — aiming to be
*"**70, 80% sold just in the first 24 hours**"* when the page opened.

- **Why**: *"until they speak up, **it's all speculative** — and one that could wind up being a financial
  disaster. You book the venue, make all your travel arrangements, and then no one shows up. **It's a blood
  bath.**"*
- ★★ **The principle**: *"**we're going to design and plan the event *with* people, not *for* them.**"*
- **What went wrong**: *"**two or three cities in we're like: oh my gosh, we're not reading the pulse of the
  city**, and I'm having to react in real time — when in fact I could have done some of this beforehand."*
  **Bucharest**: a post-communist economy with *"really hard relationships with clients who don't want to
  pay them."* **Warsaw** confounded his expectations the other way — *"that room was so fun, they exploded…
  **and I swam right in there with them.**"*
- ★ **The fix, dated 2024**: **ChatGPT for cultural pre-research** — *"what are their belief systems around
  money? What are their client relationships like?"* — *"at least I was **going into the battlefield
  prepared.**"*
- **Venues**: co-working and creative spaces — *"**they've got space and no humans**"* — reached through
  **local intermediaries**, since *"to them we're an unknown entity."* **Joanna Galvao** bought all eight
  sold tickets to force a move **from Lisbon to Porto**: *"the design, the creative community, **the pulse
  is in Porto.**"*
([[../sources/2024-08-20-yt-ufQErCvUQnc]])

## ★★ Brand Lab — the five tellings, reconciled (2026-07-27, pass 14)

_Five separate accounts across Jan–Mar 2024 were carried as open debt through two synthesis passes. This is
the single dated entry that supersedes them; the individual source pages remain as attestations._

**What it is.** A programme for **entrepreneurs and business owners who want to work on their personal
branding** — deliberately *not* aimed at creatives. Capped at **100 members**, and in early 2024 chasing
**the first 10**.

**The pivot, and the reason for it.** Chris repositioned from selling to creatives to selling to the people
who *hire* creatives. The commercial reason, given bluntly in the 2024-02-12 telling and **the one that
must not be lost**: *"**we sell to the end buyer and they're all broke.** That's why we're not doing $10
million a year."* The mission-shaped version he gives elsewhere (helping entrepreneurs tell their story) is
true, but incomplete without this.

**The pricing restructure, dated.** Originally **$36,000 for 12 months**; restructured to **$18,000 for
6 months** on **Jasmine Star's** advice — *"just chop your whole program in half"* — with his own note on
why it works: *"**you do the math, it's exactly the same price.** It's just 6 months, and they're like:
what a great price."* Her second reason, which he repeats approvingly: *"**you might not want to do this in
12 months. Just try it out for six.**"*

**Then subtraction.** A weekly 90-minute call plus a **three-day in-person workshop**. One prospect said
they couldn't attend the workshop — so **the workshop was cut, at the same price**: *"**I just get rid of
more stuff until they can't complain anymore, and now they just buy.**"* Ben's substitute: VIP access to
whatever Chris is doing — *"that works for me. **I don't even know what I'm doing.** That works."*

**The first-10 incentive** (Ben Burns's idea): a one-on-one strategy session for the first ten enrolments —
*"**execute it, Ben, let's go.**"*

**The economics he states**: 10 × $18,000 = **$180,000**; a full cohort of 100 = **$1.8M**; *"we do that
twice a year, it's **$3.6 million.** … And when we get a waitlist, **the price will just go up.**"*

**What he actually sells, in his own words**: *"**I would teach you everything you need to know about
personal branding for free. If you want the feedback from me, you'll pay me the 18 grand** — and you'll be
happy to do it."*

> ⚠️ **STATUS GUARD (unchanged, and still binding).** As of the 2024 sources this is **a newly launched,
> unresolved 2023 pivot that he describes as still being figured out** — *"we're just looking for our first
> 10"*, *"whatever new objections I hear, I just change the sales copy… until you cannot complain
> anymore."* **Do not narrate Brand Lab as a finished success.** The $1.8M/$3.6M figures are **projections
> he states aloud**, not results.

economics) · [[../sources/2024-02-12-*]] (the blunt commercial reason) · plus the earlier Jan–Feb tellings

The corpus has always recorded *that* Blind's service work wound down and The Futur took over. This is the

> ★★★ *"You've been pretty instrumental in shaping a couple of big seismic changes within my own thinking…

★★★ **The arithmetic that made it land:**

| Client work | Educational product |
|---|---|
| *"when we book a **$600,000 job**, you're not keeping $600,000 — **you might keep $150[K]**"* | *"it isn't super expensive for us to produce — **we could make 100,000 or 200,000, but almost all of it would be profit**"* |
