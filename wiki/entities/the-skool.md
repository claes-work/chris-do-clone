# The Skool (2014–2016)

The online design-education venture Chris Do co-ran with [[jose-caballer]] from 2014 until
their 2016 split — the direct predecessor of [[the-futur]].

> ⚠️ **DISAMBIGUATION:** The Skool has NOTHING to do with **Skool.com**, the community
> platform founded by Sam Ovens (later associated with Alex Hormozi). Same word, unrelated
> ventures. Any post-2019 "Skool" reference in sources almost certainly means Skool.com,
> not this venture.

## What it was

- Founded **2014** as a partnership: "The Futur was originally called the Skool and was the
  brainchild of Jose Caballer"; per Chris Do (verbatim): "Jose and I partnered to produce
  videos, events, and training materials in 2014 in the hopes of building an online school to
  teach design" [press: Motionographer, "The future of The Futur", 2017-09-28,
  motionographer.com/2017/09/28/the-future-of-the-futur/].
- Output: YouTube video series (business of design, sales, [[jose-caballer|CORE]]-style
  workshops), live events, and training materials [press: Motionographer 2017].
- The YouTube channel created for it is today's Futur channel: the legacy username URL
  **youtube.com/user/TheSkoolRocks** still resolves to the channel (checked 2026-07-14), and
  the oldest uploads date to August 2014 ("How To Ask for the Sale Pt 1", 2014-08-20, with
  Jose Caballer) — see [[thefutur-youtube]].
- The Skool era is where Chris Do learned YouTube mechanics and developed his on-camera
  presence [press-corroborated: millo.co/chris-do-interview and other origin-story interviews].

## Who ran it

- **Jose Caballer** — originator ("brainchild"), co-host, brought the CORE workshop
  methodology from his agency The Groop [press: Motionographer 2017; josecaballer.medium.com].
- **Chris Do** — co-host and partner, running [[blind]] in parallel.

## Money (self-reported)

- Year one (2014): grossed ~$15,000; 2015: ~$42,000 [self-reported:
  millo.co/chris-do-interview]. A hobby-scale side project next to Blind's ~$5M/year.

## The split and rebrand (2016)

- Per Chris Do (verbatim): "Ultimately, Jose and I had differing ideas about content,
  direction, and leadership styles." To preserve the friendship "we both needed to do our own
  thing"; friends helped negotiate an amicable separation; "We rebranded as the Futur and have
  been charging forward ever since" [press: Motionographer 2017].
- THE FUTUR, LLC was filed with the California SOS on **07/25/2016** [registry-verified,
  bizfileonline.sos.ca.gov, checked 2026-07-14] — consistent with a mid-2016 split/rebrand.
- Caballer kept his CORE framework and continued independently; CORE kits were later sold
  through The Futur's shop [self-reported: josecaballer.medium.com, "Core Discovery is Back",
  May 2026].
- No separate "The Skool" California entity was found; a definitive SOS negative search could
  not be completed (search UI became unresponsive, 2026-07-14). It may have operated as a DBA
  or under one of the partners' existing entities [unverified].

## ★★ How the split was actually decided — the cake rule (added 2026-07-27)

_Source: [[../sources/2024-03-19-yt-jd0Ijs0a0ns]] (2024-03-19), told first-person by Chris.
**Self-reported.** This is the first account in the corpus of the split's **mechanism**, as opposed to its
diplomatic summary above — and the two are consistent, not contradictory._

- **The rule came from his father.** *"Whenever my brother and I disagreed about something he says: **if
  there's a piece of cake, one person cuts it, the other person decides which half they want.** This keeps
  everybody super honest."*
- **Chris cut. Jose chose.** The two halves:

  | Half A | Half B |
  |---|---|
  | the **intellectual property**, the products and services, **and all the revenue attached** | the **YouTube channel** (~10–20K subscribers at the time), the **community**, the relationships |

  ★★ *"**One has no money attached to it, one has all the money attached to it.**"*
- **Jose chose the money.** Chris's own gloss on why that looked obviously correct at the time: *"**what
  fool would give up the IP and the products?** Because all the money was going that way."*
- **The years after.** *"For a number of years the only amount of money that came into the company, all of
  it went to Jose, because he owned the products. There were years when we were writing a six-figure check
  to him and **he did literally nothing — he was sitting on the beach, he was finding himself, which was
  awesome. That was the deal.**"* The Futur only began generating its own revenue once Chris released a
  course.
- **The origin of the content itself**: it was **Jose who insisted** Chris make YouTube content —
  *"something that I was resistant to do back in the old days"* — starting **January 2014**, when Chris
  was 42.

### The coda — and the correction

At a lunch days before the 2024-03-19 recording, Jose asked how the book launch had worked. Chris's
answer: he didn't want to write a book; the community kept asking; he ran a **Kickstarter** with a
**$30,000** threshold — *"if we can't get $30,000 worth of book pledges I'm not writing a book, **it's not
worth my time**"* — and raised roughly **$80,000** in 30 days.

Jose's verdict: *"**Chris, it's because you have the community. They showed up for you.** … Back when you
had given me that choice, **you made the smart decision to go with community.**"*

★★ **Chris's correction, which is the point of the whole story:** *"Jose, I appreciate you saying that,
but **I didn't choose. You did.**" He goes: 'I know. I know. I chose.'"*

He draws no strategic-genius moral from it. What he draws instead is a claim about the asset: *"**I will
die on that sword: my company The Futur is nothing without the brand and the relationship that we have
with our consumers.**"* See [[../topics/business]] §47 for the typography-course thought experiment that
generalises it.
