## ★★★★ Material

### ★★★★★ ⭐ Indemnification, and what large clients do to it

> ★★★★★ *"There's a whole thing you really need to have as a designer, and it's **INDEMNIFICATION**. That
> sounds really fancy, but it's basically: **if shit hits the fan and whatever we did for you messes up
> your business — it wasn't us.** You can't hold the service provider liable."*

> ★★★★★ *"**Big corporate clients are going to REMOVE the indemnification clause, because they have really
> powerful lawyers** — and they're going to require you to have **at least $2 million in insurance.**"*

✅ `indemnification` returns **0** corpus-wide. ⚠️ **The second half is the practical part**: the
protection you write in is the first thing a large client strikes, and the substitute is **insurance you
must buy.**

> ★★★★ *"**Consulting insurance** — an insurance broker will give it to you. **It's about a thousand
> dollars a year.**"*

✅ `consulting insurance` returns **0**. ⚠️ 🗣️ **A 2014 figure** — must be dated if promoted, not
presented as current.

📌 **This is framed as a threshold marker**: *"if you're making a transition from working with a mom-and-pop
to working with a larger corporate client"* — the clause fight is how you know you have crossed it.

### ★★★★ Master services agreement — the checklist

> ★★★★ Parties and **which state** the work is performed in · **what you are doing for them** · **how much
> it costs** · **termination** — *"if shit hits the fan, what do we do? How do we separate?"* · **all the
> exit clauses** · **who is in breach, based on what** · **notice periods — 15 days, 30 days** to cancel.

### ★★★★ ⭐ Statement of work ≠ contract

> ★★★★ *"**The statement of work has deliverables, price and schedule**… **It doesn't have any of the
> clauses.** It simply has what's being delivered and how it's being delivered."*

✅ `statement of work` returns **2** — ⚠️ **the separation itself is the new part**: commercial terms live
in one document, legal terms in another.

### ★★★★ Payment terms — attributed to Chris

_He is the "broadcast world" speaker, and states these as **ours**._

> ★★★★ *"Our terms are **50% upfront** before we start… I think our terms are **50, 25, 25** — 50% upfront,
> **25% as we get three-quarters of the way through** the job, and **25% on delivery.**"*

> ★★★★ *"That last 25% has **a little fudge factor** in terms of when they pay. **If we're not sure about a
> brand-new client, we make sure — before we hand over the final product — that there's a check ready to
> go.**"*

✅ `50% upfront` returns **3**, so the deposit is held — ⚠️ **the three-way split and the
withhold-delivery-until-paid safeguard are the additions.**

> ★★★ *"**Net 30, and we like net 30. We can live with that.** …If you have **net 90 or net 120**, that
> means it could be **up to four months** until you get paid. **When we first started out as a company that
> felt like an eternity** — because you're running on your own cash."*

✅ `net 30` returns **4**. ⚠️ **The new part is the early-company memory** attached to it.

### ★★★ Where you sit in the contract chain — attributed to Chris

> ★★★ *"On the motion graphics side they're more simple — **more like POs, purchase orders**, against an
> ad agency's existing master services contract with the client. **So understand that you're NOT the final
> holder.** If you're doing **direct to client, like I do — we hold master contracts.**"*

📌 He gives the analogy: *"if you're an architect, you might be contracted by the developer; the developer
contracts with the client."* ⚠️ **A structural point the corpus's pricing material does not make** — your
contract type is downstream of your position in the chain.
