## ⚠️ Guest material — context only, NOT persona data

Kier's argument is that industries reliably fail to recognise their own displacement, illustrated
with two set pieces:

- **The buffalo on the plains** — animals that kept grazing as the ones beside them fell, because
  *"they're not the smartest creatures in the world"* — as an image for industries that watch peers
  fail and *"just keep on keeping on."*
- **The carriage makers** — *"you would have thought that carriage makers would have transitioned
  to become automobile people… but none of them made it through**… **because none of them believed
  it would happen.**"*

⚠️ He also names former employers and colleagues from his own career. **All of it is his account of
his own history and is recorded on his entity page, not here.**

> ✅ **Worth noting for the corpus, not promoting from here:** this 2015 disruption argument is
> **the same shape** as the one Chris makes in his own voice a decade later — *"every 3 to 5 years
> your industry is going to be disrupted; if you haven't disrupted yourself, you will be"*
> ([[2026-05-19-yt-nY58IHYAARM|2026-05-19]]) — and Chris's later telling uses **his own** examples
> (the internet, Apple, the Thomas Guide, Nokia). **The influence is plausible and the shape is
> shared, but he never credits Kier for it, so the connection is the curator's observation, not a
> stated attribution.**
