> ⏳ **February 2016 — pre-rebrand, the show is still *The Process*.** ✅ Two frameworks the corpus

> **whoever talks first loses.**

## Summary

★★★★★ A five-tip interview clinic that is really a **questioning-and-listening clinic**, closing
with a live roleplay in which Chris plays the interviewer and gets asked his own magic question.
The strategic core — **ask first so you can tailor what you say, then shut up and gauge
engagement** — is the same machinery he later teaches as the discovery call, applied to the job
seeker instead of the studio owner.

## Key claims (2016-02-13)

### ★★★★★ The three things to do (his framing: "you can call them mistakes, you can call them what to do")

1. ★★★★ **Don't talk about your work — talk about the case study.** *"**You need to provide context
   when you're showing your work.** What's the problem, what's the solution, how did it make sense?
   **It's nice to look at pretty designs, but it's good to see HOW YOU PROBLEM SOLVE.**"*
2. ★★★★ **Share your process.** *"**How did you come to the conclusions that you came to? Why did
   you choose the things that you chose?**"*
3. ★★★★★ **Ask questions.** *"**The interview isn't just about you answering questions — it's also
   about asking questions OF THE POTENTIAL EMPLOYER.**"*

★★★★★ **Why he — as the one hiring — wants to be asked:** *"**I want to know the person on the other
end that I'm interviewing actually understands what they're getting into, and that they are CONSCIOUS
OF THE OTHER PERSON. Because I'm really about EMPATHY.**"*

### ★★★★★ The magic question

> ★★★★★ *"This is **the magic question** — this is **the holy grail of questions**: '**if [the sky]
> opened and your deity of choice gave you THE PERFECT DESIGNER to fill this role — what would that
> look like?**' **And let them describe the profile of that person.**"*

★★★★★ **And the reason for the timing, which is the whole technique:** *"**Ask that at the
BEGINNING** — because you can then **TAILOR what you're telling them to what they said to you.
YOU'RE READING IT BACK TO THEM.** And then **the fidelity between what they're looking for and what
you're telling them will be awesome.**" → with the aside: *"**that works in dating too.**"*

★★★★ **The blunter version of the same move:** *"'**what do you personally need, Mr. Creative
Director? Where your agency is at — what would you need from a designer like me?**'"* → *"and Chris
would immediately go: **let me think about that.** And then he would answer it — and at the end,
**that answer is the criteria for him hiring you. So you will know right there** that if your work
was not what he needed, or who you are wasn't what he needed — **you already know that you don't have
the job.**"*

### ★★★★★ The value principle

> ★★★★★ *"**THE VALUE OF YOU AS A PERSON IS REFLECTED IN THE QUALITY OF THE QUESTIONS YOU ASK.**"*

★★★★ **With the test attached:** *"**if you ask 'what time do people show up' — that's not a
high-value question.** But asking '**ideally what would this person do**', '**where do you see this
going**' — **those kinds of questions OPEN UP FOR DIALOGUE.**"*

> ⏳ ✅ **A precedence finding.** The corpus records this idea via the **Demartini** epigraph —
> *the quality of your life is based on the quality of the questions you ask* — from 2025–26
> sources. **He is saying his own version of it here in February 2016**, applied to hiring, with no
> attribution to anyone. ⚠️ Whether he arrived at it independently or absorbed it earlier is **not
> established by this source**; only the date is.

### ★★★★★ Then listen — "whoever talks first loses"

- ★★★★ **The permission first:** *"**just relax. Your work IS good enough. THE FACT THAT YOU GOT AN
  INTERVIEW MEANS THAT YOU'RE QUALIFIED.** Understand this: **people don't call designers or
  developers in because they want to just spend time talking to you.**"*
- ★★★★★ **Gauge engagement and stop:** *"**you need to GAUGE THE INTEREST LEVEL of the person who's
  interviewing you.** If their eyes light up… spend time talking about it. **If you see no interest
  — please, PLEASE SAVE YOURSELF. Don't dive deep into that, because YOU'RE BORING PEOPLE.**"*
- > ★★★★★ *"**When the person is talking, silence the thoughts in your head and PLAY A GAME:
  > WHOEVER TALKS FIRST LOSES. Just be quiet and listen.**"*

  ⏳ ✅ **Precedence.** This is the same discipline as the **then-shut-up** pricing rule taught at
  [[2017-02-04-yt-swiG31QGKVc|2017-02-04]] — **a year earlier, and phrased as a game.**

- ★★★★★ **Permission not to answer:** *"**don't be afraid to say 'that's a great question. I'll have
  an answer for you — I'd like to follow up with you later.' THAT BEATS ANYTHING THAT YOU'RE GOING
  TO MAKE UP ON THE SPOT.** Some of us aren't gifted to be able to answer questions on the fly…
  **we feel pressured to answer it, and you make up some crazy answer and you'll sound ridiculous.
  So do what they do in football and PUNT.**"*

### ★★★★ Be memorable — with a warning attached

*"**The interviewer is talking to several candidates and you need to stand out**… it's going to be
hard for them to figure out who they saw and when. **So little things that you do will make you
memorable.**"*

`[HOST — Jose]` tells the orange-jumpsuit story: he wore one to his first New York interview, and
*"some people thought that was just a little too much — so the people who liked that, who liked me
and all the craziness, made offers. The people that didn't, didn't."*

★★★★★ **Chris's reading of it — self-selection:** *"**his attire is self-selecting in terms of
who's going to buy in** — 'dude, an orange jumpsuit, we should hire him, he's crazy' — or 'don't
hire that guy.'"* ✅ The same logic as the **law of attraction** framing at
[[2016-03-04-yt-KlnOS_Blig4|2016-03-04]].

★★★★ **And then, unusually, he qualifies his own advice for the audience's situation:** *"**let's be
sensitive to our audience. Chances are, if you're watching this episode on how to win at an
interview, then you're NOT going to want to rattle the boat a little too much.** That's a really loud
statement — **he can do that because he's really confident in what he can do, and he doesn't really
care: 'I'm not right for you, I'm out.' But IF YOU'RE REALLY LOOKING FOR A JOB AND YOU'VE BEEN
STRIKING OUT, I WOULD PULL THAT BACK.**"* → *"**be memorable in your PERSONALITY, be memorable in
your WORK… be REAL.**"*

### ★★★★ Follow up — old-school

*"**Follow up immediately — that's a sign of professionalism.** After the interview, take the time
and effort to **write a personal note** thanking the person who interviewed you, and **try to recall
something memorable** — if you were paying attention, you were listening… **mail it out the very
next day.**" → *"**do it old-school SNAIL MAIL. Write a letter, include a personal note, PULL OUT
SOME DETAIL FROM THE CONVERSATION.**"*

### ★★★★★ The roleplay — the technique demonstrated, with real 2016 Blind strategy in it

Chris plays the creative director; the candidate opens with the tailoring question:

- **Candidate:** *"before we started, I just wanted to **contextualise how I show you my work** — so
  tell me about your vision for Blind, where you want to take it, and what kind of work you want to
  do."*
- ★★★★ **Chris, in role:** *"**wow, that's a deep question. Okay, let me think about that a little
  bit.**"* — and then answers with what Blind was actually doing: *"**what we're trying to do is to
  PIVOT OUT OF BEING A MOTION DESIGN FIRM EXCLUSIVELY and to being THE DIGITAL AGENCY OF THE FUTURE
  — to incorporate a lot of branding, design, identity — and most of it is centred around
  STRATEGY.**"*

  ✅ **Corroborates the repositioning recorded at [[2016-03-04-yt-KlnOS_Blig4|2016-03-04]]**, three
  weeks earlier, from a completely different context.
- **Candidate:** *"so if I show you a case study… that shows the strategy, the process, the results
  —"* → **Chris:** *"cool, let me show you that."* ✅ The tailoring working as advertised.
- **Candidate:** *"as executive creative director and lead strategist, **what could I bring to the
  table as a designer that would help you personally?**"*
- ★★★★★ **Chris's real answer:** *"**I'm looking for somebody who's really DRIVEN, who's a
  SELF-STARTER, who TAKES INITIATIVE — and not somebody who's waiting to be told what the next step
  is. Those are people that I find it's a lot harder to work with.**"*
- ★★★★★ **And then he breaks frame to coach the hardest part:** *"**I'm stopping myself from talking
  right there — because in my head I'm saying: THAT'S ME, DUDE, THAT'S TOTALLY ME, A SELF-STARTER.**
  … **you get an emotional response to some of the things in the other person and you want to answer
  right away.**"* ✅ *Whoever talks first loses*, demonstrated under the exact pressure that breaks
  it.
