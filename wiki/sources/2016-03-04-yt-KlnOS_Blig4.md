# How did you start your design business? — AMA (2016-03-04)

> **Attribution:** an AMA with design students. Their questions and short answers are marked
> `[ROOM]`; everything substantive is Chris's.

> ⏳ **A 2016 source, and the single richest biographical one ingested in this run.** It contains
> the **fullest Blind founding story** anywhere in the corpus, and what is almost certainly **the
> earliest recorded telling of the mechanic parable** — the framework the entire order-taker /
> diagnosis position rests on.

## Summary

★★★★★ **Biography landmark.** Blind's founding told end to end with specifics the corpus did not
have: **December 1995**, an **uncle's phone call**, a **\$100,000 ask answered with a \$5,000
cheque on the spot**, and a first office in a downtown loft he **slept above**. Then the
**breakthrough moment** he says he consciously kept track of in case he was ever asked. And then
★★★★★ **the mechanic parable**, with the pricing move most later tellings omit — **charge for the
diagnostic.**

## Key claims (2016-03-04)

### ★★★★★ Blind's founding — the fullest telling

- ★★★★★ **The date, precisely:** *"**Blind was started and formed in DECEMBER OF 1995 as a
  corporation.**"* ⚠️ Relevant to the standing 1995-vs-1997 tension on
  [[../persona/biography|biography]] — **this is a specific self-reported incorporation month**,
  not a vague year.
- **Why he left employment:** *"**I had worked at a couple of places — I worked at an ad agency and
  I worked at a PUNK ROCK MUSIC LABEL** — and **I didn't feel like I could express who I was** in
  terms of what I'd learned in school, and to display the skills that I had."*
- ★★★★★ **The uncle's call:** *"I was freelancing over at a really hot broadcast design company…
  and **my uncle reached out to me and said: DO YOU WANT TO START A DESIGN COMPANY?**"* — the
  reason being commercial: *"**he and his partner — he's an attorney — one of his client partners
  owned hotels, and so they needed design services.** I thought: they want to start a design company
  because **it's going to be cheaper to own one than to continually pass the work out.**"*
- **The timing:** *"**I'd only been out of school for probably no more than two and a half
  months.** I think I graduated in the summer."*

### ★★★★★ *The Great Brain* — a formative influence, new to the corpus

> ★★★★★ *"**It's always been my dream. I knew I was going to start a company ever since I was
> probably 11 or 12.** I thought it was going to be like a car-washing business, then it became
> I'm going to sell candy, or ninja stars. **I wanted to hustle.**"*
>
> ★★★★★ *"There's this book called ***The Great Brain***. It takes place in a small Midwestern
> town, and it's of **three brothers**, told from the youngest of the three — and his older brother
> he calls **The Great Brain**, who was always **scheming and hustling the kids out of their
> money.** **And I was like: well, THAT'S A ROLE MODEL FOR ME.** … **Whatever adventure I would read
> in one book, I would just try to apply it in school — and it got me into all sorts of trouble.
> But it was the beginning of that entrepreneurial spirit.**"*

✅ Worth noting alongside [[2026-03-31-yt-i9AqXEjbf8I|2026-03-31]], where he describes himself as
**the middle of three brothers** — the book he took as a template is about three brothers, narrated
by the youngest about the schemer.

### ★★★★★ The business plan, the \$100,000 ask, and the \$5,000 cheque

Asked to prepare a business plan for a dinner meeting, in a world without search:

> ★★★★ *"**I lived in that time when there was no internet, and then there was internet**… **this
> is pre-Google. Search was not even a thing yet.**"* — so he phoned his brother in Silicon Valley
> to ask *"**what is the internet?**"*, then remembered **a former Art Center roommate whose father
> was an investment banker**: *"I didn't even know what that meant, but I knew he knew about
> business."*

> ★★★★★ **The lesson he draws from it, offered as transferable:** *"**whenever you encounter a
> problem and you're looking for an answer — USE THE RESOURCES THAT YOU KNOW. Use the resources
> that are around you.**"*

**At the dinner**, with an eight-page plan written overnight: *"'**How much money do you need?**' …
**I just came up with a wild number, and the number was \$100,000. Completely arbitrary.**"*

> ★★★★ **Except it wasn't quite arbitrary — the reasoning is the useful part:** *"**how much money
> do I need to keep a company going for about six months if we get no business?** Because the
> assumption is **it takes time to ramp, time to get clients, to close clients, to do the work, and
> for there to be cash flow.** … **That's what they call RUNWAY.** I needed a six-month runway —
> **and I was being aggressive asking for more, cuz I knew I couldn't spend \$100,000 in six
> months. There's no way.**"*

> ★★★★★ **What actually happened:** *"**he reaches into his briefcase and pulls out a check. He
> writes a check to me on the spot — not for \$100,000**… **he wrote a check for \$5,000. 5%.** And
> he says: '**I'm going to give this to you as a good-faith gesture that we're going to move
> forward.**' **No contracts. No percentage. We hadn't discussed anything. And that's how the
> company began.**"*

★★★ **And the investor's exit:** *"**within the first year, [he] was no longer part of the
business.**"*

### ★★★★ The first office, and quitting the freelance gig

> ★★★★ *"It was started **in downtown LA, in the Brewery** — a **2,000 square foot space**.
> **I literally slept upstairs, walked down the metal stairway, and the office was there.** It was
> a big wide-open space so it could accommodate a lot of people — **we'd have our friends come over
> and work with us, and random Art Center students would find out about the company and just knock
> on the door and come in and we'd hang out and talk.**"*

★★★★ **The reaction he got when he quit:** he told the broadcast company he wouldn't renew a
two-week booking. *"I told my office mate I'm going to start a company, and they're like: **'you
barely know anything about broadcast design — how are we going to do this?'** **They just look at
me like: CALL US BACK WHEN YOU FAIL.** I knew that's what they were thinking — **and I was
thinking: there's no way I could fail. Let's just go for it.**"*

### ★★★★★ The breakthrough moment — and why he had an answer ready

> ★★★★★ *"**I remember sitting in your seat.** … They would bring studio owners to speak to the
> students, and they'd stand up on stage and present their company, and **I was thinking: gosh,
> what was your breakthrough moment?** … **and people had a hard time articulating that there was a
> moment.** So you're asking me that same question — **so I'm going to give you the answer that I
> think you deserve. I KNOW EXACTLY WHEN THAT MOMENT WAS** … **because I kept in my mind: maybe one
> day somebody's going to ask me that question. I NEED TO KNOW WHEN THAT MOMENT IS.**"*

**The answer:** *"**that moment came very clearly when we did our FIRST NATIONAL TV SPOT** — and
it's hard to get those things. **That was about a year into the business.**"*

★★★★ **The catch-22 it required solving:** *"**they won't give you a big TV campaign unless you've
done one well. So how do I get one?**"* → the chain: a film student he had helped with titles →
who introduced him to editors → who wanted a logo → *"and so **when they were cutting a commercial
they said: can you do this? And of course I said YES I CAN DO THIS.**"*

### ★★★★★ Giving — with an explicit limit

> ★★★★★ *"**I try to be as giving as I can WITHOUT HURTING MYSELF IN THE PROCESS.** I'm going to
> say that one more time: **I try to give as much of myself as I can to others without hurting
> myself — and there IS a point at which you do hurt yourself.** When you're in school and you're
> helping all your friends finish their homework **but then you failed the class — you are hurting
> yourself.**"*

> ★★★★ **What he gives away instead:** *"**what do I usually give away? Because I have to do my
> work — and if I want to help other people, what I give away is SLEEP.**"*

★★★★ **The filter, stated plainly:** *"**I give freely and I don't expect anything.** … But **if
they seem like they're just really into themselves, self-centred — I'm not going to help that
person very much. I get a vibe: you're just going to be a taker.**"*

★★★★ **The story that pays it off:** a scruffy transportation-design student in the computer lab who
*"looked like he was trying to cheat the whole system"* — *"**and I helped him. Turned out he was
an inventor, started a company, multi-millionaire — and then he hired us to do his videos.**"* →
*"**there are these threads that you start, and sometimes they evolve into something and sometimes
they don't.**"*

> ★★★★ **The first-gig problem it answers:** *"at the very beginning you think: how am I going to
> get work? Who do I know? **And you know nobody, and you have no professional experience. Your work
> is all student work, and it looks terrible, and everybody knows it's student work.** … **So
> somewhere out there you have to put enough GOODWILL out there for some of it to come back to
> you.**"*

### ★★★★★ His "law of attraction" — self-selection, not magic

> ★★★★★ *"**You attract people that are like you.** I'm motivated, I work really hard, hopefully
> I've developed a reputation for somebody who works hard, who does what they say, and has an eye
> for design — **so I'm going to attract those kinds of people.** **If you're a beach bum chilling
> in Jamaica — guess what, everybody's just chilling.** You can help a lot of those people, but
> they're not extremely motivated."*

★★★★ **The student behaviour behind it:** *"**if the teacher asks for two comps, I'm going to do
four. If they say read this — I want to read this AND do something else.** I don't want to say I'm
a teacher's pet, but **I have the mentality where I want to excel. I pick the teachers I think I'm
going to learn from, and I give it 1,000%.**"*

★★★ **And an unusually candid aside about how he reads to others.** Told the students were nervous
because he seemed *"scary"*: *"**I don't think I'm scary.** So you see me in a certain way — **this
energy I'm throwing out.** But maybe a CEO entrepreneur type thinks: **'Chris is sharp, he's
determined, he knows what he wants, he's going to tell it to us straight.' And that's the kind of
people I attract.**"* — followed by *"**and if I got that wrong, we'll fix it in the edit.**"*

### ★★★★ Blind's reinvention cycles, as of 2016

> *"We were formed in 1995 — now it's 2016, so **we're on our 21st year of business, and we've had
> to REINVENT OURSELVES THREE OR FOUR TIMES.** **This is the biggest reinvention the company has
> ever gone through.**"*

The sequence he gives: **graphic design** → **motion** (*"what you guys call motion design now, but
it wasn't even a term back then"*) → storyboarding and *"how to tell stories through sequence"* →
*"the first phase was we were only animating titles"* → **character animation** → **live action in
service of the graphics.**

★★★★ **And how they won live-action work before they could do it:** *"**we had to prove ourselves
every single time we got on the call that we can do the live action — and truthfully we were NOT
ready to do live action. But we did it anyway, and we learned each time and did a little bit
better, and then it became a natural thing.**"*

★★★ *"**I was using version 1.0 for most software. That's how old I am.**"*

## ★★★★★ THE MECHANIC PARABLE — the earliest recorded telling

**The setup.** A squeaky sound; the customer says *"I think it's the rotors"*; the mechanic swaps
them for **\$750**; the squeak persists; the customer returns; it turns out to be a **\$50 pin.**

> ★★★★★ **He polls the room — "whose fault is it?" — and then inverts them:** *"**whatever you're
> going to say, I'm going to say the opposite.** We assume it's the customer's fault — **he came in,
> told you what he wanted, and you obliged. YOU'RE AN ORDER TAKER. You don't think. YOU DON'T
> DIAGNOSE. Did you really solve the problem?**"*

> ★★★★★ **The medical version, which makes the stakes obvious:** *"imagine the same thing if you
> went to see a doctor: '**I have cancer**.' '**Okay, let's operate, let's cut that thing out,
> we're going to give you chemo**' — **when it's just a tummy ache.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The principle:** *"**we need to be educated in DIAGNOSING — talking and understanding
> what the problems are — BEFORE WE GO INTO SOLUTIONS MODE.**"* — against the grain of the training:
> *"**we're very good at making, and we're proud of our making and crafting ability. That's the
> pride you have as design students.**"*

★★★★★ **The rewritten script — and note the pricing move most later tellings drop:**

> ★★★★★ *"'**You think you need rotors — but what's the problem?**' … '**Before we change the
> rotors, I need to make sure. I'm going to do some diagnostics. IT'S GOING TO COST YOU MONEY, CUZ
> I DON'T DO DIAGNOSTICS FOR FREE. So I'm going to charge you \$60 — and that will be A CREDIT
> APPLIED to whatever it is you want to do. But YOU DON'T NEED TO DO IT WITH ME. You can do it with
> whoever.**' … '**I spotted the problem — it's a \$50 pin. So all you owe me is the \$60. And it's
> not going to cost you \$700.**'"*

> ★★★★★ **The outcome he's buying:** *"**how would you feel? 'This guy is really honest. He could
> have sold me all this other nonsense, but he didn't. He did what he was supposed to do — AND HE'S
> AN EXPERT. I came here to hire an expert.'**"*

★★★★ *"**We're afraid as creative people to just ask questions — and we're not taught these
tools.**"*

## ★★★★★ Order taker → consultant (the 2016 repositioning)

> ★★★★★ *"**The biggest transition we've gone through is that we're moving away from being a
> SERVICE PROVIDER** — a highly paid, highly sought-after service provider is really what we are —
> **and we want to move towards the CONSULTING side. Just like that story I told you about the
> mechanic.** … **We are positioning ourselves as BUSINESS DESIGN CONSULTANTS.**"*

★★★★ **The mechanism:** *"I have to engage you in what we call a **DISCOVERY SESSION**. Some people
call it strategy — **that's a loaded word for me**, so I'll just call it discovery."*

> ★★★★★ **And the willingness to talk the client out of the deliverable:** *"sometimes you go in
> and you think 'we want you to produce a video for us' — **when in truth nobody's going to watch
> the video.** That's when we say: '**based on all the things we've learned, do you think a video is
> more effective than, say, a website?**' And they say: '**well no — we want the website.**'"*

> ★★★★★ **The summary:** *"**we went from 'give me the rotor' to 'change the pin.' I charge for the
> diagnostic part. I DON'T PRESCRIBE SOLUTIONS UNTIL I UNDERSTAND THE PROBLEM. And I create value
> as an expert. I'M NO LONGER AN ORDER TAKER.**"*

★★★★ **With the client's right to refuse it, and his own honesty clause:** *"**not all clients want
that, and I understand that.** In those cases I say: '**okay, fine — you don't want to do
diagnostics, you just want to pay me. And I would do that. I CANNOT GUARANTEE YOU RESULTS.** If
you're okay with that, **I'm happy to take your money. I'm still being honest and true to who I am,
because ultimately that's not my decision — it's YOUR decision.**'"*

## ★★★★ "Creative people are the worst people to work for"

A strong, dated 2016 opinion — recorded with the caveat he attaches to it.

> ★★★★★ **The core reason:** *"**creative types tend to NOT VALUE THEIR OWN CREATIVE TIME. It's
> really weird. So guess what — THEY DON'T VALUE YOUR TIME EITHER.**"* — with the litany:
> *"'can you just change this?' · 'that's not really that hard, is it?' · 'I'm used to doing
> proposals for free' · 'how do I know what I want?' · 'I need to see a lot of examples, cuz I'm not
> sure either' · 'are you sure that's the tool you want to use?'"*

> ★★★★★ *"**They undervalue what it is that they do, and they transfer it to you — because they
> don't see DIAGNOSING, talking about the problem, as valuable. It's not what you studied in
> school.**"*

★★★★ **And he turns it on his own peers:** *"**I have friends who sit around all day and give free
advice to people, and they're happy to do a logo job at the end of it. So they'll spend weeks
giving free advice. THE ADVICE ISN'T FREE. It has an impact on your business. I helped you make
money. I helped you avoid a catastrophic business decision.**"*

★★★ **Why he prefers business clients:** *"**they're logical.** I can say 'this is out of scope' or
'we're going to move the deadline' **and they understand, because they have experience with that.
Their expectations are more realistic.**"* — with the fairness clause: *"**I'm not saying all
business people are good, nor are all artists difficult to work with.**"*

★★★★ **The premise he shares with them:** *"**you're in business to be in business. You have to make
money, you have to charge for things. NOBODY DOES WORK FOR FREE. NOTHING OF VALUE IS FREE. You get
what you pay for.**"* → and the discovery pitch: *"**you spend [a small amount] with us and it's a
\$100,000 project — it's money well spent to make sure that AFTER you spend the \$100,000, you're in
the right place. You didn't just get in your car and start driving. You had a plan.**"*

## ⚠️ A dated industry read (2016)

Recorded as a **dated prediction**, not a standing view: *"**the industry is not going in a great
place. It's trending downwards.** People don't watch commercials anymore — **and that is our biggest
segment.**"* He describes pre-roll as actively counterproductive (*"**I'm angry at the company…
you're interrupting what it is I'm doing**"*), distinguishes **lean-back TV from lean-in internet**,
and cites a Google creative officer — hedged, *"I think… I'm pretty sure that's where it came
from"* — for *"**advertising is dead. Nobody wants to be sold anything.** **The future is
ENTERTAINMENT and EDUCATION: teach me how to use your product, and entertain me, and I'll make my
own decision.**"* → hence explainer videos and shareable ads, where *"**you don't need to buy the
media.**"*

★★★★ **And the structural threat he names:** agencies building in-house motion studios, *"**which
leaves studio owners like myself COMPETING AGAINST THE INTERNAL TEAM. And that's a horrible place to
be.**"* — grounded in his own experience: in-house, *"**I wasn't looked at as an expert… I was
looked at as AN INTERNAL RESOURCE AT THE END OF A DECISION-MAKING CYCLE.** … **You become a really
good order taker.**"*
