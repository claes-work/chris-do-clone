## ★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★ The failure mode, seen from the employer's chair

> ★★★★ *"**Being on this side of the fence, being an employer, I've seen it done lots of ways. Hardly have
> I ever seen it done the right way.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"Most people ask for a raise and they get it all wrong… **they just talk about themselves.** What
> they've done, that they showed up on time. And essentially **they're just describing WHAT THEY WERE PAID
> TO DO.** From an employer, I'm just thinking: **well, yeah, that's why you have a job. Why are you
> telling me the things that otherwise I'm going to FIRE you?**"*

✅ `ask for a raise` returns **0** corpus-wide. ⚠️ **The diagnosis is the valuable half**: the standard
pitch describes fulfilled obligations and mistakes them for added value.

### ★★★★★ ⭐ The script

> ★★★★★ *"Hey boss, I've been doing this, I hope you think I've been doing a good job — I think I have.
> And **I'd like to make more money. WHAT DO I NEED TO DO TO EARN MORE MONEY FROM YOUR POINT OF VIEW? And
> if I meet that criteria, would you consider giving me a raise at that point?**"*

✅ `what do I need to do to earn` returns **0**. ⚠️ **Three things are doing the work**, and he names them:
it is **disarming** (*"nobody says that"*), it is **conversational rather than a demand**, and it produces
**objective criteria**. ✅ Pei's own report after the second run is the evidence he uses: *"**now it's more
of a conversation of how to go about doing this than me demanding.**"*

> ★★★★ *"One is **an expectant, entitled demand.** The other is: **what can I do for you?** And you're
> looking at it from their point of view."*

⚠️ ✅ **Note the consistency**: this is the same move as the documented *"why don't you just ask"* — replace
guessing (or asserting) with a question that makes the other party state the criteria.

### ★★★★ Make abstract language tangible — then get it in writing

> ★★★★ *"If it's vague — if I say **I want you to be a better designer** — well, what does that mean to
> you? **Your 'better' is going to be different than mine.** So what we have to do is **take abstract
> language and make it more tangible.**"*

✅ `abstract language` returns **2**, neither on raises. ⚠️ **He models the employer's answer** with
verifiable conditions: take two named courses, read a specific book, gain a certification, *"win two awards
within the categories that matter to me — that's certifiable, that's real"*, deliver jobs within a stated
cost percentage, and **teach two junior designers**. 📌 **The last one is the tell**: the criteria he
invents for himself are about **expanding the role**, not improving the craft — *"you have to take on more
responsibility if you want to make more money."*

> ★★★★ *"And then **follow it up in an email**: boss, we talked, **these are the four things I'm going to
> work on — and HOW WILL WE KNOW WE GET THERE?**"*

### ★★★ The trap in "more responsibility"

> ★★★ *"A lot of people come in and say: well, I've done these new responsibilities. **The problem is that
> those responsibilities — though you've added more things — aren't viewed as being helpful or valuable to
> the company.**"* ⚠️ i.e. **self-assigned extra work does not count**; the criteria have to be agreed in
> advance. ✅ This is why the script front-loads the question.
