# How do I get more work & scale my design business? (2017-02-04)

> **Attribution:** a one-to-one coaching session. The coachee is a studio owner with partners; his
> lines are marked `[COACHEE]` and are **not** persona data. Chris is coaching throughout, so the
> substantive content here **is** his.

> ⏳ **A 2017 source, and almost certainly the earliest recorded telling of PRICE BRACKETING** —
> taught live, with the reasoning, the follow-through, and his own escalating brackets disclosed.

## Summary

★★★★★ A pricing masterclass delivered as coaching. **Bracket the number, then stop talking** — with
the sharpest argument in the corpus for why the silence matters: *if you keep talking after the
number, I can hear that you don't believe it, and I'll take the job off you for more.* It also
contains his own account of **what he is actually good at**, and an unusually plain admission that
he had not yet solved the problem he was being asked about.

## Key claims (2017-02-04)

### ★★★★★ Price bracketing — taught live

`[COACHEE]` quotes a flat *"our minimum level of engagement is \$10,000."*

> ★★★★★ *"**I want you to make a few tweaks to what you say.** … **You're going to bracket a price.
> And I don't want you to say 10 anymore — I want you to say BETWEEN 10 TO 20.** And there's a
> reason why. **This is called PRICE BRACKETING: so 10 looks like a smaller number next to 20.** It
> allows them to say: **'well, he'll probably come in at 15.'**"*

★★★★ **And what to say if challenged:** *"if they say '**why is that?**' — it's: '**well, I don't
know what you need yet, and this is what I charge.**'"*

### ★★★★★ Then shut up — and why

> ★★★★★ *"**And then I want you to just BE QUIET. I don't want you to quick-pivot** — because
> [pivoting] says: **'I'm not totally comfortable with that number just yet.'** **Just let them be
> uncomfortable with the silence, and you just sit there and chill.**"*

★★★★ **The framing he gives it:** *"**it's kind of like the law from God. I just read it off the
tablet — the stone tablets from Moses — and that was it.** '**Is this something you're interested
in? If you're not, [I] don't want to waste your time.**' **And just move on.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The argument for the silence, and it is the best version in the corpus:** *"**if I ask you
> for a price and you say 'oh, it's this' — and then you TALK — I just get the feeling that you're
> not confident about the number. THAT'S WHEN I COME IN, SAY MY NUMBER, AND I'LL GET IT FOR MORE
> THAN YOU. Because you just have to say it, and be okay with saying it.**"*

★★★★ **With his own discomfort admitted, unprompted:** *"**it's funny too, cuz I'm not really that
comfortable saying the numbers all the time.** I don't totally avoid it… **but it is hard sometimes
to ask for money.**"* → and the belief that carries him through it: *"**in my heart of hearts, I
know whatever I charge the client, IT'S WORTH WAY MORE THAN WHAT I CHARGE.**"*

### ★★★★★ The exact script he dictates

> ★★★★★ *"'**I charge between \$10,000 to \$20,000 to do a discovery session. That's one day's worth
> of work. After which I'll give you documentation, and I will help you define your brand, who your
> customers are, and help you prioritise your goals in alignment with your business goals. If that
> sounds like something you'd be interested in doing, let me know.**' **And then I shut up. I don't
> say anything.** '**Do you have any questions?**' That's it."*

★★★ **Travel is not absorbed:** *"**I also don't like the idea that in some cases you pay for your
own travel. THAT HAS TO BE EXTRA.**"* → *"'**I don't need to fly first class — but you do need to
put me up, and I'll fly out with my team.**'"*

### ★★★★★ His own brackets, disclosed and escalating

> ★★★★★ *"**The numbers that I quote for people now for doing strategy work is BETWEEN 30 TO 50 —
> and soon it'll be 30 to 80, and then 50 to 100. And I'm just going to KEEP BUMPING OUT THE
> BRACKETS** — because **the more of this we do, the more PROOF I have** in case somebody wants to
> say 'well, I'm not sure.' I'm like: okay, well, you can look at these companies."*

✅ Note the mechanism: the bracket rises **as the evidence accumulates**, not as confidence
inflates.

### ★★★★ On predictability, and what actually buys it

- ★★★★ *"**In order to get a more predictable business, you have to PERFECT THE MODEL.** … **when
  you do it a dozen times, then you know: I know all the trappings, I know where all the objections
  are going to be, I know where it's going to fail, I know how to succeed.**"*
- ★★★★★ **The warning attached to it:** *"**one of the things that can kill you more than anything
  else is TO BECOME DISTRACTED WHILE YOUR GOLD MINE COULD STILL BE RIGHT IN FRONT OF YOU — and you
  don't even know it yet.**"*
- ★★★★ **Why the only lever is price:** *"**the only way you're going to be able to do that is
  CHARGE MORE** — because the way it sounds, you're already working a lot… **so you can't do more of
  it. The only way is bigger clients or bigger engagements.**"*

★★★★ **A qualified view of retainers**, which the corpus otherwise records him favouring: *"that's
an excellent strategy when the client says '**I'm not ready to go the full distance**.' **But I
don't like that — because at any point in time this thing could fall apart. It's a six-month
retainer, but they can back out, or I can back out, within 30 days' notice. SO THAT'S NOT A LOT OF
PREDICTABILITY** so you can plan and hire and get help."*

★★★★ **Runway, extended to the team:** *"**I like to have runway. I'd like to see you have 3 to 6
months runway — not just for you, but for your ENTIRE TEAM.** So you need to go and calculate what
that's going to be."*

★★★★ **And why he keeps making things:** *"**I don't shy away from the deliverables — or what you
call the executables — because THAT'S HOW I CAN KEEP A TEAM GOING**, in a way that doesn't kill you
where you're [only] managing the project."*

### ★★★★★ "I'm working on it myself" — an unusually plain admission

Asked how to get to pure strategy at scale, he describes the alternative — being embedded deeply
enough that *"they're flying you out and you have multiple strategists working at different
sites"* — notes that large management consultancies charge *"a million, 2 million, 5, 10 million"*
for that work, and then says:

> ★★★★★ *"**How you get there, I don't know. I'M WORKING ON IT MYSELF. I'll let you know as soon as
> I get there myself.** But I can see that there's a lot of room for us to grow."*

### ★★★★★ What he thinks his actual skill is

> ★★★★★ *"**Large multinational corporations pay millions of dollars just to reassure and reaffirm
> that they're moving in the right direction. THEY WANT TO BE HEARD** — and management is not
> talking to middle management, middle management is not talking to people on the front line, people
> on the front line aren't talking to customers. **It's just all this miscommunication.**"*
>
> ★★★★★ *"**So one of my skills, as it turns out, is I'M A GOOD LISTENER AND I CAN SYNTHESISE
> INFORMATION. I can repackage it so people can then HEAR THEIR OWN THOUGHTS SAID MORE CLEARLY THAN
> THEY COULD HAVE SAID IT.**"*

✅ **His own account of his core competence**, and it is not design, selling or teaching — it is
**listening and reflecting back.** Worth carrying: it explains the shape of every workshop and hot
seat in this corpus.
