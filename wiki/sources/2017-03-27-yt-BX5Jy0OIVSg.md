# How do you balance work and life? — Otis College of Art and Design (2017-03-27)

> **Attribution:** a talk/Q&A to an Otis College audience. Chris speaks almost throughout; audience
> members supply single words when he asks (*"family"*, *"sleep"*, *"video games"*, *"drums"*) and
> one attendee identifies himself as an office manager. **Everything substantive is Chris's.**

> ⚠️ **PRIVACY — subject-specific rule applies.** He talks about his wife and his two sons in this
> talk. **Per `SUBJECT.md`, family members' names are not public and are never recorded** — none are
> spoken here in any case. What is recorded below is only the **structural** point he is making
> (that family is inside the work, not walled off from it) and the **already-established** fact that
> he has a wife and two sons. Household detail, characterisations of his wife's daily life, and the
> children's ages are **deliberately not carried into the wiki or the persona.**

## Summary

★★★★★ The clearest statement in the corpus of **work–life integration over work–life balance**,
built on a four-quadrant model, and — unusually — told **against his own past**: he ran Blind on
strict work–life *separation* and says plainly that it *"creates all sorts of problems."* The second
half is a rare, concrete account of **how The Futur was actually managed in 2017**, and it closes
with a rapid-fire hobby list that is some of the best character material in the corpus.

## Key claims (2017-03-27)

### ★★★★★ Against balance, and against moderation

He opens by asking who taught the room *"everything in moderation"* — the answer is *"my parents"* —
and then contradicts it:

> ★★★★★ *"**I don't believe in work-life balance, and I don't believe everything in moderation. My
> expression is: EVERYTHING IN MODERATION LEADS TO A MEDIOCRE LIFE.** Because you just do a little
> bit — **you have to go intense and you have to go extreme.**"*

★★★★ **With the scope of it fenced immediately:** *"**I'm not talking about the gambling and all the
other stuff — drugs and whatever else — but I'm talking about YOUR PASSION, YOUR ART. You have to be
extreme about it.**"*

★★★★ **And what he thinks the alternative actually is:** *"**this is NOT a 9-to-5 situation** where
you kind of show up and it's 5 o'clock and — yeah, creativity, turn it off, I'm all good, I've got
to watch *Friends*… **This is the life you've CHOSEN. You can choose different things — but this is
the life you've chosen.**"*

### ★★★★★ The four quadrants

> ★★★★★ *"**I don't believe in work-life balance. I believe in work-life INTEGRATION.**"*

Built live with the room, the quadrants are: **work** (*"what you do for money"*) · **passion and
talent** (*"what you love to do"*) · **recreation / play** (*"what you have fun doing"*) · **the rest
of your life** (family; an attendee adds sleep).

- ★★★★ **The default state he describes** is the quadrants as separate small circles — *"by day
  [he] works as an office manager, by night he's going bananas on the drums"* — and where they
  overlap, *"you're a happy guy."*
- ★★★★★ **The move:** *"**there's no limit as to how big the circles can get.** So when this circle
  becomes this big around what you do at work and what you do in play — **the stuff in the middle is
  the stuff that makes you really happy. And it turns out, IT'S MY BELIEF, THAT THAT'S WHAT MAKES
  YOU REALLY VALUABLE TOO.**"*
- ★★★★ **The failure mode:** *"**a lot of us just pursue this and neglect these things — and WE
  MIGHT MAKE MONEY, BUT WE RUIN OUR RELATIONSHIPS, and it's really boring, because we're missing
  these parts.**"*

⚠️ He credits the quadrant model to someone he names in passing; **the auto-captions garble the name
beyond recovery** (*"I'm going to steal this from —"*). **Left unattributed rather than guessed.**

### ★★★★★ Told against his own past — Blind ran on separation

> ★★★★★ *"**I used to just run Blind and that's all I was doing — so it was ALL ABOUT WORK.** So
> when I was at home, and people didn't understand this about me: **don't call me at home, I don't
> respond to your emails** — and they get all bent out of shape. It's like: **I gave you my time at
> work. Now I'm at home. And I TRY NOT TO LET THOSE TWO WORLDS BLEED. And it creates ALL SORTS OF
> PROBLEMS, as you can imagine.**"*

★★★★ **And the symmetry he admits:** at work he didn't want to be called from home either. *"**So
there's work-life SEPARATION.**"* ✅ **He is describing his own former position as the thing he now
argues against** — a clean, dated reversal.

### ★★★★★ The Futur as the integration — and a dated prediction

> ★★★★★ *"**Now I have this company called The Futur, and The Futur is ALL THE THINGS I LOVE, ALL
> COMBINED TOGETHER AT THE SAME TIME** — and it will become, I think, **the most valuable part of our
> company. And I believe it'll become like a \$100 MILLION COMPANY.**"*

⏳ ✅ **A hard, dated, falsifiable prediction — March 2017.** Worth flagging for whatever the corpus
later records about The Futur's actual size.

★★★★ **What it combines, in his words:** *"**my passion for teaching, for learning, for sharing, for
helping others — empowering others to create and live their dreams**"* — plus *"**the things that I
have a talent for: making videos, doing design, teaching. So all these things are getting SMASHED
TOGETHER.**"*

★★★★ **On how long it took:** *"**it's taking me a little while to get there — I'm in my 40s.** So
it's taking me a while to figure this out."* ⏳ Consistent with the birth year on
[[../persona/biography|biography]].

⚠️ **Family:** he describes his household as part of that integration, and mentions that one of his
sons had begun helping with live production after school. **Per the privacy rule, no further detail
is recorded.** The transferable claim is the parenting principle, which he states in general terms:

> ★★★★ *"**I don't really care what they want to do in terms of work. I'm just looking at: what
> makes up you, and what are you good at — and thinking about HOW I CAN NURTURE THAT, and just let
> them be whoever they want to be, and NOT TO WORRY ABOUT THE SEPARATION.**"*

### ★★★★★ How The Futur was actually run in 2017

- ★★★ **The structure:** *"**very loose structure. We have a general start time and a general end
  time — that's 10am to 7pm. Other than that I leave people to do what they need to do.**"*
- > ★★★★★ *"**A sign of a good leader is somebody who TELLS YOU WHAT THE GOAL IS — is very clear
  > about the goal — BUT LEAVES YOU ALONE to let you figure out how to get to the goal.**"*
- ★★★★★ **The micromanagement failure, described as a mechanism rather than a complaint:** *"imagine
  if every hour I came by — '**hey, how's that frame?**' — **every time, he's thinking WHAT DOES HE
  WANT, and he stops thinking about what he's supposed to do. THE TALENT THAT BROUGHT HIM IN THE DOOR
  IS NOW GETTING CLAMPED DOWN, and that's shutting off his creativity.** So at a certain point he
  becomes so shell-shocked, **he now just thinks: I'm going to just do whatever, so I can get him to
  tell me what to do. WE DON'T WORK WELL WITH THOSE KINDS OF PEOPLE.**"*
- ★★★★ **The protocol instead:** *"here's what we need, this is what the client asked for. **Do you
  understand? How much time do you need? Is it reasonable to get this thing done in 3 days?** And you
  say yes — **then that's what we work towards.** I'll check in on you from time to time; **if you
  need me, come find me.** But in 3 days I expect it to be done. **There's no babysitting, no
  helicoptering — we do not have that kind of time.**"*
- ★★★★ **The one exception — the redirect:** *"**from time to time I do sit down and say: I've
  noticed you're doing this, there's a couple of other ways I want you to think about how to do it.
  And I'll sit down with you and we'll sketch and we'll draw and we'll talk — AND I'LL ASK YOU LOTS
  OF QUESTIONS,** hopefully to **recharge you and redirect you.**"* ✅ Even the intervention is
  questions.
- ★★★★★ **The metaphor for the whole approach:** *"**all I want to do is put the GUIDELINES ON THE
  FIELD, so you don't go out of [bounds] — and within that, IT'S VAST AREAS FOR YOU TO EXPLORE.**"*
- ★★★★ **A named authority:** *"**as we found out from DANIEL PINK — AUTONOMY is what makes you more
  creative, allows you to solve more complex problems.**"*
- ★★★★ **Why the office does dress-up days:** *"**we don't want you to start to BOX YOURSELF IN. We
  want you to be OKAY WITH MAKING MISTAKES, MAKING A FOOL OF YOURSELF, AND NOT TAKING YOURSELF
  SERIOUSLY AT ALL.**"*

### ★★★★ On judging a company culture — with his own limits stated

*"**To be honest, I've worked at very few companies in my life, for a very short period of time — so
I can only guess.**"* → the advice: *"**go out and see with open eyes, and then go to another company
and have context, and then you can compare. Because the first company you go to work for, you're
going to think THAT'S the best company, that's the only way it can be done. It might have been. But
it might not.**"* → *"**this is where you trust your gut: 'you know, I feel kind of funny — I'm here
4 nights in a row until 4am and everybody's still here. Maybe there's a different way.'**"*

### ★★★★★ The rapid-fire list — character material

Asked what he does for play, *"my list of activities and hobbies is GINORMOUS":*

- **Comic books** — *"**Marvel over DC.**"*
- **Skateboarding** — *"I used to be really into skateboarding, so that's part of my past."*
- **Volleyball.** **Drawing.** **First-person shooters.**
- ★★★★ **MMA, from the beginning:** *"I've been watching the UFC from **UFC number one**. I have a
  massive video collection **that I paid for** — **Dana White, I've sent you a lot of money, my
  friend** — because **I ordered, legitimately, every single pay-per-view. A couple hundred of
  them.**"*
- ★★★★★ **Collecting, and the ethic under it:** *"**I'm a collector. I like to collect and buy
  things. And the thing I like to collect and buy the most are BOOKS. If I see a book I think might
  be good, I'M THAT GUY AT THE BOOKSTORE — I ACTUALLY BUY IT.** I buy music, I buy things — and **I
  BELIEVE IN BUYING THINGS TO SUPPORT THE PEOPLE WHO CREATE THOSE THINGS.**"*
- ★★★★ **The Tower Records story:** someone seeing his stack of CDs said *"I can't believe you're
  buying it"* — *"**yeah, I'm buying it. How else is the artist going to [make it]? How's this
  industry going to keep going?**"* → with his own dry coda: *"**but we know the end of that story
  already. That doesn't work, right.**"*
- ★★★★★ **The self-diagnosis** — prompted by Skyrim: *"**it's a very addictive game and I try to stay
  away from it, because I'M A DEEP DIVER. Whatever I do, I do with ALL OF MY HEART — and then I'm
  bored and I move on to the next thing.**"*
- ★★★★★ **The illustration he immediately supplies:** *"I like fishing — and I'll tell you the kind
  of crazy person I am. **When I was into fishing, I started thinking about ORDERING A KIT TO RAISE
  MY OWN EARTHWORMS.** That's kind of a nutjob — I am. **I started calculating all this… I want to
  tie my own lures** — and **I bought like every book and just [kept] reading, BECAUSE I LIKE TO
  LEARN.**"*

### ★★★★★ The teaching rule he set himself

> ★★★★★ *"**All my life I've wanted to teach.** I told myself when I was going to **Art Center**:
> **you're going to be a teacher one day. It just doesn't feel right to teach right away — GO DO
> SOMETHING WITH YOUR LIFE FIRST.** I had these weird conversations with myself. I said: **the rule
> has to be — YOU CANNOT TEACH UNTIL FIVE YEARS AFTER YOU'VE GRADUATED.**"*

★★★★★ **And the theory behind the rule, which is the real content:** *"**if you're an amazing
instructor, what's the highest percentage of knowledge I can extract from you? 70% — and I'm a good
student. Some bad students, 20%.** So I always thought it was a little strange when a student
graduates and then teaches that course again: **YOU'VE ADDED NOTHING. I CAN NEVER BE BETTER THAN MY
TEACHER in that one discipline.** But **if I go out and I learn about architecture, interior design,
or about business — I smash those — then maybe ALL MY PARTS ARE MORE THAN THE TEACHER**, and then you
have a shot of gaining that knowledge as well."*

★★★★ **The close:** *"**I love running businesses, I really do. I like seeing people grow. I like
coaching and teaching.** … **And I love to learn. NOTHING MAKES ME HAPPIER THAN TO DO SOMETHING NEW
THAT I DIDN'T DO YESTERDAY. I'm not saying it's new to the world — just NEW TO ME. I'm just really
happy then. And THAT'S WHAT FUELS MY FIRE.**"*
