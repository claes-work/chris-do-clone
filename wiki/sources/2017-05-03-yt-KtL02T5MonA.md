## Summary

★★★★★ **Low speech share, unusually high biographical value.** Blair Enns is one of the most-cited
influences in this corpus, and this is the episode where Chris says **what the book actually did to
him** — including a documented causal link between reading it and **wanting out of the commercial
motion-design industry**, and a rare, self-deprecating admission that one of his own best-known
client lines came out of Blair's book rather than his own head.

## ★★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★★ The cold open — "I did read it in a book"

He frames the episode with a story against himself:

> ★★★★★ *"**Two years ago I was in Hawaii giving a talk** — in front of a whole gathering of
> advertising folks, for the **Paley award show** — and during my talk we got into this conversation
> about **what do you do when clients prescribe solutions to you**… to which I responded: **this is
> what I say to my clients: 'YOU'RE PROBABLY RIGHT — BUT LET'S MAKE SURE.'** And afterwards **my
> fellow panellists came up to me and said: that was a great piece of advice. They don't teach this
> stuff in school — it's not like you could read it in a book, right Chris? — and I SMILED A LITTLE
> GUILTY SMILE and I said: well, actually, I did read it in a book.**"*

✅ **A signature client line, sourced.** *"You're probably right, but let's make sure"* appears across
this corpus as Chris's own move; **here he attributes it to Blair's book, unprompted, on his own
show.** He follows it with: *"**one of the seminal books that have changed the way I do business —
and if you think I'm really smooth, YOU'VE GOT TO GO TO THE SOURCE.**"*

### ★★★★★ How the book changed his business — the causal link

> ★★★★★ *"This is one of those **sensational titles** — *The Win Without Pitching Manifesto* —
> because **in our line of work, in terms of a motion design studio, IT'S KIND OF THE COST OF ENTRY:
> you have to pitch for advertising commercial work, at least in the United States.** And I was
> thinking: **there's no way this book can tell me I can't pitch. These are the rules that are
> written — [otherwise] you can't enter the game.** So **with some kind of RESISTANCE I read the
> book — AND IT REALLY TRANSFORMED MY THINKING.** And as I thought at first: **this is perhaps not
> applicable to this industry that we're in — BUT IT'S APPLICABLE TO EVERYTHING ELSE I'M DOING. AND
> I WANT TO GET OUT OF THAT INDUSTRY. So that began to spark.**"*

✅ **Biography-grade.** A dated, first-person account of a specific book contributing to his decision
to leave commercial motion design. ⚠️ He says it *"began to spark"* — **a contributing cause he
names himself, not a sole cause**, and it should be recorded with that hedge intact.

### ★★★★ How he actually reads

> ★★★★ *"**People recommend books to me all the time, and admittedly I'M NOT A PROLIFIC READER —
> like I sit down and read bits, and then I get distracted, and then I lose my place, and I start
> over.** So I go ahead and order your book, and then **IT SITS ON MY SHELF FOR A WHILE**, and then
> somebody mentions it again — **it takes a little bit of prodding for me. I'm not that quick with
> that kind of stuff.**"*

> ⚠️ **Reconciles with, rather than contradicts, [[2017-03-27-yt-BX5Jy0OIVSg|2017-03-27]]** — where
> he says *"the thing I like to collect and buy the most are books… I'm that guy at the bookstore,
> I ACTUALLY BUY IT."* **He buys prolifically; he does not read prolifically.** Both statements are
> from the same six-week window, so the distinction is his, not the curator's.

### ★★★★★ His own pricing rule, stated plainly

> ★★★★★ *"**My trick is always to CHARGE AS MUCH MONEY AS THE CLIENT IS WILLING TO PAY — AND MORE.**
> And that way, when they call me… **I love what you say about the TELEPHONE TEST: if they call you,
> are you happy to answer it or not.** So **I try to get in that position.**"*

★★★★ **And why charging more is a service, not a grab:** *"I love in the book how you talk about
**one of the motivations for charging more is TO PROVIDE BETTER CUSTOMER SERVICE**… because **THE
NATURE OF THE CREATIVE PERSON IS TO TRY TO GIVE MORE OF THEMSELVES, TO OVER-DELIVER — and the
conflict comes in when they feel like they're being TAKEN ADVANTAGE OF.**"* ⚠️ The telephone test and
the charge-more-to-serve-better argument are **Blair's**; Chris's own contribution is the
*"as much as they're willing to pay, and more"* rule and the over-delivery diagnosis.

### ★★★★ Guilt about charging for what you love

> ★★★★ *"**I think some of them FEEL GUILTY FOR CHARGING MONEY FOR SOMETHING THAT THEY TRULY
> ENJOY.** Like — there's a hobby of mine — **I like fishing, I like SALMON FISHING — and for me to
> charge somebody to do what I love seems like a strange concept.**"*

✅ **Corroborates the fishing hobby** disclosed six weeks earlier at
[[2017-03-27-yt-BX5Jy0OIVSg|2017-03-27]], and specifies it as **salmon fishing**.

★★★★ **His read of his own audience's resistance**, which he puts to Blair as a challenge:
*"**people are just holding on very tightly to this idea that THEIR ART IS EVERYTHING, and
SPECIALISING IS GOING TO KILL THEIR CREATIVE SOUL; that charging money is going to CHEAPEN the entire
process, devalue what they do as a creative human spirit.** How do you respond to that? **Give me
your tough-love coach talk here — say something that's going to really wake them up.**"*

### ★★★★ On positioning — "preaching to the choir"

Of Blair's pick-a-door argument for niching: *"**you're preaching to the choir, because I FULLY
BELIEVE IN THAT.**"* ✅ Consistent with the market-one-thing position the corpus records from 2016
onward — **but note the direction of influence is unstated here.**

### ★★★★ Two things he says about himself in 2017

- ★★★★ **On books he hasn't written:** *"**I have ideas for books, but I've not taken that step.
  I'VE NOT PASSED THE TIPPING POINT ON THAT.**"* ⏳ A dated marker, useful against whatever the
  corpus later records about his own publishing.
- ★★★★★ **Blair's "no exit" constraint visibly lands:** *"**that's awesome — and now YOU'RE MAKING
  ME RETHINK MY APPROACH here. But it's a lot of food for thought.**"* ⏳ A dated moment of him
  publicly reconsidering his own exit thinking, on his own show.
- ★★★★ **On saying what you think:** *"**I love that idea, AND I PRACTISE THAT MYSELF.**"*

### ★★★ Hosting register

- ★★★ **He interrupts his own interview to flag importance to the viewer:** *"**now, you may want to
  listen close, because what Blair is about to say is REALLY IMPORTANT.**"* ✅ A documented hosting
  habit, not a claim.
- ★★★ **He runs the back half as a "lightning round"** of business questions — model, minimum

  FUNNEL, at how the CUSTOMER JOURNEY comes to you and then ultimately why they make a purchase.**"*
- ★★★ **He credits Blair with practising what he preaches:** *"**you too have had to make the tough
  choice of being a consultant or a productised business — you're following your own advice.** I can
  see it all over your site: **you write a lot, and you establish your expertise that way. You've
  chosen a niche; you're the expert for which there's no replacement.**"*

## ⚠️ Guest material — context only, NOT persona data

Recorded here in compressed form; **the home for all of it is
[[../entities/blair-enns|blair-enns]]**, which the next synthesis pass should extend.

- **Win Without Pitching** — a sales-training organisation for creative professionals; Blair
  describes himself as *"a recovering consultant"* who pivoted a solo consulting practice into a
  **productised training company** about three years earlier.
- **The book**: published **July 2010**, in its **fourth printing** by 2017; its genesis was a
  December 2006 post, *"Twelve Resolutions for the New Year."* His intent: *"you'd be able to read
  this in two hours on an airplane, put it down, and **be determined to see a different way**… **I
  wanted RESOLVE**"* — and *"**it's my full intention that that book will OUTLIVE ME.**"*
- **On writing books at all:** *"**you don't just need to have something to say — YOU NEED TO HAVE A
  POINT OF VIEW. And if you don't have a point of view, there is no point in publishing your
  book.**"* Plus a novelist friend's warning: *"**you can't unpublish.**"*
  > ⚠️ **Forward link added 2026-07-30:** Chris uses the phrase ***"you can't unpublish a book"*** himself
  > at [[2025-12-22-yt-Zo_SxRuUGjQ]] — as the stated reason his *Unbland* workbook is **digital-first** —
  > **crediting no one.** ⚠️ **Possible transmission from this interview, NOT established**; the idea is
  > simple enough to reach independently. Recorded so the question survives.
- ⏳ **A forthcoming book, unnamed date**: *Pricing Creativity: A Guide to Profit Beyond the Billable
  Hour* — *"**it'll be the only pricing book in the world that is PRICED ACCORDING TO THE PRINCIPLES
  IN THE BOOK.**"*
- **The creativity/expertise paradox** (crediting **Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi**): creativity is *"the
  ability to SEE… to bring perspective to problems"*, so creative people are drawn to unsolved
  problems and bore easily — while **expertise requires repeated observation of the same problems**.
  *"A creative expert is almost a paradox"*, and that conflict *"is at the heart of most creative
  people's challenges with selling."*
- **Craftsman-producer**: marketers see a market gap and must solve production; producers know how
  to make X and must solve marketing — *"**and I've just described 99% of the design firms on the
  planet.**" Because the thing sold is personal, *"**if you get rejected, IT'S A PERSONAL
  REJECTION.**"*
- **The room full of doors** — his positioning metaphor: pick one door, walk through, never look
  back; *"**your hesitation is you think that on the other side of that door is one boring empty grey
  room… but what's on the other side of that door are MORE DOORS**… **you get in there and IT OPENS
  UP LIKE NARNIA.**"*
- **The economics of not feeling guilty**: wealth is created through **trade**; ideas are a
  **non-rival asset** (*"I could sell you an idea and then you have the idea AND I have the idea"*);
  the **"double thank you moment"**; and *"**think of each individual dollar bill as a CERTIFICATE OF
  APPRECIATION.**"*
- **The five constraints of "Blairtopia"**, of which two are named: **"no exit"** (*"imagine you can
  never sell and never retire"* — *"**as soon as you start to have one eye on the exit, YOU QUIT
  MAKING THE DIFFICULT DECISIONS**"*; *"the most liberating decision I've ever made"*) and **"say
  what you're thinking"** (a company core value, also called **"kind ruthlessness"**): *"**take a
  minute to think about how you would phrase it kindly — but say what you're thinking. Because so
  many of your sales problems stem from the fact that you're NOT.**"*
- **On pitching itself:** *"**there's no moral issue around pitching as far as I'm concerned — it's
  a function of free-market economics.**" Discounting or working free isn't the mistake: *"**the
  mistake is in NOT HAVING THAT OPEN AND DIRECT CONVERSATION with the client about what your
  intentions are.**"*
- **Business facts, 2017**: ~80 firms in the program; team of eight going on nine, four in-office and
  the rest distributed; minimum engagement **\$5,000** self-directed or **\$500/month over 12
  months**, one-year minimum commitment; three onboarding periods a year; **retention** named as the
  main scaling lever — *"**retention is PROOF OF DELIVERY OF VALUE.**"*
- He names **Peter Thiel's *Zero to One*** as *"possibly the single best business book I've ever
  read."*

⚠️ Blair also discusses his own schooling, family and career history at length. **That is his
biography, not the subject's, and is not recorded here.**
