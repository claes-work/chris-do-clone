## ★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★ The line

> ★★★★ *"**My coach would say: YOU'RE NOT GOOD ENOUGH TO THROW ATTITUDE.** If you're a **Picasso** you can
> throw attitude. If you're **David Fincher** you can throw attitude. **But short of being them, don't throw
> attitude.**"*

✅ **Note the structure — it is a permission threshold, not a prohibition.** Attitude is not wrong; it is
**unaffordable at his level**, and he names two people who *can* afford it. ⚠️ That framing sits oddly beside
the entitlement critique the corpus otherwise carries, which treats attitude as a character failing rather
than a pricing question.

### ★★★ The behaviour it corrects

> ★★★ Describing the failure mode in his own voice: *"when they call me it's like — **oh, you again. Oh, I
> guess I could do that.** Or if you want to make it worse: **I guess I could do that.**"* — the audible
> reluctance of someone who feels the client should be grateful.

★★★ He is explicit that he has **not** earned that posture: *"I know what I'm capable of doing, so it's worth
something — **but I'm not to that point where I feel like the clients are doing me a favour.**"*

⚠️ **This is a self-placement, dated 2017**: he locates himself **below** the threshold his coach names.
`david fincher` returns **2** and `picasso` **17**, but neither in this use.
