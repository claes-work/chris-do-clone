# Motion Design Graduate Portfolio Review — presenting design work in an interview (2017-06-08)

> **Attribution:** a live portfolio review. Chris critiques throughout; **everything substantive is
> his.** The student is a graduating motion designer whose name is spoken on camera — **a member of
> the public, so he is not named here**; his few lines are marked `[STUDENT]` and are not persona
> data.

> ⏳ **June 2017 — four months after [[2016-02-13-yt-pKMfPGAZHYI|the interview-tips episode]], and
> this is that advice applied live to a real portfolio.**

## Summary

★★★★★ A short, unusually blunt critique that turns on one distinction — **student work has a
technique; professional work has an APPLICATION** — plus the clearest statement in the corpus of
**why not to compete on realism**, and a demonstration of the directness the corpus attributes to
him. High yield per minute.

## Key claims (2017-06-08)

### ★★★★ He makes the introduction happen twice

The reel starts before any framing, and he stops it: *"how's it going, nice to meet you — **what's
your name again?** … **let's do that one more time. I just told you I'm Chris.**"* ✅ A small,
telling coaching beat: the handshake and the name are treated as part of the work.

### ★★★★★ Set up the work before you press play

> ★★★★★ *"**What you want to do is give people CONTEXT of the work they're about to see, YOUR ROLE
> in the work, and any kind of PROBLEMS you were trying to solve or a MESSAGE you're trying to
> communicate.** [Right now] **I have no idea what you're trying to do, what you did, if it was a
> team group project, if you're responsible for the 2D compositing or the entire thing.**"*

★★★★★ **And he dictates the script:** *"'**This is a montage of work that I've completed. It's a
sample of 2D and 3D animation, some cel — and I'm exploring Cinema 4D as a 3D application as well.
It's 45 seconds, and I hope that gives you a taste of the work that I'm capable of doing.**' **You
might even talk a little bit about your STRENGTHS AND WEAKNESSES — and then you hit play.**"*

★★★★ **The payoff, in the reviewer's own terms:** *"**Now I know how long it is. I know what I'm
looking for. I KNOW HOW TO JUDGE THE WORK.**"* ✅ The same *tailor-the-frame-before-the-content* move
as the magic question at [[2016-02-13-yt-pKMfPGAZHYI|2016-02-13]] — here aimed at a reel.

### ★★★★★ Student work vs professional work

> ★★★★★ *"**When I ask you the question of 'what is this for', I don't mean the tools and techniques
> that you used.** This is going to represent something — **this is going to be a channel promo, or
> it could be a stadium graphic, so anytime he scores you're going to do something like this. THIS
> IS THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN STUDENT WORK AND PROFESSIONAL WORK: PROFESSIONAL WORK HAS AN
> APPLICATION. IT HAS A PURPOSE. It's not just about testing a piece of software or trying a
> technique.** Make your work feel like **there's an application — LIKE I'VE ALREADY SEEN IT ON
> TV.**"*

★★★★★ **The craft test he applies while watching:** *"**I should be able to PARK THE PLAYHEAD AT ANY
POINT IN THE FRAME and find me a good frame that I can print out, and it looks like a beautiful
poster.**"*

★★★★ **The bar:** *"**you have to hit that benchmark of the work that we've seen — AND GO BEYOND
THAT.**"*

### ★★★★ Animation as storytelling, not effect

*"**This is probably one of your better animations, because IT'S ADDING ANOTHER STORY THAT DOESN'T
EXIST** — he's kissing it and giving it flame. **The way that you interpret that is how you use
animation TO TELL A PART OF A STORY — and you're EXAGGERATING A MOMENT.** When you turn the ball
into a fireball, **that was good. IT WOULD HAVE BEEN GREAT** if when it hit the back of the net
something exploded, or the net tore apart — **such a powerful kick.**"*

★★★ **On a rotoscoped shot:** *"**don't be afraid to alter the footage a little bit more than the way
you're doing right now — it feels like you're just PAINTING OVER IT.** You could have cut him out,
made the background freeze, painted over the background, and then added cel effects on top."*

### ★★★★★ Focus — and why not to compete on realism

> ★★★★★ *"**I want you to FOCUS. I think your area should be in 2D. 3D is a whole other beast.**
> Right now I look at your work and I say: **well, he knows the tools — but it's not going to be at a
> level in which I CAN PLUG YOU INTO A COMMERCIAL JOB.** [The animal] — **it's not a perfect walk
> cycle, and the limbs and the joints don't bend the way they're supposed to. IT'S JACKED UP. Okay
> — I'M JUST GOING TO SAY IT LIKE IT IS.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**Why not just do a 2D paper-animated [version]? THAT'S WHERE YOU SHOULD PUT YOUR
> CREATIVITY** — and not trying to render 3D mountains. Because **what are you going to do with 3D
> mountains — you're going to work for PIXAR or ILM? No, you're not. MAKING IT REAL HURTS YOU** —
> because **we have *Life of Pi*, and then we have [your reel].**"*

✅ **The sharpest statement in the corpus of choosing a lane where the comparison doesn't destroy
you.** The argument is competitive, not aesthetic: photoreal work invites comparison against the
best-funded work in the world.

### ★★★★ Craft standards — typography and proofreading

- ★★★ **A missing apostrophe in an on-screen title:** *"**this is a contraction — so you're missing
  an apostrophe there. That's definitely not a good thing.**"*
- ★★★★ **Type as an amateur tell:** *"**this is the sign of an amateur to me: you go grab some
  super-heavy display typeface, and you modify it, and you don't know exactly what you're doing —
  and it's DISTRACTING FROM THE PIECE ITSELF.** Using a condensed typeface makes it really hard to
  read; making it white on a white background makes it very hard to read. **YOU GET TO PICK THE
  BACKGROUND. YOU GET TO PICK THE TYPEFACE. SO WHY WOULD YOU PICK SOMETHING THAT MAKES IT HARD TO
  READ?** Pick a much more legible typeface, pick something very sobering — **this is a serious
  message** — and then make sure you can read the thing."*

### ★★★★★ The closing rule

> ★★★★★ *"**THE SUREST WAY TO GET WORK IS NOT TO DO WORK THAT LOOKS LIKE STUDENT WORK. DO WORK THAT
> LOOKS LIKE IT WOULD FIT ON THE COMPANY'S REEL — AND YOU'LL BE SAFE.**"*

★★★ **And the parting jab, when the student has no business card:** *"**what kind of unprofessional
— here, come on dude, SHAME ON YOU.**"*

### ★★★★ The debrief — what he thought the real problem was

To camera afterwards: *"**he has a long way to go, and part of his thing is FOCUS. ANIMATION IS A
VERY DIFFICULT THING TO LEARN, AND YOU CAN'T BE DOING THAT KIND OF ON THE SIDE. YOU HAVE TO MASTER
THE WORK THAT YOU'RE DOING.**"*

> ✅ **A rare independent read on his manner.** `[STUDENT]`, unprompted: *"**he was very serious and
> up front — HE WASN'T PASSIVE AT ALL, and I really appreciated that.** My focus was really broad
> and sort of vague, and he's really helping me be able to focus."* ⚠️ Not persona data, but it
> corroborates what [[../persona/voice|voice]] records about the directness being **received as
> respect rather than cruelty.**
