# How To Structure A Proposal For Web & Branding Projects — ep. 3 w/ Rebecca (2017-09-27)

> **Attribution:** episode 3 of an ongoing coaching series. Rebecca is a designer in year 1–2 of her
> own business, recently out of agency life; her lines are marked `[REBECCA]` and are **not** persona
> data. Chris coaches throughout — **the substantive content is his.**

> ⏳ **September 2017 — four months after [[2017-05-03-yt-KtL02T5MonA|the Blair Enns interview]]**,
> and he quotes Blair's proposal script from it by name.

## Summary

★★★★★ **One of the strongest sales sources in the corpus.** A single question — *how do you estimate
a website you haven't scoped?* — opens into the **four things every first meeting must accomplish**,
the **qualifying checklist**, the **paid-estimate script**, and two of the most valuable
attributions in the wiki: **Kier McLaren as the origin of "just ask"**, and **Blair Enns as the
source of the proposal script**. It also contains an unusually candid admission of the game he used
to play, and an equally candid account of a competitive tactic he uses on purpose.

## Key claims (2017-09-27)

### ★★★★★ You cannot bid what you have not scoped

> ★★★★★ *"**Personally I feel like IT'S IMPOSSIBLE TO BID A PROJECT THAT YOU DON'T KNOW THE SCOPE OF
> WORK [for]. You're only then making just a CRAZY GUESS.**"*

★★★★ **The house analogy:** *"a website is kind of like **building a house** — **no two are the
same.** You can say '**give me a quote on a house**' — **well, how many bedrooms? Where is it
located? What kind of features and amenities do we need?**"*

★★★★ **And the contrast that makes the point precise** — branding *is* scopeable: *"for a lot of you
that do branding work, unless there's some giant unforeseen thing, **you kind of know what you're
getting yourself into**: some research, initial exploration, a couple of rounds of refinement, then
you deliver a style guide, maybe messaging, applications… **that's somewhat contained.**"* **The web
build is not.**

★★★★ **He also names the paperwork sequence:** an RFP gets **a response**, followed by a **scope of
work / statement of work (SOW)**.

### ★★★★★ Charge for the strategy that produces the scope

> ★★★★★ *"**This is why most times when I'm working with a client I'll say: WE'RE GOING TO CHARGE YOU
> FOR STRATEGY, and that's going to be between this dollar amount and this dollar amount.** Once
> we're done with that, then we'll know **what features and functions need to be built into the
> site, who the users are — OR IF WE EVEN NEED TO BUILD A WEBSITE IN THE FIRST PLACE.**"*

★★★★★ **He never assumes the deliverable:** *"**I NEVER MAKE THAT ASSUMPTION THAT THEY NEED A NEW
WEBSITE.** Because if their goals aren't transactional, or it's not about building a campaign or
email capture — **then there's really no point to update the site.**"*

★★★★ **The fallback when a client refuses discovery** — bucket by type: *"**what's the site kind of
like? Is it just me re-skinning your site? Is it adding e-commerce? Is it a portal? Is it more like
software, or is it a couple of pages?**"* → *"**then we can put them into different BUCKETS.** If you
do a lot of web projects, **you kind of know it's between this and this** — **'it sounds like it's
going to be this kind of home: it's a two-bedroom with a really nice modern kitchen.'**"*

### ★★★★★ The four things — the spine of the episode

He asks Rebecca what leaves the strongest impression on her in a first meeting. She answers:
**comfortable · knowledgeable · rapport · easy to work with.** He adopts her four as the agenda —
*"those are excellent, great observations"* — and then answers each:

- ★★★★★ **Comfortable = not being in a rush.** *"**How do I make you feel comfortable? WELL, I'M NOT
  IN A RUSH.**"* — versus *"'hey, we've got to wrap up, I have another session, I've got two more
  calls' — **does that make you feel comfortable? No.**" Instead: *"**I've got time to sit down and
  talk to you. I want to learn about your problem, the challenges you're facing. I WANT TO GET TO
  KNOW WHO YOU ARE.**"*
- ★★★★★ **Knowledgeable = listening.** *"**I DEMONSTRATE MY KNOWLEDGE BY BEING A GOOD LISTENER** —
  and when I hear a problem I may be able to advise you, but **I CAN'T ADVISE YOU UNTIL I HEAR WHAT
  THE PROBLEMS MAY BE.**"* ✅ The operational form of *"I'm a good listener and I can synthesise
  information"* from [[2017-02-04-yt-swiG31QGKVc|2017-02-04]].
- ★★★★★ **Rapport = matching and mirroring.** *"I'm trying to build rapport **to make sure we share
  values and beliefs, that we SEE THE WORLD IN THE SAME WAY.** **If you're a really slow,
  smooth-talking person, I want to be slow and smooth-talking. If you're talking really fast, I need
  to talk really fast with you** — so it doesn't feel like you're being left behind, or that I'm
  making you wait, **because your patience is going to be really thin.** **So I'm going to change my
  TEMPO, my TONE, my LANGUAGE, my BODY LANGUAGE — everything — TO MATCH AND MIRROR how you're
  behaving.** … **I just want to SYNC UP to you.**"*
- ★★★★★ **Easy = the audition for the whole engagement.** *"**I want to make it an easy process,
  because I WANT THEM TO ENVISION WORKING WITH ME — that THIS IS HOW IT'S GOING TO BE THE ENTIRE
  ENGAGEMENT.** If I feel like you're in a rush, if I feel like you don't respect my time — **all
  those things send little SUBLIMINAL SIGNALS that we're probably NOT A GOOD FIT.**"*

★★★★★ **And the gut, stated as data:** *"**we know this in real life, because you get that gut
feeling: I'm not connecting to you, you're not my kind of person. SOMETIMES WE DON'T LISTEN TO THAT
— and then we take on the worst client**, and then we're like: I should have listened. **YOUR GUT IS
VERY INTELLIGENT. You need to pay attention to what's going on.**"*

### ★★★★★ Run the meeting on an agenda

> ★★★★★ *"**When you go to the meeting, we LAY OUT AN AGENDA: what would you like to get from this
> meeting? What would I like to get from this meeting? And I like to be VERY UPFRONT about that:
> THIS IS NOT A MEET AND GREET.** This is not 'hey how are you, let's talk about our kids.' **This
> is a business thing.**"*

★★★★★ **Know your questions before you leave:** *"**in future, what you want to do is TO KNOW WHAT
THE QUESTIONS YOU NEED ANSWERED ARE BEFORE YOU LEAVE A MEETING — and don't spend your time dealing
with all that small talk that doesn't add up to anything ACTIONABLE for you.**"* ★★★ And if you
forget, the recovery is simply to email or call — *"**that's totally okay**… let's just jump on the
phone, **I don't need you to type. I want to make it easy for you.**"*

### ★★★★★ The roleplay — taking control of a vague client

★★★★ **What his own clients actually say** (offered when Rebecca's roleplay answer doesn't match):
*"**most of my clients say what they want from me: I need to know if you're a good partner for me. I
need to know if you can do it in the time and the budget. I need to know that you have the right
experience.**"* — or bluntly: *"**I'm considering three other people, Chris. I want to make sure
you're on the right budget. I heard good things about you, but WE'VE NEVER SEEN YOU WORK — I just
don't know.**"* → and he **reflects it back**: *"I need to know what the project scope is, what the
timeline is, how much money you have, and when you're going to make a decision."*

★★★★★ **When the client doesn't know what they want, he takes the wheel:**

> ★★★★★ *"**Rebecca, it sounds like you're pretty early in the stage of considering a firm, and YOU
> DON'T KNOW WHAT YOU WANT YET. So why don't you just LET ME TAKE CONTROL OF THIS DIALOGUE for half
> a second. Here's what I think — if I'm being an ADVISOR to you, here's what you want to know**,
> because you're going to talk to a handful of people and you'll need to make a decision. **You're
> probably looking for FIT — do we believe the same kinds of things — you're looking at SCHEDULE,
> you're looking at SCOPE, you want to know we have the EXPERTISE and a dev team, and HOW ACCESSIBLE
> AND RESPONSIVE am I. Does that sound about right?** … **If you don't mind, I'll just go through
> these and answer them for you. I WANT TO MAKE THIS REALLY EASY FOR YOU.**"*

★★★★★ **Then he breaks the fourth wall to name the mechanism:** *"**to break the fourth wall here —
the META LAYER — I'm going to explain to our audience what it is I'm doing. FIRST OF ALL, I'M JUST
SAYING WHAT I THINK. HAVE THE COURAGE TO SAY WHAT'S ON YOUR MIND.** 'You seem confused. You seem
early on in the stage. Can I help you? Is this what you need to know?' **And I KNOW that you're going
to talk to other people — SO LET'S NOT EVEN PRETEND LIKE YOU'RE NOT GOING TO.**"*

✅ ⏳ **Four months after telling Blair Enns *"I love that idea [say what you're thinking], and I
practise that myself"*** ([[2017-05-03-yt-KtL02T5MonA|2017-05-03]]) — **here he is teaching it as
method.**

★★★★ **On budget, always:** *"**I know budget's a concern. IT DOESN'T MATTER IF YOU'RE WARREN BUFFETT
OR A MOM-AND-POP SHOP DOWN THE STREET — budget is ALWAYS a concern, because YOU NEVER WANT TO OVERPAY
FOR SERVICES.**"*

### ★★★★★ The confession — and the origin of "just ask"

> ★★★★★ *"**There's this game that we think we have to play: that we have to IMPRESS THEM BY NOT
> SAYING ANYTHING; that it's a game of ONE-UPSMANSHIP; that we can't be fully transparent and honest.
> AND THAT'S A GAME THAT I USED TO PLAY. I used to — believe it or not — GO INTO CONFERENCE CALLS FOR
> SIX-FIGURE JOBS AND NOT ASK THE CLIENT WHAT THEY WANTED US TO BUILD.**"*

★★★★ **Why:** *"**because it was the game that I thought we had to play**— that we have to **INTUIT**
it… **you feel like you should know, and you want them to think that you know that you know — but
then you don't know.** And then they walk away thinking: **I don't think Chris knows what he's
talking about. HE DIDN'T EVEN ASK ME WHAT I WANTED. They just beat around the bush.**"*

> ★★★★★ **THE ORIGIN, ATTRIBUTED:** *"**This is something I learned from MY BUSINESS COACH, KIER
> MCLAREN. He's the one who said: 'KIDDO, WHY DON'T YOU JUST ASK? Just be yourself and just ask.
> Just ask for what you want'** — and here's a couple of ways of asking. **As soon as he said that,
> IT WAS LIKE THE HEAVENS PARTED, THE CLOUDS PARTED AND THE SUN CAME THROUGH AND SHONE IN MY FACE.
> And it was like: THAT SEEMS SO OBVIOUS AND SIMPLE — THEN WHY THE HECK HADN'T I DONE THIS?**"*

✅✅ **A documented origin for the ask-don't-tell principle**, one of the load-bearing ideas of the
whole corpus, credited by name to [[../entities/kier-mclaren|Kier McLaren]]. ⚠️ **This belongs on
Kier's entity page and in `persona/biography`.**

★★★★★ **The metaphor he draws from it:** *"**we walk around with a set of rules — AN OPERATING SYSTEM
— THAT IS INCORRECT. IT'S FLAWED. And most of the time, IT'S US WRITING THE CODE. So that's the good
news: BECAUSE WE CAN REWRITE THE CODE.**"*

★★★★★ **And the frame for the whole exercise:** *"**NONE OF THIS IS ABOUT SELLING YOU ANYTHING.**
You're either going to like me, like my process and like my work — or you're not. **AND I CAN'T
CHANGE THAT AT ALL. I'M NOT IN THE GAME TO CONVINCE YOU that what was black is not white, that what
is grey is orange and orange is purple.**"*

### ★★★★★ "The fact that we're having the meeting says I'm qualified"

> ★★★★★ *"**Know that the fact that we're having the meeting says I'M QUALIFIED, I'M READY AND I'M
> CAPABLE — BECAUSE IF I'M NOT, PEOPLE DON'T MEET WITH US. That's the bottom line.**"*

★★★★ **With the evidence from his own inbox:** *"**there are hundreds of people who send me emails
all the time saying 'look at this, look at this' — and I have no response, because it's kind of bad.
It's just bad.**"* → *"**if I agree to have a meeting with somebody, it means I'VE ALREADY CHECKED
OUT THE WORK, or you come highly recommended from people I trust. SO YOU GOT THE MEETING — AND IT'S
HARD TO GET A MEETING.** Know that you're ready. **The client sees you in a certain light EVEN IF YOU
DON'T SEE YOURSELF IN THAT LIGHT.**"* ✅ The same argument as *"the fact that you got an interview
means you're qualified"* at [[2016-02-13-yt-pKMfPGAZHYI|2016-02-13]], restated for business owners.

### ★★★★★ The estimate you *can* give — a range, labelled as assumptions

> ★★★★★ *"'**Without doing further discovery or strategic work with you, MY BEST GUESS — AND THIS IS
> A BEST GUESS AS IT IS — you need a landing page, sub-pages, an about page, and some kind of
> transactional thing. And for that it's going to be somewhere between THIRTY TO SEVENTY-FIVE
> THOUSAND DOLLARS. I DON'T KNOW — DON'T HOLD ME TO IT. So what we'll do is: if this sounds
> interesting and you want to dive deeper and further define it — LET'S DO DISCOVERY. I'll charge you
> ten, fifteen thousand dollars, it'll take this amount of time**' — and you describe that process
> very clearly."*

★★★★ **Say the word "assumptions", repeatedly:** *"**it's important you keep emphasising that.**"*

### ⚠️★★★★★ The car-repair parable — and a candidly-named tactic

*"**You ever get in a car accident before?** … **Have you ever had an appraiser just talk to you on
the phone and say 'yeah, it's \$13,000'? And if they did, WOULD YOU TRUST THEM?**"* — no — *"'just
send me one or two photos, I'll figure it out' — **no. YOU WANT A FULL ASSESSMENT. You want to look
at the underlying structure of the body — what I call the FRAME — see if it's been damaged**…
because **your family is going to be in that car, and THIS IS NOT SOMETHING YOU WANT TO SAVE \$10 ON.
You want it DONE RIGHT AND RIGHT THE FIRST TIME, BECAUSE YOU DON'T REPAIR THE CAR THREE TIMES.**"*

> ⚠️ ★★★★★ **He then states plainly what the parable is for:** *"**that's why I PLANT THE SEEDS OF
> DOUBT. If someone else doesn't give you a thorough DIAGNOSTIC before giving you a quote, I WOULD BE
> CONCERNED — but I DON'T SAY THOSE WORDS. I just plant the seeds, subtly.**"* → *"**anybody who
> gives them a budget without wanting a conversation, without doing discovery, is giving them a bogus
> [number]. SO WHAT YOU'RE DOING THERE IS YOU'RE SETTING SOMEBODY ELSE UP TO FAIL.**"* → *"**you're
> either getting overcharged, or they're UNDER-BIDDING and you're getting hit with OVERAGES all the
> time.** … **I'M TRYING TO DIFFERENTIATE THE WHOLE TIME.**"*

⚠️ **Recorded as stated.** This is an unusually candid disclosure of a competitive tactic — he names
it as seeding doubt about competitors without naming them, and the corpus should carry it as he
framed it rather than soften it.

★★★★ **And the principle behind it:** *"**I think it's IRRESPONSIBLE to provide a bid that has NO
CONTEXT, NO UNDERSTANDING, NO DIALOGUE.**"* — which is what *"the desire to HURRY UP AND BID just to
SEEM RESPONSIVE"* produces.

### ★★★★★ Qualifying a lead

> ★★★★★ *"**When we get a call for a new project, I'M GOING TO GET TO THE NUMBER REALLY FAST — as
> fast as it seems not rude to do.**"*

★★★★ **The in-house example**, of an executive producer bringing an exciting-sounding brand project
with no budget attached: *"'**how much is the budget?**' — 'I didn't ask' — **'DON'T TALK TO ME. GO
FIND OUT.'** … it's \$12,500 — **you're wasting my time. We can't do that for that.**"* → the rule:
*"**DON'T GET EXCITED OVER THE PROSPECT OF DOING WORK THAT YOU FORGET TO ASK ABOUT THE BUDGET.**"*

★★★★★ **The four qualifying questions**, built live: **budget · timeline · project type (creative
fit) · personality fit.** *"**Anything can be done given enough time and money — SO THAT'S WHY TIME
AND MONEY ARE YOUR FIRST TWO QUESTIONS.**"*

★★★★★ **The written filter:** *"**we WRITE PROTOCOL after every one of those [bad clients] and say:
how do we prevent this from happening? So WE TIGHTEN THE FILTER.** We have **extensive
documentation**, a whole **checklist**: '**you haven't secured financing — see you later. You haven't
sold the job through to your superiors — see you later. You're actually going after new business
yourself, so THIS ISN'T EVEN A REAL JOB YET — see you later.**' **We have all our rules set up — and
WHEN WE DON'T LISTEN TO THEM, when our memory fades, we make that mistake every once in a while. And
then WE HATE OURSELVES. And it's never worth it.**"*

★★★★★ **Gut over bank account:** *"**I would suggest you LISTEN TO YOUR GUT REGARDLESS OF HOW MUCH
YOU THINK YOU NEED THE MONEY**… **because your life is too short.**"* — and, asked whether he's ever
regretted overriding it: *"**every time.**"*

★★★★★ **The downside spelled out:** *"imagine — heaven forbid — **they're yelling at you all the
time, berating you, belittling you, you hate life, your margins disappear, now you're UPSIDE DOWN on
the job AND THEY WANT A REFUND — and then they go on Yelp and tarnish your name.** … **You get hit by
a truck the day after. THAT WAS YOUR LIFE. IS THAT THE LIFE YOU WANT?**"* → *"**you start to think:
I WILL PAY YOU MONEY TO LEAVE. I don't want this job any more.**"* → and the reduction: *"**you're
better off doing nothing — sitting in your room staring at the wall. THAT WOULD BE MORE ENTERTAINING
AND COST LESS.**"*

★★★★ **What a solo owner faces that an agency employee doesn't** — the client refusing to pay while
staff still must be paid: *"**not only are you not doing the work and not getting paid — you still
have to pay [your team]. So now you're LITERALLY PAYING TO MAKE THE THING GO AWAY.**"*

★★★★★ **Faith, with the loophole closed:** *"**I have faith. I have faith in MYSELF, MY PROCESS, MY
WORK, MY NETWORK.** … **it's darkest before dawn — just have faith that the sun does come up.** **THAT
DOES NOT MEAN YOU CAN SIT ON YOUR LAURELS** and expect work to come in, expect the work to get
better, expect new leads. **YOU'VE GOT TO POUND THE PAVEMENT. You pick up the phone, you call three
new clients or three old clients. YOU HAVE TO DO THAT.**"*

### ★★★★★ The paid-estimate script — credited to Blair Enns

> ★★★★★ *"**Here's the response — I got this STRAIGHT UP from Blair Enns' *Win Without Pitching
> Manifesto*. It's a book I highly recommend. YOU NEED TO READ IT. NOT ONLY DO YOU NEED TO READ IT —
> YOU NEED TO MEMORISE IT, YOU NEED TO PRACTISE IT, YOU NEED TO BE ABLE TO TEACH IT. THAT'S HOW WELL
> YOU NEED TO KNOW THIS MATERIAL.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"'**Mr and Mrs Smith, as much as I want to do this, I'M NOT IN THE BUSINESS OF BUILDING
> PROPOSALS that cost time and money to do. I need to know how real this job is, how much the budget
> is — because I'M NOT IN THIS GUESSING GAME. It takes me DAYS to put this together if it's of any
> value to you, and I don't get paid to do that. So we can do one of two things: YOU CAN SIT DOWN
> WITH ME and we can go over this so I understand the project better and we can talk about what's
> realistic; OR YOU CAN PAY ME A THOUSAND DOLLARS AND I'LL BUILD YOU AN ESTIMATE. I'll bill you for
> the time of building it — then you can comparison-shop, you can do whatever you want. BUT I NEED TO
> BE PAID FOR MY TIME. I CAN'T GET THAT BACK. So how does that sound?**'"*

★★★★★ **Why he prefers option one — increasing commitment:** *"**the fear is that they're not going
to buy**… **I'm only going to build the proposal because we have LEVELS OF INCREASING COMMITMENT from
you. Every time you decide to do a meeting with me, a follow-up meeting, a discovery — YOU'RE MAKING
LITTLE BITS OF AGREEMENT. SO I'M BRINGING YOU IN.**"*

★★★★ **Cited: Robert Cialdini's *Influence*** — *"**we need to SEEM TO BE CONSISTENT with our
thoughts and our actions. So when you make a small commitment, you're MORE LIKELY TO MAKE IT AGAIN
AND AGAIN.**"* → *"**you're ONBOARDING THEM SLOWLY — versus 'give me a little information, I'll go
away and do all the work, only for you to tell me weeks later — OR NOT EVEN CALL ME BACK — that it
didn't work out.**"* ⚠️ He draws the analogy to telemarketers and door-to-door solicitation
explicitly; **recorded as stated.**

★★★★★ **The order of questions, and the budget confirmation:** *"**before we even get into features
and functions: WHO IS THIS FOR? What are the business and marketing objectives you're trying to
accomplish?**"* → then *"**based on that, something like this should be somewhere between EIGHTY TO A
HUNDRED AND TWENTY THOUSAND. Is that kind of where you're at?**"* → *"**so if I came back to you with
a budget of one hundred and eighteen thousand, you're good to go?**"* → *"**what else are you going to
use to decide who you work with?**"*

### ★★★★★ 90% of the proposal is already written

> ★★★★★ *"**Keep in mind, 90% OF THE PROPOSAL IS ALREADY DONE.** Once you get a good template going,
> **you're just modifying the relevant parts — because WE DIDN'T CHANGE WHO WE WERE. We're not
> changing our staff overnight, we're not changing our client testimonials. So really what we're
> changing is THE COVER LETTER, THE BUDGET AND THE TIMELINE.**"*

★★★★ **What's in it:** *"**our qualifications, a couple of client testimonials, our approach, our
process, a couple of relevant projects, and then a timeline and a budget.**"* ★★★ And the scale:
*"**we have probably HUNDREDS of proposals that we've put together**… we have those things worked
out."*

⏳ ★★ **A dated product fact:** *"if you don't want to go through the painful process of putting a
proposal together, **you can buy one of ours — we sell them for 59 bucks.**"*

### ★★★★ The close

*"It went a little differently than I thought — **I thought we were actually going to go into the
nitty-gritty, but we're not there yet, BECAUSE WE DON'T KNOW WHAT WE'RE BIDDING ON, and so it's
impossible for me to advise.** But I can give you the best advice I can, which is: **learn to ask
really smart questions upfront so that you don't walk away from the meeting not knowing what you
need to know — make the client feel COMFORTABLE, demonstrate your KNOWLEDGE, build RAPPORT, and make
it seem SUPER EASY.**"*
