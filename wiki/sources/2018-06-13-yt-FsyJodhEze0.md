# Designers React to Design Critique — Young Guns Ep. 6 (2018-06-13)

> **Attribution:** a reaction episode. **The great majority of the runtime is the Young Guns
> participants** — a cohort of young designers — reacting to a critique Chris delivered in Ep. 5.
> Their lines are `[PARTICIPANT]` and are **not** persona data. **Chris speaks only in the framing
> at the top and the closing at the end** — but the closing contains the most valuable item in the
> batch.

> ⏳ **June 2018 — thirteen months after [[2017-05-03-yt-KtL02T5MonA|the Blair Enns interview]].**

## Summary

★★★ **Low speech share, one ★★★★★ item.** The episode's structure is itself the finding — he devotes
a whole episode to **the recipients critiquing his critique**, including open disagreement. Then he
closes by **assigning *The Win Without Pitching Manifesto* as the next challenge**, replacing a design
brief with a reading brief and promising to test it live.

## ★★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★★ He assigns Blair Enns's book as a challenge — and says he will test them on it

> ★★★★★ *"**I'm going to throw a little curveball, because I wouldn't be me if I didn't throw you a
> curveball. Get this: this is *The Win Without Pitching Manifesto*, written by Blair Enns. INSTEAD OF
> DOING A DESIGN CHALLENGE, I WANT YOU TO READ THIS BOOK.** This book is **chock full of wisdom about
> HOW TO POSITION YOURSELF, HOW TO BECOME AN EXPERT, HOW TO SELL WITHOUT SELLING.** I want you to read
> this book **TOP TO BOTTOM, FRONT TO BACK. I want you to LEARN EVERYTHING IN THIS BOOK AND TO APPLY
> IT TO YOUR LIFE.** How am I going to know that you actually read the book? **Because for the very
> next challenge WE ARE GOING TO DO A GAME SHOW — we're going to do it LIVE ON AIR — I'm going to
> TEST how many of these concepts you've been able to master. WINNER TAKES ALL.**"*

> ✅✅ **A third, BEHAVIOURAL data point on Blair Enns's influence**, and the strongest of the three.
> The first two are verbal — he sources a signature line to the book
> ([[2017-05-03-yt-KtL02T5MonA|2017-05-03]]) and quotes its script verbatim
> ([[2017-09-27-yt-UHrjn11F6hc|2017-09-27]]). **Here he interrupts a DESIGN mentorship programme to
> make it read a SALES book, and builds an examination around it.**
>
> ✅ It also **cashes the instruction he gave nine months earlier**: *"not only do you need to read it
> — you need to MEMORISE it, you need to PRACTISE it, you need to BE ABLE TO TEACH IT. That's how
> well you need to know this material."* **He is now enforcing that standard on other people.**

### ★★★★ He invites critique of his own critique — and the episode exists to carry it

The framing at the top, and the reason the episode exists at all:

> ★★★★ *"Guys, **now you've heard my thoughts — I'm curious to hear what you're thinking and feeling
> ABOUT THE CRITIQUE THAT I GAVE YOU. DID YOU FEEL IT WAS FAIR? DID YOU FEEL IT WAS HONEST? WAS THAT
> TOO HARD, OR TOO EASY ON YOU?** And moreover I wanted to know **WHAT YOU LEARNED** from taking on
> this challenge itself. **Did you struggle anywhere — with design, ideation, typography, picking
> colours and materials, or the building itself? What did you learn?**"*

✅ **And the disagreement was published, not edited out.** One participant says plainly: *"**I'm not
sure if I agree with Chris that I should have used the negative space — I think it works best as
negative space.**"* Another explains that in their country the product is handed over by a pharmacist
rather than picked off a shelf, so *"the user journey I have in mind is different from the one Chris
has in mind"* — a direct challenge to the premise of his critique. ⚠️ **Both are participants'
statements, not Chris's**, but the editorial choice to include them is his.

### ★★★★ The closing — what he says the sting is for

> ★★★★ *"Thank you for sharing with me your honest feedback, guys. **And I'm glad you guys have the
> FORTITUDE to take the lessons and the criticisms I've shared with you. I'm sorry — but I believe
> this is all on our path to growth, and SOMETIMES THESE THINGS STING A LITTLE BIT.** And you can use
> what you learned into the next challenge."*

★★★ **A hedged apology that does not withdraw the critique** — *"I'm sorry, but"* — which is
consistent with what [[../persona/voice|voice]] records about bluntness delivered as respect rather
than softened.

### ★★★ A documented critique technique, observed

`[PARTICIPANT]`: *"as soon as **Chris got the TRANSPARENT PAPER out and started TRACING OVER MY
DESIGN**, it just threw me back to school, when we used to have our work critiqued."*

✅ **Tracing paper over the student's work** — an art-school critique method he uses live. ⚠️ Recorded
as **observed behaviour reported by a participant**, not as a claim of his.

## ⚠️ Participant material — context only, NOT persona data

The bulk of the episode. Recorded here only in shape, not in detail: the participants discuss active
ingredients on pharmaceutical packaging, net-weight placement on the front of a package, brand
integrity through a rebrand, the three-islands layout problem, legibility at arm's length, and
whether a word-mark motif reads as a smile or a Wi-Fi signal. **All of it is theirs.** ⚠️ Several
participants are named on camera; they are **early-career designers and members of the public, and
are not named here.**
