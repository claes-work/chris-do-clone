# Difficult Client Role Play — Who Is Fishing For A Price (2018-11-06)

> ⚠️ **`transcript: whisper`** — no speaker turns, proper nouns UNVERIFIED.
> ✅ **Attribution:** a **live role-play in front of a room**. Chris plays himself; **Anton** plays the
> client; **Suzie** and **Joe** ask debrief questions. ✅ The negotiation lines and the whole debrief are
> his — the room addresses him and he answers about his own method.

## Summary

★★★★★ **A complete price-fishing negotiation, played out rather than described** — and a debrief in which
he names the thing that makes it work, which is not a technique.

## ★★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★ The sequence he actually runs

Not a script but an order, visible in the transcript: **diagnose → quantify the goal → test realism →
surface the budget → establish the competitive position → name a number → refuse to proceed on a guess.**

> ★★★★ *"**What are you hoping to achieve** by improving your sales funnel? …**If everything works out in
> a year's time, how much revenue do you want to generate?** …**Is that realistic?**"* — the client says
> ~$1M today, wants $2M.

> ★★★★★ *"**And what criteria are you going to use to judge who is a good partner for you?** …So are you
> suggesting that **we pitch ideas to you** to determine?"* ⚠️ **He surfaces the pitch demand and names
> it** rather than complying — the client immediately retreats to *"I want to understand your process."*

> ★★★★★ *"**And in what order are you calling us? How many people are you talking to? …ARE WE YOUR LAST
> CALL?** …So **you already know what the other budgets are**, because they've already submitted them,
> right?"*

✅ `last call` returns **0** corpus-wide. ⚠️ **Call order as information** — he treats his position in the
sequence as a fact worth extracting, because it tells him what the client already knows.

### ★★★★★ ⭐ The number, and where it comes from

> ★★★★★ *"**Most people in the consulting space charge somewhere about 20% of their first year of
> realized revenue** — and **it's been discounted for uncertainty.** So **are you prepared to spend
> somewhere north of $200,000** to do this work?"*

✅ `first year of realized revenue` returns **0**. ⚠️ **The derivation is the value**: 20% of the $1M
*increase* the client just named, i.e. the price is computed from **the client's own stated goal**, not
from scope. 📌 *"Discounted for uncertainty"* is doing real work — it pre-answers the objection that the
outcome isn't guaranteed.

### ★★★★★ ⭐ He refuses to submit a proposal against an unknown budget

> ★★★★★ *"**I think we should HIT PAUSE on this conversation**, check with your assistant, and let me
> know. Because **the last thing I want to do is submit a proposal for a budget that you don't want.**
> I'll tell you right now: **it's going to be north of $200,000.**"*

⚠️ ✅ **Note what he does NOT do**: he doesn't lower the number when the client hedges (*"I'll need to
check"*), and he doesn't withdraw it. He stops the process instead. ✅ He then concedes the client's
reasonable ask — a **term sheet or one-page proposal** so the offer can be compared on content, not price
alone. `hit pause` has **1** other hit ([[2024-04-04-yt-LJVP2wRk7Cc]]), a different use.

### ★★★★★ ⭐ The debrief: none of it was technical

> ★★★★★ *"**I have no idea about development. I have no idea.** None of this was technical — **you do
> realize that ZERO of it was technical.** **I sell stuff I don't know how to make all the time, because
> I'm talking human, I'm talking business.**"*

✅ `I sell stuff I don't know how to make` returns **0** corpus-wide. ⚠️ **He bookends the recording with
this line** (it opens and closes the video), so it is the point he wanted taken away. ✅ The safeguard he
names: *"If Anton is not clear, I'll ask — **could you say it a different way, because I don't understand
what you just said.** …And **there's a whole team behind me** who can understand this work."*

### ★★★★★ ⭐ Where the composure comes from — asked directly, answered flatly

> ★★★★★ *"**Where did you get that level of finesse?** It seemed like he was throwing things out to trip
> you up, but **you kept it calm.**" — "**Did it look like that? Because I DON'T CARE. The secret is I
> just don't care.** …**I don't care [about] the outcome. I just don't care.**"*

⚠️ **He declines the compliment's premise.** Asked about skill, he answers with a **disposition** — the
calm is not technique but indifference to the result. 📌 `I just don't care` has **1** prior hit
([[2015-12-13-yt-mnSgHLBpOpw]]), but that is **social** indifference (*"I drive a Toyota… I don't give an
F if you think that's cool"*). ✅ **This is the same disposition applied to money on the table** — a
distinct and more consequential instance. ✅ Consistent with that page's *"I like to go in completely
unprepared… I don't do any research, because then I don't jump to any conclusions."*

⚠️ **Suzie's observation is worth keeping as third-party evidence, attributed to her**: *"there were
difficult moments where **if you didn't know your stuff really well you could get tripped up**… **most
people would stop at that moment.**"*
