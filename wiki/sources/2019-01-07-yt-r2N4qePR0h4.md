# Career Advice: Follow Passion or Money? Listen To Your Heart

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — live audience Q&A; Chris Do is the sole teaching voice.** Recorded in front of
> a live workshop audience. Chris answers a student named **Rebecca** (aspiring Pixar/Disney
> character designer / storyboard / concept artist), with brief interjections from audience members
> **Samara** and **Larry** (named only as examples of "super passionate about web design"). Only
> Chris's material is persona-eligible; the audience members contribute no substantive claims.

## Summary

A short, high-energy live answer to a classic question: should I chase the stable, realistic career
(web/UX design) or the improbable dream (Pixar/Disney animation)? Chris reframes the question three
times. First he names the cultural frame directly — asking Rebecca "you're Asian, right?" and
riffing on Asian-immigrant parenting (doctor or lawyer; art needs a backup plan; degrees stacked on
degrees) as the source of the question. Second he attacks the premise that "reality" is realistic
at all: a UX/UI job at Google or Apple is itself a dream job by global standards, and "what Rebecca
sees as reality is not even reality." Third he delivers the actual answer: passion is the
differentiator, defined operationally as **what you do when nobody pays you and you have all the
free time in the world**. He dramatizes it with a clock at 1:00 a.m. — the passionate ones are
reading blogs, going to conferences, buying books; the merely-pragmatic one is asleep — and
concludes that pursuing "the reality thing" is what actually becomes the nightmare.

Notable for a rare piece of **self-description**: "I'm a super passionate guy… whatever I get into,
I really get into, and that's why I'm gonna be a hard person to beat."

## Key claims — Chris Do, dated 2019-01-07

- ★ **Operational definition of passion (mindset).** "The definition to me of passion is like, when
  somebody doesn't pay you and you have all the free time in the world, what the heck do you do?"
  He calls tuning into this "listening to your heart's desire." A crisp, testable definition
  distinct from generic follow-your-passion advice. ★
- **Passion as competitive moat, not sentiment.** "That passion is going to be the thing that
  separates you from the average people." The 1:00 a.m. illustration: while the money-chaser sleeps,
  the passionate ones are "reading the latest blog… attending the conferences… buying the books…
  doing everything they need to do because you can't stop them."
- **Against pursuing money as the goal (mindset/wealth).** "If it's not deep within your bones that
  this is what you want to do, don't waste your time, because I've known many people in my life who
  pursue money that aren't happy and that aren't rich." Note the double failure claim — not happy
  *and* not rich.
- ★ **"Reality" is a relative, often false frame.** "What Rebecca sees as reality is not even
  reality." A UX/UI designer at Google or Apple would be "killing it financially," working at one
  of the top companies in the world "doing things that make a difference to a lot of people on
  planet earth" — and "that's a kid in India, he'll tell you that's the dream, because you know what,
  they're gonna make $2,000 that year." Chris explicitly frames this as keeping things in
  perspective; the comparison is his, including the income figure (unsourced, illustrative). ★
- **Asian-immigrant parenting as the generator of the question (biography-adjacent, self-reported
  framing).** "I think when they handed out parents, Asian kids had it a lot tougher than Caucasian
  kids": Caucasian kids get "you could dream to be anything you want, honey"; Asian kids get "be a
  doctor or a lawyer… your career options, as long as it's one of those two, we're good." Then "oh,
  you know, that art thing — you should probably have a back-up plan," so the main plan goes on the
  back burner under accounting classes and stacked degrees. Chris speaks as an insider to this
  culture; it corroborates rather than adds to his documented Vietnamese-refugee background.
- **Calibrate the gap, then take the practical next step (career craft).** He asks Rebecca to rate
  herself against Pixar on a 1–5 scale; she says 3, and he immediately reframes: "you're more than
  halfway, you're on your way, you're almost there — so maybe an internship would work out for you."
  A small but characteristic move: convert a vague dream-gap into a number, then name a concrete
  next action.
- **The elite are obsessives (design-craft).** People who get in the door at Disney/Pixar are
  "bleeding out of their eyes" over the work — they know the projects, the frames, "the secret
  entrances and all the Easter eggs and the DVDs."
- **Self-description (voice/biography, self-reported).** "I'm a super passionate guy… whatever I get
  into, I really get into, and that's why I'm gonna be a hard person to beat, and that's what you
  want to be."
