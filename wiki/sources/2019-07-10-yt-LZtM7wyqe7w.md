## Summary

Blind builds the Hamilton Family Brewery website. The episode walks the full agency web process —
discovery → sitemap → wireframes → visual design → development — then documents an unplanned
methodology change: rather than the studio's usual custom WordPress build, they adopt **Webflow**
after Ben sees its founder speak at a conference, and hand the build to a design intern with **no
coding experience**, who finishes it in three days. The stated driver throughout is a
**client-capability constraint**: the owners must be able to maintain the site themselves without
hiring anyone, so "the white screen of death" and plugin-maintenance burden of WordPress are treated
as a design failure, not just a technical one. The episode ends with a live training handoff in
which Josh edits and publishes a new beer page himself in about twenty minutes, which Ben calls the
most surprising outcome of the project.

Also documented: the two user personas driving the IA (**"Jessica," the soccer-mom** needing hours
and tasting-room rules; **"Kurt," the beer snob** needing per-beer detail and beauty shots), a
filtering scheme that offers both brewery vocabulary (ales, lagers, stouts) and human vocabulary
("sweet, dark and roasty"), and a design critique in which too many competing material textures are
cut back in favor of color blocking, value contrast, and layering.

## Key claims

_All statements attributed to Blind employees or the clients, NOT to Chris Do. Dated to publication;
retained as Blind-process / entity context only._

### Narrator (voiceover)

- [2019-07-10] Blind is "a brand strategy design consultancy based in Santa Monica, California;
  since 1995 Blind has used the power of design to help diverse clients reach their customers and
  stand out in the marketplace." (quote — recurring series boilerplate, an entity fact about Blind)
- [2019-07-10] The website process in five phases: **discovery** (users and their goals) → **sitemap**
  ("a 30,000-foot view of the website and the navigation as a whole") → **wireframing** (single
  pages, content, fewest steps to the goal) → **visual design** (aesthetics, buttons, colors, type,
  spacing) → **development**. (paraphrase — Blind's documented web methodology)

### Ben Burns (Blind creative director)

- [2019-07-10] The project's real constraint is **resources, not deadline**: "the real issue is not
  that we're running out of time, because we have kind of flexible deadlines on this — it's that
  we're running out of resources," as the designers who worked on it for months rotate to other
  projects. (paraphrase)
- [2019-07-10] His stated success criterion for the deliverable: "when we hand the keys over to them
  they know what to do with it, they can update it… I don't want to add another expense and more
  trouble for them." (paraphrase — client-capability as a design requirement)
- [2019-07-10] Why not WordPress: with a custom theme, "if you don't have somebody that knows
  WordPress running updates and maintaining it, you'll get the white screen of death, everything
  will break — there's so many pieces stitched together that it requires knowledge." Blind had **no
  developers in-house** and had historically hired developers out. (paraphrase — notable org fact)
- [2019-07-10] **Webflow adoption story (org history).** He saw Webflow's founder speak at a
  conference, was "previously skeptical" of visual website builders, was intrigued, went home and
  built his own personal site with it, found it "pretty easy" — then assigned the client build to
  intern Ji-eun, who "has no coding experience," which worried him. It was done **three days later**.
  Corroborates and dates the Webflow adoption already noted in
  [[2019-07-11-yt-8FMb2DETdgw]]. (paraphrase)
- [2019-07-10] On the handoff: he normally braces for "this two-hour-long conversation… trudging
  through the backend of WordPress… teaching them how to use plugins"; this time he could "slide
  that laptop over to them and say go ahead, edit it." Outcome: "it literally took 20 minutes,"
  they stayed on budget, and the clients don't have to hire anyone to run the site. (paraphrase)
- [2019-07-10] A recurring critique heuristic surfaces in the design review: **hidden information is
  a cost.** On a scroll/carousel treatment — "the only bad thing is that we're hiding information,"
  repeated twice, plus a responsive-behavior objection. (paraphrase)

### Matthew Encina (Blind designer)

- [2019-07-10] **Deliberate cropping below the fold** as a scroll cue: overlapping/cropping an
  element "is begging for the user to scroll down… because sometimes if you don't do it well and
  things are lined up perfectly, people don't have the feeling that they should scroll down any
  further — they don't know that there's anything below." (paraphrase — reusable UX craft note)
- [2019-07-10] Critique of texture overload: "bare wood… a parchment texture… a solid color…
  concrete… a different kind of wood… burlap — it's too much." The fix he endorses is **specific
  rules** for where a texture is and isn't used, plus color blocking and value contrast rather than
  "getting a little gimmicky with just random textures." (paraphrase)
- [2019-07-10] Praises layering achieved through value alone: "things are stacking in z-space,
  they're coming out towards us, and it's very subtly done but so elegantly done" — with no drop
  shadows. (paraphrase)
- [2019-07-10] Brand-attribute check applied to the visual direction: it "balances that heritage and
  fun" — bright colors carrying the fun, typography carrying the heritage. Consistent with the
  "heritage but also fun" attributes recorded in Ep. 9. (paraphrase)

### Josh & Kristen Hamilton (clients)

- [2019-07-10] Currently minimally involved in the website; want to run it themselves "for a while
  just to understand it" before hiring anyone down the road. (paraphrase)
- [2019-07-10] Josh's reaction after publishing a beer page himself: "well, I guess if Josh can do
  it, I could do it too" (Kristen), and "that's it? … it's super awesome." (paraphrase)
