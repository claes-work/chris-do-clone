## ★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★★ The rebuke, stated against his own self-assessment

> ★★★★★ *"I have experience, I talk to clients — **I thought I was doing a great job UNTIL MY COACH TOLD
> ME: you're not doing a great job.** And I'm like: **what do you mean?**"*

✅ **The sting is the point.** The 2017 telling gives the advice; this one gives **the correction that
preceded it** — he was confident, and was told plainly he was wrong.

### ★★★★ The correction, as a question

> ★★★★ *"And he told me: **how can you GUESS at what the client wants? Why don't you just ASK them what
> they want?**"*

⚠️ Note that McLaren corrects him **with a question**, not an instruction — the same mechanism Chris
later reports his therapist using on him ([[2020-02-15-yt-hgn7sr4PNrg]]) and the one he uses on clients.

### ★★★★★ ⏳ The consequence, at company scale

> ★★★★★ *"And after some practice we did it, and **it CHANGED THE ENTIRE TRAJECTORY OF OUR COMPANY** — by
> learning this one basic technique."*

⚠️ **A large causal claim attached to a small technique.** 🗣️ Self-reported and unquantified. **Record it
as his account, not as established company history** — but it is exactly the kind of claim the
`kier-mclaren` page exists to hold, since it is what makes the intervention *causal* rather than
decorative.

### ★★★★ And the principle he draws from it

> ★★★★ *"[The technique is] **staying in the conversation, unafraid of it taking longer than you want** —
> because **it's not about expediency, it's about understanding. And I choose understanding over
> speed.**"*

✅ **This names the cost the technique asks you to accept**: discovery takes longer, and he treats that
as the price rather than a problem. ⚠️ `understanding over (speed|expediency)` returns **4** — the
sentiment is held; **its attachment to the McLaren intervention is not.**
