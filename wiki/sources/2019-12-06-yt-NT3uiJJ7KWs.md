# "Chris Do Is A FRAUD" — answering his critics live (2019-12-06) — livestream

> ✅ **Attribution:** Chris hosting; the entire premise is him responding to criticism **of himself**,
> so the material is unambiguously his. The critic is quoted, not present.

## Summary

★★★★★ **The corpus's only source in which he is ACCUSED of being a fraud and answers the charge.**

⚠️ **This is the inverse of what the corpus already holds.** `fraud` returns **22** hits — all of them
about **imposter syndrome**, the *fear* of being a fraud (*"are they going to think I'm a fraud?"*;
*"imposter syndrome is theft"*). **None is about being publicly called one.** Different situation,
different behaviour, and the behaviour is the find.

## ★★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★★ The format — his own "mean tweets"

> ★★★★★ *"This is **my version of reading mean tweets.** I'm going to read something mean that
> somebody said about me and **try to answer this as objectively as I can.**"*

He found it unprompted: *"recently I got on **Reddit** — Reddit's kind of an interesting dark place
sometimes — and **I was searching something and I saw this when I was just searching for my name.**"*

⚠️ 🗣️ **Note the incidental disclosure: he searches his own name.** Recorded as stated, without
inference about how often.

### ★★★★★ ⭐ He steelmans the critic BEFORE answering — this is the notable behaviour

Before touching a single charge, he reconstructs who the critic is, generously:

> ★★★★★ *"We know a couple things about this person. They're saying I'm not a graphic designer… **their
> teacher — we have that in common, that's awesome.** They've been an artist… and recently they started
> doing a few things to teach themselves [Illustrator, Photoshop, InDesign] **for the yearbook group I
> started at my school.** … **I LOVE that they're trying to learn something to teach others**, because
> they want to help the yearbook group. And **as a high school student myself I was also on the
> yearbook staff** — that was **my entrée into graphic design.**"*

✅ **He finds common ground with the person attacking him and states it first.** Then he accepts the
premise of the objection without conceding the conclusion: *"**alright, okay, I accept it — something
about me bothers you.** Could be my face, could be my voice, whatever it is."*

### ★★★★ The four charges, quoted as stated

He lists them rather than paraphrasing them away:

1. *"what I'm doing has **no real substance**… I'm **not very good at design**"*
2. *"the **design critiques** that I offer are **mediocre**"*
3. ⚠️ **the hypocrisy charge:** *"although I criticize other people for **low percentage of engagement**
   on Instagram and Twitter, **I myself have low engagement** on Instagram and Twitter"*
4. *"that I'm also **arrogant and full of it**"*

★★★ And the thread's own closing line: *"is it me, or am I crazy — what do you guys think?"*

### ★★★★ His rebuttal method — evidence, plus the alternative reading left standing

> ⏳ ★★★★ *"Here we are on The Futur's YouTube channel, and we have **six hundred thirty-seven thousand
> subscribers.** So there's an indicator that **either we're pretty good at putting out low-level
> surface-y information and we've TRICKED 637,000 people or robots to follow us** — so maybe there's
> something there."*

✅ **He states the deflationary reading of his own evidence out loud rather than letting it go
unmentioned.** That is the rhetorical move worth recording: the number is offered as *an indicator*,
not a proof, with the unflattering interpretation named.

★★★★ The other evidence he brings: the **pricing-design-work video at 2.3M views**, *"from a workshop
that I did that people paid money for — and **to the best of my knowledge not a single person asked
for their money back.** Those are usually pretty good signs."*

★★★ On the no-competition point he **agrees with the critic**: they *"wish that other people who were
knowledgeable, who were good at presenting, who had the equipment, the time and inclination, would do
this to compete against us — because it seems like we don't have a lot of competition in this space."*
