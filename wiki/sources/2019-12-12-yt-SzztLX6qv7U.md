## ★★★★★ Chris-attributed material — how he handles it

### ★★★★★ He thanks the accuser, and names why that matters

> ★★★★★ *"**First of all, I appreciate you coming on the air and just bringing it out. Yes, I'm
> aware** — I'm aware that there's this criticism that I'm a snake oil salesman, that I don't know what
> I'm talking about, that I'm just trying to make a quick buck."*

> ★★★★★ *"**Whenever I see these comments, I do my best to invite these people to come on the show.
> And without a doubt, they ALL refuse** — for one reason or another. They have a hair appointment.
> They got to feed their cat."*

> ★★★★★ *"**It is easy to say bad things about people behind their back. It's a lot harder to say it in
> front of their face.** So you're not saying it per se — **you're saying it for them.** So let's have
> this conversation."*

✅ **Two distinct moves here.** He converts an absent critic into a present proxy so the argument can
actually happen — and he **credits the person willing to say it aloud**, which costs him nothing and
reframes the exchange as collaborative rather than defensive.

### ★★★★★ ⭐ He asks the accuser to DEFINE the accusation

Rather than rebutting the label, he sets the format — *"you say whatever you're going to say, and
I'll say what I think they would say"* — and then:

> ★★★★★ *"**So what does a snake oil salesman DO, Mike?**"*

✅ **This is his documented Socratic/diagnostic method turned on an attack against himself.** He makes
the charge specify itself before answering it — the same *"diagnose before you prescribe"* discipline
the corpus records for client conversations, applied to his own reputation. Beeple's answer narrows to
something concrete and rebuttable: *"taking their money for classes and making a quick buck off young
designers who don't know any better."*
