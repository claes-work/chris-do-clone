# How to Improve Your Sales Process (After Hours) — the caretaker countermeasure (2020-03-07) — livestream

> ✅ **Attribution:** Chris in conversation, describing his own therapy and his own practice in the
> first person. A co-host prompts him.

## Summary

★★★★★ **The countermeasure to the caretaker pattern — a three-question filter his therapist gave him —
and evidence that he runs it in client meetings.** `was the request being made|statement…request`
returns **0** corpus-wide.

⚠️ **The caretaker DIAGNOSIS is already well recorded** at [[../../persona/biography|biography]] §252:
his therapist **Joan Lightfoot** told him *"you're a caretaker"*, he rejected it, she asked who mowed
the lawn. **What the corpus never had is what she told him to DO about it** — and that it transferred
into his professional behaviour.

## ★★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★★ The three-question filter

> ★★★★★ *"**I learned this from my therapist and she identified I'm a caretaker.** It's hard to believe
> when you look at me — *he's the caretaker*. **I'm a middle child, so I'm a caretaker.** … **She gave me
> a couple of questions to ask myself.**"*

1. ★★★★★ **Was a request actually made?** *"When somebody says **'I'm hungry'** — **that's a
   STATEMENT.** It didn't say *Dad, I'm hungry, Chris do you have anything to eat?* They just said *I'm
   hungry.* **So if no request was made, you just move on.**"*
   > ★★★★★ **The instinct he is defending against, named:** *"the **caretaker instinct** in you is like:
   > somebody's hungry, **I got to go make food right now, I'm going to go to the supermarket, I got to
   > take care of this thing** — I don't want to do that."*
2. ★★★★ **Can I do it?** *"Well, **I can drive, but I can't cook.**"*
3. ★★★★★ **Do I want to do it?** *"**Yes, I CAN do it** — but you know what, I just got home from work,
   and you blew off your opportunity to eat earlier, and **now your problem is my problem.** Well, no. **I
   don't want to do it. I can, but I'm not go[ing to].**"*

✅ **The filter separates capability from obligation.** *"I can, but I'm not going to"* is the whole point
— the caretaker's failure mode is treating *able* as *must*.

### ★★★★★ ⭐ And it shows up in client work — observed, not claimed

Before he explains any of it, his co-host names the behaviour:

> ★★★★★ *"**I love how you do that in client meetings too, or even in role plays — you'll be SILENT until
> you feel that you've either been given space to speak, or you've been DIRECTLY ASKED to speak.**"* →
> *"Yeah. I learned this from my therapist…"*

⚠️ **This is the valuable part, and note its structure: someone else identifies the pattern and he
confirms the source.** It is stronger evidence than a self-report, because the behaviour was visible to a
colleague before the explanation was given.

✅ **It supplies a psychological mechanism for a sales behaviour the corpus documents as technique** —
the disciplined silence in discovery calls is, on his own account, **a boundary habit learned in
therapy**, not only a negotiating tactic.
