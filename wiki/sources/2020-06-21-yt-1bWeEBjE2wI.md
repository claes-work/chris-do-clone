# Should You Show Process Books In Your Portfolio? (2020-06-21)

> ⚠️ **`transcript: whisper`** — no speaker turns, proper nouns UNVERIFIED.
> ✅ **Attribution:** Chris answering an interviewer's question, with a second voice agreeing
> (*"Yeah, I agree"*). ✅ The material below is his — the characteristic move *"**I thought the question
> was going to be a lot deeper and more interesting**… I'm going to answer the question I thought you
> were going to ask"* is his own reframe, and everything after it is his answer.

## Summary

★★★★ **A distinction the corpus does not hold — process vs. ingredients — with an analogy that carries it**,
plus a portfolio position that cuts against common teaching.

## ★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★ The portfolio answer: don't make people work for it

> ★★★★ *"There are differing schools of thought on this. **I personally don't have time to look at your
> process. I want to see your work. I want to see that it's amazing.** And **if I really love it, I might
> ask you about your process.**"*

✅ `don't have time to look at your process` returns **0**. ⚠️ **The order matters** — process is what you
earn the right to show, not what you lead with.

> ★★★★ *"A lot of teachers tell students **it's all about the process books.** And then you sit there with
> a potential employer and **you flip through a 118-page process book of every stupid sketch you've ever
> drawn.**"*

⚠️ **He names the source of the bad habit** — teaching — and he taught for ~15 years himself.
✅ The one existing `process book` hit is a **different sense** (**Paul Rand's** presentation books,
[[2023-03-31-yt-0begsejrxjI]]) — not this critique.

> ★★★★ *"Everybody's got **one very talkative friend.** They could care less if you're listening or not…
> **Don't be that person with your portfolio.**"*

✅ **The condition he attaches**, rather than a flat no: *"If you want to show process, **give it the
respect that it deserves. Design it. Document it really well. Build mock-ups around it.** Make sure it's
telling a very clear story — versus **this is literally everything I've ever done, unedited.**"*

### ★★★★★ ⭐ Process vs. ingredients — the pasta-sauce analogy

_He redirects to the question he wanted: **"Is process important? Process is SUPREMELY important."**_

> ★★★★★ *"If you go in the kitchen and you make a pasta sauce — we see there's basil, there's tomato,
> oregano, herbs and spices, salt and pepper. **People mistakenly think: well, there's the oregano,
> there's the tomato — that's a process. THAT'S NOT A PROCESS AT ALL.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"A process is: **this is what you have to do when you go to the supermarket. This is what you
> need to buy. This is how long the prep time takes. This is how long you have to let certain things
> simmer for. This is when you incorporate things. This percentage of that with this equals this result
> — TIME AFTER TIME.**"*

✅ `pasta sauce` returns **0** corpus-wide. ⚠️ **The distinction is between an INVENTORY and a
PROCEDURE** — listing what is involved is not describing how it is done, and only the second is
repeatable. 📌 **This is a strong voice datum**: a domestic, concrete image doing analytical work.

### ★★★★ ⭐ What the absence of process actually costs

> ★★★★ *"If you're a young designer, you work on a project, **all you care about is getting to the finish
> line.** And then when it's done, **you have no idea how you got there, or if you can recreate it again.**
> And you go through that every single time. **And THAT'S where the stress comes from. That's where the
> CREATIVE BLOCK comes from.**"*

⚠️ **He locates creative block in a missing procedure, not in inspiration or mood** — a mechanical
explanation of a state usually described psychologically. ⭐ Worth promoting as its own claim.

### ★★★★ Habit is not process

> ★★★★ *"They feel like they're the same, but they're not… **[habits] are merely doing something over and
> over again.** Process is **understanding and having the wherewithal to know that this is how I got from
> here to here to here.** …**We all have habits. Not all of us have a process.**"*

✅ `we all have habits` returns **0**. ⚠️ **Repetition without a model of the causal chain is not process** —
consistent with the pasta-sauce point, stated abstractly.

### ⚠️ Provenance: one line here is NOT his

> ⚠️ *"So I posted this thing — **I RESHARED something.** It says: **the difference between amateurs and
> professionals** on Twitter. **Amateurs have results and professionals have a process.**"*

⚠️⚠️ **He states plainly that he reshared it**, and does not name the author. ✅ `amateurs have results`
returns **0** — **and it must NOT be recorded as his formulation.** 📌 The aphorism is the *seed*; the
pasta-sauce analysis is what he adds.
