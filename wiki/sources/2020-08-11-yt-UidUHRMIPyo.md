## Summary

## Key claims (2020-08-11)

### ★★★★ What he used to do — 16 logos, cut out and gridded

> ★★★★ *"**When I was younger, sometimes we'd literally walk into a client meeting and have 16
> LOGOS.** We figured out a way to present them that the clients just adored: **we printed them out,
> mounted them on FOAM CORE and cut them out in — I think — 4-inch by 4-inch squares, and we would lay
> them out in a NEAT GRID on the table.** And what's really cool about doing it this **MODULAR** way is
> **they could take the ones they don't like and MOVE THEM OUT TO THE SIDE — and then we reduce it down
> to one.**"*

★★★ **And the era, plainly:** *"**back then we didn't do mock-ups and all this stuff. We didn't have
all these ideas on how to present logos. WE JUST PRESENTED THE LOGO.**"*

★★★★ **The self-assessment, which is the honest part:** *"**I would present lots of ideas. I DIDN'T
KNOW WHAT WAS RIGHT OR WRONG in terms of what was right FOR THE COMPANY. I knew that the form it took
AESTHETICALLY was right — but…**"* ✅ **The diagnosis is that volume substituted for a judgement he
couldn't yet make**, not that the old method was lazy.

### ★★★★★ What replaced it — and why one logo is possible at all

> ★★★★ *"**Now I'm in my 40s, so I would try to just present ONE logo. And THE PROCESS IS DRAMATICALLY
> DIFFERENT.**"*

★★★★★ **The sequence that makes one logo defensible:**
1. *"**have a conversation with the client, get into DISCOVERY**, and find out what we're trying to do,
   **what the logo or the brand should ACHIEVE**."*
2. *"**form an idea about WHO THE END CUSTOMER IS — what their life looks like, THEIR PAIN POINTS and
   their challenges.**"*
3. *"we would build **very complex MOOD BOARDS. WE CALL THEM STYLESCAPES** — or **EMPATHY MAPS**."*
4. ★★★★★ **Get agreement on the customer before anything is designed:** *"'the ideal customer of yours
   **looks like this, speaks like this, dresses like this, and lives in this world.** They're active,
   they're eating these kinds of things, these are the kinds of colours they live in.' **And we get
   ALIGNMENT AND AGREEMENT on that.**"*
5. ★★★★★ **Then the logo is a fit test, not a beauty contest:** *"**what we want to do is be able to
   find a logo that FITS NEATLY INTO THIS WORLD of their ideal customer.**"*

★★★★ **The test, made concrete:** *"take images from the [luxury car] brand's website that speak to who
they are and the lifestyle — **and just put in the new logo. AND IF IT FITS, IT FEELS RIGHT.**"*
★★★ **With the limit stated:** that works **for an existing company**; but *"often the clients we work
with are either **wanting to grow their market to a different customer**, or **rebranding**, or **a
brand new startup**"* — which is why the empathy map has to be built rather than borrowed.

★★★★ **And what the alignment step is really buying:** *"**instead of saying 'oh my god, that's
incredible', their expression should be: OF COURSE. YOU DID EXACTLY WHAT WE'D HOPED YOU'D DO.**"*

> ✅ **That reframes the goal of a presentation** — not delight, but **confirmation of a jointly-agreed
> brief.** It is the reason a single option can be presented at all.
