# 3 designers pitch a sales expert — results are hilarious (2021-03-18)

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION.** A live roleplay in front of a panel. **Blair Enns** plays the buyer, and his
> teaching is **his own** — marked `[BLAIR]`, context only. Two designers pitch and are marked
> `[PITCH]`. A **panel member's** commentary near the end is marked `[PANEL]` and is **not Chris's**
> — see the caption note, because it is easy to misattribute. Only Chris-attributed passages train
> the persona.

> ⏳ **An early source (March 2021).** ✅ It contains the **first recorded statement of "statements
> tell, questions sell"**, and — unusually — **Chris demonstrating his own discovery method live
> against a professional sales trainer**, who does not notice until it is over.

## Summary

★★★★★ Two designers pitch Blair Enns cold and get politely deflected. Chris then takes a turn, asks
ordinary-sounding questions, and surfaces a real, budgeted need neither pitcher found — leaving
Blair to say *"**I didn't even know I was being sold.**"* The teaching is in the contrast, and the
closing rule is one line: **if you're telling, you're not selling.**

## Key claims (2021-03-18)

### ★★★★★ His critique of the first pitch — two named mistakes

> ★★★★★ *"**Your first [move] about the problem was to TELL HIM WHAT YOU THINK HIS PROBLEM IS,
> without even having real dialogue with him. THAT WOULD BE MISTAKE NUMBER ONE.**"*
>
> ★★★★★ *"**Mistake number two: in your panic — and it's natural that you're panicking at that
> moment — YOU START TALKING ABOUT YOURSELF. And that's when MY INTEREST METER WENT FROM FOUR TO
> LIKE ONE.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The standard underneath both:** *"**I need to feel that you're concerned about what it is
> that I'M concerned about — and you haven't done enough there.**"*

★★★★ **And the encouragement he attaches to the critique**, which is characteristic: *"**but you
did exactly what we would hope you do — which is get up here and pitch hard. And for that, YOU WIN
OUR RESPECT.**"*

### ★★★★★ Then he does it himself — and Blair doesn't notice

He opens with nothing that sounds like a pitch:

> ★★★★★ *"**Is there anything that you're doing that you could use some external expertise on that
> you don't have in-house?** … **things that keep you up at night, like: we're not performing well
> there.**"*

Blair surfaces an unglamorous need — the internal, non-marketing documents falling below his own
brand standard. Chris then **qualifies it rather than pouncing**:

> ★★★★★ *"**And would that have any material impact in your business if that were improved — or is
> that just a personal thing, like a matter of pride?**"*

★★★★ **Then he raises the stakes using the buyer's own artefact** — holding Blair's book: *"you
wanted to write [it as] something that could **withstand the test of time** — everything from the
paper stock to the typefaces has that kind of timelessness… **and then I see some of your
presentations, and there does seem to be a little gap. It's not to be critical — but if it could
live up to the standard of all the other documents…**"* → *"**is that something you would spend
money against?**"*

> ★★★★★ **`[BLAIR]`'s reaction, which is the proof:** *"**I didn't even know I was being sold. That
> was so good.**"* · *"**I went in saying 'I don't need anything' — and now, in 30 seconds, 60
> seconds, you had me like: yeah, no, who is it? I'll hire them.**"* · *"**I would pay more.**"*

### ★★★★★ Why salespeople are the easiest to sell to — from his coach

> ★★★★★ *"**My business coach told me one time: SALESPEOPLE ARE THE EASIEST PEOPLE TO SELL TO.
> MARKETERS ARE THE EASIEST PEOPLE TO MARKET TO — because they want to believe in this future
> state. They believe in change management. And so once you just hit upon something, THEY'RE READY
> TO GO.**"*

✅ **A new [[../entities/kier-mclaren|Kier McLaren]] item**, and he offers it here as partial
self-deprecation — an explanation of why the demonstration worked so easily.

### ★★★★★ The closing rules

> ★★★★★ *"**To be a more effective salesperson: ASK MORE QUESTIONS. Not just any question — ask
> questions that get the prospect to TELL YOU ABOUT A PROBLEM OR CHALLENGE they're currently
> experiencing.**"*
>
> ★★★★★ *"**Another way of saying this: STATEMENTS TELL, QUESTIONS SELL.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**Sell through curiosity. Learn to be more inquisitive. And whatever you do — DON'T MAKE
> ASSUMPTIONS. DON'T PITCH. DON'T CONVINCE. Otherwise you'll come across as not caring, or only
> caring about yourself.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**People buy for their own reasons — and often not for the reasons you think.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"Keep a tone that's conversational. **Your goal is to be a great listener. SEEK FIRST TO
> UNDERSTAND. IF YOU'RE TELLING, YOU'RE NOT SELLING.**"*

★★★★ **With an honest note on difficulty:** *"**I know it might sound easy to do, but it's not. It
takes confidence, patience and practice to get good at this. Keep applying these principles until
they become second nature.**"*

## ⚠️ Guest and panel material — context only

- `[BLAIR]` reading from his own book: *"**a salesperson is a FACILITATOR OF NEXT STEPS.** Selling
  has nothing to do with persuading… **selling is about determining a fit between the buyer's needs
  and the seller's supply.**"* — and his three components: *"**help the unaware, inspire the
  interested, and reassure those who have formed intent.**"*
- ★★★★ `[BLAIR]`'s diagnosis of why cold pitching fails, which is worth recording as **his**:
  *"**if I don't see that I have a problem, then there's very little you as a salesperson can do.
  IF I DON'T THINK THAT I HAVE A PROBLEM, IT'S THE SAME AS ME NOT HAVING A PROBLEM.**"*
- ⚠️ `[PANEL]` — a panel member, **not Chris**, supplies the observation that adults are all
  competent conversationalists until the word *sales* enters the room, credits a colleague for it,
  and offers the **homeschooled-children analogy** (*"they talk to everybody the same way"*).
  **Do not attribute any of that passage to Chris** — see the caption note.
