## Summary

★★★★★ **A landmark for `topics/mindset` and one of the best-reasoned dated positions in the corpus.**
He splits the question cleanly — **good for creators, worrying for speculators** — argues the
aesthetic case against digital-only ownership from museums and the artist's hand, hedges his own
strongest claim (*"I could be totally wrong on this"*), grounds the speculation warning in **his own
comic-book collecting**, and openly hands the technical explanation to someone else because
*"I know when I'm out of my league."*

## Key claims (2021-03-23)

### ★★★★★ "I know when I'm out of my league"

> ★★★★★ *"**Non-fungible token — NFT — well, what is that? I'd love to tell you I know. AND I DON'T.**
> And I've watched enough videos — but **I KNOW WHEN I'M OUT OF MY LEAGUE**, so let's kick this over
> to [our] resident crypto expert."*

✅ **A dated, on-camera instance of the `know your depth` guard** the corpus records as a principle.
He does not attempt the explainer; he hands it off and returns for the judgement question.

### ★★★★★ The museum argument — why the original still matters

> ★★★★★ *"Anybody can take a picture of the Mona Lisa, but **that doesn't mean you own the Mona
> Lisa.** When we're talking about a painting like the Mona Lisa or one of Van Gogh's, we're seeing
> **a TRANSLATION of something that's PIGMENT ON CANVAS, translated into things recorded with light
> and then reproduced on a screen that's emitting light to your eyes. You know that's not real. You
> know there's a difference there. AND THAT'S WHY MUSEUMS EXIST. MUSEUMS WOULDN'T EXIST IF THE
> FACSIMILE OF IT WAS AS POWERFUL AS THE THING ITSELF.**"*

★★★★★ **The frame argument** — a designer's version of the same point: *"**I believe the frame** — and
I don't mean literally just the frame around the painting, but **the frame, THE WALL THAT IT'S ON,
THE HALL THAT IT'S IN, THE VENUE THAT IT RESIDES IN — CONTRIBUTES TO THE ENTIRE EXPERIENCE.** And
that's why gallery designers and people who do exhibition design put a lot of thought into **how you
see these pieces of art in a sequential manner, where it sits, the amount of light that it gets.**"*

★★★★ **And he immediately tests his own argument against a case where it fails** — the Louvre: *"it's
behind eight inches of glass, and there's a line separating you from the artwork that's maybe 10 feet
away, and then **a sea of people in front of it. So that's not [great] either — I don't even know
what's behind all that.**"*

★★★★★ **Then the version that survives the test:** *"but if you're **in close proximity to a MARK
ROTHKO or an ANDY WARHOL painting**, and you can see **the undulations, the unevenness in the
painting, the canvas, the discoloration — THAT'S THE REAL VERSION. That's why I went to the museum: I
wanted to SEE IT WITH MY OWN EYES and to EXPERIENCE it.** And I don't know if there's something with
**the SHARED ENERGY between myself and the wall and the painting**, versus a facsimile of it, a
digital approximation."*

> ★★★★★ **The compressed crux, and the best line in the source:** *"**When you talk about NFTs — THE
> FACSIMILE IS THE THING.**"*

### ★★★★★ The Picasso test — what ownership is actually for

> ★★★★★ *"If I buy a Picasso — first of all because **I love Picasso**, and Picasso is dead so they
> can't make any more, so **we know the finite supply of art.** But **I look at it and I love it: the
> colours, the form, the size, the scale, the materials and how he handles it — THE ARTIST'S HAND IN
> THE WORK.** I see that and I can appreciate that. **If the painting sells for 60 million dollars —
> that's just value I had. But EVERY DAY THAT I GET TO SEE THIS THING in my house or in my office, IT
> MAKES ME SMILE.** And **I would love to share that feeling with the world** — that's why I think
> people loan art to museums, because they want other people to enjoy it."*

★★★★ **The question he puts to the digital case:** *"**what are we enjoying? What are we seeing?**
Because these images are available online. **There's ZERO DIFFERENCE between that image and what I
can search on my phone and just display** — it's literally the same image, except one has a unique
blockchain code attached saying **this one person owns this IMPRESSION of an image.** Do I display it
on my wall? **Is it more impressive because it's on my phone on the wall? I DON'T THINK SO.**"*

★★★ **His social diagnosis, offered lightly:** *"I think people are just **buying into the idea of
something that's rare** — and **maybe people are really bored right now.**"*

### ⚠️★★★★★ The Ponzi concern — hedged, and worked through

> ⚠️★★★★★ *"**My concern — AND I COULD BE TOTALLY WRONG ON THIS — is it's like a PONZI SCHEME. The
> last person to buy that piece of art ultimately might be the sucker.**"*

★★★★ **He then works the mechanism rather than leaving it as a slur:** as long as there is a next
buyer, everyone before is fine — *"I sell it for a thousand, whoever paid a thousand is the sucker —
but they sell it for four thousand, well then the next person's the sucker, and they keep going and
everyone gets value. **And ultimately THE LAST PERSON IS THE ONE WHO PICKS UP THE TAB, if you will,
for everyone else before them.**"*

★★★★★ **Grounded in his own collecting history** — ✅ the comic-book collecting he discloses at
[[2017-03-27-yt-BX5Jy0OIVSg|2017-03-27]]:

> ★★★★★ *"**I've seen this phenomenon happen before — in the COMICS WORLD IN THE 90s**, when they
> were printing MILLIONS of copies, **which makes it NOT RARE AT ALL** — and then assigning every kind
> of gimmick: an alternate cover, a foil cover, a black-and-white cover, **just to try and drive
> demand. And a lot of SPECULATORS came into the market — PEOPLE WHO HAD NO INTEREST IN COMIC BOOKS**,
> but because they were going to buy the comic and then resell it and make money. **It was a cash
> grab. And eventually some people got caught at the end and LOST A LOT OF MONEY — AND IT ALMOST
> RUINED THE ENTIRE COMIC INDUSTRY.**"*

### ★★★★★ The verdict — split by role, not by technology

> ★★★★★ *"**Artists should TOTALLY care about NFTs** — it's all the buzz… And I think it's important
> because **if you're the CREATOR of the NFT, you're FIRST IN — you're creating it, someone's going to
> buy it, and you're going to receive value from it. I JUST WORRY ABOUT THE INVESTORS** who get in,
> hype up prices, and then somebody's going to get caught at the end. **You're not responsible for
> that, obviously.** But **it's good to get in — STRIKE WHILE THE IRON IS HOT.**"*

★★★★ **And the honest close:** *"**I don't really know what's going to happen with this. But hey — if
you're an artist and you're making art and this is good for you, MORE POWER TO YOU.** A lot of people
who are in for a quick buck are going to run into this and buy some project because somebody told them
it's going to go up."*

⚠️ **A quantified claim, probably his:** *"**97% of NFT PROJECTS — not the NFT space — are very
vulnerable to not being successful investments. I believe that to be true out of history, out of
knowing human psychology.**" **Speaker is probable-but-not-certain** from the captions; recorded with
that flag.

### ★★★★★ On Beeple — consistency as the thing worth admiring

He knows the artist personally (*"our friend"*) and had him booked on the show.

- ★★★★ **The character read:** *"he has a kind of vibe about him — **very plain spoken, plain dress**,
  shaggy hair, kind of dishevelled. **HE IS WHO HE PRESENTS HIMSELF TO BE, AND HE'S NOT CHANGED ONE
  BIT.**"* ✅ Reads directly onto the **authenticity-gap** material.
- ★★★★ **The craft read, with the honest qualifier:** *"he's a fantastic artist… **a hybrid between CG
  3D rendering and Photoshop**, and he does this at such a prolific pace — one every single day — that
  it's incredible he has pieces that are very consistent and they're good. **NOT ALL OF THEM ARE
  WINNERS, of course — but for the volume in which he's doing it, it's incredible.**"*
- ★★★★★ **The argument for why he deserves it:** *"if someone's going to cash in on NFTs, **I CAN'T
  THINK OF ANYONE MORE DESERVING.** The guy is doing something **because he loves it**, and he couldn't
  imagine over 10 years ago that one day he'd sell a digital copy of his artwork for millions."*
- ★★★★★ **The line, and it is measured against his own experience:** *"**I know what it's like to
  create 45 days' worth of content — so forget about 10 YEARS of doing it 365 days a year.** … **WHAT
  HAVE YOU DONE IN YOUR LIFE THAT YOU WERE THAT CONSISTENT — BESIDES BREATHING?**"*
