## ★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★★ Information ≠ education

> ★★★★★ *"**People confuse information with education, and they're very different things.**
> **Information is a commodity — it's at the end of a search string on Google.** The difference
> between information and education is **there's an OUTCOME that we're trying to help you [reach].**"*

### ★★★★ The asymmetry of obligation — the part that gives it teeth

> ★★★★ *"**The person who publishes a book — the book is information. Their only purpose in life is
> to make a book that people want to buy, and they market it to you, after which they do
> nothing.** Whereas **educational institutions have a COMMITMENT to you** to do the very best they
> can to make sure **you have some mastery over the thing you signed up for.**"*

✅ **This is a definition by obligation, not by content.** A book and a course can carry identical
material; what separates them is that **one party owes you an outcome and the other owes you
nothing after the sale.**

### ★★★★ And the test he derives from it

> ★★★★ *"They **invest in facilities, invest in their teachers**, and they **charge you a hefty
> premium** for that. So… **if we strip out the information and put a value on that**, then the
> difference between what we'd pay for the information and **their commitment to education** — plus
> the facilities and the networking functions — **we have to say: is it worth it?**"*

⚠️ **Note that this is a genuinely NEUTRAL framing**, which is worth flagging because his recorded
positions on design school are largely critical. Here he **names what a school actually sells**
(commitment, facilities, network) and poses the price question as an open subtraction rather than a
rhetorical one. **A fair reading of the clone should not make him more anti-school than this.**
