## Summary

★★★★ **A concrete, physical learning method the corpus does not hold** — and the reason he says it worked.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)

- **[2021-11-04] Copywork — copy the work as closely as possible, "as if you're a forger."** He concedes
  it is *"boring, technical, tedious"*, which is why most designers skip it.
- **[2021-11-04] What made it work was drawing structure onto someone else's work, not reproducing its look.**
- **[2021-11-04] Copywork is a stage, not a destination** — *"at some point you move away from that, but in
  the very early stages copywork is great."*

## Notable verbatim quotes

### ★★★★★ ⭐ The practice, with the instrument named

> ★★★★★ *"Back in the day there was **no bigger designer than DAVID CARSON**. He was the king of all
> design… and they **copied his aesthetic** — **what they didn't understand, [they] didn't copy his
> STRUCTURE and his THINKING.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"But what I would do is **I would open up these booklets and, with PEN AND RULER, just draw over
> it** — so I can see the connections. When I started **drawing grid lines over everything he did**, I got
> a very strong sense that **this man understands grid and design** — these things were **not just
> happening by accident.** So I felt like **I discovered some kind of secret key** just by doing that."*

✅ `drawing grid lines` returns **0** and `as if you're a forger` returns **0** corpus-wide.
⚠️ `copywork` returns **1**, but that hit is only a **tag** on a `do-not-train` page about *writing* and
Seth Godin — **the design practice is effectively absent.** ✅ `David Carson` returns **3**, none of them
this story.

⚠️ **The method's logic is worth preserving exactly**: copying the *surface* teaches nothing; **overlaying
a grid is a way of forcing the underlying structure to become visible.** 📌 It is also **falsifiable in his
own telling** — he was testing whether Carson's layouts were deliberate, and concluded they were.

### ★★★ The framing

> ★★★★ *"If you want to get really good at anything, **it's just copy it as closely as possible.** A lot of
> designers don't do this **because this kind of work is boring, it's technical, it's tedious** — but
> I've got to tell you, **that work paid off.**"*

> ★★★ *"**Copywork is underutilised and underrated**… Do your best to copy the work **as if you're a
> forger.** Do that first."*
