## ★★★★ The four types

| Type | The tell | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| **The Fisher** | *"very indecisive… **information gathering**… talking to different vendors"*, and **"they often will ask about price first"** | ⚠️ **They're assembling an RFP** — you may be unpaid research for someone else's shortlist |
| **The Square Pegger** | *"most often times **they're not the final decision maker**… they have this mindset that **their job's on the line**, so they ask a lot of **funky weird questions** because they're doing their due diligence"* | The odd questions are **fear, not curiosity** — they are defending a decision upward |
| **The Cost Conscious** | *"very **budget driven**… **views design as a commodity**"* | ✅ Ties to the corpus's existing commodity/differentiation doctrine |
| **The Perfect Client** | *"very **familiar with the design process**, **respectful** of it, they **appreciate** it, they **value design** — so it's **not always lowest bid wins** — and they're **ready to start right away**"* | ⏳ Note the last criterion: **urgency is part of his definition of ideal**, not just respect |

### Why the taxonomy earns a page

✅ **The corpus is rich on how to HANDLE clients and thin on how to CLASSIFY them on first contact.**
Each type here is defined by an **observable behaviour in the first conversation** — asks price
first; asks defensive questions; treats design as interchangeable; already knows the process — which
makes it a **triage tool**, not a set of personality labels.

⚠️ **The Square Pegger reading is the non-obvious one and the most useful**: it reframes an
irritating prospect as a frightened intermediary, which points at a different response (arm them to
defend the choice) than the doctrine's usual advice to reach the decision-maker.
