## ★★★ The hierarchy, in his words

> ★★★★ *"The four priorities is: **first we want to win without pitching** whenever possible — just
> use conversation to win the job. **Our positioning and our expertise** allows the client [to] just
> say *we want to work with you*. **If not, we try to derail the pitch.** And **we want to gain the
> inside track.** Or, when things don't work and we don't have the terms that we want, **we just walk
> away.**"*

| # | Priority | The condition it depends on |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Win without pitching** | positioning + expertise strong enough that conversation closes it |
| 2 | **Derail the pitch** | you cannot avoid the process, so you change its shape |
| 3 | **Gain the inside track** | you must compete, so you compete from inside |
| 4 | **Walk away** | the terms are unacceptable |

### ★★★★ The gloss — and it is the part that is his

> ★★★★ *"**We can't be an expert when we make too many concessions. We have to walk away.**"*

✅ **This ties the ladder to the expertise doctrine the corpus already carries extensively.** Walking
away is not framed as pride or leverage — it is framed as **the thing that keeps the expert claim
true**. Concede enough and the positioning that made step 1 possible stops being real.

⚠️ **A descending ladder, not four tactics.** Each rung is what you do when the rung above is
unavailable — which is why recording it as a unit is worth more than the fragments.
