## ★★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★★ The bias, stated plainly

> ★★★★★ *"There's this concept called **hyperbolic discounting**, and the concept is just this, it's
> very simple: **the farther away something is, the less you're motivated to do it.** So that's why
> we go for **short-term reward versus long-term gains.**"*

### ★★★★ The worked example — how it eats a revenue target

★★★★ *"Let's just say **you have to get to five million dollars this year in revenue** — you just
have to. But today, looking in front of you, is **an email you need to respond to**, or whatever
number of **a thousand distractions** that you have in your life. Those seem **more present, more
immediate**, and then you'll do that instead."*

★★★★ **And the version that names the real cost:** *"Maybe you know that in order for you to get to
five million dollars this year, **you've got to read 17 books and do four lectures** — and that's
hard to do. But instead of reading the book, you do [the easy thing]."*

✅ **Why this earns a page rather than a `dup-of`.** The corpus records the *conclusion* in many
places — think long-term, delayed gratification, the three-to-six-month operator who *"robs their
client"* ([[2023-02-16-yt-bNgRrcIcaFY]]). **What it did not have is the named mechanism**, and the
mechanism is what makes the advice actionable: the problem is not weak discipline, it is that
**distance itself discounts motivation**, so the fix is to shorten the distance rather than to try
harder.
