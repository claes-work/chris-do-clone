## Key claims (2023-02-16)

### ★★★★★ The frame

> ★★★★★ *"**What is sales? WE ALL HAVE THE WRONG CONCEPT OF WHAT SALES IS.** What I found helpful is
> to remember sales as an acronym: **SERVICE, ASK, LISTEN, EMPATHIZE AND SUMMARIZE.** Notice — **in
> SALES, the word 'sales' is not in sales at all. It's not giving a big old presentation. It's not
> manipulative. It's none of those things.**"*

★★★★★ **The dependency, which is what makes it a system rather than a list:** *"**if you can get
number one right, ALL THE OTHER ONES FALL IN LINE. If you screw up this idea of servitude and being
of service to other people, THE QUESTIONS THAT YOU ASK WILL BE DIFFERENT, HOW YOU LISTEN WILL BE
DIFFERENT, YOU WON'T EMPATHIZE AT ALL — you won't even think about summarizing, because you don't even
understand the other person's problem.**"*

### ★★★★★ S — Serve

> ★★★★★ *"**What does it mean to serve someone? It's to TEMPORARILY PUT ON HOLD YOUR OWN NEEDS AND
> WANTS and be of service to others.** And there's some element of **SELF-SACRIFICE** in here. If you
> think about people who have served, we think about the military — **you're literally going to put at
> risk your life for a bigger, more noble cause.**"*

★★★★★ **What it means operationally:** *"**you're going to spend the time to learn WHAT IT IS THAT
YOUR CLIENTS WANT, WHERE THEY FAILED BEFORE, WHAT IT MEANS FOR THEM TO SUCCEED, WHAT'S AT STAKE IF
THEY FAIL** — these are very important things for you to think about **BEFORE YOU EVEN BEGIN TO TAKE
ANY ACTION WHATSOEVER.**"*

### ★★★★★ A — Ask

> ★★★★★ *"**The best way to sell is to ask really smart CALIBRATED QUESTIONS** — to use **CHRIS
> VOSS's** language in ***Never Split the Difference***. And I want to ask an **OPEN-ENDED** question.
> **KEVIN DALEY**, who writes about this in ***Socratic Selling***, calls it **THE SOCRATIC OPEN** —
> to ask a question that **PRIMES YOUR PROSPECT into thinking about what it is they want to
> achieve.**"*

★★★★ **The openers**, with one credited: *"'**so, what's on your mind?**' — this is a **MICHAEL BUNGAY
STANIER** opener. **When a prospect agrees to meet with you, THEY HAVE SOMETHING ON THEIR MIND.**"* →
alternatives: *"**let me ask you this question: why are we having this meeting?**"* and *"**what is a
big problem in your business that you haven't been able to solve that you'd like help with?**"*

★★★★ **And the warning against sounding scripted:** *"**this is what you all want to work on — because
you don't want to sound like a robot, you don't want to sound like a person that's reading a script or
doing something that's very TEMPLATIZED.**"*

★★★★★ **The discipline that follows the answer:** *"when they give you a hint as to what the problem
might be, **RESIST THE COMPULSION TO IMMEDIATELY SOLVE THE PROBLEM.** What you don't know yet is
**THEY'VE JUST PRESENTED WHAT THEY THINK THE PROBLEM IS.** And like that good doctor, **we've got to
put our finger on the pulse a little bit and take their temperature.** … '**Before we get into the
solution, let me just ask you a few more questions to make sure this is correct.**' And by doing this,
**you're walking them through your own thought process — so INSTEAD OF YOU CONVINCING THEM, THEY'RE
GOING TO GET CLARITY on what the problem is and what the potential solution might be.**"*

### ★★★★★ The hardest objection — "what if the answer isn't your service?"

`[HOST]` puts the real problem to him: her instinct used to be *"well, I do video, so surely the
foregone conclusion is that the solution is going to be video — **that's not putting my needs on
hold**"*, and asks how to resist forcing the sale.

> ★★★★★ *"**You have to ask yourself: WHAT KIND OF PERSON AM I? WHAT KIND OF COMPANY DO I WANT TO RUN?
> HOW LONG DO I WANT TO PLAY THIS GAME?** If you're committed to doing this **only for three to six
> months**, well — **you're going to get as much as you can and you're going to run. It's going to be
> a SMASH AND GRAB situation, and you're going to ROB YOUR CLIENT.** That won't solve their problem
> and ultimately not serve them at all — **and then they wonder why they don't have a business in
> seven, eight months.**"*
>
> ★★★★★ *"**So YOUR MINDSET DETERMINES YOUR ACTIONS, AND YOUR ACTIONS DETERMINE THE RELATIONSHIPS
> YOU'RE GOING TO HAVE.**"*

★★★★★ **The ethics argument, stated plainly:** *"**what is the point of having ethics, morality, if
you don't ever act on them?** I'm not asking you to change, to be someone else — **I'm asking you to
think about WHO YOU ARE AS IT RELATES TO WHO YOU WANT TO BE.** How you act, if it's consistent with
who you are, **brings you joy. WHAT SENDS YOU INTO THE LAND OF GUILT AND SHAME IS WHEN YOU ACT OUTSIDE
OF WHO YOU THINK YOU ARE.**"*

★★★ **Credited:** *"**Jordan Peterson** talks about this — **every time you're asked to make a
decision and YOU FEEL WEAK, IT'S A SIGN THAT YOU'RE GOING TO DO SOMETHING THAT'S AGAINST YOUR CORE
BELIEFS. So he says: FIND THE COURAGE TO ACT in this moment of weakness.**"* ⚠️ Recorded as the
attribution he gives; no endorsement beyond the cited idea is implied.

### ★★★★★ Customer service is a memory

> ★★★★★ *"When we talk about customer service, what we don't understand is what customer service
> really is: **YOU'RE CREATING A MEMORY FOR A PERSON. THAT IS CUSTOMER SERVICE.**"*

★★★★ **The two cases:** *"this is why **Nordstrom** is still in business today — they have one of the
most generous return policies anywhere; **they don't prioritise lower prices, they prioritise better
customer service.** This is why **Zappos** was sold to Amazon for a billion dollars, when they sold a
commodity with fairly low profit margins — **because they prioritised customer service.**"* → *"**WHAT
IS THE MEMORY THAT YOU'RE CREATING?** When you understand this, your relationships with the people who
matter in your life — your friends, your family, your parents, your teachers, your students, and
especially your customers — **will change. IT'S A MINDSET THING. It's why we call it SALES
PSYCHOLOGY.**"*

### ★★★★★ L — Listen (and his own method, concretely)

> ★★★★★ *"**Active listening is very different from how we see people listen today. Most people just
> STAY QUIET LONG ENOUGH SO THAT THEY CAN FORMULATE WHAT THEY WANT TO SAY NEXT.** … you can see their
> eyes drift — **they're here physically, but they're not PRESENT. They're thinking about lunch,
> they're thinking about a smart follow-up, they're thinking about saying something that's going to
> IMPRESS you.**"*

★★★★★ **His podcast practice, which is the most concrete account of it in the corpus:** *"I do some
very light initial research… I ask them to introduce themselves and to tell a little story, and then
the conversation begins. **And I might have a whole list of questions I want to ask them — MOST OF THE
TIME I NEVER EVEN LOOK AT THEM AGAIN, because WHATEVER THEY ARE ABOUT TO TELL ME IS A CLUE to what is
important to them.** If you ever look at my notepad after a podcast interview, **you'll see there are
like EIGHT PAGES written on both sides — words, rabbit holes, things I circle, things I draw an arrow
to, 'follow up on this', 'that's a good hook', underlining something.** And what I'm doing in that
moment is: **I'M TRYING TO RELIVE THE EMOTION WITH THEM.**"*

### ★★★★★ E — Empathize

★★★★ **Credited again to Kevin Daley:** *"in *Socratic Selling*, Kevin Daley says: **IN ORDER FOR US
TO GET TO THE FUTURE, WE MUST GO TO THE PAST FIRST.** Despite the name of the company and the podcast,
**we do need to revisit the past — because what we're trying to do is CLOSE A SALE IN THE FUTURE, we
want their money in the future, AND WE RUSH TO IT.**"*

★★★★★ **What it sounds like, and why it pays:** *"**tell me how you FAILED in the past, and what did
that do to your business.** And what the client's doing is **they're RELIVING THE PAINFUL MOMENT with
you — and they feel it, and you feel it. AND YOU'RE GETTING VALUABLE INFORMATION:** last time the
person promised this and didn't deliver; last time I called them they were non-responsive; last time
they promised three solutions and gave me one."* →

> ★★★★★ *"**It would behoove you to present them a solution at some point that ADDRESSES ALL OF THESE
> CONCERNS. THIS BECOMES YOUR MARKETING COPY.**"*

### ★★★★★ S — Summarize, and the closing sequence

> ★★★★★ *"There's only one thing left to do — and the last S is **NOT TO SELL. The last S is to
> SUMMARIZE**: '**here's what I heard you say — does that sound right? Is there anything else?**'"*

★★★★★ **The hypothetical close:** *"once you get a complete understanding of the problem and a
potential solution, **you're going to close with a HYPOTHETICAL QUESTION**: '**if you saw a proposal
that did X, Y and Z in X-Y time for Z price, would you be interested in moving forward?**' At this
point, **if you've done your job, IT SHOULD BE A SMOOTH, EFFORTLESS YES.**"*

★★★★★ **The pause question — pre-empting buyer's remorse:** *"'**if I put a proposal in front of you
at the close of business day, IS THERE ANYTHING ELSE THAT WOULD GIVE YOU PAUSE in terms of moving
forward?**' And we ask this question because **people get into this thing called BUYER'S REMORSE —
because there's so much rapport building and THEY FEEL SO GUILTY TO SAY NO.**"* → what surfaces: a
partner who must be consulted, payment terms, a nagging concern.

★★★★★ **Then hand them the problem:** *"'**okay — so what might be a solution to that?**' **And you
ACTIVATE THEM to help you solve this. HOW MIGHT WE SOLVE THAT? And then THEY DESIGN A SOLUTION WITH
YOU.**"* → *"and sometimes you can't deliver, **and that's okay — and it's good to know.** If you
can't, you need to ask another question: **IS THIS A DEAL KILLER? Is this not happening contingent on
someone being able to say yes to this?** And if they say yes it's a deal killer, then you say: **if I
can't deliver that, that's unfortunate. It's the one thing I can't give you.**"*

### ★★★★★ Why the proposal is not the sale — Blair Enns credited

> ★★★★★ *"**Most people HIDE BEHIND A PROPOSAL.** They're not willing to have this conversation up
> front, so **they do a lot of work on the back end to prepare incredibly well-crafted proposals that
> the clients (a) don't read, and (b) skip to the bottom line and say 'this is not going to work for
> me.'** **To quote BLAIR ENNS: THE PROPOSAL ISN'T THE DOCUMENT THAT YOU SEND — IT'S THE WORDS YOU
> SAY. THE DOCUMENT IS JUST A RECEIPT FOR THE WORDS.**"*

✅ **A fourth dated Blair Enns credit** (2017 ×2, 2018, 2023) — see
[[../entities/blair-enns|his entity page]].

★★★★ **The consolation he offers if it still doesn't close:** *"even if you don't close as often as
you'd like, **you've at least now saved yourself a lot of work and heartache.** One of the most
difficult things to do in most enterprises that provide highly bespoke custom services is **the
proposal writing process.** If nothing else, **I've saved you some time from doing unnecessary work
for clients who are not ready to buy — and I think that's worth something.**"*
