## ★★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★★ "What makes you weird makes you wonderful" — an adaptation he credits and dates

> ★★★★★ *"If we say **only about two percent of adults survive as a creative person** — within that
> group **they're still questioning things**: 'Chris, I'm still trying to fit in a box.' There's this
> **need to conform. WE LEARNED A LONG TIME AGO THAT TO GET ALONG WE HAVE TO GO ALONG — and BEING
> WEIRD GETS US OSTRACIZED.** It gets us funny looks; sometimes we're put on a different bus, or
> teased. Those patterns of thinking and the need to fit in **are so strong and prevalent EVEN WITHIN
> THE TWO PERCENT THAT SURVIVE. And I do everything I can to push people to EMBRACE YOUR INNER
> WEIRD.**"*
>
> ★★★★★ *"**JAMES VICTORE** has this expression which I love — **which I CO-OPTED AND MODIFIED a
> little bit.** His thing is: **'what makes you weird as a kid makes you a creative as an adult.'**
> That's a lot to say. **I just say: WHAT MAKES YOU WEIRD MAKES YOU WONDERFUL.** And when we learn to
> lean into that, **it becomes your superpower.**"*

✅✅ **A rare, explicit act of showing his working.** He names the source, states the original,
says he shortened it, and gives his version — in one breath. ⚠️ **The corpus should never present
*"what makes you weird makes you wonderful"* as wholly his**, and should carry the Victore original
alongside it.

### ★★★★ A fifth dated Blair Enns credit

> ★★★★ *"**Blair Enns told me: do your due diligence, write a good book — BECAUSE YOU CAN'T UNPUBLISH
> A BOOK.**"*

✅ **Corroborates the 2017 interview**, where Blair attributes *"you can't unpublish"* to a novelist
friend ([[2017-05-03-yt-KtL02T5MonA|2017-05-03]]). **Chris received it from Blair directly**, and is
still quoting it six years later. See [[../entities/blair-enns|Blair Enns]].

★★★ **On what a book does:** *"there's something **very permanent about a book**, and how seriously we
take it as a work of ideas — and maybe even within the realm of academia, **you're legitimate as soon
as you write a book.**"*

### ★★★★ On ghostwriting — and his own limits

> ★★★★ *"The first thing whenever you read a book is **you wonder who's the ghostwriter**, because
> these days there's a lot of people who co-sign onto things — maybe they told somebody a story and
> then that person does the heavy lifting. **And of course I respect that too, there's nothing wrong
> with that — BUT IT'S MORE ADMIRABLE WHEN IT'S YOU AND IT'S ALL YOU, beginning to end.**"*

★★★★ **And immediately against himself:** *"maybe there's some **shots fired**, because **I did write
my own book — and I'm like: I CAN'T DESIGN ALL THESE THINGS, I CAN'T DO ALL THESE THINGS, I NEED A LOT
OF HELP.**"*

### ★★★★ His own arc, in one sentence

> ★★★★ *"**Somewhere along the way I found my confidence, I found my voice, and I LET MY INNER FREAK
> FLAG OUT — and I raise it proudly.** But I take it that **it's rare amongst people, because we
> wouldn't be talking about it if everybody already knew how to do it.**"*

### ★★★★ Interviewer craft, visible

- ★★★ **He flags his own speculation as speculation:** *"**this is me playing amateur psychoanalyst**
  — if you don't turn outward for validation, then you turn inward… **you learned to love yourself**,
  and to look at the world as: **I'm an independent person, an operating system — I don't need
  external inputs to tell me if I'm moving in the right [direction].**"* ✅ The **operating-system**
  metaphor again (cf. [[2017-09-27-yt-UHrjn11F6hc|2017-09-27]]).
- ★★★ **He narrates why a story landed:** *"the reason you got such a big reaction from me is **this
  story took an ABRUPT TURN** — I thought you were setting up 'I'm a city boy, and then my childhood,
  I'm all outdoors, and Chris, I'm moving to the woods' — **and then you're like: no, I don't like the
  outdoors.**"* ✅ A worked example of expectation-then-disruption, applied to a guest's story in real
  time.
- ★★★ **The affirmation he gives:** *"I don't think there's anything really strange or weird about it
  — **it's just your acceptance, your acknowledgment of it, DESPITE HOW OTHER PEOPLE MAY REACT.**"*
- ★★★ **A small aesthetic disclosure**, on New York: *"**the concrete jungle, the gleaming towers of
  glass and steel, the brick-stone buildings that have been around for a couple hundred years**… you
  could point the camera at any angle at any time of day and **it's breathtaking.**"* → *"you don't get
  that in the woods — **you get trees. A different kind of skyscraper.**"*

## ⚠️ Guest material — context only, NOT persona data

*The Laws of Creativity* is structured in three parts — **foundation, process, excellence** — written
as laws. His account: he took notes on his phone for ten years, wrote it during the pandemic at his
wife's prompting, and finished in **11 months** by **never going backwards** (*"once you're done with
a chapter… you save it, you move on"*), because *"a lot of people get stuck in HYPER-ANALYSING what
they've done and treat it as progress, when it's really THE FACADE OF PROGRESS."* He designed, wrote
and illustrated it himself — while noting *"that's not my philosophy in general at Baron Fig."*
⚠️ His mission-origin story, his self-acceptance account and his *"I don't like being outside"*
disclosure are **his own** and are not reproduced here.
