## Summary

★★★★ **One strong, specific AI argument** — not *"will it take our jobs"* but **"what happens when you
remove the rungs people climbed"** — plus a compact statement of **caring about the work without being
destroyed by feedback.**

## ★★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★★ The removing-the-ladder argument

> ★★★★★ *"**The people I know who are professionals, who are creative, who know how to direct and lead
> teams and put things together — THEY'RE NOT AS THREATENED BY AI as the people who have yet to make
> their living doing it.** The low-hanging fruit that **AI is going to be really good at replacing is
> that STOCK-PHOTOGRAPHY LEVEL of things — sitting through hours and hours and hours of footage.**
> That's not what we strive to do at the higher level — **BUT WE HAD TO DO THAT TO GET TO THE HIGHER
> LEVEL.** If we start to remove that and **it's just a push button, MY FEAR IS THAT SOME OF THE NEWER
> GENERATION ARE NOT GOING TO BE TRAINED ON THE SAME SORT OF FUNDAMENTALS that we may have been
> trained on.**"*

✅ **Note what the argument is NOT.** It is not that AI produces bad work, and not that seniors are
safe because they are better. It is that **the grunt work was the training**, and automating it
removes the path rather than the destination. ⚠️ **A concern, stated as a fear**, not a prediction.

### ★★★★★ Caring about the work without being destroyed by it

> ★★★★★ *"There's **a very tricky line — or tightrope — to walk: TO INJECT YOURSELF WHERE IT'S
> NECESSARY, TO CARE DEEPLY ABOUT THE WORK, BUT YET NOT SO MUCH THAT YOU LOSE YOURSELF IN THE WORK** —
> so that when the idea is not approved, or clients want to make changes, **you feel MORTALLY WOUNDED
> because they didn't love that thing that you were so passionate about.** It's a real hard thing **to
> unplug at the end of the day and not take all those things personally** — but I think there's **a
> huge talent in getting to that next level of being able to BE MORE OBJECTIVE ABOUT THE WORK.**"*

✅ The operational form of the **passionate detachment** material the corpus records elsewhere, and
notably framed as **a skill to be acquired** (*"a huge talent"*), not a temperament.

## ⚠️ Guest material — context only, NOT persona data

The guest's own career: an early start at **Fuel** as it was splintering, the spin-out of **Brand New
School**, the dot-com-era acquisition context, and his current role at a long-established theatrical
advertising / trailer house that **pivoted after the pandemic** from film marketing toward design and
branding. His strategic read — *"**content is now the product**"*, and that big tech brands have
become content providers — **is his**, though Chris does not dispute it.
