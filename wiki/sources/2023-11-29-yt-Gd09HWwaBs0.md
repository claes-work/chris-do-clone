# Build An Irresistible Personal Brand That Is Authentic & Vulnerable

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do is effectively the sole voice.** Opens with a short question from an
> unnamed audience member (a studio-of-one owner asking whether to build a personal brand or a studio
> brand); everything else is Chris. `attribution: sole-subject`. A clip, likely excerpted from a
> longer workshop or masterclass.

## Summary

A dense, compact statement of the philosophy underneath Chris's personal-branding teaching — and the
clearest articulation in the corpus of **why he refuses the standard corporate-branding vocabulary**.

He opens by casting doubt on a category most of his industry takes for granted: *"I'm not sure there's
such a thing as a business brand."* What people call a business brand is really "the efforts of a
collective team of marketers, writers, designers creating an experience." The problem with importing
that language into personal branding is that corporate branding exists to make you do something — and
"I'm just tired of people trying to sell me something. I'm allergic to it now."

He then gives a potted **history of branding** — subsistence → industrial specialisation →
differentiation between local producers → the grocer who no longer knows you personally, so the
company must carry the recommendation → counterfeit protection → today's unlimited choice — and lands
on a two-word definition: **branding is differentiation and preference.** Preference he defines
frankly as irrational: *"an irrational emotional connection to a thing that I shouldn't have, but I
do."* He illustrates with bottled water (Arrowhead ~35¢ vs. Fiji ~$3 vs. Voss ~$5 — same category,
different stories) and with a personal example about a Korean teriyaki restaurant he'd buy a thousand
chickens from.

The final third is the payoff and the most valuable part: **Carl Jung's persona and shadow**, applied
to personal branding. The persona is who you pretend to be to be accepted; the shadow is "the ugly
stuff… the stuff that brings you shame." The persona is installed by parents around age seven or
nine. And the conclusion — the line the whole clip exists for: *"when you show up and pretend to be
somebody that you're not for long enough, you forget who the hell you are… All I'm trying to do is get
people to remember. **It's not invention. It's memory.**"*

## Key claims — Chris Do, dated 2023-11-29

- ★ **Personal branding, for him, isn't a selling technique — it's self-knowledge.** *"When I try to
  teach people about personal branding, it's not so much I'm trying to sell you anything. It's mostly:
  I want to know who the hell I'm supposed to be in this life, and I want to heal and to show up 100%
  as I am. And when I can do that, oddly enough, I seem to attract more people to me."* ★
- ★ **He doubts "business brand" is a real category.** *"I'm still not sure if I fully am committed to
  what I'm about to say, but I'm not sure there's such a thing as a business brand. There are things
  that people refer to as a brand as a business, but what they're referring to is the efforts of a
  collective team of marketers, writers, designers creating an experience."* Note the hedge — he flags
  his own uncertainty on air. ★
- ★ **Why importing corporate branding language into personal branding is the "wrong way."** "A lot of
  us understand what brands do on a corporate level, which is to market to us — their whole goal was
  to get us to do something." People teaching personal branding "use the same literal language from
  corporate branding… they're teaching you to go and help you sell more things. I don't know about
  you, but I'm just tired of people trying to sell me something. I'm just kind of allergic to it now."
  He states the stakes plainly: *"if you do it the way I hope you'll do it, you'll have a competitive
  advantage over everybody else who's doing it the wrong way — and I'll just be as polarising as: there's
  a right way and there's a wrong way."* ★
- ★ **Branding = differentiation + preference; preference is irrational.** *"Branding is preference.
  Branding is when I have an irrational emotional connection to a thing that I shouldn't have, but I
  do — cuz we're irrational emotional people. We're creatures designed to really respond emotionally."*
  And the commodity test: *"Do you want people to have a preference for what you do? Probably. Then you
  have a brand. If not, you're a commodity."* ★
- **His history of branding (business/branding).** Specialisation under industrialisation creates
  competition → producers create identity purely "as a point of differentiation" → the town grows and
  "the grocer no longer knows you on a personal level, can't make recommendations anymore, and so now
  that job is put on the companies" → packaging, design, materials, colours, messaging → counterfeit
  protection to defend the customer relationship. He dates the modern concept of branding to "only like
  30 years old or so," heightened by internet and social media, and frames the shift as: with more
  wealth, less disease and longer lives, *"instead of survival we're looking for meaning."*
- **Every product has a story; some aren't worth mentioning.** Arrowhead — "I'm almost certain it's just
  filtered water," ~35¢. Fiji — *"artisanal water, untouched by humans, filtered through lava rock of
  the Fijian islands,"* a squared-off bottle with the island printed on the back so you see it through
  the water, ~$3. Voss ~$5. His aside: "as far as I know this came from Colorado and came out of a tap
  — but it's a good story." The prompt he turns on the audience: *"what's your story? How manufactured
  is your story? What is the integrity and the authenticity of your voice? Is it there?"*
- ★ **The teriyaki-restaurant story (biography/values, self-reported — a good preference example).**
  He and his wife love a small Korean teriyaki place called **Terry and Yaki**; his older son says only
  they eat there and it'll go out of business. Chris: *"if they're going to go out of business, I'm
  going to go over there and buy a thousand chickens."* Why? *"I don't know, I can't even tell you
  why. Maybe because when I was an ArtCenter student, or working around here, it was an affordable
  meal, and I have some weird connection to that. Every time I take a bite, maybe it transports me back
  30 years — I'm reminded of my starving self."* ★
- ★ **Jung's persona and shadow, applied to personal branding (mindset — the clip's core).** *"There's
  the persona… it's who you pretend to be to be accepted in society, and all of us are in our persona —
  it's undeniable. And then on the other side of the persona is your shadow self. It's the ugly stuff,
  it's stuff that no one wants to talk about, it's the stuff that brings you shame and you feel like if
  you were to expose that you'll be ostracised — and there's a good chance you might be."* He dates its
  installation: "from childhood, maybe we're like seven or nine years old, you've gotten the persona put
  on you by your parents." His examples of the instruction set: *don't stare at that person, it's not
  nice; that's just you being you* — and the deliberately absurd booger bit ("it's tasty, it's salty,
  it's a good snack, it's all natural, it's non-GMO") to make the point that the ego learns to regulate
  what shows up in public. ★
- ★ **The conclusion: "It's not invention. It's memory."** *"When you show up and pretend to be somebody
  that you're not for long enough, you forget who the hell you are. And when we get into the personal
  branding stuff, all I'm trying to do is get people to remember. That's it. It's not invention. It's
  memory."* ★
- **Marty Neumeier, credited (influence).** From *The Brand Flip*: *"we don't want to be sold things but
  we love to buy"* — and as it evolves, *"we don't want to buy, we join tribes, because we're looking
  for connection and meaning in our lives."*
- **On spending as a values act (cited).** Attributes to "Anna Lappé" the line that *"every dollar you
  spend is a vote for the kind of world you want to live in"* — his application: "when I buy Arrowhead
  versus Voss versus Fiji, I say I would like for those companies to thrive."
- **A characteristic self-flag (voice).** *"I tell people I say dangerous things because I pretend like
  I know what I'm talking about"* — offered just before introducing Jung, i.e. as a hedge before a
  claim outside his field.
