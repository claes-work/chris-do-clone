## Summary

A solo coaching video on why outreach to ideal clients fails, framed entirely around **limiting
beliefs**. The guest presents a **"momentum map"** — belief → feeling → action → result → reinforced
belief — and argues that "80% of the results we get in our life and business are determined by how we
think about those things and 20% by what we actually do."

He then gives three shifts: **active over passive**, **serving over selling**, and **the fortune is in
the follow-up**.

Notable for the corpus mainly because the **serve-don't-sell** material closely parallels Chris's own
long-standing doctrine — but here it is a *different person's* articulation, promoted on Chris's
channel, and must not be attributed to Chris. The guest also explicitly cross-references "an
irresistible offer" and tells viewers to Google *his* name for it, which is worth noting because
"irresistible offer" is also established Futur/Chris vocabulary in the corpus (see
[[../topics/pricing/pricing]] §44) — the phrase is shared, the treatment here is not Chris's.

## Key claims

_All statements attributed to the guest (Matt Essam), NOT to Chris Do. Dated to publication; retained
as context only._

### The momentum map (his framework)

- [2023-12-08] Beliefs inform feelings, which drive actions, which produce results, which reinforce
  the original belief — "that's why we call it the momentum map, because it goes round and round." He
  names the mechanism **cognitive bias**: "even if we're presented with evidence outside of that
  belief, because the belief is so strong we tend to dismiss that evidence as one-off." (paraphrase)
- [2023-12-08] Worked example: believing you're unattractive → low confidence and shyness → you don't
  approach anyone → no one approaches you → belief reinforced. Also uses UFO belief as a
  motivated-reasoning illustration. (paraphrase)

### Shift 1 — active over passive

- [2023-12-08] The dangerous belief among accomplished creatives is *"my work should speak for
  itself"* — "once I get to a certain stage of my career everybody should just be coming to us." He
  calls this "one of the most dangerous beliefs you can have when you're growing your business."
  (paraphrase/quote)
- [2023-12-08] **Active** = networking events, reaching out by email/LinkedIn/social, engaging people
  who comment on or like your posts, and email marketing done as actual two-way conversation ("we
  actually reply manually to those emails"). **Passive** = word of mouth, referrals, SEO, inbound
  forms. His claim: most people are 80% passive / 20% active and should invert it. (paraphrase)
- [2023-12-08] Cites **Daniel Priestley's "oversubscribed"** as the goal state — more demand than
  capacity. (paraphrase) *Note: structurally the same idea Chris credits to **David C. Baker** in
  [[2023-11-26-yt-6rZQPhXGOlk]] — different attribution, same principle.*
- [2023-12-08] Passive channels working is itself a good sign — "if they weren't, then it might mean
  that you're not very good at what you do" — they should just be the smaller share. (paraphrase)

### Shift 2 — serving over selling

- [2023-12-08] "Selling is: hey, I do this thing, do you want it. **Serving is: hey, what problem or
  challenge do you have right now, and maybe I can suggest some ways that you can solve it.**" (quote)
- [2023-12-08] Stop leading with services (web design, branding, social media marketing), because
  "the only people that's attracting are people who are currently looking for that thing."
  (paraphrase)
- [2023-12-08] **Detachment from the outcome** is the enabling mindset: "if I wasn't the person that
  could help them, then it was okay to say I can't help you, but I know someone that can — or I know a
  really great book you should read." The payoff he claims: "you're creating a network of advocates."
  (paraphrase)
- [2023-12-08] **The six-stage considered-purchase model:** unaware/satisfied → aware → decide to act →
  research the market → purchase → review. Talking about what you sell engages people only at **stage
  four**, where they compare on **time, price and quality** — "the good/fast/cheap trap… the race to
  the bottom." Engage at **stages one and two** instead, and articulate their problem better than they
  can: "consciously or unconsciously those people give us permission or credit us with having the
  solution." (paraphrase) *Structurally parallel to Chris's own problem-aware/solution-aware framing
  elsewhere in the corpus, but this is the guest's formulation.*

### Shift 3 — the fortune is in the follow-up

- [2023-12-08] Non-reply is not rejection: spam folders, busyness, personal circumstances. "If you know
  you have the ability to help somebody… it's actually your duty to go out there and engage with people
  — but more importantly, follow up until you get a response." (paraphrase/quote)
- [2023-12-08] **Claims research showing 60% of replies come on the third or fourth message.** Presented
  as "doing the research" without a source — `attribution: self-reported, unsourced`. (paraphrase)
- [2023-12-08] Yes and no are both useful; **maybe and silence are the waste**: "yeses and nos give you
  clarity… the danger is the maybe or the people that just ignore you." (paraphrase) *Compare Chris's
  own harsher formulation — "maybe is a vampire, it's worse than no" — in
  [[2023-11-26-yt-6rZQPhXGOlk]].*
- [2023-12-08] Promotes helping for free up front via prepared assets as part of "an irresistible
  offer," and directs viewers to Google **his** name for it. (paraphrase)
