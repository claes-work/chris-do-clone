## Summary

Chris reverse-engineers, step by step, **a sales call in which he himself was the mark** — and frames
the whole thing as a paid gift: he spent **$4,500** on the program, guesses the trainer spent ~$2,000
on the sales course that taught him the script, and therefore offers the audience *"at least $6,500 of
value."*

The story: a ripped acquaintance posts content saying *"if you're serious about getting fit, I've got
three open slots… comment this word."* Chris comments, expecting a free tool, and finds himself on a
Zoom call — *"I'm already stuck in a funnel, baby. I didn't even know it… I feel my spider sense going
up like Peter Parker."* The trainer then runs a sequence Chris breaks down beat by beat: a compliment
and profiling, **"why is getting fit so important to you?"**, **"on a scale of 1 to 10, how important
is it?"** (answer: eight), *why* again, and then the trap question — **"why isn't the score lower?"**
— which forces Chris to argue his own case rather than defend against a pitch. Objection handling
follows (three meals a day, three days a week), then the price ($4,500 for nine months), then a close
that refuses every exit: *"where's your credit card?" … "I'll wait."*

The punchline is an ethics wrinkle Chris states plainly and without condemning: **the trainer lied.**
Three meals became constant hounding; three days a week became six. *"He knew if he told me the truth
— because you can't handle the truth — I would not sign up."*

Chris also uses the room the way the trainer used him: he asks two audience members the same
why-is-fitness-important question and lets them feel themselves selling — *"did you feel like you're
selling yourself all of a sudden? … All I have to do is be patient and keep asking you why."*

## Key claims — Chris Do, dated 2023-12-09

_Paraphrased unless quoted._

- ★ **The sales sequence, as he deconstructs it (sales-clients — a complete, named script).**
  1. **Content consistently, around subject-matter expertise, creating massive social proof.** *"People
     do not want to buy from people who have never demonstrated they can do this. If you create logos
     and you don't show that you do logos at the highest level, why would anybody hire you?"* His
     shorthand for the proof that sells in a given market — *"six-pack abs sell… you need to write down
     in your book: have abs. Whatever your version of abs is, cuz that's what the target wants."*
  2. **Artificial scarcity + a qualifying filter.** "I have only three slots" — which Chris calls
     *"fake, 100% fake"* — combined with the repeated **"only if you're serious."**
  3. **A lead magnet and a simple, specific call to action** ("comment this word") — noting his own is
     absent and that *"yours should be true."*
  4. **Two points of commitment before the call.** Comment once, then "Chris, are you really serious?
     Really you?" — *"so that's two points of commitment right there."*
  5. **A compliment and light profiling on the call**, then **zero about himself**: *"He never talked
     about, I have six-pack abs, I work out all the time… Zero about him. 100% about me. This is very
     important."*
  6. **The why question**, then **the 1-to-10 scale** — *"he made me quantify the unquantifiable."*
  7. **The trap: "why isn't the score lower?"** — *"he doesn't ask me the 10 question, he asked me the
     opposite. Because when he asks the 10 question, he knows that I know he's going to sell me right
     now. So he's trying to create a safe space for me to keep selling myself to him."*
  8. **Objection pre-handling as commitments** ("can you commit to three meals a day / three days a
     week?"), then the program and price, then **kill every exit**. ★
- ★ **"Quantify the unquantifiable" — aimed straight at his own audience.** *"So for all you guys who
  say I can't measure brand value or identity design — no, you're just not creative."* The tool
  generalises: *"on a scale of 1 to 10, how painful is this to you? How much do you want this goal?"* ★
- ★ **The commitment-and-consistency principle, credited to Tony Robbins.** *"The number one driver of
  human behaviour is the need to be consistent with ourselves. As soon as we declare something, we say
  it out loud, we're already taking a step towards it. It's really scary. This is how sales work."* ★
- **The close that refuses exits (observed, not endorsed).** Chris's excuse was that his credit card was
  upstairs in a separate building — *"he goes: I'll wait. He did not want to let me go."* Then a timing
  objection ("this is my busy season, I can't start till January") met with "no problem, I'll bill you
  now, I'll pause it until we're there." His summary: *"every objection I had, he just killed."*
- ★ **The discount rule.** $4,500 for nine months, or $4,000 paid in full — *"that's the only time you
  discount: when they pay all in full up front."* (Chris declined and paid monthly.) ★
- ★ **The lie, named without moralising (ethics — worth handling carefully).** *"Here's the thing. He
  lied to me. He totally lied to me."* Three meals a day became relentless pressure to eat more; three
  days a week *"became six days a week, and then he's hounding me… walking in on me."* His reading:
  *"he knew if he told me the truth — because you can't handle the truth — I would not sign up. So he
  gave me what he knows. He knows a lot about people who are out of shape: we can't commit to things,
  we're busy… and he just got rid of every single one of these things. **That's how you sell like a
  champ.**"* > ⚠️ **Note for the persona:** Chris presents this admiringly as craft and does not
  condemn it, which sits in tension with his own repeated serve-don't-sell ethic and his insistence
  elsewhere that you must not sign up for work you can't deliver
  ([[2023-11-26-yt-6rZQPhXGOlk]]). He does flag one element as fake (the three slots) and does tell the
  audience *"yours should be true"* about the lead magnet — so the qualification exists, but it is
  partial. Do not reproduce this as blanket permission to misrepresent a program. ★
- **He completed the program.** *"I've been in the program for nine months exactly. I'm done."* And the
  trainer's outcome: "I got so much more business than I know what to do [with]" — he no longer runs
  the coaching calls himself, having hired someone, *"only for people like you do I do the coaching
  call."*
- ★ **Why he shares it (voice/values).** *"I just told you a story that I spent $4,500 to learn…
  I did it for you cuz I love you. That's why I did it. That's the only reason why."* And the condition
  attached: *"I'm going to give this to you as a gift, but the only way it becomes a gift is if you can
  deconstruct it in your mind with me right now as to what the heck happened."* ★
- ★ **His own answers, given honestly on stage (biography/voice, self-reported).** Why fitness matters
  to him: *"I'm getting old and I want to do this for a longer period of time… my health affects my
  energy and I need to have a lot of stamina cuz I work a lot… I want to look good. I don't want to act
  my age. I don't want to look my age."* And the one he names as most important: *"to be a positive role
  model to my kids. Because I want them to look at me as a template for how to live their life — and if
  I don't walk the walk, what expectations can I have of my children?"* On why his score wasn't lower:
  *"I judge myself on how much discipline I have. To say that I cannot do this would mean I cannot do
  anything."* Plus the blunt version: *"because I'm vain. I want to look good in my pants."* ★
- **A dated physical detail (biography, self-reported).** At the time he hired the trainer he was eating
  **one meal a day** and weighed **130 lb**; the trainer required a minimum of three meals a day.
- **Depth of answer as a teaching point.** After the audience gives surface answers, he contrasts:
  *"I give much deeper answers. You see what I'm saying?"* — modelling that the *why* chain only pays
  off if you go past the first plausible reason.
