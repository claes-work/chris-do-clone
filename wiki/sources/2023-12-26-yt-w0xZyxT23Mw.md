## Summary

A structured guest workshop on **client experience** as a growth lever, built around her own turning
point: in late 2018, facing her biggest-ever project (**12K** against a then-average of 2–3K and a
personal high of 4K), she spent two days rebuilding her proposal and even her email signature to
"show up like the kind of business that charges 10K without making a big deal out of it." She won the
project; the client's feedback — that it was the most thoughtful and easy experience she'd had with a
contractor — produced a referral days later. She then re-engineered her whole process, reporting
**revenue per client up 40%** and **referral rate doubled**.

Her framework is five stages — **research, analyse, implement, delight, review** — with the argument
that client experience starts long before the project (first website visit, a friend's recommendation)
and that its real payoff sits **after** delivery, in a **nurture sequence**.

Chris's own contribution is minimal but includes one candid admission worth recording.

## Fenced — Gigi (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_All claims attributed to the guest. Context only._

- **The origin story (self-reported):** the 12K project against a 2–4K baseline; two days rebuilding
  the proposal and email signature; the client's wrap-up feedback; a referral email days later;
  then systematic re-engineering. Reported outcomes: **+40% revenue per client**, **doubled referral
  rate**, repeat clients, and less admin time through templating and automation.
- **Definition:** client experience is every interaction with you and your brand — *plus* "all those
  micro interactions… those tiny almost invisible touch points," e.g. whether your contact-form
  confirmation is a generic "thanks, will be in touch" or a personalised note with a time frame, next
  steps and useful resources.
- **Expectations vs. delight:** the restaurant analogy — greeting, cleanliness and good food are
  *expected*; the chef coming out, the half-portions of both desserts, cookies for the road are what
  get talked about. Counter-example from a trip to Turkey: a restaurant that posted its WhatsApp number
  as an **image** rather than a link, so they went elsewhere.
- **The five-stage framework:**
  1. **Research** — ask past clients what made them choose you, what they found most valuable, what
     they wish had been different. Her own surprise: clients preferred a **live strategy workshop** to
     a long questionnaire, and the questionnaire wasn't even saving her time (review, clarification and
     a call happened anyway).
  2. **Analyse** — map every step and scenario (inquiry → sale → onboarding → project → offboarding →
     nurture), then decide what to **streamline, automate, delegate, or delete**. Her own before/after:
     sales call → proposal → confirmation → contract → signature → deposit invoice → payment, collapsed
     to closing on the call plus terms and an invoice — *"I get the client within 30 minutes, whereas
     back in 2018 this would take me days if not weeks."*
  3. **Implement** — incrementally, not all at once; cites Adam Mosseri's staged Instagram rollouts as
     the model.
  4. **Delight** — small, prepared surprises: a handwritten note, a Starbucks card at the halfway mark,
     an unpromised brand teaser video, a launch graphic they can share, a "brand-versary" card.
  5. **Review** — client feedback *plus* an internal project review with your own rating, because
     "maybe the client is really happy… but for you it was a complete disaster."
- **Templates as sanity, not just speed:** proposals, presentations, process emails — and specifically
  *"emergency emails"* for uncomfortable situations, which as a self-described people-pleaser she would
  otherwise agonise over for days.
- **The nurture sequence** (her stated "missing piece"): scheduled check-ins over 6–12 months after
  launch, celebrating client wins publicly, sending referrals, a one-year brand-versary card. Her
  claim: *"suddenly the 10K project that you just wrapped up is bringing you an extra 7,000 through
  client referrals and repeat work."* An example: spotting a broken pop-up on a past client's site,
  offering to fix it free, which became a **5K project**.
- **Past clients are your sales force** — *"especially if you're an introvert like myself,"* since
  clients do the marketing for you. She reports having largely stopped promoting her design business
  while its revenue still increased.
