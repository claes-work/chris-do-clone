# How To Unf*** Your Life | 6 Steps To Reinvent Yourself In 2024

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do is the teaching voice throughout.** His interlocutor is **Mo**
> (Mo Ismail), the recurring Futur co-host, who asks questions, pushes for the "how," and coins a
> first version of the DAPPER acronym that Chris then rewrites live. Mo's questions are context;
> everything substantive is Chris. `attribution: sole-subject (teaching)`.

## Summary

Chris opens by flagging this as deliberately off his usual beat — *"you hear me talking about design,
about typography, about business, sales and negotiations, but at the end of it all it always comes
down to this: what is your mind state."*

The argument runs in four moves. **First**, there is only "a very narrow sliver of what we would
identify as objective truth"; everything else is interpreted through a subjective lens set by your
emotional state — demonstrated with a parking-meter scenario worked from both the aggrieved and the
objective side, and anchored on Krishnamurti: *"to observe without judgment is the highest form of
intelligence."*

**Second**, a long worked example with an unnamed friend who wants to be a public speaker and has
body dysmorphia: Chris said essentially the same sentences to them twice, months apart. In a low
state it registered as judgment; in a positive state the *same words* were "actually really
empowering." His conclusion — you are in control of your emotional state, and *"when we choose… to
look at the world from a negative state… almost no input that can enter us will feel good."*

**Third**, the mechanism and the practice: grooved neural pathways (the vinyl-record metaphor),
meditation reframed as **witnessing** rather than emptying, and the **DAPPER** acronym, delivered
twice — Mo's version first, then Chris's rewrite.

**Fourth**, the biographical anchor: the **lost red envelope** at nine or ten, which he presents as
the first time he ran the process on himself — and which he offers with an unusual disclaimer about
mythology-building.

It closes on **letting go of identity** as the real barrier to change (crab moulting, snake shedding,
the full cup), and a blunt account of why most people who watch his videos don't change.

## Key claims — Chris Do, dated 2023-12-28

_Paraphrased unless quoted._

- ★ **The narrow sliver of objective truth.** *"There's this very narrow sliver of what we would
  identify as objective truth — it's tiny… An objective thing happens and we interpret through our
  very subjective lens and we tell ourselves a story about what's going on, and that lens is largely
  influenced by our emotional state."* The failure mode: *"most often what we do is we mistake that
  subjective lens as the absolute objective truth."* Cites **Krishnamurti**: *"to observe without
  judgment is the highest form of intelligence."* ★
- **The parking-meter worked example.** The subjective read: the meter maid is ruining your day, the
  city is robbing you. The objective read: the meter doesn't lie, you overstayed, their job funds the
  city, and someone waiting for the spot would see you as the rule-breaker. The strategic read: which
  posture is more likely to get you out of the ticket — chasing them "with your hands waved in the air
  full of emotion and rage and victimisation," or "I messed up, I lost track of time… could you find
  it in your heart to let me go with a warning, and I understand if you don't."
- ★ **Same words, different state — the friend example (mindset, self-reported).** Told a friend that
  losing weight would give them more clothing options and signal self-control and self-determination;
  the friend heard judgment ("it makes me feel like I'm stupid"). Months later, with the friend in a
  positive state, he said "almost literally the same thing" and it landed as empowering. The friend's
  own realisation: *"when I'm in a positive state of self-love… nothing that you say is then
  interpreted as negative or judging."* ★
- ★ **Get permission before helping.** *"If you give people unsolicited advice, if you offer to help
  them when they haven't asked for it, it actually can get the opposite effect… they'll walk away
  feeling disabled — like, are you saying I don't know how to do this?"* So: "don't give advice or
  offer help to people without first checking in with them and getting permission." ★
- ★ **The Platinum Rule.** *"Often times we think the golden rule is the best way to treat people —
  treat people how you want to be treated. But in fact that turns out to be not very good at all. So
  what I always want to do is apply the **Platinum Rule**: treat people how **they** want to be
  treated."* Hence asking what people prefer, whether they want support, whether they want
  accountability. ★
- ★ **You choose the lens, and the choice is the hard part.** *"When we choose — and this is a choice
  — to look at the world from a negative state, a place of scarcity, fear, jealousy, self-criticism,
  almost no input that can enter us will feel good, positive or helpful. Whereas when we're in a
  positive state… no amount of negative input can impact us negatively."* Credits **Marshall
  Rosenberg**'s *Nonviolent Communication* for the reinterpretation mechanism. ★
- **His stated daily practice (mindset).** *"I live in a place of extreme gratitude, and I wake up
  each and every single morning grateful just to be alive, to be able-bodied, to still have my mind
  with me — because at some point that will go away… to be in a house that's warm, to have food in
  the fridge, and to have friends that care about me."*
- ★ **Reframing criticism as care.** *"If someone said to me some critical things… my interpretation
  would be: you really do care about me, you care enough to speak truth to me… the fact that you've
  dedicated some part of your brain to think about me and you have the courage to tell me says to me
  you're a true friend — because it's easy to deliver empty platitudes; it's much harder to speak
  truth to someone when you know it'll hurt."* ★
- ★ **The vinyl-groove metaphor for neural pathways.** Credits "a professor of anatomy" for the point
  that repeated experience-plus-emotion "develops deep grooves and it's very hard to escape," then
  supplies his own image: a record worn so deep it repeats — "if you let it repeat itself so many
  times I think you'll literally go insane." ★
- ★ **Meditation reframed as witnessing (his own understanding, self-corrected on air).** *"My wife is
  trying to correct me. I must practise some form of passive meditation because I do no act of
  meditation."* His definition: not emptying yourself of thought but *"for you to feel all that you
  feel, to think all that you think, but to be witness to this"* — an out-of-body vantage with
  "detachment, objectivity." Two applications he gives: with his son, the witness says *allow this,
  encourage this, give openly* — "I want to hug him deeply, I want to feel his embrace"; with a
  difficult client, the witness notices the negative reaction and asks **"what is the outcome that I
  want right now, and will being emotional help me achieve that outcome or take me further away from
  it?"** ★
- ★ **Cruelty is a mirror of self-talk.** *"People who are cruel, who are mean-spirited to other
  people — it is a mirror to how they talk to themselves."* And the origin story he gives for the
  inner critic: a parent trips on a toy, reacts in the moment ("all you care about is yourself… you're
  so ungrateful"), apologises later — but "your three, four, five-year-old undeveloped mind has now
  recorded that thing as a traumatic experience," and those words become the inner dialogue. ★
- ★★ **The lost red envelope — the origin of the practice (biography, self-reported, ~age 9–10).**
  Offered with an explicit disclaimer: *"I know the reaction from our audience… it's going to sound
  like mythology building, and I'm always wary of people who tell tales of heightened awareness at too
  young of an age — but I tell you because I'm this weird shy introverted person, I spend a lot of
  time thinking about what I think about."* The story: at Lunar New Year, children get red envelopes
  with money — "for us as a person who's unemployed, it is our way of having some financial freedom…
  that's all the money you're going to get aside from your birthday and Christmas." He loses one
  containing **$5** out of a roughly **$100** haul, searches the house, and marches to his mother
  accusing someone of theft. Her reply stops him cold: *"honey, do you think someone in this family
  would steal money from you? Because I would like to know who that person is."* He realises he lost
  it, spirals into self-attack ("you're a dummy, you're a freaking idiot"), and then catches himself:
  *"I asked, is beating myself up over this lost $5 going to change the outcome? Will it magically
  make a new red envelope appear? And the answer was no."* The learning he extracted — keep everything
  in one place — became a lifelong system: *"from that point forward I very rarely misplace or lose
  things… the constant joke at the house is Mom's always looking for her phone, Dad always knows where
  his is, because it's only one or two spots."* He also notes the red-envelope social mechanics:
  uncles and aunts stuff them with equal amounts, "and they have special envelopes that they keep in a
  different pocket for their favourites"; you never open them in front of the giver. ★★
- ★ **Emotional detachment as life philosophy.** *"A part of my life philosophy and the things that
  have served me well is to practise some form of emotional detachment."* His examples: people sobbing
  over a burned house while holding their children and pets — *"you lost a house, not your identity";*
  and his own — *"there was a point in time in which I had hair. I don't have hair anymore. And if I
  said, well, I feel less of a person because physically I've changed, then I'm going to be constantly
  battling those inner demons."* Also names declining eyesight as an attachment to release. Cites
  **Tony Robbins** on people who've lost limbs being among the most inspiring and least complaining. ★
- ★★ **Why people don't change: the devil you know.** *"You don't love your current identity, you
  don't love the progress that you have in your career, you don't love the relationships that you have
  with the people around you… but when someone offers you a way out of it, you revert back to that.
  It's better to deal with the devil you know than the devil you don't. **So we love — in a weird way,
  and it's a strong word for me to use, love — we love the miserable state that we're in, because at
  least we know it.**"* And the sharper version: *"if someone presents to you a solution that you're
  not ready to hear, or don't want to hear from that specific person, it's a threat to your own
  identity."* ★★
- ★ **The real question is speed of evolution.** *"There will be a time when you don't dress this way
  anymore, cuz we all evolve. And the only real question here is: how long do you want to let it take
  for you to evolve? A day, a year, 10 years? You're just robbing yourself of your new potential, the
  longer you take."* ★
- ★ **Growth requires shedding, not adding.** The crab moults its shell and becomes "a soft shell crab
  — very vulnerable, they're yummy" in order to grow a new exoskeleton; the snake sheds its skin.
  *"You have to let go of the things you found comfort and safety in, and the things that protected
  you, if you want to grow."* The follow-up question he says matters: *"if you ask most people, do you
  aspire to grow, their answer is usually yes. **The harder question is: what have you done to let go
  of your old identity so that you can adopt a new one?**"* Plus the cup metaphor: filled to the brim,
  "adding to it is impossible — you have to empty some of it." ★
- **The longest distance is heart to brain.** Restated here in his own frame: "you can understand
  things logically… but your emotional irrational state is like, oh, I'm afraid — who am I if this
  happens, what do I lose? So you get into this scarcity mindset, this loss-aversion thing really
  quickly." *(Note: the same aphorism appears in [[2023-11-11-yt-mUoyOZH1R4I]] attributed to a
  "philosopher prophet" from the guest's childhood; here Chris uses it as his own without
  attribution — a soft attribution drift worth watching.)*
- ★ **Why most viewers don't change — the harsh version.** People who succeed tell him "I used to earn
  minimum wage, I used to do 12 bucks an hour, and last year I did 150 grand." Everyone else "makes up
  a thousand reasons why it's not applicable to them" — he lists the ones he actually hears: *"easy
  for him to say, he has the perfect life, he was born into wealth… because he lives in LA… because
  his face looks a certain way… his minority is getting all these opportunities."* His diagnosis:
  *"because if they were actually to do what it is that I say, they would have to admit a really harsh
  truth… **I'm stuck because of me.** I'm stuck because of the decisions I made in my life, and I
  continue to stay stuck because I continue to tell myself this narrative. So to accept that this
  actually works would destroy your own identity."* And the compact portrait of who does change:
  *"just dumb enough to believe a stranger on the internet, brave enough to do something, stupid
  enough not to know all the steps that they need to do so that they can actually do it, and grateful
  enough to let it happen when it happens."* ★
- **How he receives praise now (voice/mindset).** When people thank him on tour: *"this is where the
  witness says, Chris, open yourself up to feel this. I don't pass it off, I don't dismiss it… I say
  thank you, and it makes me proud to hear you say this. **You are fuel for me to do this for another
  five years.**"* Followed immediately by his running joke — "and give me my 10%."
- ★ **DAPPER — the framework, in his final form.** **D** — **don't react.** **A** — become **aware**
  of your emotional state. **P** — **pause**, take a breath. **P** — **ponder** what options are
  available. **E** — choose the **emotional state** that serves a path toward empowerment. **R** —
  **respond**, deciding how you want to. *(Mo's earlier version, which Chris politely rewrites on air:
  Decide / Awareness / Pause / Assess / Pivot / Execute / Rewire. Chris's response is a good voice
  sample: "I admire your wit, your intelligence, to be able to pull this out of thin air — but the
  letters and the meaning don't quite work for me, so I'll say it in a different way.")* ★
- **Closing line.** *"If you are ready to receive, the coach will appear."*
