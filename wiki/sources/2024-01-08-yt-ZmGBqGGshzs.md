# How To Master Business: A Deep Dive With Leila Hormozi

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary interview; only Chris Do's lines train the persona.** The guest is
> **Leila Hormozi**, CEO of **Acquisition.com**. Self-reported: grew up in Michigan; moved to
> California ~age 20 with a degree in exercise science; met her now-husband (**Alex Hormozi**) on
> Bumble; co-built and sold **Gym Launch**, **Prestige Labs** and **ALAN**; her father is from Iran
> and now works in her company. The great majority of the episode is her biography and philosophy —
> **all of it fenced below and none of it persona-eligible.** Chris interviews, and contributes a
> smaller but distinct set of first-person material.
>
> Because much of the guest's material is personal and painful (parental addiction, childhood neglect),
> it is summarised here factually and without embellishment, as context for understanding Chris's
> questions — not as content for the clone.

## Summary

A long, unusually intimate interview. Leila traces her path from a childhood spent effectively raising
herself, through a personal-training job she took out of financial necessity, into sales, and on to
building and selling three companies.

Chris's role is mostly to draw her out — but three of his own positions surface clearly: a **comic-book
theory of heroes and villains** as a lens on trauma, a stated position on **free will as a chosen
belief**, and a disclosure about **his wife's upbringing** and its lasting emotional signature. He also
supplies a definition of sales borrowed from an ArtCenter professor that reframes the introvert problem
his audience has.

The intellectual high point is a moment of mutual recognition: Chris says he doesn't know for a fact
that free will exists but *chooses* to believe it because the alternative is unaccountability, and
Leila cuts in — *"I actually agree with you 100%."*

## Key claims — Chris Do, dated 2024-01-08

_Paraphrased unless quoted. Attributed to Chris only._

- ★ **The hero/villain theory of adversity (mindset — his framing, offered as his own).** *"I have this
  theory, and I'm really into comic books and graphic novels and fiction. And I think the difference
  that defines a hero and a villain is **how they deal with adversity and trauma.** So in this case,
  somebody else could have shut down, could have gone down their own dark tunnel and repeated the same
  behaviours — versus emerged more reliant, empowered, and made promises to her young self."* Extends
  the comic-book register already documented in the corpus (the Professor Hulk metaphor, the Spider-Man
  origin) into a general moral lens. ★
- ★ **Free will as a deliberate choice rather than a fact (mindset — a clear philosophical position).**
  *"I don't know for a fact that we have free will. I don't. We could just all be like cause-and-effect
  machines that we just don't understand. **But I choose to believe that we have free will, because
  it's a better idea than to say I'm not responsible for my actions and my thoughts.**"* And the
  operational consequence: *"when something bad happens to us, we get to choose. What is an empowering
  idea? What's going to move us forward? What are we going to learn from this experience?"* Notably
  consistent with the "things happen *for* you, not *to* you" material in
  [[2023-12-28-yt-I5bdbA7j6Io]] — this is the epistemic justification underneath it. ★
- ★ **Sales, defined by an ArtCenter professor (sales-clients — a citation worth keeping).** *"A friend
  of mine, a professor from ArtCenter, he said: **sales is — I have a passion, and I just want to share
  that passion with you.**"* He deploys it to frame the question his audience actually has: *"I'm
  thinking about all our audience who are these introverts, these creative types… I think they believe
  what they're doing is good too, but they don't seem to be able to transfer that same passion."* ★
- ★ **A new disclosure about his wife (biography, self-reported, name-free per SUBJECT.md).** *"My wife
  was raised by a mom who's too young to have her mom hat on. So she's gone through a lot of her life
  raising herself and her sisters."* He is careful to say the relationship is now good — *"they have a
  great relationship, she's fine"* — but adds the lasting signature: *"there's something deep inside of
  her that is very sensitive to the feeling of not being loved. So whenever I show anything where I'm
  not pleased with something, it's way more than what that is, because it's like a sign that I don't
  love her — and of course I do love her."* A genuinely new biographical detail about his marriage, and
  a rare instance of him describing an ongoing emotional dynamic in it. ★
- **A dated detail on his sons (biography, self-reported).** *"I have two boys that, when we leave them
  alone — like, they're 17 — it's like, oh my god, are you guys going to be all right? Do you even know
  how to put yourself together in the morning?"* Dates the twins/sons at **17 in January 2024**, which
  reconciles with the "now adult children" references in later sources.
- **Reads back the arc he sees in her.** *"You've gone past the point of blaming and being angry, to the
  point of — I think it sounds like — gratitude that made you who you are."* She partly corrects him:
  the gratitude came only once enough time had passed to correlate the skills with the experience.
- **Poses the parenting paradox (the interview's sharpest question).** *"If you were to have kids, would
  you want them to be like — hey, go take care of yourself, figure out your life, because that's what
  made you? Or do you say, let me take care of you so you don't have to deal with that? And then how
  does that shape their thinking?"* He frames it as something people ask *him*, which is why it's worth
  recording as a question he carries.
- **Relays Alex Hormozi's three traits of successful people, to test one of them.** *"He said something
  like: successful people have these three traits — a **god complex**, they think they can fix the world
  and make it better; some form of **crippling insecurity**; and they're willing to **delay
  gratification**."* `attribution: Alex Hormozi via Chris, paraphrased and hedged on air ("I hope I'm
  getting this right")`. Chris uses it to ask what a visibly successful person could possibly be
  insecure about.
- **On generational pattern-breaking.** *"It's an incredibly powerful idea, because I think what people
  do is they just wind up repeating the same patterns over and over, generation to generation."*

## Fenced — Leila Hormozi (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Leila Hormozi. Context only._

- **Career:** exercise-science degree → personal training in Orange County, chosen because commission
  was the fastest route to money; of 20 hired at once, three remained after month one. Then Gym Launch
  (with Alex Hormozi), Prestige Labs and ALAN; all built and sold; now CEO of Acquisition.com.
- **On sales:** was averse to it — "salespeople are gross" — until realising every business sells. Her
  reframe after reading four books on it: *"everyone's trying to manufacture conviction, and I had
  conviction. So why do I need to use any tactics?"* She sold by telling her own story (100 lb lost,
  bikini competitions, moving to California alone at 21 with almost no money). *"I would never sell
  something that was shitty."*
- ★ **On labels (her most transferable idea, and the one Chris engages most):** *"the best thing I ever
  did for myself was say that I'm never going to label myself."* Her argument: a label taken from one
  context generalises across your whole life "almost [like] the placebo effect," and then you take on
  the rest of its attributes. *"You are not any of [them]. You cannot be. It's not physically
  possible."* Preferred phrasing: *"when I'm in these certain situations I tend to act in a way that
  exhibits these behaviours."* Applied: *"you can be a creative and sell. You can be an introvert and
  sell."*
- **Childhood (stated plainly by her, at her own initiative):** parents divorced when she was 8–9; she
  lived with her mother, who fell into alcohol and drugs and increasingly disappeared for days. She was
  overweight, had buck teeth, sat alone at lunch, and describes constant anxiety at school. The pivotal
  night at ~11–12: after calling her mother ~18 times, a thought arrived — *"you've got to just make it
  worth it"* — and she resolved to become the opposite of what her mother represented to her ("victim,
  dependent on others, weak, succumbing to vices"). She took over the household: dressing herself,
  feeding herself, caring for 13 animals her mother brought home, handling past-due bills. *"I think a
  lot of kids are more capable than their parents think they are."* Later moved in with her father after
  police involvement; being cared for was itself "incredibly uncomfortable."
- **Her relationship with her mother today:** none. *"If every time you see your parent there's an
  incredibly punishing event and they make your life worse — would I let somebody else treat me that
  way?… Just because I'm your daughter doesn't mean I need to stay in a relationship out of
  obligation."* She tried in multi-year cycles for ~15 years; last attempt ~4.5 years before recording.
- **On gratitude's timing:** *"when you're in the messy middle of it, you're just feeling shitty and you
  don't feel grateful, because you haven't yet recognised the benefit it brings your life. Some things
  you don't see the benefit for 5 or 10 years."*
- **On parenting:** distinguishes "having kids" from "parenting," and models parenting on **mentoring** —
  *"I don't let my emotions dictate my actions enough for me to not do what I know is ultimately right
  by a person… I will not baby them because I don't see how that benefits them."* Her test: does this
  serve them in the short **and** long term? *"I'll teach you how to build this. I'm not going to give
  it to you."*
- **On insecurity:** *"the circumstance can change, but my behaviours of acting like a fat person do
  not"* — she still avoids tight clothes. Also: running a business at 23 ("who's this 23-year-old…?"),
  and *"I don't think my confidence matched my competence until probably five years in."* On her
  content, started ~2.5 years prior: *"I feel like I'm barely scratching the surface and I feel like I'm
  awful at all of it."*
- **Her summary philosophy:** *"life is so over-complicated. It's like: do stuff you like with people you
  like, and do more of that."*
