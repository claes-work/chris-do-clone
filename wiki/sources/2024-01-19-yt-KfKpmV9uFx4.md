## Summary

A solo teach on choosing a niche, structured as two axes, three principles and one scoring exercise.

**The axes:** you can niche on **output** (what you produce) or on **industry** (who you serve), each
running from broad to narrow. Both narrow = a **micro-niche**; both broad = **no niche**. He argues the
safe zones are "fairly specific on both," and warns that a very specific *output* carries risk if
demand for that output disappears, while an industry like personal training will always exist.

**The three principles:** (1) you are in a **unique position to solve a problem** — your "zone of
genius," the accumulated themes, skills and network of your life, surfaced through an exercise he calls
the **River of Life**; (2) **your target is not your market** — the dartboard analogy, and the
observation that prospects are aspirational and will approach you from outside your stated target;
(3) **date first, then marry** — test a niche with real conversations and pilot projects before
rebuilding your brand and website around it.

**The exercise:** list candidate niches, score each out of 10 on four criteria — how much you'd *enjoy*
working with them, how *big* the problem is for them, how much *money* they have to solve it, and how
*easy* it is for you to start conversations (route to market) — then add them up. *"Typically if it's
over 30 I would say it's probably got some legs."*

Recorded here mainly because the framework is genuinely useful and appears on Chris's channel, so a
later pass could mistake it for his.

## Key claims

_All statements attributed to the guest, NOT to Chris Do. Dated to publication; context only._

- [2024-01-19] **His own origin (self-reported):** running an agency that was "always trying to be
  everything to everybody," several years of "treading water" — busy, with clients, but revenue flat or
  profit not keeping pace, and a burnt-out team — telling themselves it would improve with a bigger
  portfolio. *"That never really changed until we decided to niche down."* (paraphrase/quote)
- [2024-01-19] **Why creatives resist niching:** *"intrinsically as creatives we love variety… we don't
  want to create this kind of cookie-cutter factory where every project is the same."* His own barrier
  was fear of losing that variety. (quote/paraphrase)
- [2024-01-19] **Two axes, not one.** Output vs. industry, each broad→narrow. *"Web design isn't the
  niche — web design is the output,"* and still a broad one; usability design or design sprints are
  narrower. Both-narrow = micro-niche; both-broad = no niche. (paraphrase/quote)
- [2024-01-19] **Value is not created equally — the pizza analogy.** The best pizza in the world is worth
  nothing to someone gluten-intolerant or low-carb, and a great deal to a foodie whose favourite
  toppings are on it. *"What we offer — our output — is not as valuable for everybody."* (paraphrase)
- [2024-01-19] ★ **Zone of genius / River of Life.** *"All of the things that have happened to you in
  your life, all of the themes, all of the skills, all of the network that you have, that puts you in a
  unique position to solve a problem for someone."* His own example: panic attacks and anxiety in his
  twenties (after a breakup, a stolen car and a course he disliked) led to two or three years studying
  psychology — so **psychology** sits in his zone alongside business and creativity. (paraphrase/quote)
  > *Note: **"zone of genius" is Gay Hendricks's term**, credited as such by a different guest in
  > [[2023-11-18-yt-t7PZ6eD2lEQ]]; it is used here without attribution.*
- [2024-01-19] **Your target is not your market.** The dartboard: you aim for the bullseye in your
  marketing, but darts miss and prospects are aspirational. His own case: they market to agencies
  "north of about 250,000" and still get enquiries from well below that. The real payoff of narrowing:
  *"your prospects will start to feel like you understand them better than anybody else, and that's why
  they will trust you."* (paraphrase/quote)
- [2024-01-19] **Date first, marry later.** Don't rebuild branding and website on an untested niche —
  he reports clients arriving having spent money on exactly that and then changing their mind. Test
  with conversations and pilot projects: *"make sure that the problem that you want to solve is the
  problem they actually have."* (paraphrase/quote)
- [2024-01-19] ★ **The scoring exercise (the video's practical core).** For each candidate niche, score
  out of 10: **enjoyment** (*"if I had a totally free day, would I choose to go and spend a day with
  these people?"*), **problem size** (10 = their single biggest problem), **money available**, and
  **ease of starting conversations** — which he clarifies is really *route to market*. Sum them; pick
  the highest; *"typically if it's over 30 I would say it's probably got some legs."* The stated purpose
  is to *"take the emotion and the stress and the confusion out of it."* (paraphrase/quote)
- [2024-01-19] **Promotion:** an "agency roadmap" tool — 25 questions, a custom strategy built from
  "thousands of data points from agencies that we've successfully helped to scale," plus a free copy of
  his book.
