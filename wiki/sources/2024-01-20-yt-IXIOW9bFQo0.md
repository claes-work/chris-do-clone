# Find Your Purpose — Ikigai Breakdown (Archive Series)

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do is the sole voice.** An archive-series re-release of a talk to what
> sounds like a student/graduate audience (he addresses a "Ricky" in the room). `attribution:
> sole-subject`.

## Summary

A clean, self-contained teach of the Japanese concept **ikigai** — and, more valuably, **Chris using
it to explain his own career pivot in the fourth circle's terms.**

He sets the frame as a post-graduation dilemma: do you do what you **love**, what you're **good at**,
what **pays well**, or what the **world needs**? His argument is that the world teaches these as
isolated choices, and that the fourth — what the world needs — is the one nobody considers.

He then walks the four-circle Venn and names each pairing: love + world-needs + good-at but unpaid =
**mission**; love + good-at but unpaid and unneeded = **passion**; good-at + paid = **profession**;
good-at + paid but unloved = **a job**; world-needs + paid but not good-at = **vocation**. He draws a
distinction between profession and vocation on training: one requires formal education, the other
rests on innate skill trained differently.

The centre of the talk is his own case, and it is the most economical version of the Blind→Futur pivot
anywhere in the corpus: **23+ years** running a company he loved, was good at, and was paid handsomely
for — *"and I don't know why, but I felt like there was a hole missing inside of me."* The missing
circle was **what the world needs**. And the person who surfaced it was his wife.

He closes with a four-column exercise and an honest note on the paid circle: without it, "after a
while it could feel like you're going to get into burn up."

## Key claims — Chris Do, dated 2024-01-20

_Paraphrased unless quoted._

- ★ **The framing question, and the missing circle.** *"Are you going to do something that you love,
  something that you're good at, something that pays really well, or something that the world needs?"*
  His observation: *"did any one of your influences, or did you ever consider, what does the world need
  from you? Nobody thinks about this — and this is usually the missing component."* He notes most Venn
  versions in circulation drop it: *"you may have seen a diagram like this before… what you love, what
  you're good at, what you're paid for — **they leave out this part, what the world needs.**"* ★
- ★ **The generational reframe.** *"In our generation it was just about going to work — you did your
  nine to five and then you live for the weekends, you become a weekend warrior… And it seems like
  we're in a different era now, where **the thing that you love, the thing that you're good at, should
  also be the thing that you get paid for. And if we can design our lives around that idea, we'll be
  happier, healthier and wealthier than we've ever been.**"* ★
- **The five named regions.** **Mission** — love + good-at + world-needs, unpaid ("missionaries do
  this… their purpose is filled but they don't make any money"). **Passion** — love + good-at, unpaid
  (his in-room example: Ricky is good at baseball and loves it, but only ~0.01% make professional
  sport). **Profession** — good-at + paid. **A job** — good-at + paid, unloved: *"you show up, you do
  the work, you get paid, and you can't wait for work to be over."* **Vocation** — world-needs + paid,
  not necessarily good-at. On the last distinction: *"profession usually requires training and
  education… while vocation is probably like somebody who doesn't go to school but they have innate
  skill — they still have to get training, but it's a very different kind of training."*
- ★ **Seth Godin on work vs. art, cited.** *"When you go to work you feel like: when is it over? And
  when it's art it's like: how long can I do this for?"* Attributed to Godin's appearance on the show. ★
- ★★ **His own ikigai — the compact Blind→Futur account (biography, self-reported).** *"For 23-plus
  years I ran this company. I love design, I love business, I'm pretty good at these things — and
  motion graphics and making video — and we got paid handsomely for making commercials, less so music
  videos. But we were part of **this** circle, and **I don't know why, but I felt like there was a hole
  missing inside of me that I couldn't fill, and I wanted to get out of this — even though I loved it,
  I was paid for it, I was good at it. There was something else.**"* ★★
- ★★ **His wife asked the question that started the search (biography, self-reported — name-free).**
  *"It wasn't until I started to teach, and my wife had noticed something. She had said to me: it's
  really great that you're able to impact the lives of a few students — eight, ten students every
  semester — and you spend time and you give so much of yourself, and I can see their eyes light up
  when you share something. **But is there more? Can you do more with your time? Is this a good
  exchange of your time?**"* The consequence: *"it set me down a three-to-six-month rabbit hole of
  searching and trying to figure it out."* This dates and attributes the trigger for the pivot more
  precisely than most corpus accounts, and credits his wife with it. ★★
- ★ **His stated answer.** *"Then I figured it out. It's doing what I'm doing with you right now. I get
  to teach you the things that I know, what I'm good at, the things I care about that I love — and I'm
  going to make money off this, but I get to help the world, the world needs this. So for me, my ikigai
  is exactly this: it's The Futur, teaching a billion people on planet earth how to make money doing
  what they love."* ★
- **The immigrant-parent frame, again.** *"This is usually the voice of your mom or your dad… they
  usually want to protect you from harm, so they advise you to do things that pay well. For me, my
  parents wanted me to become — this is pretty typical of immigrant families — a doctor or a lawyer."*
  And his own childhood answer: *"when I grew up I love to draw, so I thought what I really want to do
  is to draw comics."*
- ★ **The exercise.** Four columns, filled with no regard for realism: (1) *"think back to when you're
  7, 8, 9, 10 years old — what did you love to do?"*; (2) what you're good at — noting that this
  *"requires skill, requires training and requires practice… sometimes they're the same [as what you
  love] and sometimes they're not, it just depends on the opportunities you were given in life"*;
  (3) **where opportunity exists today** — *"what is trending, what is shifting in the culture"*; and
  (4) what the world needs — *"forget about your skill set, what you love; just sit down and think:
  what do we need? What are big problems that really could use some help?"* Then: *"step back and look
  at those four columns and try to build a bridge between as many of the words you wrote as possible."* ★
- ★ **His stated daily rhythm, and the burnout caveat (biography/voice).** *"Today, every single
  morning I get up when the sun comes up, I go to sleep usually around 12 or 1 o'clock at night, and
  I'm not tired, because I'm fuelled with this purpose, the mission, the passion — **and I get paid for
  it. When you don't get paid for something, after a while it could feel like you're going to burn up.
  So you have to make progress there as well.**"* ★
- **Closing optimism.** *"We're living in the best time ever in the history of man, in that we have
  great technology, we're connected… anybody out there that's listening to this already has all the
  tools and technology that they need to be able to achieve their own level of success."*
