> conversation published four days later ([[2024-01-29-yt-uzZK4-_Nx50]]) — treat that as the parent.

> context** only.

## Summary

A short, practical clip on **how to measure whether design work actually worked**.

Friedman's method: establish a **baseline** on the existing site before designing anything, using
**10–12 representative "top tasks"** (for a banking app: send money, withdraw money, add money) run
with **at least 15 people, ideally 18**, which he says is where results become statistically stable.
Measure two things only — **time to complete** and **success rate** (aim for **≥80%**) — then re-run the
same tasks with the same *segment* after the redesign ships, giving a clean before/after.

His lightweight version for people without the time or budget: *"just take five people and speak with
them"* — a conversation, not a study — *"you will find incredible insights already."*

Two craft notes worth keeping: he deliberately **does not** ask people to think aloud during timed
tasks, because narrating distorts both the timing and the behaviour ("that's just not what people do in
real life"); and he frames his own motivation as risk-elimination — *"I want to guarantee, even before
I start designing a single pixel, that it is going to work better than what we have right now… I do not
want to be in a position where I have to get into defensive mode saying, well, we tried our best but
we failed."*

Chris's only real contribution is a forward-looking question about AI generating per-visitor versions
of a site, which prompts the clip's most quotable passage — Friedman's defence of **imperfection**.

## Key claims

_All statements attributed to **Vitaly Friedman**, NOT to Chris Do. Context only._

- [2024-01-25] **The five-user shortcut.** *"Just take five people, five users, and speak with them
  about whatever they are going through — if they encounter any issues, is everything clear… You don't
  need a lot of time. I think that very often we think this is going to take an incredible amount of
  time, this is going to be extremely expensive, it's going to delay the project — not necessarily."*
- [2024-01-25] **The baseline method.** 10–12 representative top tasks; **≥15 people, 18 better**;
  measure **average time per task** and **success rate**; target ≥80% success; re-run with the same
  segment ~5–6 months after rollout for a before/after. Prefers moderated sessions on Zoom over
  unmoderated tools, and observes silently: *"I'm not going to speak, I'm just going to observe…
  we're not testing you, we're testing the website. Feel free to fail — if you fail, I learn from that."*
- [2024-01-25] ★ **Don't ask for think-aloud during timed tasks.** *"When they start speaking this
  doesn't represent the reality — they need more time to complete the task, and that's just not what
  people do in real life."* Debrief afterwards instead. A genuinely counter-conventional craft note.
- [2024-01-25] **On templates:** the UX community on LinkedIn has no shortage of them, but *"we always
  need to take templates with a bit of a grain of salt, because it needs to be customised for whatever
  your needs are."*
- [2024-01-25] ★ **On hyper-personalised, AI-generated sites (answering Chris's question):** he wouldn't
  mind it *"as long as people… can get their work done fast and understand what is going on"* — but
  doubts it's necessary, and names the risk: *"at some point you also get to the situation where the
  system maybe understands the human better than the human does himself or herself, so it becomes a
  little bit freaky, a little bit dangerous."*
- [2024-01-25] ★★ **The defence of imperfection (the clip's best passage).** *"Sometimes I can smell
  ChatGPT text… it's a little bit too perfect, and a bit too clean in a way, too sterile — and that's
  not how the world is. **The world is messy and complex, and people make mistakes and typos — and I
  want to see typos.** Not maybe in formal documents, but I want people to be just respectful and kind,
  and sometimes forget about things and sometimes remember things and sometimes be inconsistent. **We
  do not have to have 100% consistent experiences just for the sake of being consistent.** Just make me
  surprised, or smile, or worried about that footer that is not aligned. That's okay — that's the web."*

## Chris Do's contribution (the only Chris-attributed lines)

- Asks for the shorthand version for people without time or resources — *"is there a shorthand version
  where you know: never do this, always do this, and sometimes do this?"*
- Asks how to measure effectiveness in design and branding specifically, where *"it's a little harder to
  measure."*
- Poses the AI question that produces the imperfection passage: *"in the age of AI, do you see a future
  where… depending on who shows up, it creates a version of the site just for them — not just the way
  it looks, but even the offers and the copy written differently? So hyper-targeted, super-narrow
  niche."* Recorded as context; it shows the direction of his curiosity but states no position.
