# How to Negotiate a Lowball Offer

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do is the sole voice.** A very short clip (~2 minutes) from a live workshop.
> `attribution: sole-subject`.

## Summary

A two-minute clip on **anchoring**, built entirely around a story in which the anchor was used
*against Chris* and worked.

He opens with the room's instinct — who should say the number first? Almost everyone says *second*.
He accepts the received wisdom, names the mechanism (**anchoring**; the car-dealership example), then
tells the story that complicates it.

A client told him *"I don't think we can afford you… do you know somebody in your network?"* and named
**10** as his budget. Chris relayed it to **Ben Burns**, who said *ask for 15* — and Chris pushed back
on his own colleague, repeating the client's number as fact: *"no dude, he said he can't afford this,
he told me 10 is what he's got."* Only later, at home, did he catch it: *"damn it, he just dropped an
anchor on my face **and I fell for it.**"*

The recovery is the teaching. He let the anchor's effect wear off, then emailed: *"I can help you find
it for 10, but if you want me to do it, I can do it for 30"* — **ignoring the old anchor and dropping a
new one.** The client refused, Chris genuinely moved on to finding a referral, and days later — while
actually writing the introduction email — the client came back: *"we really thought about it, we
totally want to work with you, we'll make the money work."*

He closes with the practical rule: when you're lowballed, **ignore it**; and if you're stuck on their
number, make them negotiate against themselves — *"that's not going to work, can you come with another
number?"* But his stated preference is to **say your number first**.

## Key claims — Chris Do, dated 2024-02-01

_Paraphrased unless quoted._

- ★ **Anchoring, named.** *"It's called anchoring — whoever says the number first, you're thinking about
  that number."* And the shorthand: *"there's this thing called **heuristics**, and it's just a
  shorthand for you to remember things. So whatever you say first, people get stuck on."* ★
- ★★ **The story where it worked on him (biography/negotiation, self-reported).** The client's framing
  was disarming — *"Chris, I don't think we can afford you, I'm even embarrassed to ask you, but do you
  know somebody in your network?"* — and it landed the number 10 without a negotiation ever starting.
  Chris then **carried the anchor to his own colleague**: he asked **Ben Burns** to help, Ben said
  *"ask for 15,"* and Chris argued *against* his own side — *"no dude, he said he can't afford this, he
  told me 10 is what he's got."* The realisation came later: *"I went home and I was thinking about it
  — I'm like, damn it, **he just dropped an anchor on my face and I fell for it.**"* Recording this
  matters: it is a rare, dated instance of him being outplayed and saying so. ★★
- ★ **The recovery — let it wear off, then re-anchor.** *"So I let it chill out, the effects of the
  anchor have worn off me. I sent him an email, I said: look, I can help you find it for 10, **but if
  you want me to do it, I can do it for 30.** See? So **I ignore the anchor, I chop that thing off, I
  drop a new anchor — 30.**"* ★
- ★ **What happened next.** The client refused ("no no no, we can't afford this"), Chris accepted it
  genuinely and dropped the job to *"my super low priority list."* Days later, while actually composing
  the referral introduction, the client's email arrived: *"Chris, we really thought about it, we
  totally want to work with you, we'll make the money work, let's get really creative on this."* His
  reaction, delivered as the punchline: *"I delete the email."* — i.e. he binned the referral he was
  mid-way through writing. **New anchor.** ★
- ★ **The rules he draws.** (1) *"When a client goes in and lowballs you, **you've got to just ignore
  it.**"* (2) If you can't get unstuck from their number, make them bid against themselves: *"the one
  thing that you can do as a strategy is to say **that's not going to work — can you come with another
  number?** So you let them negotiate against themselves."* (3) His own preference, stated flatly
  against the room's instinct: *"**say your number first**, and say it."* ★
- **Why the received wisdom fails.** He acknowledges the room's answer (say it second) and the reason
  people believe it — the car-dealership move of getting the seller's number so you can lowball — but
  his own story is the counter-case: once their number is in your head, *"you're stuck on the number,
  you can't even enter it, because you get stuck on that number."*
