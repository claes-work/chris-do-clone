# Proven Strategies to Attract Your Ideal Clients (Business Bootcamp)

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do is the sole teaching voice.** An excerpt from an eight-hour Business
> Bootcamp tune-up, run as open Q&A. Participants heard by name: **Ian**, **Tim**, **Dan**, **Kine/
> Kizia**, **Scott**, **Will**, **Abby**, **Candela**, **Ellen**, **Jed**, and **Kiro** (a UX
> designer working with AI startups). Their questions are context. `attribution: sole-subject`.

## Summary

A wide-ranging Q&A that produces one of the more revealing self-disclosures in the corpus.

It opens with **the Brand Lab pivot restated for the room** — and here he gives the cleanest one-line
version of the insight: creatives learning business and business people learning design are *"not two
different people, we're just two sides of the same coin."*

The middle is a long exchange on **portfolios without results**: what to show when the client
imploded, the company went nowhere, or the work never shipped. His answer is **speculative work** —
design your own case study, never pass it off as a client's — illustrated with a spec car commercial
he and a former student produced, put on the demo reel unlabelled, and which won awards and generated
real car work. Which leads directly into the disclosure: he used to be *"a guy on the white side,"*
watching people play looser and *"eating my lunch,"* and discovered late in life that he is
*"pretty comfortable"* in **moral ambiguity** — bounded by one hard rule: **don't lie.**

He also gives the **director's cut** portfolio rule (show your version, not the client-mangled one),
the **A–F client scoring** habit (fire the Fs, continuously), and a warning about volunteering
information, illustrated with *Cops*.

The last third is live avatar work, including an elegant solve for a participant serving two opposite
audiences, and a demonstration that a well-formed avatar can survive the room's attempts to break it.

## Key claims — Chris Do, dated 2024-02-09

_Paraphrased unless quoted._

- ★★ **The Brand Lab pivot, in one line.** *"For the first eight or nine years of The Futur I was
  trying to help creatives learn business, and then I realised fairly recently that there were
  businesses — or business people — trying to learn art or design, and that **we're actually not two
  different people, we're just two sides of the same coin.** That was the epiphany for me. I just
  didn't even know they existed, cuz every suit I've ever met is the least creative person."* And the
  correction he insists on when a participant says "switching": *"**I'm adding, not switching.**"* ★★
- ★ **What the pivot is for, beyond revenue.** *"There's a bigger master plan here besides just taking
  money from rich entrepreneurs… I do like money, I'm not going to say no to it, but I think it's to
  **connect our two worlds**."* Concretely: he posts qualified job leads into the Pro Group, refusing
  to circulate any that won't state a budget — *"the jobs that I post in there are already qualified at
  a certain budget."* His ambition: *"I'd love to just be able to feed you a steady stream of them."*
  With a blunt rider on why some members never win them: *"if your website looks like a WordPress
  template and it's horrific, and the language is unclear, and you can't find more than one example of
  what you do… **they don't change their website. I put up another position, they submit their website
  again, they don't get selected.**"* ★
- ★★ **Spec work as a marketing strategy — with the ethical line drawn.** The catch-22 he names: *"how
  do I get a client in an industry I've never worked for, because they'll only hire people who've
  worked in that industry?"* The answer: *"you do speculative work. You design your own case study —
  and **you don't try to pass it off as that was your client.**"* His own case: wanting car
  commercials, *"no one will hire you to do a car commercial unless you shot a car commercial — it's
  that simple, too much risk."* So he and a former student/protégée storyboarded and produced one, put
  it on the demo reel *"I never claimed it to be for Mini Cooper, I just put it on the demo reel"* —
  and *"next thing you know we're getting work that looks just like that."* They also entered it into
  award shows *"as long as we read the rules and we didn't feel like we were crossing the line"* and
  won. The rule that bounds it: *"**you can present anything — just don't lie.** If you start to lie
  you dig yourself into deeper lies. So if anybody asked: spec project, spec project."* ★★
- ★★ **The moral-ambiguity self-disclosure (mindset/biography — unusually candid).** *"My wife and I
  [call it] moral ambiguity. **I used to be a guy on the white side.** There's people on the black side
  — like illegal hacks — and then I was like, I'm a Boy Scout over here, **and these people are eating
  my lunch.** What am I doing? I'm playing by rules that I imposed upon myself. So how comfortable am I
  in the moral ambiguity? **Turns out, pretty comfortable. I didn't know — I discovered this pretty
  late in life.**"* And he hands the calibration to the audience rather than prescribing: *"you have to
  figure out where you net out on that spectrum… between 'I always played by the rules, colour within
  the lines' and 'I'm going to break laws and get arrested.' Somewhere in between is where all of us net
  out."* ★★
- ★ **The director's cut portfolio rule.** *"When we were making commercials and music videos we didn't
  always show the one that we finished for the client — we'd show **our version, the director's cut**…
  the next client doesn't care. **It's your portfolio, you do with it what you want.** So let's say they
  said make the logo 18 times bigger — you're like, okay, I've got to do it. Then the one that you put
  on your website is the one that's appropriately sized. **I don't know what design school you went to
  where they told you you must show what the client [approved]. You don't.**"* ★
- ★ **Score clients A–F and fire the Fs — continuously.** *"Create some criteria and score all of your
  clients, then **get rid of your bottom two** — and keep doing that. You just rebuild all of your
  clients constantly. **This never stops. It's how you evolve as a company.** Score your clients from A
  to F, then fire the Fs, cherish the As, and try to get more clients like your As."* Credits **David C.
  Baker** (*The Business of Expertise*) and **Ron Baker** (*Implementing Value Pricing*). ★
- ★ **Don't volunteer information.** *"Don't say more than you need to say. It's annoying, first of all,
  and it feels super suspicious."* Illustrated with *Cops* clips: *"every single person they talk to
  tells them too much information"* versus the ones who know their rights and say nothing. *"You're like
  the drunken criminal — you gave them way too much information. Just watch some of those things and
  look in the mirror: am I acting like this person, or this person?"*
- ★ **One hard rule about former clients.** *"**Never talk bad about your former clients.** New clients
  [think]: what's going to happen when it doesn't work out with us? It's not a good kind of character."*
  So present unshipped work truthfully *"in a way that you don't hurt other people."* ★
- ★ **"Whatever your complaint is, it's your calling."** Told via a designer who, frustrated with a big
  Silicon Valley app, redesigned it unbidden — *"such a marked improvement that the internet went
  bananas, and then she got so many job offers."* His instruction: *"if you complain about it so much,
  you're so angry about how bad it is — **go fix it**, and you can write about it: no one hired me to do
  this, but here's what I think would be a vast improvement."* And the general statement of the job:
  *"you have to be in the business of solving problems and learning how to present those problems to
  prospective buyers. **That's the name of the game.**"* ★
- ★ **The Aaron / Farm Design story — persistence beats the first idea.** A designer who worked alone
  from a rented Santa Monica apartment for decades and *"didn't think like an entrepreneur."* Chris
  relayed an idea (a studio that shipped small-batch artisanal ice cream to prospects — including one
  that arrived melted and rotten because the recipient was on holiday, and *"she still gave them the
  opportunity, because they made the effort"*). Aaron persuaded a printer to co-fund an elaborate
  self-promotional package around a local artisanal ice-pop brand, hand-delivered it to local businesses
  — and got **zero** new business. *"It didn't work for you; it's not that it's a bad idea."* What
  actually worked: a recent-grad employee posted the work to **Behance** without being asked, it was
  picked up by **The Dieline**, *"and his phone never stopped ringing since."* Outcome: six or seven
  employees, better projects, more money, less work. *"I told him: what took you so long? He finally
  decided to put on his big-boy pants and be an entrepreneur."* ★
- **On choosing a market — the PVP-style filter.** References **Allan Dib**'s **PVP** exercise
  (personal fulfilment / value to market / profit) and insists on the ordering for creatives: *"if it
  doesn't pass the first test, forget the rest. **You all didn't choose to suffer through the ridicule
  of your parents and everybody in your community to then just go make money doing something you
  hate.**"*
- **"The market is bigger than your target."** Credits Dib and **Blair Enns**. Illustrated with Apple's
  *Think Different*: *"how many rebels are out there? Not that many, it turns out — **but the market is
  ginormous, because it's aspirational.**"* Same mechanism as Nike selling to non-athletes: *"we're all
  connected to this potential of greatness."*
- ★ **The elegant solve for two opposite audiences.** A participant serves both corporate funders and
  small-business/cause recipients, needing opposite tones. The room offers two brands, two websites,
  "find the common problem," "communicate to the money" — and a participant (**Candela**) supplies the
  answer Chris endorses: *"a landing page that says **make a decision — are you this, or are you
  that?** As soon as you click, it takes you to that page, and you can tailor the language to be very
  different. Doesn't affect the brand."* His framing of the lesson: *"the challenge here is all of you
  are like, I can't figure it out, nobody can solve this problem — **and within three minutes, there it
  is.**"* ★
- **Live avatar stress-testing.** He walks a participant from a read-off list to a compressed avatar
  (*"one word at a time"*): **serial entrepreneur / AI / funded >$10M / B2B**, then invites the room to
  *"punch holes in it."* When it survives — and the participant reveals seven clients including Mozilla
  Firefox — Chris's response is deadpan: *"so no problem then. Why are you wasting our time? …Are you
  just flexing on us?"*
- **A dated personal note (AI).** *"I'm involved in two AI projects myself… I'm going to develop tools
  because I want to get in on it as well."* (February 2024.)
