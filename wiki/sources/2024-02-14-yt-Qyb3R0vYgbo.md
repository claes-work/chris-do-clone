# How To Grow a Million Dollar Business w/ AppSumo CEO Noah Kagan

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary interview; only Chris Do's lines train the persona.** The guest is
> **Noah Kagan**, CEO of **AppSumo** and author of ***Million Dollar Weekend***. Self-reported: early
> employee at Facebook and Mint; dislikes day jobs; frames entrepreneurship as the route to becoming
> *"a time millionaire"* before a cash millionaire. Chris notes he came to know Noah through a mutual
> former colleague (**Aman**, rendered "aan" in the captions) who had previously been a guest —
> *"that episode was straight fire."*
>
> The great majority of the episode is Noah's story and method and is **not persona-eligible**. It is
> recorded here in summary, as context — and because **this is the conversation that produced the
> "what are they trying to avoid?" prompt** that Chris then teaches as his own borrowed tool twelve

> and a half — I need to share this to my community."*

## Summary

A wide-ranging founder interview. Noah's central claim is democratic and stated up front: *"is it
possible for everyone in America to make a million dollars if they follow a certain blueprint? I've
done it, and I've seen it — everyone else can too."* His definition of the discipline is deliberately
unromantic: *"business is really **listening to problems and then solving the ones people want to pay
you money for.**"*

He is candid about the parts founders don't publicise — *"you don't hear about all the things that
failed"* — and about the fear that shaped a major decision: *"I was afraid of who I could become, and
I was afraid of ruining AppSumo,"* which is the context for his hiring someone else to run the
company rather than doing it himself. (Chris references that arrangement approvingly in
[[2024-02-15-yt-HJ9IW1w2bDc]], noting Noah was *"shrewd enough to set up a deal where both people
benefit"* without giving up equity — while cautioning that he hears far more stories where that
arrangement fails than succeeds.)

Chris's own contribution is mostly interviewing, but he opens on a theme that recurs across his work —
the false split between creative and commercial — and presses Noah for the specific moment of change
rather than the tidy version.

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★ **The creative/business false dichotomy, stated as his own framing.** *"I think there is this stigma
  and this belief that **you can either be creative or you can be in business** — and I always think
  about painters and artists: **the most famous painters are definitely not the best.**"* A compact
  version of an argument he makes at length elsewhere (Brand Lab, "two sides of the same coin"), and the
  painters line is a sharp, quotable form of it. ★
- **His interviewing move: press for the moment, not the summary.** *"I want to know Noah's story
  specifically — like, what happened? Somebody came in the office [and] you're like, that's it, I'm done
  with this… **was there a moment like that**, to really personalise the story?"* Characteristic of his
  method across the corpus: refuse the abstracted arc, ask for the scene.
- **Credits the connection.** Notes he got to know Noah through **Aman**, a former AppSumo colleague and
  prior guest — *"that episode was straight fire, he was just dropping gems."*

## Fenced — Noah Kagan (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Noah Kagan. Context only._

- ★ **The thesis.** *"Is it possible for everyone in America to make a million dollars if they follow a
  certain blueprint? I've done it and I've seen it — everyone else can too. This is the craziest part
  about business: **you don't have to be short, you don't have to be tall, you don't have to be
  Asian.**"*
- ★ **His definition of business.** *"Business is really **listening to problems and then solving the
  ones people want to pay you money for.**"*
- ★★ **The avoidance prompt** — the idea Chris takes away and teaches: rather than asking what a
  customer wants, ask **what they are trying to avoid**. (Chris's fuller working-out of it, with worked
  examples, is on [[2024-02-26-yt-nNRWt9-XML0]].)
- **On survivorship bias in founder stories.** *"You don't hear about all the things that failed."*
- **On the fear behind stepping back.** *"I was afraid of who I could become, and I was afraid of ruining
  AppSumo."* Which led to hiring someone else to run the company — an arrangement Chris separately
  describes as unusually well-structured.
- **On partnership and life design.** Opens with it unprompted: *"if you want to cheat in life, find a
  great partner — find a great sense of your own self that you like, and then find someone else that
  supports that."* Calls it *"one of the biggest life upgrades I've had in the past decade."*
- **On what entrepreneurship is for.** *"I figured out I don't like day jobs, and then I figured
  entrepreneurship is the way that you can control your livelihood and **become a time millionaire** —
  then you can also be a cash millionaire."*
