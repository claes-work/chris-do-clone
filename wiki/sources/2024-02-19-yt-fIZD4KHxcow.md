# How To Attract New Customers & Grow Your Freelance Business

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do is the teaching voice.** The middle session of the three-part hot seat
> with **Chris Franklin** (video business, $300K → $150K), with **Richie** contributing. The coachee's
> lines are context. `attribution: sole-subject (teaching)`. See also [[2024-02-15-yt-HJ9IW1w2bDc]] and
> [[2024-02-26-yt-nNRWt9-XML0]].

## Summary

The "why people buy" session, and the most theoretically ambitious of the three.

It opens on **findability**: *"to be found, you must be findable"* (Austin Kleon), and the harder truth
underneath it — *"we only hire the people that we know."* Chris demonstrates it against himself: needing
a shooter at short notice, he hired **Sam**, whose reel he had never watched, purely because Richie
vouched for him. Then Simon Sinek's babysitter example: we choose the familiar 16-year-old over the
objectively superior professional nanny. Hence **know, like, trust**.

The centre is a **merged Maslow model**. He takes Hormozi's *health, wealth, relationships*, adds
**status** and **identity**, and maps the stack onto Maslow: health (survival) → wealth (safety,
options, belonging) → relationships → **status** (what buying this signals to others) → **identity**
(what it signals to yourself). His claim: the bottom is where people pay least, the top is where they
pay most, and **luxury goods prove it** — nobody needs Louis Vuitton, and LVMH's owner is among the
richest people alive.

The practical instruction is **"mind walking"** — an exercise he says he used with his students — to
inhabit the buyer's senses rather than your own, requiring **imagination and empathy**. And he delivers
a genuinely unkind-but-useful line about his own audience on the second of those.

The back half applies the **problem / sample / tool** framework from the companion session to the
coachee's actual YouTube channel, diagnosing that his content attracts only peers and sponsors.

## Key claims — Chris Do, dated 2024-02-19

_Paraphrased unless quoted._

- ★ **Findability, and why it beats qualification.** *"To be found, you must be findable"* — credited to
  **Austin Kleon**'s *Show Your Work*, with a wink: *"I'm not even sure that's a real sentence, but it
  sounds really good to me."* The operational version: *"you've got to make it easier to find you than
  it is to find someone else that has comparable skills, because **we only hire the people that we
  know**."* And the diagnostic: *"when your pipeline isn't full of new leads, it's an indicator that
  it's very hard to find you right now."* ★
- ★★ **He demonstrates the bias on himself.** Needing someone to film an event: *"I reached out to
  Richie, who's not available; I reached out to another guy, not available. And Richie's like, hey, you
  remember Sam? Just hire Sam. **I've never looked at Sam's reel. I don't know anything about Sam** —
  except for the first time he helped us out. But because he comes from Richie, there's a link there."*
  The conclusion he draws: *"**we trust people that we know more than we trust qualified people.**"*
  Reinforced with **Simon Sinek**'s babysitter example — the familiar 16-year-old over the "infinitely
  more qualified" professional nanny — because *"we saw them go to church, go to the same school… it's
  a familiarity."* Hence **know, like, trust**. ★★
- ★★ **The merged Maslow / buying-motivation stack.** Starting from **Hormozi**'s three (*health,
  wealth, relationships*), he adds **status** and **identity**, and maps them onto Maslow's hierarchy:
  physiological/**health** at the base (*"in the desert without water you'll pay a lot; in everyday
  life, clean water is abundant so you won't pay much"*), then **wealth** — which he defines broadly:
  *"money or options or freedom or choice, because people who are wealthy have the freedom to
  choose"* — then **relationships**, then **status**, then **identity**. ★★
- ★★ **Status vs. identity, distinguished.** **Status** is outward: *"if I buy this, what am I signalling
  to other people about my beliefs and values? That's why people drive premium cars or buy luxury
  goods — to signal to others, and to their tribe, that they've achieved something."* He groups
  credentials with it — a Harvard degree, magna cum laude, national champion — as **heuristics**,
  *"a shortcut for an idea."* **Identity** is inward: *"when we buy things, not only are we signalling
  to other people, but **we're forming an identity in our own mind about what kind of person we
  are**."* And he ties it to the spending-as-voting line he credits via **Kevin Finn** to Anna Lappé:
  *"every dollar you spend is a vote for the kind of world you want to live in."* ★★
- ★ **Needs vs. wants, and where the money is.** *"Down here, people pay the least amount of money.
  [Up here] is the want."* Needs are what you must have to survive; wants ascend with development —
  *"if you talk to somebody in a developing country: I don't need freaking identity, I don't even need
  status, because I don't have clean water."* The selling error he names: *"often times what we do is
  **we're selling a need** — oh, you need a video, you need an editor."* ★
- ★ **Luxury as the proof.** *"Nobody needs any of that stuff, and they're willing to pay… 10×, even
  100× of the next cheapest viable alternative."* His figures: **Louis Vuitton** as *"perhaps the most
  influential luxury goods company in the fashion sector — the first to surpass $20 billion in annual
  sales, I think in 2022,"* and *"that's why the owner of LVMH is considered one of the richest people
  in the world."* `[self-reported]` figures. ★
- ★★ **"Mind walking" — his own named exercise.** *"We're often looking at the world through our own
  lens and not through the lens of our buyer. **So we sell what we want to sell to ourselves — but we
  ain't buying**, because no other videographer is looking at you like, I need to buy more video
  services from you."* The corrective: *"it's an exercise I would do with my students, I call it **mind
  walking**. We have to walk in the shoes of this person: what do they see? What do they hear? What do
  they taste? What do they do?… It requires **imagination and empathy** — two of the most important
  skills when it comes to marketing and branding."* ★★
- ★★ **The uncomfortable claim about creatives and empathy.** *"Imagination, as a creative person, you
  should have a lot of. And **empathy is something a lot of creative people champion but are actually
  really poor at.** And I'm going to say this, and it's going to be a little controversial: the reason
  is **a lot of artists are tortured souls. They're very self-centred.** They just keep looking at the
  world through their lens: why is this happening to me? Why don't people desire what I do more? **But
  if all they did was study what people wanted and gave it to them, they would be more successful.
  Maybe not rich, but more successful.**"* ★★
- ★ **The "Dear Son" content technique.** Credited to a name the captions render as "Yasmin Alex"
  (`attribution: uncertain`): write the piece as a letter — *"dear son"* — give the advice, sign off
  *"love, dad,"* then delete both lines before posting. *"The voice is very kind. It's generous. It's
  patient. **It's a beautiful way to think about how you can make content in a generous spirit.**"* And
  the self-deprecating contrast he draws: *"mine is like **dear mother** — because it's not nice, it's
  not patient. It's like, do you get this or not? …**Dear sucker.** So we're going to do dear son."* ★
- ★ **Word of mouth, dignified.** *"There's a term I heard from [Errol Garrison]: **WMA — word of mouth
  advertising.** I like the way he describes this because **it adds the word advertising**, so it makes
  it a legitimate-sounding thing. Otherwise it's just word of mouth."* (`attribution: uncertain` on the
  name.) ★
- ★ **Diagnosing the coachee's content.** His channel targets *"people just like you, or sponsors"* —
  and Chris names the ceiling: sponsors are limited to camera manufacturers, and *"other videographers
  can already shoot their own stuff."* Applying the **problem / sample / tool** framework: the content is
  *"mostly problem, a little bit of sample"* and **no tool** — with the correction that *"the tool is
  something you **give** them; it's mostly digital. **It's not the tools that you use.**"* Worked
  through on **Caleb Pike** (DSLR Video Shooter): a camera-cage video could ship a **PDF blueprint**, a
  **parts link list**, or — best, because it costs nothing to scale — **the 3D-print file**. *"These
  tools have to cost you very little because it needs to scale."* ★
- ★ **The dynamic he's trying to flip.** *"Instead of chasing people, **you give value up front so then
  they chase you.**"* The imagined outcome: someone inside a company's video department says *"instead
  of me trying to figure this out, why don't we just hire Chris to consult?"* ★
- **On selling the wrong thing (carried from the prior session).** *"Are you just selling the person who
  creates videos, or the person who edits? **You're selling the wrong thing. You're not selling the
  transformation that matters to them.**"*
