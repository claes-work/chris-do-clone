## Summary

An SEO teach framed around a diagnosis Chris's audience will recognise: *"y'all call yourselves brand
strategists, but the average person out there doesn't know what the hell that means."*

Kim's structure: SEO rests on a **tripod** — **content** (the only leg fully in your control), **speed**
(technical performance), and **popularity** (backlinks, explained via a *Mean Girls* popularity
analogy, with the nuance that a link's value depends on how freely the linker gives links). Then the
keyword taxonomy from a study she cites: **branded** keywords (~10%), **product/service/job-title**
keywords (the "money keywords," together ~20% with branded), and **topic** keywords — *"the whopping
80%."* Her point: the money is in the 20%, but **the opportunity is in the 80%**, which is exactly the
content most creatives never make.

She also gives the practical mechanics — mining Google/YouTube autocomplete and related-searches for
free, and building **topic clusters** rather than single keywords (her wedding-photographer example
found ~eight phrasings all circling *cost*).

The episode is notable for how directly it uses **The Futur itself** as the worked example: Kim reports
checking future.com unprompted, estimates *"over 20,000 backlinks,"* and names the specific gap — a
huge amount of content on YouTube and social, and comparatively little on the website.

Chris's contribution is one strong closing synthesis and a running self-deprecating bit about how dry
he finds the topic.

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★★ **His synthesis of the whole episode — "rewind the tape."** Delivered as the closing summary:
  *"the problem is, whatever it is that you do, **you assume everybody knows everything they need to
  know about you** — and that they already have the problem, they've looked into solutions, and now
  they just need to know how long and how much. **And so then you create content for that last little
  bit.** But what Jule told us to do is to **rewind the tape** — go all the way to the beginning, to
  the unaware… and just think through **their eyes and not your own**. What is it that they need to
  know before they even get to know that they need you?"* Then the method: map it in *"a logical linear
  sense… maybe there's three [phases], three is a pretty good number,"* and write content for each.
  And the instruction he repeats: *"**empathise.** Use your imagination to look at the world through
  their eyes and think: what is it I'm going through right now? What does my world look like? What is
  some pain that I'm experiencing?"* ★★
- ★ **The four questions every website should answer.** *"On every website you should be answering four
  basic questions. **What is it?** … **Who is it for?** — this is where a lot of people drop the ball,
  they don't actually define who [it's for]…"* (the remaining two follow in the same sequence). A
  compact, checkable standard. ★
- ★ **Why 2024 was set as a lead-generation year (business/strategy).** *"We've set the intention for
  2024 as the year for us — for all of us — to focus on generating **higher quality leads.** Higher
  quality leads means we have more prospects to talk to. It means **they're looking for us versus us
  chasing them.** And we're going to be able to command a higher price, hopefully a price premium. And
  with leads you can pretty much do anything: hire more people, work less hours, pivot to other
  businesses — **as long as you have enough high-quality leads.**"* Consistent with the Pro Group's
  stated 2024 focus in [[2024-02-26-yt-nNRWt9-XML0]]. ★
- ★ **An honest admission about his own attention to SEO.** *"I don't know the number of backlinks we
  have. I'll take your word, cuz your memory for this is better — **cuz I haven't even looked at it.
  That's how much I care about this stuff.** But I know it's very important; the team works on this."*
  A third instance of the honest-about-gaps pattern, alongside "I'm a terrible teacher" and the client-
  experience admission. ★
- **On paid backlinks — a stated line he won't cross.** People offer to pay for guest posts with
  backlinks: *"literally there are people out there who see you as an authority who want a backlink from
  you, and you can make some money on that if you want to. **More often than not, we just turn them
  down**, because it's not a product or anything that we're interested in — so we tend to just save our
  audience from *'oh, that was a paid post, wasn't it?'*"*
- **The awareness-stage restatement.** He plays the framework back in his own vocabulary: *"depending
  on where somebody is in the marketing funnel you have to target different keywords. If they're
  **problem unaware**, you need to educate them on what's the problem they're having. Then they go into
  **solution unaware**… and then only once you get down to **product unaware**, it's like: this is the
  product, this is how long and how much it costs."*
- **Voice.** A running bit about the topic's dryness — *"I see you, Chris. I see your face. You're like,
  oh god"* — and a closing dad-joke exchange (*"not all math jokes are bad, Jule. Just some. S-U-M."*).
  Plus the mock-defensive greeting: *"didn't I ask you, please don't [look at our site]? I have enough
  problems."*

## Fenced — Jule Kim (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Jule Kim. Context only._

- **The tripod:** **content** (*"purely in your control today"*), **speed** (*"who's going to wait
  around for something to load for 30 seconds?"*), **popularity** (backlinks). *"Content is a little
  bit like — all boats rise, when you've got the tide going."*
- **Backlinks as popularity, with a nuance.** The *Mean Girls* analogy — the new kid who befriends the
  popular kids becomes popular. But link value depends on how freely the linker links: *"if you're just
  throwing your dollars in every which direction, it starts to count for a little bit less…"* — so a
  trusted mid-size site can outweigh a link from forbes.com. She also flags **negative SEO**: people
  buying shady links *pointing at competitors* to damage their trust score.
- ★ **The keyword taxonomy (her central framework).** From a study she cites (a professor categorising
  hundreds of thousands of search terms): **~10% branded** (searching your name or company — *"they
  already know the idea of you"*), **~10% product/service/job-title** (the **money keywords**, bottom of
  funnel), and **~80% topic keywords** (*"what is brand strategy? How do you hard-boil an egg?"*). Her
  conclusion: the money is in the 20%, *"but the real opportunity is in that 80%, where you're probably
  not generating content that teaches what you know."*
- ★ **Start with the questions you already get.** *"You probably get a million questions all the time,
  the same questions from people."* Chris supplies the example — *"how much should I charge for a
  logo?"* — and she closes it: *"if you don't already have content on your website, you should be
  creating that."*
- ★ **The brand-strategist mismatch (the episode's sharpest practical point).** *"Y'all call yourselves
  brand strategists, but the average person doesn't know what the hell that means. **The average person
  is like: I don't like how my website looks.** They're ashamed to hand out their business cards at
  networking functions. They're ashamed to even share their website… **They don't know that y'all brand
  strategists could solve this problem.**"*
- **Finding keywords for free:** Google/YouTube **autocomplete** suggestions, and the **related
  searches** at the bottom of a results page.
- ★ **Topic clusters, not single keywords.** Her wedding-photographer example: under "wedding
  photographer" sat *"about eight different ways of people all searching for the concept of basically
  how much does a wedding photographer cost"* — average cost, packages, and so on. Pull all of them into
  the list as a cluster.
- **On The Futur specifically:** an estimated *"over 20,000 backlinks"* driving its domain authority, and
  the diagnosed gap — *"you have a ton of video content on YouTube and on all the platforms, **but you
  don't actually have a lot of content on your website.** This is where we could close the gap for
  you."*
- **On her own pivot:** leaving SEO for life coaching was *"probably one of the hardest moves I've ever
  made,"* and *"there is a part of me that does miss talking about marketing, content marketing, SEO."*
