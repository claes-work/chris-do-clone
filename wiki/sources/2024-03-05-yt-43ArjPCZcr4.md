# How To Ethically Charge More Money For Your Creative Services

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary.** A short live-stream answer, essentially solo teaching. One
> audience member (**Anthony**, a web developer) contributes a question and a technique he says he
> learned from Chris. Everything else is Chris-attributed and persona-eligible.

## Summary

The question: *"How do you introduce strategy to clients who come to you for design?"* Chris's answer is
a named move — **embrace and pivot** — followed by his sharpest single statement of what creatives should
actually be selling.

**Embrace and pivot**: thank them for the design ask, affirm you'd love to do it, then introduce the new
thing — *"I've incorporated a new thing into my business that really helped my clients grow. Are you
curious about this?"* They always say yes. Then run 20–30 minutes of scaffolding questions about their
core customers and pain points, and — critically — **assign a price to what you just did**: *"since I'm
asking you to do this, this first meeting is on me. The next meeting will be on you, because **typically
I charge $10,000 to do this**."* The method is show-don't-tell: *"demonstrating strategic thinking and
not telling them about it."*

Roughly the middle third is an unprompted review of **Seth Godin's *This Is Marketing***, including a
rare admission that he initially disliked the book. His summary of Godin's central distinction —
advertising vs. marketing — is the quotable takeaway.

Anthony's contribution is the **value-add ladder**: offer strategy free the first time to build your own
confidence, then charge for it. Chris endorses it with a limit: *"within two or three iterations of that,
time to charge for it."*

## Key claims (2024-03-05)

- **Embrace and pivot** is the technique for introducing strategy to a design-shaped brief. Affirm first,
  then pivot hard: *"you know, I've got this other thing that I think could really help you — do you want
  to do it?"*
- **Price the free thing while you give it away.** *"Typically I charge $10,000 to do this"* — said while
  doing it for free — is how the value gets assigned without a fight later.
- **Discovery questions are diagnostic, not exhaustive**: *"I don't need for you to tell me where the
  bull is, just tell me which wall it's on."*
- ★★ **Sell clarity of thinking, not the artifact.** The central claim of the video (below).
- **Positioning by product = commodity.** *"Too many of us position ourselves around the things that we
  do, so then we're stuck competing against other makers, and that's where you're going to get
  slaughtered — because the only thing that differentiates you from somebody else in the client's eye now
  is price."*
- **80% of the work is agreeing on the problem.** *"You need to get clarity: what is the problem that's
  worth solving? Aligning the key stakeholders, the decision makers, that this is the problem worth
  solving — and **80% of the work has been done at that point.**"*
- ★ **"No" as an affirmation that you've changed.** When you raise your positioning, expect a lot of
  rejections — and read them as evidence, not failure.
- **Value-add as a ladder, with an expiry**: free/value-add for the first two or three engagements to
  build confidence, then charge.

## Verbatim quotes

- ★★ *"I want you to move away from **the thing that you make** as what defines you. I want you all to
  sell **clarity of thinking and communication** — and just by the way we make websites, just by the way
  I do branding. That's what you want to do."*
- ★★ *"Nobody thinks like you, so **when you put you in your brand there is no competition** — and then
  you can just raise your rates."*
- ★ *"Be prepared to get a lot of nos, 'cause that's what happens when you change. **The no is an
  affirmation that you've changed**, therefore the people that you talk to have to change as well."*
- ★ *"Very rarely do the people you work with today follow you along this journey of becoming the new
  you. They want to hold on to the old you… **the person who made the purchasing decision to hire the old
  you is stuck in that world.** So eventually — I'm sorry to say this — you don't have time for them
  anymore."*
- ★ On Seth Godin's distinction: *"**Advertising is: I have a key and I look for a lock.** He said
  **marketing is looking for locks and inventing or fashioning a key** to solve their problem."*
- *"Everybody isn't running away from a problem, **they're running towards a solution.**"*
- On *This Is Marketing*: *"At first I didn't like it, I got to be honest with you… I thought, God, this
  is like a collection of a bunch of essays he's written and he doesn't respect my time and intelligence.
  … And I got to tell you, **this is really freaking good. I've reread half the book three times
  already.**"* And on why it repeats: *"he's really trying to explain something to you that most people
  probably just don't believe… **because they're resisting change.**"*
- *"Then you can run like the Flash or Quicksilver, depending on which universe you come from — then you
  can run and execute like a mother-effer."*
- On the ladder: *"I'm all for you doing it for free or value-add the first time so you can build up the
  confidence and you can have practice — but I would say **within two or three iterations of that, time
  to charge for it**."*
