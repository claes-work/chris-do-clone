## Fenced summary — the guest presenter (does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to the guest ("Matt", likely Matt Essam). Context only._

The pitch: a **weekly planner** he says he has used for five or six years and teaches to agency clients,
sitting inside a larger **creative life planning system**. He is explicit that it *"definitely isn't some
kind of productivity hack video."*

The cascade, top down:

1. **Ultimate vision** — usually a **10-year** horizon. Deliberately intangible; a *"guiding compass."*
2. **Milestones** at **1, 3 and 5 years**, which he calls the **MAP**: **M**easurable, **A**ccountable,
   **P**urpose-driven. His worked example: not *"I want to learn Spanish"* but *"I want to have a
   five-minute conversation with a local in Spanish by the end of 2024"* — then external accountability (a
   booked ticket, a friend, a small bet) and a real reason why.
3. **Projects** — the *"big rocks"* that make up a milestone.
4. **Rituals** — the daily and weekly practice.

The weekly layer is the operational one: a brain dump, chunking, and a priority matrix to separate what
moves the business from what merely fills the week; then pick a small number of things (he works through
a first, second and third) and protect the time for them. His stated failure mode is starting the day
inside someone else's agenda — *"before I know it… my day just starts totally randomly based on somebody
else's priorities."*

The week closes with a **team retrospective**: what were your wins, and what did you learn. His framing:
*"there is no such thing as failure… you either win or you learn."* The stated purpose is partly morale —
people *"don't even appreciate what you've achieved over a week or a month because you're so busy working
your business."*

Named influences in passing: **Cal Newport**, **Tim Ferriss**, ***The One Thing***.

The call to action is a downloadable copy of the week planner, and the positioning line for his own
practice: helping creative agencies and studios move *"from disorganised, running around everybody else's
priorities"* to *"building the business that they want to build rather than continually building their
client business."*
