## Summary

Three long movements.

**1. Chunking, and how to get inside a budget you can't win.** Big project, big budget, too many
approvers → break it into the smallest piece the buyer can commit to. The concrete tactic: ask directly
whether they have a **discretionary budget** they can approve without escalation — *"in the kind of
companies you're thinking of, the answer is 100% yes; whether they tell you or not is another story."* He
says he only learned the number game because clients told him: *"Chris, you put in the budget for $84,000
— if you put in for $79[,000] I can approve it tomorrow."* Underneath it is the **sunk-cost bias** — once
they've spent anything with you, restarting elsewhere feels worse than continuing — which he admits works
on him too: *"I teach this stuff and I still fall for it every single day of my life."*

**2. The predictable proven process, and the smile curve.** Every business should be able to show a
**five-step** process (*"five is the magic number"* — 17 is too complicated, three is not thought
through). Worked live with Diego down to **plan → pre-production → production → post → distribution**,
with Chris insisting on fifth-grader vocabulary: *"I'm going to use the simplest terms that a fifth grader
would understand. **It's called a plan.**"* Then the trap: he asks the room to allocate $10 across the five
steps. Seven people vote **production** — and every one of them works in production. His verdict: value
concentrates at the **planning/idea** end and the **distribution/marketing** end, and dies in the middle
(**Stan Shih's smile curve**), with the iPod and the made-in-China chair as evidence. He follows it
immediately with the AI question — *"where is AI going to eat your lunch first?"* — and answers it:
production. *"That's why all the artists are crying, because they just see themselves as production
people."*

**3. ★★ Quality ≠ results, and what to sell instead.** Karina argues quality drives results. Chris
refuses the correlation, teaches his son's **corgi story** to separate correlation from causation, and
brings receipts: a client whose marketing videos are *"terrible — they even tell me, we're embarrassed to
show you,"* to which he replies *"I'm embarrassed to show you my profit and loss statement";* production
teams shooting on a RED Dragon 8K then **degrading the footage** — adding camera shake and poor exposure —
because *"the more polished it looks, the more we feel it's not real."* When she presses on how you sell
branding then, he pivots to the actual answer: **you sell status**. Which unpacks into the logo quilt of
recognisable clients (buy the job if you have to — *"do it for free… I paid them"* — but never
misrepresent: claiming Nike because they sent you a package is *"so far out of the grey"*), reviews and
case studies, and then **scarcity**: supply below demand, made visible. *"If you look at every luxury
brand, they operate under false scarcity… the simple concept is: **is it difficult to buy?**"* He points
to Daniel Priestley's ***Oversubscribed*** as the deep dive.

Two other sustained pieces: **the farm/die-line lesson** (go where the fish are; make your work
trivially easy to find and trace back to you — the photographers who lock their images are *"saying
please do not hire me"*), and the **build / borrow / be / buy** ladder for authority, credited to Mira
Kotand, with the fourth B (buy) added by Chris and illustrated by Marty Neumeier licensing his thought
leadership to a branding firm.

## Key claims (2024-03-10)

- ★★ **The one-degree pivot**, credited to his business mentor: *"oftentimes we look at solutions that are
  180 degrees away… but in fact the success that you want is sometimes **one degree different**."*
- ★★ **The commercial-real-estate pivot** — the origin story of Blind's most valuable client (below).
- **Chunking / scaffolding**: same concept from opposite directions; shrink the first commitment until it
  is easy to say yes to.
- **The discretionary-budget question** as an explicit sales move for large organisations.
- **Sunk-cost bias** is why the first small yes matters — and why he distrusts free samples.
- **Five steps, always five.** Design thinking, user-centric marketing — *"if you start to look around
  you'll start to see that the people who do this for a living almost always break things down to five."*
- **Name each step in fifth-grade language** so the client is never guessing; write it up and publish it.
  *"When I ask entrepreneurs what is it you want, they almost always say: we want to have a **predictable
  proven process.**"*
- **Stan Shih's smile curve**: value sits in the thinking and the marketing; the middle is commoditised.
  The iPod cost cents to assemble; Apple, the marketer and distributor, keeps the money.
- **AI takes production first.** Planning and distribution are the defensible ends.
- ★★ **Quality does not equal results.** *"There have been a lot of quality films that don't have good
  results. There have been a lot of crappy reality shows that have tremendous results."*
- **Correlation vs. causation**, taught via the corgi story with his son.
- **A counter-intuitive corollary**: *"the less money you have, the more innovative your solutions will
  be."* Plus his theory that first-time directors who score big make bad second and third films —
  *"they were much better just being scrappy"* — tempered live by his own concession that consistent hits
  come from a **predictable proven process** (The Beatles, the Rolling Stones, The Weeknd).
- ★ **People buy status.** *"You will pay an unreasonable amount for me to do something that doesn't
  really matter, because doing so would give you pleasure."* Told against himself: the business-class
  argument he has with his wife, and his solution — *"I only fly business class now [when] someone else
  will pay for it, so I'm free of the guilt."*
- **Status tactics**: the **logo quilt** of high-profile clients (acquire the logo by any legitimate means
  — free, or even paying for the project — but never claim work you didn't do); reviews and testimonials
  (*"we outsource most of our thinking"*); case studies; awards (**which he says did not pay off for
  him**: *"I haven't won 100 awards but it feels like 100 awards — that strategy has not paid off that
  well for us"*).
- ★ **Scarcity is engineered**: supply below demand, communicated. Decrease supply *and* increase demand —
  *"attack the equation on both sides."*
- **Demand tactics** listed live: social following, PR, awards, public speaking, writing a book, podcasts,
  workshops, paid ads (including a two-email-address campaign targeting Pinterest's founders), listicles
  that sandwich you among nine better companies (*"I hate these bastards because they're not good"*),
  Spotify playlists.
- **The threshold of credibility on Instagram**: he insists it is nearer **100,000** followers than
  10,000 for general authority — while conceding *"it might not be 100"* and that a niche changes the
  answer.
- **Pick the channel your buyer is on, not the one you want to be on.** *"Only focus on where they're
  going to be."*
- **Post → look at results → one small tweak → repeat.** Don't write 35 posts and publish 35 in a row —
  *"you're not learning from every post."*
- **Build / borrow / be / buy** (credited to Mira Kotand; the fourth B is Chris's addition) as the four
  routes to expertise-by-association.
- **Make yourself easy to steal from**: metadata, titles, findability. Locking your images is
  self-sabotage. *"Your number one priority is to make sure everybody can steal your image."*
- **Performance marketing vs. brand building** (from a Harvard Business Review May/June article he
  bought the issue for a *different* article): performance marketing wins attention because ROI is
  legible; brand building is where the world's most valuable companies spend. *"If you spend all of your
  money and effort [on] performance marketing, eventually your brand will be worth nothing."* Tesla and
  Elon Musk as the illustration — *"forget about his politics and his craziness, just focus on what he's
  done that's working."*

## Verbatim quotes

- ★★ *"Oftentimes we look at solutions that are 180 degrees away — we think we have to do something
  completely different — but in fact **the success that you want is sometimes one degree different.**"*
- ★★ **The Trojan Storage story.** They were not hired off the Audi or Xbox work: *"[it was] **Trojan
  Storage** — a low-rent self-storage facility."* The client's reasoning, as he tells it: *"**if you can
  make self-storage facilities look good, you can make our A-class buildings amazing.**"* The result: a
  $13B commercial real-estate client, won *while being the most expensive bidder*, beating every
  specialist real-estate marketing company — *"that's how we **flipped a $1,000 job into a multi-million
  dollar opportunity.**"*
- ★ *"It's not that they don't have money — **they just don't have money for us.**"*
- ★ *"I'm going to use the simplest terms that a fifth grader would understand. **It's called a plan.**"*
- ★ *"Jason's excellent at making what he makes. I know it, I've seen his work, he's very good. **But if
  it don't make me a dollar, I don't care.**"*
- ★ *"So quality — **not equal — results.**"*
- *"Let's be sober when we make decisions. **Romanticism is for movies and poetry and for Valentine's
  cards, not for your business**, because that doesn't pay the bills."*
- On the client with bad videos: *"They're terrible. They even tell me: we're embarrassed to show you. And
  I'm like — **I'm embarrassed to show you my profit and loss statement.**"*
- ★ *"The more polished it looks, **the more we feel it's not real.**"*
- ★ *"Nobody thinks like you"* → here in its status form: *"we're social creatures and **we're not that
  smart — we're all sheeple.** … If you worked with Nike, you're way better than what we can do."*
- On honesty limits: *"'I received a package from Nike, I've worked for Nike' — no you haven't. **That's
  dishonest. That's black.** That's so far out of the grey."*
- ★ *"If you look at every luxury brand, they operate under **false scarcity**… the simple concept is:
  **is it difficult to buy?** If it is, we want it more, for some reason."*
- *"**Hard to upgrade, impossible to downgrade.**"* (on first class, as a model of how status ratchets)
- *"My friend, a fairly successful entrepreneur, just told me: **if you go to the top of the mountain,
  work with the top company, all the other companies fall in line.**"*
- ★ *"When your competitors are using your work as a sample of what to do, clients get real smart. … **You
  have to put out the work that's really easy to find, that traces all back to you.**"*
- On locked portfolios: *"They're saying **please do not hire me**, please do not trace this back to me."*
- On brainstorming discipline: *"The way we want to do brainstorming is **not [to] enter new ideas until
  we've exhausted the old idea.**"*
- ★ *"I'm going to be strategic in picking the one that has the highest potential of success with the
  least amount of effort."*
- On the corgi: *"Causal means A equals B. **Correlation is sometimes A equals B, but not always — and
  we're not going to bet on sometimes. I'm not a gambler.**"*
- Dale Carnegie, as he retells it: *"I love strawberries and cream, but it doesn't matter what I love,
  **because fish care about worms.** … So: **what results matter to you?** That's the only question that
  really matters."*
- *"Hi everybody, my name is Chris, **I've been client-free for five years.**"* (a running AA-meeting bit;
  he dates the end of client work to **2018**)
