# Generate Demand, Get More Sales, Clients On Repeat: Masterclass Workshop (Bootcamp Part 4)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary; live workshop with named participants.** The final part of the
> four-part Business Boot Camp series, recorded at the same event as
> [[2024-03-10-yt-h1voCMAT9Qc]] and opening mid-argument from it. Attendees (**Jake, Cam, Scott, Jason,
> Mo, Abby, Kezia, Ehor, Will**) ask questions; their lines are context only.
>
> Frameworks credited to others and **not** Chris's: the staging-area/scarcity sequence is **Daniel
> Priestley's** (*Oversubscribed*); the workshop-design method is **Rob Fitzpatrick's** and **Dr Samuel
> Holzman's**; *"get the job done or be right or be popular"* is credited to a mentor he calls **Keir**;
> the purpose/gift line he attributes (loosely) to **Picasso**; the program-halving advice is **Jasmine
> Star's**; the alliteration/rhyme rules come from **Scott**, via **Eben Pagan**, and **Sam Horn**'s *POP!*.

## Summary

The most quotable single session in this stretch of the corpus, and unusually transparent about the
business's own numbers and decisions.

**1. Manufacturing scarcity (Priestley's method, applied live).** Pre-announce → get the market to raise
its hand → move them into a **visible staging area** where they can see each other → announce a fixed
number of slots and a fixed sale date. The visibility is the whole mechanism: the movie-theatre line, the
restaurant with empty tables, the club queue, the Chanel appointment. Then the discipline: **do not
increase capacity** when it sells out. Handle surplus demand by offering existing buyers **2×** to sell
their ticket back — you take no margin — which makes two people happy and, if they decline, *revalues the
thing upward in their own mind*. **Supreme** as the pure case: one New York store, a drop every Thursday,
never remade — *"their genius is they just took the collection and released it once a week"* — and the
decline after the parent company opened more stores. His generalisation back to the room:
*"**you don't have to change what you do, just change one factor of what you do**, and all of a sudden
it becomes a whole new thing."*

**2. ★★ Full transparency about The Futur's own application of it.** The Pro Group is capped at
**1,000 members** (he wanted 3,000; Ben talked him to 1,000) with **30 enrolled per month** — currently
around 530. Result: *"Ben sent an email to the entire team — **we broke a sales record** in terms of
community revenue in a given month between Accelerator and the Pro Group."* With the honest caveat
attached: *"**ask me in six months if it's working. I do not know.** Conceptually it works… and if it
doesn't work, I don't think the theory is bad, I think our application of it is bad."* Waiting-room
members get dripped archive content from ~300 recorded calls. And the endgame he describes with obvious
relish: at 1,000 he will not expand — he will **pay people to leave**.

**3. ★★ Brand Lab, decided in public.** Capped at 100, chasing the first 10, at **$18,000 for six
months** — restructured from $36,000 for twelve on **Jasmine Star's** advice (*"you do the math, it's
exactly the same price"*). Ben's incentive idea for the first 10: a one-on-one strategy session —
*"execute it, Ben, let's go."* Then the counter-intuitive move: **removing** deliverables. One person
said they couldn't attend the three-day workshop, so the workshop was cut — **same price**. *"I just get
rid of more stuff until they can't complain anymore."* His justification is a value question, not a
volume one: *"you have to remember **what is it that you're buying? Where's the value?** And when you can
identify that, **get rid of everything else.**"* Also stated plainly: *"I would teach you everything you
need to know about personal branding for free. **If you want the feedback from me, you'll pay me the 18
grand.**"*

**4. ★★ "Get the job done, or be right, or be popular."** Taught via a costly story he tells against
himself: an unethical sales rep quit, then invoked an auto-renewing contract; his attorney (**Stuart**)
talked him out of suing; he paid roughly **$32,000** — *"a painful check for me to write"* — to buy out a
rep who had stopped selling for him. The lesson is arithmetic, not virtue: one new six-figure project is
$300–500K, and every month without a rep costs more than $32K. Plus the closing note on grievance: *"the
worst thing is you signed the check and you're still angry."*

**5. Teaching method — chunk, then name it.** From **Rob Fitzpatrick** (*The Workshop Survival Guide*)
and **Dr Samuel Holzman** at ArtCenter: find the one learning outcome, break it into five, break those
into five, until you hit the irreducible skill; then teach one at a time. A good workshop prompt has
**clear directions but many right answers**. His own confessed failure: asking questions that were clear
to him and unclear to the room. Then the *"killer"* move: **give it a name** — Maslow's hierarchy, Retina
Display, Adidas Boost — *"as soon as you give it a unique name, **you own it**."* Plus the mnemonic
mechanics: hard K sounds, alliteration, threes, rhyme, portmanteaus (smog, hacktivist, *disruption*).

**6. Personal brand vs. company brand — and the one life hack.** The answer is blunt: nobody believes a
company account is a person. *"When you see a tweet from Tesla, do you think it's Elon? … We are smart
enough to know that the founder — the people we connect with, the personality — is not the person running
the account."* Hence Elon > Tesla, Branson > Virgin. The corollary for delegation: **be the first source
of yourself.** He recounts hiring social media firms who had nothing to work from, and the intern (**L**,
from the UK) whose Futur account outgrew his own — *"you are borrowing from my source code, how is it that
the Futur's account is growing faster than me?"* — and answers his own question: she was consistent and
knew the platform's rules; he wasn't and didn't. **He states plainly that he creates none of The Futur's
social content.**

The session closes on **sacrifice**, answered concretely when pushed (below), and on the gift.

## Key claims (2024-03-14)

- **Scarcity is a sequence, not a claim**: pre-announce → raise hands → visible staging area → fixed
  number, fixed date. Visibility of the other buyers is the active ingredient.
- **Don't expand capacity on sellout**; buy tickets back at 2× instead and take no margin.
- ★ **The line he won't cross**: *"this is where it gets into **manipulation marketing**, which I don't
  like… if you say I have two [slots] but then you take on 55 clients, **it's not going to work**."*
  Scarcity must be true. Pairs with — and constrains — the *"false scarcity"* language he uses in
  [[2024-03-10-yt-h1voCMAT9Qc]].
- **You only need two buyers** to bid something up; a large audience is not required. Told via the
  **$4M Secret Wars page** (Mike Zeck / John Beatty, first appearance of the Venom costume, badly drawn,
  asking price ~$80K) versus a beautiful **Todd McFarlane** page at $400K. *"**The story of that page was
  more important than the art**, the rendering, the quality of it."*
- ★★ **Pro Group and Brand Lab numbers**, self-reported and dated (see summary).
- **Simplify by subtraction.** More inclusions read as distractions and invite discounting: *"they're
  like, well I want this but I want that, therefore I want to pay less."*
- ★★ **Get the job done / be right / be popular** — pick one.
- ★ **Pick one master and follow them exactly.** *"Find one person you trust… and whatever that monkey
  tells you to do, you just do it."* The failure mode he names: *"before you can actually execute the plan
  that you're given, **you change the plan** — and you wonder why later it doesn't work."*
- **He applies this to himself, on camera.** Priestley told him to cap the group; *"next day I called my
  team and said let's do it — do it exactly the way we're instructed to do it, don't change it."* And on
  advice he has *not* yet taken (a mentor's *"cut to one product and one channel and you'll have a $10M
  company by year end"*): *"I'm still thinking, man, I'm processing this… **if I want to listen to this
  man's advice — which I'm not 100% [sure] I do just yet — then I either do it or I don't do it.**"*
- **The most coachable people execute without asking whether it will hurt** — which is why he is careful
  what he says to them. Ben Burns named as an example (told to fire his clients; asked only *"what do I
  say to them?"*).
- ★ **Anneli's story** (a friend, told with her presence acknowledged): three years of resistance before
  she posted vulnerably, then the support arrived. His framing of the delay: *"everybody has their own
  timeline… **it takes as long as it takes.**"* And his own sharp edge: *"imagine where you would have
  been if you had done this three years ago."*
- **Teaching**: one learning outcome → five → five again → irreducible skill; one question at a time;
  clear directions, many right answers.
- ★ **Name it to own it.**
- **Cite your sources — it compounds.** *"It's the way to honour the idea and it builds credibility, and
  you're building relationships with them."* Evidence: **Blair Enns** started out angry at him and is now
  a $1,000 Platinum sponsor of his book and sends him lightsabers.
- **Personal brand > company brand**, because nobody believes the corporate account is a person. And the
  corollary: a corporate brand borrowing your voice needs **source material** — write the book, record the
  video — *"you have to be **the first source of yourself.**"*
- **Reading method**: pick a topic, buy **five books by five different authors** (~$150 total), and after
  every chapter ask *what did I learn / what can I apply* — writing in the book. Two chapters with no
  answer → **stop reading**. *"Don't be like me, don't be a completionist."*
- ★ **Sacrifice, answered concretely** when a participant asks what it actually means: he stopped playing
  video games (Skyrim *"till 3 in the morning"*), stopped reading comics, sleeps less at **51**, and
  *"[doesn't] go to every family event or see my son at every little thing."* Contrasted with ~100 cousins
  who are *"attorneys, dentists and doctors"* with a tight family life and unachieved professional goals —
  *"**those are their goals. Hallelujah.**"* His own: *"I want to be so successful that if any family
  member needs my help, I can help them."*
- **The gift**, attributed loosely to Picasso and immediately flagged as borrowed: your purpose is to
  discover your gift; your mission is to give it away. His extension: *"some of us discover a gift and
  won't give it away; some of us know our gift, will give it away, but can't do it at any kind of scale —
  **that's where I'm operating out of.**"*
- **The gift is not what you think**: *"a combination of your weaknesses and your strengths and where
  those overlap"* — including physical attributes, voice, energy. **Lizzo** given as the example of
  self-acceptance as power.

  job done — not to be right, popular, or to be liked.**"*
- ★★ *"**I'm just not horrifically attached to anything but success.** That's it. **The only thing I love
  more than my own idea is success.**"*
- ★★ *"What is it that you're buying? **Where's the value?** And when you can identify that, **get rid of
  everything else.** It's that simple."*

  written nothing. I wanted somebody to speak like me when I've said nothing."*
- ★ *"As soon as you give it a unique name, **you own it.** … It's kind of stupid, but it works — and I
  just want to do what works."*

  it simply, maybe you're not a good practitioner of it** — you're making it too complicated."*
- ★ *"**You have to give up what you don't want to get what you want** — but people hold on to all this
  crap that they don't want."*
- ★ *"It's not what I can get from people today, but **what they want to give you tomorrow** — and that's
  the game I want to play."*
- ★ *"The longer you stay here, **the longer you will suffer.**"*
- *"This is the difficulty of anybody who asks a question: **are you willing to hear the answer** — or do
  you only want to hear the answer that aligns with what you already want to know?"*
- *"You're not that smart to change the direction before you do it."* And on himself: *"my wife's always
  wondering, why are you following the directions? … **I want to do it the way I'm instructed**, over and
  over again, and then eventually I'll spot a pattern."*
- On the $32,000: *"Kier — one time in my life I want to sic my attorney and tear somebody to shreds down
  to the bone and grind the bones down too. You telling me I can't do it?"* And the resolution: *"**she
  beat me like three times: first quit and lied, second got me [to] pay — and then got to haunt me in my
  dreams. I'm not going to give her number three.**"*
- The submarine analogy for hard calls: *"**who will you let die in order for everyone to live?**"*
- On respect and dress code, to Mo: *"When you go to a Japanese home, do you take off your shoes? … Respect
  the customs. **You adopt the language of the person you want to show respect to.**"*
- On the Pro Group endgame: *"I will not open it to more people. **I will then now pay people to leave** —
  people who annoy me first. I'll pay you more than what you paid. *Please leave.*"*
- On selling emotion: *"**we're primarily emotional decision makers and then we use logic to
  post-rationalise.**"* Worked example: $100K for a motion graphics video that cost under $15K to make,
  because *"the value to the CEO not to feel embarrassed, or to feel proud, is worth more than $100,000"*
  — and *"$100,000 to a company like that is nothing, it's a luncheon budget."*
- ★ *"Maybe production value isn't all that. **Maybe it was just about casting and setting up the
  argument.**"* (on the Dr. Squatch and Harmon Brothers / Sandwich Video long-form ads people watch
  voluntarily)
- On his own reach: *"some of my accounts have actually reached a pretty high state, and from that point
  now I'm able to get all kinds of crazy good deals… I just go home, I tell my wife, and **she wants to
  vomit.**"* Evidence given: a pay-what-you-want fireside chat where he expected zero and people paid $1,
  $5, $20, some $100.
- *"You know, here we are, 36 hours later, stupid video on the internet, and here we are having a
  relationship with each other that I think is genuine **because it comes from a genuine place — and I
  think people can smell that.**"*
- *"You can see those **marketer scumbags** out there doing their thing. You might buy their product…
  but you got no relationship with them, you owe them nothing."*
- *"I don't know about aura and energy, but… **I think there's something there. I'm a little lighter in my
  step.**"*
- *"That's all I got — **diagrams and some ideas** — and I will sell you that, I will give you that,
  because that will be my gift to you."*
