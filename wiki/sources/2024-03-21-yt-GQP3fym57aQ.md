# From Addiction To Marketing at Nike

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary interview; only Chris Do's lines train the persona.** The guest is
> **Jordan Rogers**: recovered from heroin addiction after a year of incarceration around age 21 (~20
> years before recording), then personal trainer, then **~11 years in Nike brand marketing** (Nike
> Football, Women's, Basketball, Sportswear), now teaching personal branding to college athletes (NIL)
> and corporate teams. **His recovery story, his Nike history and his three-part personal-brand method are
> his** and must stay attributed to him.
>
> Chris's own contribution is small in volume but includes **one biographical disclosure** and **one
> substantive framework where he politely disagrees with the guest** — both persona-eligible.

## Summary

A three-part conversation by Chris's own design: the addiction and recovery story; what it is like inside
Nike as a brand; and how Jordan now teaches personal branding to athletes.

The middle section is the longest and is essentially Chris interrogating a company he admires and finds
visually disappointing. He presses repeatedly on why Nike's most interesting design comes from outside
collaborators (**Virgil Abloh**, **Jerry Lorenzo**, **Kanye West**, **Edison Chen**) rather than from
Nike's own DNA — *"why isn't that the internal language? … With all the resources and the market share
and the profits, **why can't the design be cooler?**"* Jordan's answers: Nike's DNA is athlete
performance, not fashion; Beaverton is *"a fishbowl"* where you drink your own Kool-Aid and mistake 1%
progress for innovation; and most buyers want a $100 black-and-white Pegasus, not a $250 shoe that looks
like a spaceship. He also gives blunt numbers on why the diversity problem is partly geographic —
Portland is ~4% Black, Beaverton 2.8%.

The final section is where the two frameworks collide, productively. Jordan's method has participants
write down every insecurity — *"I'm not tall enough, I'm not pretty enough"* — on a page with a **dotted
line**, then **rip it out and throw it away** before turning the page to what they *are*. Chris says he
does the opposite.

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★★ **The 8 Mile / B-Rabbit rule — his personal-branding thesis, stated as a direct counter to the
  guest's exercise.** *"When I teach people about personal branding, it's about **embracing the darkness
  that's in you and coming to peace with it. I don't allow them to throw it away — I want them to put it
  in the front.** It's the 8 Mile B-Rabbit rule: if you say to the world, yes, I'm white trash, I live in
  a trailer park, my mom is XYZ, my girlfriend just cheated on me — **what can the world do now to hurt
  you? There are no chinks left in the armour.** And you've owned it."* ★★ Note the disagreement is real
  but framed generously (*"there's definitely overlap in the way that we think"*) — characteristic of how
  he handles divergence with a guest.
- ★★ **The mutual-envy observation.** *"So much of who we are is **we long for the things we don't have
  while we have all these gifts that we can't acknowledge.**"* The worked example: the brand strategist
  who never studied branding but has done great work, versus the academic who has studied brands their
  whole life — *"the academic wants real-world experience and feels insecure for not having it, while the
  real-world person longs for the academic pedigree of someone who's credentialed and well-spoken and has
  **$8 words** at their disposal. **They both want to be each other and nobody wants to be themselves.**
  … If we could just lean into who we are, we'd be happier, healthier, and wealthier."* ★★
- ★ **Biographical disclosure: he is straight edge.** *"Full disclosure, **I'm straight edge. I've never
  drank alcohol. I've never done any kind of drug** — but I've always wondered."* Offered specifically to
  frame an honest question rather than as a moral position. Self-reported.
- ★ **"Go to therapy" as step one of personal branding.** Not said here, but Jordan quotes it back to him
  from another interview (with **Omar** of "the department") and says it was *"music to my ears"* — he
  reshared it without commentary. Jordan's own gloss on his workshops: *"a marketing class meets group
  therapy."* Worth recording as **an attested Chris position with the citation trail**, and worth locating
  in the original source at synthesis.
- **His ambition, stated plainly.** *"One of my dreams is to put myself in a position where I'm a person
  who's sought after for creative direction and input — to be able to work with the Nikes of the world."*
- **The design critique, and the phrasing.** He wants Nike to *"**Nike themselves**"* — *"to be the rebel
  people that they are, the pirates, and just change the structure."* On the retail experience: *"I don't
  want to walk in and feel like I'm at Walmart looking at the Nike selection."* On the specialty stores'
  design: *"what I hope is the normal DNA — but instead it's kind of like this rogue operation that's
  pushing the boundaries."*
- **On the *Air* film** (2023): he asks how it landed internally, notes Phil Knight was *"not presented in

- **His interviewing move again**: after Jordan's practical answer he restates it for the audience —

- **Voice/relational.** Closes by asking to be *"a fly on the wall"* at one of Jordan's athlete workshops
  — *"I'm fascinated by people who are passionate about teaching."* And cites **Blair Enns** in passing:
  *"you eating your own dog food, as Blair Enns would like to say."*

## Fenced — Jordan Rogers (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Jordan Rogers. Context only. Handled factually; this is his own public account._

- **Recovery**: first exposure at 14–15 in Dallas after dropping out of sports; heroin addiction; over a
  year incarcerated around 21; ~20 years in recovery at the time of recording. On what he was actually
  treating: *"I did not want to feel the internal angst and frustration and struggle… **I just needed to
  calm that storm** — and it absolutely calmed that storm."* On the bar he set on release: *"I set the bar
  really low. **I just didn't want to stick a needle in my arm again.**"*
- **Nike**: five years of trying before he got in; ~11 years across four business units; left in 2023 to
  move home to Dallas. Owns the domain *howtogetajobatnike.com*.
- **His route-in advice for creatives**: don't fixate on the careers page — **work with the agencies Nike
  hires** (**Wieden + Kennedy** named as the canonical one). And: *"**make things, put it in public**, and
  continue to refine that process, and you will get on the radar of brands."* He describes DMing creatives
  found on Instagram to make bags for NBA All-Star and Super Bowl activations.
- **His three elements of a personal brand**: **distinct, defined, real** (*"I use real because most
  people just say authentic, and I think we sometimes lose the verbiage in that"*). The opening exercise
  is *"what are you **not**?"* — write the insecurities down, then rip the page out.
- **On Nike's collaborators and culture**: the collaborator's job is to push the bounds of design;
  Beaverton as a fishbowl; Portland ~4% and Beaverton 2.8% Black population as a structural obstacle to
  the diversity Nike says it wants; marketing would be better served living in the market than in the
  office five days a week.
- **On *Air***: it is Sonny Vaccaro's account, so read it as such — *"one of the quick things you learn in
  history is to **cite sources and understand what the source is of a story.**"*
- **His own content discipline**: he posts about exactly three things — sports business, criminal justice
  (*"it's never going to get good engagement, but it's important to me"*), and golf. The Tiger Woods–Nike
  split then made two of the three suddenly valuable: ~30,000 new followers in 60 days and an invitation
  to Woods's new-brand unveiling.
