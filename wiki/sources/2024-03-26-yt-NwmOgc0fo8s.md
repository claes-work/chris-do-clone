# 6 Steps To Increase Lead Generation GUARANTEED (Free Framework Included)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary.** A short workshop excerpt, teaching to a room. Two books are credited
> in-line: ***101 Things I Learned in Advertising School*** (the expanded customer journey) and ***How to
> Become a Rainmaker*** (the dollarised-value pitch). The prompt list is adapted from **Rob Fitzpatrick's
> *The Workshop Survival Guide***, which he names.

## Summary

A compact, practical piece: how to build one lead-generation tool that converts a cold lead into a warm
one. He is explicit that this is **not** an attempt to close a sale in one move.

The frame is the customer journey, expanded past the usual funnel: **awareness → consideration →
interest → preference → purchase**. He colour-codes purchase separately and lowers the bar deliberately:
*"I would consider it a win if you just got through stage one — that now they're aware of who the heck
you are, and you give them a strong and compelling reason to consider you the next time they have a
problem like the one that you solve."* The reason the stages must connect is the video's best line:
awareness without consideration is just noise (below).

Then the constraint most of his audience ignores: **time-to-purchase scales with price relative to the
buyer.** Impulse buys sit at the supermarket register — *"when you think you've been a really good shopper
and you haven't purchased any junk food, **they know you're vulnerable**"* — and creative services are the
opposite of an impulse buy. Hence: *"let's not try to go out and create one piece of marketing that's
going to get somebody to spend $10,000 with you tomorrow."*

The body is a prompt list to design the tool against, and one specific technique — **dollarising the
offer** — which is the most immediately usable thing in the video.

## Key claims (2024-03-26)

- **The expanded journey**: awareness → consideration → interest → preference → purchase. Design content
  knowing which transition it is responsible for.
- ★ **Awareness alone is worthless** without consideration.
- **Price relative to the buyer sets the number of touchpoints.** High-consideration purchases need trust
  built over time and consistency; one asset will not do it.
- **The design prompts** (adapted from Fitzpatrick): Who is your prospective client? What problem are
  they trying to solve, and why should they care? **What level of awareness do they have about the real
  problem?** Where are they most likely to find out about this solution? How will this benefit them —
  and how will it make them **feel**? What makes your solution unique?
- ★ **Build where they already are.** *"Creating content or a sales funnel out of YouTube is not going to
  help you if they don't go to YouTube."*
- ★ **If you can't articulate your difference, you're a "me too" brand.** Stated as a hard gate: *"if you
  can't articulate this you're going to have a big problem, because you're just like everybody else."*
- ★★ **Dollarise the value** (from *How to Become a Rainmaker*): calculate the client's saving or earning
  in their own currency and lead with the number. His worked script: *"how would you like to **save 14
  cents on every product you make, for an annual saving of $267,000** — would you be interested in talking
  to me about that?"* The point: *"you've already put it in terms the client cares about."* Note this
  applies even when your product is **more expensive** than the incumbent, if it lasts ten times as long.
- **Social proof lowers perceived risk**: testimonials, reviews, and *"a conversational atmosphere with
  people that look and talk like you."*
- **Worldview alignment**: *"when we're able to align what it is that we think and feel to what they think
  and feel, it's a win."*
- **Urgency triggers buying, and urgency is built on scarcity** — limited units, closing windows. (Stated
  neutrally here; the honesty constraint on manufactured scarcity is in

  your hair on fire, you'll get awareness — but not consideration.**"*

  $267,000? Would you be interested in talking to me about that? **You've already put it in terms the
  client cares about.**"*
- ★ *"I saw this funny t-shirt and I remembered it: **'not looking for Mr Right, just looking for Mr Right
  Now.'** If you make your clients [and] customers feel like that — forget about it."*
- ★ *"**I want you to know what my pain points are** and I'm going to trust you, I'm going to give you my
  money, I'm going to pay you a premium over your nearest competitor, I'm going to go out of my way, I'm

  have that deep connection with you.**"*

  a Snickers bar right there."*
