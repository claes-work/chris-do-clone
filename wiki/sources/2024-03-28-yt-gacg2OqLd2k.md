# How To Deal With Imposter Syndrome

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary**, teaching to a room (one participant, **Tyler**, is quoted back).
> Fully persona-eligible. Credited in-line: **Meryl Streep**, **Marshall Rosenberg** (*Nonviolent
> Communication*), and the adage *"comparison is the thief of joy."*

## Summary

His most complete treatment of imposter syndrome, and unusually personal: *"for a long part of my life I
have suffered from imposter syndrome, so **I know this emotion very well**, and I've developed some
mental tricks to get over it."*

The diagnosis: creative people are creative *because* they are in touch with their emotions and
vulnerable — *"that's where their power comes from"* — and that same vulnerability is what opens the door
to the fear of being found out. He cites **Meryl Streep**, *"the most nominated actress of all time"*,
saying she fears people will get bored of her and realise she has no talent.

The mechanism he names is a **gap between the true self and the created self**, widened by ego: *"we tell
ourselves the story that we're supposed to be somewhere other than where we are, and it starts to push
the two images of ourselves apart."* His grounding move is deflating and specific: *"I don't have to tell
you guys I'm a five-foot Asian guy, because I am. I'm not [going] to tell you I don't have any hair,
because I don't. But in my mind I'm like, well, I'm six foot two, I'm very debonair."* The cost is
resource, not feeling: maintaining an image that doesn't exist consumes *"a lot of your energy, a lot of
your mental space and your creative energy."*

The most transferable idea is borrowed from **Marshall Rosenberg**: the language you use about your own
work is either observational or **violent**. *"This is my work"* is an observation. *"This is my really
good, complete work"* imposes a judgement that then reflects back on you. His reframe: treat the work as
**a timestamp** — *"as to where I'm at today in my life. Am I learning? **How could that be an imposter
thing that you have to fight with?**"*

Then his own breakthrough in public speaking, the **inverted lens**, and the closing instruction on
comparison.

## Key claims (2024-03-28)

- **Imposter syndrome defined, in his words**: *"I fear that people will, after some time, discover I know
  nothing, I provide no value, and I'm talentless."* A lifelong fear of being discovered as not as good as
  others credit you with being.
- **Creativity and the vulnerability are the same faculty** — which is why creative people are especially
  prone to it.
- **The true self vs. the created self.** Ego widens the gap; the disassociated self is expensive to
  maintain.
- ★ **Violent vs. observational language about your own work** (Rosenberg). Strip the judgement and the
  imposter has nothing to attack. Treat the work as a dated timestamp of where you are.
- ★★ **The public-speaking breakthrough: "I'm here for the other person."** Letting go of the projected
  image is what made the stage survivable.
- ★ **The inverted lens.** *"If I focus the lens on myself, everything I look at magnifies — my skin, the
  pores on my nose, the freckle I have. But if I invert that lens and I'm just neutrally observing myself
  in this community, in this audience, then it becomes a lot easier for me to deal with."*
- **The learner posture as a defence**: *"I don't know. I'm here to learn."* Stay grounded and the
  imagined version of yourself — *"that nobody sees anyways"* — stops mattering.
- ★ **Remember the core skill.** You are competent somewhere, and the competence travels; when the voice
  says you don't belong, *"go back to that core [and] have a conversation with yourself, because you
  bring a lot to the conversation."*
- ★ **Taste and skill oscillate.** Freshman: taste exceeds skill. Foundations arrive and skill overtakes
  taste. Then exposure to the next tier resets it — *"oh, I suck again."* Permanent, cyclical, and not
  evidence of fraudulence.
- ★★ **Comparison: look backwards, not forwards.** People compare themselves to a future imagined self or
  to others at their peak — never to those people's year one. Use your own past self as the marker.

## Verbatim quotes

- ★ *"Meryl Streep said… **one day I fear that people are gonna just get really bored of me and realise I
  have no talent.** Meryl Streep. The most nominated actress of all time."*
- ★ On grounding: *"Almost everybody in this room already sees your true self. **I don't have to tell you
  I'm a five-foot Asian guy, because I am. I'm not to tell you I don't have any hair, because I don't.
  But in my mind I'm like — well, I'm six foot two, I'm very debonair.** No. I just don't think that of
  myself."*
- ★★ *"One of my biggest breakthroughs in public speaking was just to say to myself: **I'm here for the
  other person.** I'm not here to prove to me or to you that I'm an incredible speaker, that I'm the
  smartest person, that I've got the best slides or the coolest transitions. **Once I let go of that
  projected image of who I thought I wanted to be, it became a lot easier for me to get on stage and look
  at hundreds of people and not feel like I want to cry and run off stage** — because those are very much
  the true feelings."*
- ★ *"This is my work — that's a pretty objective statement. *This is my really good complete work* — well
  now we've gone on the other side and **we're using violent language**, because we're imposing our
  opinion on this work and it reflects on us."*
- ★ *"If we can just say **this is my work, and it's a timestamp** as to where I'm at today in my life —
  am I learning? — how could that be an imposter thing that you have to fight with?"*
- ★ *"There are times when I've gone to certain parties and functions and I just felt like — **look at all
  these people, they've done all these things, and who the hell invited me? Somebody made a mistake. Send
  me back home right now.**"*
- ★★ *"**Everybody sucked at one point. Everybody was a beginner at one point.** … Very few people will
  say, yeah, I'd like to be like Gary Vaynerchuk **when he was year one** — you watch those videos, like,
  oh, that's terrible. Or Marques Brownlee — that kid was rough."*
- ★★ *"**When you compare, look backwards, not forwards**, because that could be debilitating. I don't
  want that for you."*
- *"If you could swim from shore to a mile marker, that's fantastic. And then when you can swim to the
  second mile marker, that's even more amazing. **Just look how far you've come from shore.**"*
- *"Those negative emotions will kind of **kill your creativity.**"*
- On the learner mindset: *"maybe the work I do is good or not good — **I don't really care. I'm only
  interested in growth.**"*
