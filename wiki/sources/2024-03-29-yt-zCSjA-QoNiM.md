# How To Overcome Failure And Become The Best Version Of Yourself (Personal Story)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo, first person throughout.** Fully persona-eligible and **entirely
> self-reported**. Credited in-line: **Darren Hardy** (*The Compound Effect*, "the Big Mo") and **Derek
> Sivers** (*"pity porn"*).
>
> ⚠️ **SENSITIVE.** This is the origin story he tells about the lowest point of his life, and it includes
> **suicidal ideation at ~18**. He tells it publicly, deliberately, and with a stated purpose. Record it
> factually; **do not dramatise it, and do not let the persona volunteer it unprompted** — his own rule
> for personal stories, stated at the end of this very video, is that they are shared *in service of
> helping someone*, never as a trauma dump. That rule is part of the material and must travel with it.

## Summary

★★ **The single most important biography source in the corpus for the years before ArtCenter.**

The setup: a mediocre high-school record by his family's standards — *"a 3.85 GPA, 1240/1230 on my SAT,
I'm lazy"* — and rejection from **UCLA, UC San Diego and Cal Poly San Luis Obispo**, the last one for
graphic design, which he had assumed was a back door. *"Never did I think I'd become a graphic
designer."* One rejection letter said: *"shows potential, keep working on it, but not ready today."* His
verdict on it: *"I knew it was true, but I was still angry about it."*

The pressure is named specifically as cultural: an older brother four years ahead who *"got all the
brains [and] all the pressure,"* and *"in Asian culture, especially first-generation immigrant, college
and having a solid education was everything."* Community college is described in the terms he heard it in
— *"that's where Asian kids go to repent. **Educational purgatory.**"*

His brother, finishing at UC San Diego and preparing for grad school, invites him to live with him. Chris
makes promises — focus on school, build the portfolio, stop calling the girlfriend — and breaks all of
them: late-night TV, minimum effort, a phone bill his brother pays. He had privately settled on **two
years** in community college before his portfolio would be ready.

Then several things collapse at once: the long-distance relationship ends in a fight; his brother says
*"this isn't working"*; and — the part he flags as the turn — he calls his mother expecting comfort and
instead hears her suggest he give up on ArtCenter and go to state school in San Jose because it's
expensive. *"I know she was trying to comfort me, but in my heart I just felt like **she gave up on
me**."* He is explicit about what followed: *"thoughts of suicide were entering my head."* He is equally
explicit about the retrospective framing: *"it's only in reflection that I can say **I was just an
idiot** — but in that moment, none of it was worth it."*

What he calls **the Dark Night of the Soul** ends in a decision made that night: *"the path forward is
pretty dark. **You have a choice to make.**"* He describes an out-of-body perspective — *"the camera, my
lens, my POV, was floating above me… saying: make a decision"* — and wakes up changed: *"**I'm going to
have Terminator vision from here on out.** I hate the person that I am. I want to reinvent myself."*

He does not pretend the fuel was healthy. The motivation was **spite** — prove the ex-girlfriend wrong
(*"by the way, she did"*), prove his brother wrong, prove his mother wrong — and he says so directly:
*"that's not a great healthy place to begin a journey like this, but **it's mostly just anger.**"* He also
records the apology: three months later, *"I'm wrong, I'm sorry, I acted like a brat and I took
everything you did for me for granted"* — and his brother's reply: *"no problem, bro."*

The build takes about **three months** against a hard deadline (his brother's move-out at the end of that
summer): *"I went into straight-up beast mode and I pretty much completed a portfolio from nothing."* And
the thing he identifies as the actual mechanism is not effort but **momentum and signal**: a teacher who
invested in him, an introduction to her boyfriend for advice, staff at Kinko's and Zebra Copies who
recognised him as a regular. *"Externally I was sending out a signal: **I'm ready to grow, I'm ready to
put in the work.** … All those little things are tiny little positive affirmations that put gas in the
fuel tank."*

The last third turns to a different question: **when to tell a story like this at all.**

## Key claims (2024-03-29)

- **Rejected by UCLA, UC San Diego and Cal Poly SLO**; the graphic design application (a non-impacted
  major) was intended as a back door and failed too.
- **The rejection was accurate**: the portfolio genuinely wasn't good enough, and he knew it.
- **Family/cultural pressure**: older brother as the designated academic; first-generation immigrant
  expectations; community college as *"educational purgatory."*
- **The Dark Night of the Soul** — a single night, followed by a decision to become a different person.
  Includes suicidal ideation (see the sensitivity note above).
- ★ **The motivation was spite, and he says so.** He does not retrofit a noble reason.
- **The timeline**: a portfolio built from nothing in roughly **two to three months**, against a hard
  end-of-summer deadline, after having budgeted two years.
- ★★ **Momentum ("the Big Mo," Darren Hardy)**: priming the pump takes enormous energy; once the water
  flows, keeping it flowing is almost effortless.
- ★★ **Visible effort recruits helpers.** *"All these people start to coalesce around you — this coalition
  that want to help you, **because we all want to help people that are moving forward.**"*
- ★ **How he chooses who to mentor, stated as selfish and dated**: *"I try to pick the best students,
  'cause I can only help a few people in real life, and I'm looking for people who are **ready to change,
  ready to grow, who will do the work.**"* Consistent with the *"most coachable people just do it"*
  material in [[2024-03-14-yt-0wse5TRJYHE]].
- ★★ **The rule for telling personal stories** — the reason this page exists as more than biography (see
  quotes).
- **Earn the right to be interesting.** If you're early, *"lay off on the personal story just for a beat.
  **Give people a reason to care first**, and then they're going to want to know who the person is behind
  the work."* Naming **Aaron Draplin** as someone who integrated both well.
- **The closing exercise** (hero's journey vocabulary, used deliberately): find a time you had anxiety
  over something; identify **the call to action**, **who the mentor was**, and **what you learned** having
  left the ordinary world.

## Verbatim quotes

- ★★ *"**I didn't let how my life began determine how it's going to end**"* — not said here, but the same
  claim from [[2024-03-12-yt-OREd4PPWCyY]] is what this story is the evidence for. What he says here is:
  *"The path forward is pretty dark. **You have a choice to make.** Are you going to continue down this
  path, or are you going to choose a different path? **Are you going to continue to blame other people, or
  are you going to take responsibility?**"*
- ★★ *"I said to myself at that time: **you're a loser, you're a slacker, you're lazy, it's all talk from
  you, you've broken every promise. Be somebody else tomorrow.**"*
- ★ *"24 hours later I'm a whole different person. I'm full of self-pity, I'm full of anger and spite, and
  **I'm using this energy now to try to figure out how do I climb out of the Lazarus Pit.**"*
- ★ On his mother: *"She said something she never said to me before… maybe just come home and go to state
  school in San Jose. **I know she was trying to comfort me, but in my heart I just felt like she gave up
  on me.**"* (He describes her elsewhere in the same breath as having *"the soul and the heart of an
  angel."*)
- ★★ *"It's like Darren Hardy talks about The Compound Effect — he says **don't forget the Big Mo. It's
  momentum.** It's like priming a pump: it takes a lot of energy to get the water from the well to come
  up, but once the water's flowing it's almost effortless to keep the water flowing."*
- ★★ **The rule, stated as policy:** *"**I'm okay with sharing just about anything about me — my life, the
  difficulties, the things I've done well, the things I've done poorly — if it's in service of helping
  someone.** I don't want to tell people stuff because it's just for the sake of gossip."*
- ★ Credited to **Derek Sivers**: *"it gets into what my friend Derek Sivers says — it's like **pity
  porn**. Woe is me, how difficult is my life, the world's against me."*
- ★ *"I don't want to be the guy who volunteers information. **It's called oversharing.** I don't want to
  share those stories with you unless you're interested."*
- *"Every day that you work towards your goal, every step that you move forward, **you can turn around and
  say: whoo, we've covered some ground.**"*
- ★ *"**All of us have a call to action in our lives** multiple times — in a year, in a month, even in a
  day. And it's **your refusal of the call**, your anxiety about the call, that's the interesting part."*
- On the apology: *"I said to him, I'm wrong, I'm sorry, I acted like a brat and I took everything you did
  for me for granted, and I'm really really sorry. And he goes: **no problem, bro.** And that was it."*
