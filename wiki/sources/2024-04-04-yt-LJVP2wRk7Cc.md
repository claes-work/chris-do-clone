## Summary

The source interview behind the scarcity material Chris then taught at the Business Boot Camp — see the
cross-link note below, which matters for dating.

Priestley's argument in one line: *"**get rid of this idea of fairness** — that if you're doing a good job
you get paid really well. It's not. **If you're able to create demand and supply tension, you get paid
really well.**" His evidence is margin structure: airlines 3–7%, supermarkets 3–5%, a Rolex 75%. The
mechanism is **marketing for signals, not sales** — Glastonbury's 700,000 registrations for 130,000
tickets, sold in a 30-minute window, with the bands unannounced. Signalling requires only 10–20%
certainty from the buyer; buying requires ~90%. So you collect far more signals than you need, then reveal
the imbalance (**transparency**) to create market forces.

The rest is the how: **waiting lists** (future capacity) and **registration-of-interest lists** (current
capacity, gated); then the advanced play, **assessment-based selling** — a 30–40 question scorecard, sold
*instead* of the solution, on the doctor's logic that nobody casts an arm before an X-ray. The best-
performing formats are **"Are you ready to ___?"** and **"Which type of ___ are you?"** He gives a live
ScoreApp demo building a restaurant-branding quiz, landing page, results logic, social launch copy and an
AI-written follow-up email in roughly five minutes.

Chris's role is interviewer, translator-for-the-audience, and — twice — someone who invents something on
the spot.

## Fenced — Daniel Priestley (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Daniel Priestley. Context only._

- ★ **The unfairness thesis**: doing a good job does not set your price; demand/supply tension does.
  Airlines 3–7% margin, supermarkets 3–5%, Rolex 75%. Adam Smith's water-and-diamonds paradox as the
  first principle.
- ★ **"Everything is downstream from lead generation"** — his first mentor's mantra (John; Priestley was
  employee number three, and watched the company go from a kitchen table to 60 people). The Kim Kardashian
  thought experiment as the proof.
- ★ **Signals, not sales.** Glastonbury: 700,000 registrations, 130,000 tickets, a 30-minute annual window,
  bands unannounced. Signalling needs 10–20% certainty; buying needs ~90%.
- **Transparency creates the tension**: publish the imbalance (capacity 20/year, 200 on the list), or make
  it visible via a group, an event, or a queue.
- ★ **The buyer pyramid**: value-perception is hierarchical; process people one at a time and you default
  to the base of the pyramid and its price. Assemble the pyramid with data first, then talk to the top.
- **Waiting list** (future capacity) vs. **registration of interest** (current capacity, gated). The
  speaker example: replacing "book me / download my rate card" with a registration form asking attendance,
  paid/free, keynote vs. workshop, sponsorship opportunities.
- ★ **Assessment-based selling**: refuse to discuss the solution until an assessment is done. The doctor
  and the X-ray. 30–40 questions, categories, a results page keyed to the answers.
- ★ **The influencer story**: he won a large contract against other applicants purely by leading with his
  **24 Assets** 48-question assessment — *"everyone else was just telling us what an amazing coach they
  are… **you were the one who told us about the problems that we didn't even know we had.**"*
- **Best-performing formats**: **readiness** ("are you ready to ___?") and **type** ("which type of ___
  are you?"). His own Key Person of Influence scorecard has run seven years and ~**120,000** completions.
  *"People want that mirror."*
- **Robot Mascot** (UK) and its *Pitch Ready* scorecard given as the relatable, non-glamorous case study.
- ★ **The expert's blind spot**: *"they've been in their business so long that **they can't empathise with
  people who don't know what it's like to not know**… even when you think you're dumbing it down, you
  don't dumb it down anywhere near enough."*
- **Anatomy**: landing page (with testimonials *for the assessment*, not the service) → questionnaire with
  logic → results page. Built in ScoreApp; AI wizard drafts ~1,200 words, then enrichment and AI-written
  follow-up emails keyed to each respondent's answers.
- **The closing discipline**: signal limited capacity honestly and be willing to say no — *"in two-thirds
  of the cases the answer is no… **the other thing that makes that really believable is if it's true.**"*
