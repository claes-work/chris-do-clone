# How To Build An Online Community (Complete Guide w/ Tom Ross)

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary masterclass; only Chris Do's lines train the persona.** The guest is
> **Tom Ross** (CEO of **Design Cuts**, ~1M members; founder of **Learn Community**), on his **third**
> appearance. He explicitly frames the session as a free condensation of his own 9-hour, 12-module paid
> community course — *"how can I take all of that material and condense it down to less than one hour and
> give it away for free?"* **The eleven-step method is his.**
>
> Chris's role is respondent and stress-tester. His contributions are short but include **two dated
> operational facts about the Pro Group** and one flat statement of position on discounting.

## Summary

Tom Ross's step-by-step: choose a topic at the intersection of passion, credibility and opportunity (he
and Chris agree this is **ikigai** by another name — *"ikigai sounds kind of nicer and fancier than
p-p-o, however you say this; **it's a terrible acronym**"*); craft a one-sentence value proposition
(*"[name] helps [this person] achieve [this outcome]"*) that survives **the podcast test** — can you say
it verbatim every time you're interviewed?; then the four kinds of value a community offers — **help,
action, learning, connection**; then launch mechanics, pricing, the landing page (*"not needed at
launch"* — he ran without one for six months), and ongoing growth.

His warnings are the useful part: community is not social media (*"it is not like social media, all eyes
on you"* — you are a **facilitator**, not the dominant voice); **don't offer lifetime memberships**; you
can't be hands-off; and *"members often won't actually do what you want."*

Chris's answer to that last point is the passage worth keeping — he confirms it from the Pro Group and
describes what The Futur has actually tried.

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★ **The honest Pro Group answer** — dated operational reality, freely admitted. *"Every point you've
  outlined is 100% true. **They don't do what you want. They're like cats in a room** — sometimes they
  behave and sometimes they don't. We introduce new learning paths, new tools and resources, **we have an
  AI agent to help them** — and they just don't do it."* His conclusion: *"you can put the best intentions
  forward, but you have to constantly — I don't know if this is the right word — **train or encourage
  people to use the things you've built for them.** You kind of have to nudge them along the way."*
  ★ Note the **AI agent in the Pro Group** as a dated product fact (April 2024).
- ★ **The Pro Group's value proposition, said out loud on the spot**: *"**The Futur Pro Group helps
  creative entrepreneurs scale their business.**"* Ross's approval identifies why it works: the audience
  self-identifies, and it names both the identity and the goal.
- ★★ **"I abhor discounts."** Asked whether scarcity beats discounting for a launch: *"Yeah, I think
  that's the preferred way to go. You want to say **this group is valuable — and the reason it's valuable
  is because it's going to be very intimate** and we're going to spend time working with you and learning
  from you. So I prefer doing that versus giving a discount. **I abhor discounts, by the way.**"* ★★ The
  flattest statement of this position in the corpus, and it dates to eighteen days before he demonstrates
  the alternative in [[2024-04-27-yt-MC3lr_BT1Wg]].
- **The diagnosis he supplies for bad value propositions**: *"it's just a lot of **word salad**."*
- **On formulas**: he pushes back gently on Ross's growth equation — *"interesting formula. I think in
  concept a little bit harder to actually do as a formula, but they're good general areas to focus on."*
  Characteristic: accepts the direction, declines the false precision.

## Fenced — Tom Ross (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Tom Ross. Context only._

- ★ **What communities are for**: serving people at scale; a **recurring-revenue** model against the
  feast-and-famine of project work; and *"members helping members… **while you sleep**."*
- ★ **The warnings**: you must be a **facilitator**, not the star; **never sell lifetime memberships**;
  community *"doesn't sleep"*; and members are autonomous — *"they're not always going to behave like
  robots."*
- **Any niche works**: he cites communities for **face painting** and for **arborists** (*"I learned, me
  — tree surgeons"*).
- ★ **The podcast test** for a value proposition: if you can't repeat it verbatim every time, it isn't
  one. His own: *"Learn Community helps you to launch and grow a successful online community."*
- **Four types of value**: help, action, learning, connection.
- ★ **Scarcity over discounts at launch**: *"increasingly I'm going off the idea of discounts… **it's much
  better to say we're only going to let in 20 members for this first cohort** — and people fight over those
  20 slots, and there's no discount required."*
- **Price-lock as retention**: tell members the price will rise; those locked in feel good and churn less.
  His own example — a studio consulting package whose price rose ~300% in 18 months: *"now I look at that
  price and I'm like, man, I got a steal."*
- **Landing page anatomy**: qualification → value → features/benefits → CTA → objections/FAQ → second CTA,
  with case studies woven throughout. And the permission: you don't need one at launch (he had none for
  six months — a waitlist and a payment page sufficed).
- **His growth model**: community value × market size × positioning fit × marketing effort/skill — with
  the observation that people over-invest in the first term (*"I need to stuff more things in there"*)
  when the real weakness is usually marketing or validation.
