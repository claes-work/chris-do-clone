## Summary

Enns's sequence: **select a focus → articulate it as a claim of expertise → then rapidly add proof.**
Chris asks the obvious objection — *"shouldn't you build those first and then make the claim?"* — which
is the pivot the whole segment turns on. Enns says no: make the claim **before you're comfortable making
it**, because *"the act of making the claim will hurry you up, will sharpen your focus to build the
missing skills, capabilities and processes like nothing else."* He admits the mechanism is pride: *"I'll
put a brave claim out there… and then I will run like hell to go get what's missing to be able to support
that claim."*

Chris's second contribution is to flag the manifesto's most confrontational passage as deliberate —
*"that almost sounds like fighting words, and I love it, because it's like: are you in or are you out?"*
The passage: average people avoid the difficult decision, and **most people will change their desires,
even their values, before they will change their behaviour** — so the question you must put to a client,
or yourself, is *"**are you most people?**"*

The back half is the **ditch-digging parable**: dig one ditch and you learn nothing; dig fifty and you
develop a diagnostic method, a tool selection, a shape, a timeline. Then *"let's draw this out"* — because
a **codified methodology**, shown as a visual to a nervous late-stage prospect, does specific work:
*"little variability in process equals little variability in outcome."* Enns is precise about when it
matters — **late** in the buying cycle, to reassure; **early**, what matters is the client seeing a
beautiful future, which the portfolio does.

He closes on positioning: *"sometimes it's better to be different than it is to be better"* — and on
playing to win, explicitly picking up a line of Chris's from earlier in the conversation.

## Fenced — Blair Enns (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Blair Enns, *The Win Without Pitching Manifesto*, seventh proclamation. Context only._

- ★ **Make the claim before you're ready.** *"Make the claim before you are perfectly comfortable making
  it. Your positioning… can be a little bit aspirational, and recognise that **the act of making the claim
  will hurry you up.**"*
- ★★ **The line Chris flags as fighting words**: *"average human beings avoid the difficult decision, and
  **most people will change their desires — even their values — before they will change their
  behaviour.**"* And the diagnostic: *"**most people will live with the undesirable circumstances rather
  than do the undesirable work.**"* Illustrated with the 90–95% who won't fix a fixable ailment with
  sleep, diet or exercise.
- ★ **The ditch-digging parable**: fifty ditches produce a codified methodology — ground diagnosis, tool
  selection, ditch shape, timeline — that a once-a-year digger will never have.
- ★ **Codified methodology as late-stage reassurance**: draw it, walk the prospect through it, and let
  them infer *"little variability in process equals little variability in outcome"* → low likelihood of
  screwing up. *"It's not important early on. **What's important early on is your ability to get the
  client to see a beautiful future** — and you can do that through your portfolio."*
- ★ **"Sometimes it's better to be different than it is to be better"** — or more precisely, *"it's easier
  or more powerful to show up differently than it is to try to claim to be something better."* How you
  behave in the sale is part of backing up the claim of difference.
- **Playing to win**: have an advantage, outwork, or **change the rules in your favour** — anything but
  competing on 1-in-N odds.
