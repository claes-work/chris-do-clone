# How To Build A $1,000,000 Personal Brand (Detailed Breakdown)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo, first person throughout.** Fully persona-eligible. Credited in-line:
> **Pixar's 22 rules of storytelling**, **Alan Dib** (*The 1-Page Marketing Plan*), **Brian Tracy** (*The
> Psychology of Selling*), **Joseph Campbell**, **Frank Herbert** (*Dune*), and **Simon Sinek** as an
> example of a credo. Third parties analysed (Godin, Vaynerchuk, Kanye West, Taylor Swift, Trump, Andrew
> Tate) are **subjects of his analysis**, not sources — their views are not his.

## Summary

★★ **The definitive statement of his personal-brand framework** — the video the rest of the 2024 personal-
branding material refers back to. He builds it by testing famous people against his own criteria rather
than asserting it.

**The test.** Is fame a component? He runs **Seth Godin** through it: 22+ books, a daily blog post for
over a decade, lectured on the world's biggest stages, a past guest on this channel, and someone Chris
openly admires. Then: is he married? What is his spouse's name? Children? Pets? Hobbies outside
marketing? *"It becomes really opaque."* Verdict: famous, respected, sought-after — but by Chris's
definition, arguably **not a strong personal brand**. Then **Gary Vaynerchuk**, who passes, and the
proof-test that separates them: **does association with you make an ordinary thing more valuable?** A
three-pack of white tees costs about the price of materials; when Gary puts his name on a $4–5 bottle of
wine and sells it for $55, *"the premium he adds is that $50 difference of intangible value."*

**Kanye West** is analysed as a case, explicitly bracketing the content: strong emotional bond (*"every
time he does something, we all care"*), maximally strong point of view — and the cost, which Chris states
factually: *"I think he's lost over a billion dollars in sponsorships and brand deals because of the
things he said about Jewish people. **It is seen as antisemitic and so that's very very problematic.**"*
The lesson he draws is about the mechanism, not an endorsement: a strong POV makes friends *and* enemies.
**Taylor Swift** is the positive case — mastery of craft, the re-recordings as a *"genius business
move,"* and the thing he singles out: *"she still gets down on one knee and looks you in the eye and says
thanks for being here."*

**Three traits of an attractive character**: high **self-awareness** paired with **self-acceptance**;
**self-confidence** (redefined, below); and **vulnerability/transparency** — the highs *and* the lows.
Anchored to **Pixar's rule #1**: people admire characters for their struggles and failures more than for
their successes. Hence Superman is unrelatable and Bruce Wayne and Peter Parker are not.

**Four components of a personal brand**: (1) **origin story** — cultural currency, an inciting incident,
crossing the threshold, and ongoing relapse/resurrection; (2) **community and culture** — mentors,
coaches, sidekicks, tribe; (3) **defining attributes** — credo/catchphrase, duality of strengths and
vulnerabilities, and a recognisable uniform, lexicon and *"Sonic branding"*; (4) ★ **the enemy**.

## Key claims (2024-04-16)

- ★★ **Fame is not a personal brand.** The test is an *irrational emotional connection* strong enough that
  people go out of their way — buy the book, queue for the film, attend the workshop.
- ★★ **The premium test**: a personal brand lets you take an ordinary thing and make it command a premium
  by association. Many famous actors fail this — *"they can't sell a vodka line… because that's not how we
  see them."*
- ★ **You need a strong point of view**, and a filter: information passed through you should come out
  sounding like *"a Vaynerism"* or a Godin-ism. *"We can't say things the way that other people say them
  and expect anyone to believe we have a personal brand."*
- ★ **Self-confidence redefined**: *"self-confidence is **a belief in your skills to solve a problem**…
  It's not about having a high opinion of yourself or being arrogant or treating people poorly. It's not
  about that at all."*
- ★★ **Self-conscious ≠ self-aware.** Self-conscious is looking at yourself through your own lens (*"am I
  being too loud, am I beautiful, am I ugly"*); self-aware is perceiving yourself **through other people's
  eyes**. Low self-awareness is talking on while the room falls asleep. Illustrated with a former
  cinematographer who would walk in front of a crowd to take a shot and feel no heat at all, and with an
  explicit, non-pejorative note that this is harder if you are neurodivergent — *"what comes natural to
  people may just require a little bit more effort from you."*
- ★ **Self-acceptance is the harder half.** *"The problem with some celebrities is they have
  self-awareness but they don't have self-acceptance… so they're building a barrier, that wall that keeps
  us away from them."*
- **The wall is the variable.** *"Think about the wall that exists between you, your content, your
  beliefs, your values, your ideas, your message, your story — and your audience. **How much you will let
  them in on that stuff will determine that connection.**"*
- **Vulnerability is not age-dependent.** Some teenagers have it; some 50- and 60-year-olds are *"still
  wandering the Earth not knowing what they stand for."*
- **The journaling prescription**: catalogue thoughts and experiences, then re-read two or three weeks
  later and ask whether it was true — *"is this how other people saw you, or is this you living in your
  own head?"* Tied to **Brian Tracy**'s **self-concept**.
- ★★ **The enemy** (via Alan Dib): name what you are against; *"anger as well as love are two powerful
  emotional drivers to building your tribe."* Examples analysed, not endorsed: **Andrew Tate**'s "the
  Matrix", **Donald Trump**'s "lamestream media" — *"he doesn't have to tell you what he believes in, he
  just needs to tell you that we hate these things together."*
- ★★ **His own enemy, corrected on the record**: *"people ask me all the time, 'Chris, you're
  anti-education.' I said **no — I'm anti-crippling student debt. I'm all for education.**"* The full
  charge: cost, *"jobs that may or may not exist,"* and *"structures that don't account for individual
  learning styles that are atypical."* Hence The Futur as *"private art school without the crippling
  debt"* — *"as soon as I say that, shots are fired, and I'm okay with making some enemies."*
- **The uniform test, dated and personal**: at a café that day, someone recognised him **from behind**.
  *"You want to develop your uniform… such that someone can recognise who you are purely based on your
  silhouette, or from 30 feet away."*

## Verbatim quotes

- ★★ *"Does he have a strong personal brand? … **Seth Godin arguably is a famous person, a sought-after
  public figure who has lectured all over the world on some of the biggest stages** — but what do we know
  about him outside of his subject-matter expertise? And the answer is very little."*
- ★★ *"A person who has a personal brand **is able to take ordinary things and make them extraordinary by
  associating themselves with this.**"*
- ★★ *"**Self-consciousness is thinking about yourself through the lens of yourself. Self-awareness is
  being able to perceive yourself from the eyes of other people.**"*
- ★ *"Self-confidence is **a belief in your skills to solve a problem** — you have a process and a way of
  looking at the world that you know you can solve most problems."*
- ★★ *"There's another word for vulnerability: **it's called transparency.** There are good things that
  happen in my life and there are bad things that happen in my life, and **I'm going to dish both out to
  you in equal measure.**"*
- ★★ Pixar's rule #1, as he renders it: *"**people admire characters more for their struggles and their
  failures than they do for their success.** This is why characters like Superman are not relatable but
  Bruce Wayne and Peter Parker are highly relatable."*
- ★★ *"**I'm not anti-education. I'm anti-crippling student debt.** … That is my enemy. So that's why we
  see The Futur as **private art school without the crippling debt** — and as soon as I say that, shots
  are fired, and I'm okay with making some enemies. And you need to as well."*
- ★ *"There's a line from *Dune* that has followed me since I was first introduced to Frank Herbert's
  work: **the sleeper must awaken.** … **We're all just sleepwalking through life.** We're not really
  aware and awake in the present moment."*
- *"That doesn't mean that the rest of us **ugly short people** are just [out of luck]."*
- On Kanye: *"**Put aside all the crazy stuff he said** — we're not here to judge whether what he's saying
  is good or bad. It's just the power of influence he has in **directing, capturing and leveraging
  attention.**"*
- On Taylor Swift: *"she is able to make women and men of all ages feel like she is their champion, that
  **she creates space for them to be them.**"*
- *"Keep me honest and accountable."* (closing ask to the audience — run the test and report
  counterexamples)
