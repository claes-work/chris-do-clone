# How To Beat Procrastination & Not Fear Failure

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary**, teaching to a room. Fully persona-eligible. Credited in-line:
> **Michael Janda** (*Burn Your Portfolio*) and his own business coach **Keir McLaren**.

## Summary

Short and dense. The thesis is borrowed and then made concrete: **procrastination is masking fear.**
*"We delay it because we're really just truly afraid, and it's a stall technique — I'm not going to ask
that girl out, I'm not going to submit that bid for that number, because what if."*

Applied to the specific avoidance he sees most: marketing yourself. *"There can be probably **no more
important function in your company than to market yourself**, because without sales and marketing there's
no company"* — and yet *"we have a two-hour block of time and we fill it with stuff… you know what
happened? The day is gone."*

The remedy is mechanical: **a time audit.** Map the week sunrise to sunset, write down every task, then
review two weeks later and ask how much time actually went to sales and marketing — against the honest
prior question, *"is establishing my expertise, is getting known, important to the growth of my company?
**If it's not, don't do it.**"* He explicitly excuses anyone who's already winning: *"if you're hitting
home runs all the time, please don't even listen to me… **why change the angle of your swing?**"*

Then **do only the thing only you can do** and offload the rest, with a sharpened version of Janda's rule
(below). And the framing that ties it together: **resistance** — opportunity knocks and *"the door's so
far away."*

He closes with the intervention that changed his own behaviour, from **Keir McLaren**.

## Key claims (2024-04-23)

- ★ **Procrastination is masking fear.** (He is careful about attribution: *"I read somewhere — I don't
  know where I read it — that somebody said something like this."*)
- **You will always be the best salesperson for your own company.** *"Not easy, but you'll always be the
  best."*
- ★ **The time audit**: log every task sunrise to sunset for two weeks, then review against your stated
  priority. The point is the gap between what you say matters and where the hours went.
- ★ **Permission not to bother**: if being known isn't important to your growth, don't do it — and if
  what you're doing already works, change nothing.
- ★★ **The delegation rule, upgraded.** Janda: if you can hire someone at minimum wage to do something,
  hire them. Chris's version: *"**if you can hire somebody to do work for less than what you're charging
  somebody else to do, hire them — 'cause you just make the difference.**"*
- ★ **Reinterpret the fear signal.** *"Every time something looks like a challenge, something that we're
  afraid of failing and looking bad and embarrassing ourselves — **to me I reinterpret those signals as:
  hey, I think you need to do this.**"*
- ★★ **The intervention that changed him**, dated to *"many years ago"*: his business coach told him
  *"**they're hurting your company because you're hiding in your room. Go out and meet the client.**"*
  His account of what happened next is unusually plain about how little it took: *"and that's all it took.
  Somebody points out to me something is wrong, there's a better way, we'll make it easy for you, let's go
  do it — and I did it. **And I learned that most of it was just mental. It was just me getting stuck on
  myself.**"* Then: *"once I got over that, it's like: go do public speaking. I'm like, oh my god,
  really?"*
- ★ **Keir McLaren's closing question**: *"there are two types of people in the world — **people who run
  towards change and people who run away from change. Which person are you?**"*

## Verbatim quotes

- ★★ The cold open, on hoarding expertise: *"**You have a wealth of knowledge and talent but you won't
  share it with anybody** — and then we sit around and we have calls on how do I get work, how do I get
  more leads, how do I get invited to speak at this conference. My God, **it's almost like you're trying
  to hurt yourself.** … That doesn't work anywhere in life. **It works nowhere in life.**"*
- ★★ *"**The first time I cook, I expect to be a Michelin-star chef. The first time I play golf, I want to
  be under par. How about we just love the sport?**"*
- ★ *"There's opportunity knocking at the door. **Our duty is to open the freaking door and let opportunity
  come in** — and what we do is, we're like: ah, the door's so far away, who knows who's going to be at the
  door."*
- ★ *"There's a hundred things that you probably should be doing that would help your career, but **the
  resistance, the old habits, the routine — those are the things that are fighting you. It's like gravity
  holding you back.**"*
- ★★ *"They're hurting your company because **you're hiding in your room.** Go out and meet the client."*
- ★ *"**I learned that most of it was just mental. It was just me getting stuck on myself.**"*

  hire them. 'Cause you just make the difference."*
