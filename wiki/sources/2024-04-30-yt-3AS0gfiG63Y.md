## Key claims (2024-04-30)

### ★★★★★ What branding is — and the definitions he stacks

> ★★★★★ *"**Branding is THE ACT OF INFLUENCING POSITIVE ASSOCIATIONS with your product, service,
> organisation.**"*

★★★★ **The stack, each credited:**
- **Marty Neumeier** — *"a person's gut feeling"* → *"**I think that's pretty good.**"*
- **Jeff Bezos** — *"what people say when you're not in the room"* → *"**so that's your
  REPUTATION.**"*
- **A researcher he names as Dr Christine Lucer** ⚠️ (caption spelling uncertain) — *"**impression
  management**."*

★★★★★ **The conclusion he draws from stacking them:** *"we start to understand that **branding CANNOT
be just what it looks like — because what it looks like is very SUPERFICIAL.**"* → the other
components: **does it perform as promised**; **after-service** — *"after the sale, what happens"*;
and **the culture of the company** — *"you can make a great product, provide a great solution, but
**anytime they run into anybody from the company and they act like jerks — THAT'S THE BRAND.**"*

★★★★ **Credited:** *"that's why the late **TONY HSIEH** says in his book *Delivering Happiness*:
**forget brand, get the culture right — when you get the culture right, the brand follows.** There's
a lot of truth to that, because **BRAND EMANATES FROM WITHIN.**"*

### ★★★★ Why corporate branding worked, and what broke it

★★★★ *"In the corporate space, what people have tried to do in the last 50 or so years is **to
influence, VIA ADVERTISING, the impression or the associations that people have with them.** And for
a period of time they could do this — because there were **mostly three major networks, a few
newspapers and a couple of magazines**, and through **DISTRIBUTION AND REPETITION** the message is
ingrained in us from the moment we're cognisant."* → the example: *"Coca-Cola, refreshing the world…
have a Coke and a smile — **then we start to associate those ideas together.**"*

★★★★ *"But something wonderful happened in the early 2000s — **something called SOCIAL.**"* ✅ The
setup for the personal-brand argument: **the distribution monopoly that made corporate branding work
is gone.**

★★★ **On the current appetite:** *"branding and personal branding is **super hot right now** — you
can't go to a bookstore, you can't throw a stone in a circle of people, and somebody's not talking
about branding."* → and the standing complaint: *"**even still within creative circles, the word
branding has a lot of misunderstanding. People think branding is what things look like — THAT'S A
SMALL PART OF IT.**"*

★★★ **His framing of the problem he's addressing:** *"I see that many of them that are trying to do it
— **and this is no shame on them — are missing key components for doing it successfully.**"*

### ★★★★★ Asking for help — the principle, told through a personal episode

⚠️ **Recorded under the privacy rule: the framework and the principle only.**

Describing a period when household conflict had become entrenched — *"they both are so drawn in the
sand that they cannot move from their position; **simple acts of us going out to eat became traumatic
ordeals**"* — he says:

> ★★★★★ *"**So I do what most people do in my situation: I CRIED FOR HELP. I went to find help.** And
> I want to put a flag in the ground so that people can follow the clues here: **often when you're
> stuck in your life — and you're building your brand or your business, you can't get to a sale,
> whatever's happening — YOU JUST NEED TO KNOW HELP IS AROUND THE CORNER. You have to be WILLING TO
> ASK FOR IT, to SEEK IT OUT, to be able to ARTICULATE WHAT YOU'RE LOOKING FOR — AND THEN TO PUT IN
> THE WORK.**"*

★★★★ **The motive he states for seeking it**, in his own words about himself: *"I said: I've got this
problem, **I just feel like I'm not going to be a good father**, and I'm doing everything I can **not
to become [an] old-school Asian father** and just whip my children — **because I don't believe that's
necessary.**"* ✅ Consistent with the parenting and heritage material already in
[[../persona/biography|biography]]; ⚠️ **no further household detail recorded.**

★★★★★ **The framework he was given** — the therapist's, credited as hers: *"**children, when they act
up, CRAVE ATTENTION. They PREFER positive attention, but they'll TAKE negative attention.**"* → his
own reaction, recorded because the resistance is the point: *"I was sitting there thinking **THAT
CANNOT BE TRUE. No child wants to be yelled at and reprimanded and argued with.**"* → the instruction:
*"**the best way to resolve this is to STARVE THEM OF THE ATTENTION — because what they're doing is
seeing: IF I BEHAVE THIS WAY, I WILL GET WHAT I WANT. Not the OUTCOME that I want — THE ATTENTION that
I want.**"*

> ★★★★★ **And the compliance, which is the transferable part:** *"**I went to the room and did EXACTLY
> as I was told, because I'M A REALLY GOOD STUDENT. I didn't try to improvise. I didn't try to flip
> it.** I came in there like: **I don't know if this is going to work, but I'm going to do it exactly
> as I'm instructed — because if it doesn't work, at least I can go back and say: I DID EXACTLY WHAT
> YOU SAID. This is a bunch of BS. Tell me what really works now.**"*

✅ **A method, not an anecdote:** run the expert's instruction **unmodified** so that a failure is
informative. It is also a rare piece of self-description — *"I'm a really good student"* — that
matches the 70%-extraction theory he states at [[2017-03-27-yt-BX5Jy0OIVSg|2017-03-27]].
