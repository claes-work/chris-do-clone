## Summary

Short and unusually self-analytical. The premise is a compliment he was given and didn't understand:
*"Chris, **you have incredible latitude with your audience** — you're able to say and do things and they
still feel like they trust you, because you've hit all three points."* His reaction is characteristic:
*"and I'm leaning in right now, like — **what are these trust triangles? I need to know what it is that
I'm doing subconsciously or unconsciously.**"*

**The three points:**

1. **Authenticity** — *"do you appear to be the person that you present yourself to be?"*
2. **Logic** — *"do you have a logical way of thinking that somebody can follow?"* If the logic doesn't
   track, trust drops even when the person is genuine.
3. **Empathy / care** — *"does the person feel like you genuinely care?"*

**Where salespeople fail**, worked through live: not logic (they have a process — current state → desired
future state → problems → steps). He first guessed authenticity, since that's what audiences say out loud
(*"you're so fake, you're a scam artist"*). But Dr Christine's answer, which he accepts: *"**I don't think
they genuinely care about me. All they care about is a sale.**"*

The worked example is his own inbox: the cold DM that opens *"hey Chris, big fan, curious what are you
doing with your emails."* His read — *"**number one, you're not a big fan.** You don't follow me, you don't
know that we send out emails, you don't know anything about our business. So it's one of the most
inauthentic things you're doing, and I know all you care about is getting to the next step in the sales
process."* Response: **block, delete.**

The last third is on why authenticity is rarer than people assume, and what to do about it.

## Key claims (2024-05-02)

- ★★ **The trust triangle**: authenticity + followable logic + felt care. All three, or trust degrades.
- ★★ **His own answer for why he gets latitude**: *"**deep down inside I believe in my heart of hearts that
  they know I genuinely care** — and my way of showing that I care is to tell people the truth. I'm not
  going to mince words, I'm not going to be cruel, I'm not going to judge you, **but I'm going to tell you
  the truth as directly and as clearly as I can.**"* This is the most explicit statement in the corpus of
  *why* the "charming razor blade" register works rather than alienating.
- ★ **Brené Brown, quoted and applied**: *"**choose discomfort over resentment.**"* His gloss: clear
  boundaries and clear communication are uncomfortable in the moment but prevent resentment — *"when
  someone overstays their welcome and you don't say anything, you're repressing your true feelings,
  creating resentment in your heart that the other person should have figured this out. **This is the root
  of a lot of miscommunication between lovers, between friends, and between clients and vendors.**"*
- ★ **Most people are not authentic, and it isn't malice.** *"There's lots of people who are still
  confused — not that they're evil or malicious or trying to trick you, **but they don't know who they
  are**, so they try to project an image that they think you will accept."* Cause: socialisation. *"We go
  along to get along… we repress our individualism for fear of being ostracised by the group."*
- **The compromise mechanism, in miniature**: you want sushi, everyone else wants burgers, you say you
  like burgers too — *"and in a lifetime of these small compromises **you start to forget who you
  are.**"*
- ★ **The fix is inner work plus other people's testimony.** Sit with people who love and trust you and
  ask: *"**what is your favourite memory of me? What is the thing that I do that really irritates
  you?**"* With the warning that makes it hard: *"you have to be ready to hear the truth, **because they
  can also sense your discomfort — and when you show your discomfort, they'll start to lie to you.**
  This happens all the time, because it's what we've learned to do to be polite."*
- **The trade-off he names as the price of authenticity**: *"just show up as you are and **accept the fact
  that you're not going to be for everybody** — a certain group of people will like you for who you are,
  and the rest of the people, you don't need to get to know them."*
