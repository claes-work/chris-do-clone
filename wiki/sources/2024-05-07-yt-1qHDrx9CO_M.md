## Summary

★★ **The most personal, least transactional material in this stretch of the corpus**, and the fullest
statement of his position on presence.

**Travel and the "checking in" problem.** He describes his own evolution: early on, homesickness, light
sleep, hotel noise, and *"a duty or an obligation or responsibility to call and check in."* Then his
wife's pragmatic response — *"what do you want, babe?"* — and his realisation of what the call actually
was: *"I've done **two wrong things**. The first is to leave, so she's carrying the weight of the
household by herself as the **chief home officer**. Number two is **now I'm interrupting whatever she was
doing so that I can feel like I'm connected. So this wasn't about her — it was about me.**"*

His current practice: **asynchronous by default**. No calls; photos, DMs, texts — *"that form of
asynchronous communication allows the other person to interact with it when they feel it's appropriate."*
A *"hey babe, I miss you"* is the cue to call. He also goes *"pretty radio silent"* on social media while
travelling, which reliably provokes *"is he dead?"* in his DMs.

**Then the question that gives the episode its spine: what is "missing" someone?** Prompted by a friend
saying they'd missed talking to him, he interrogates it rather than accepting it — *"I've kind of
developed this natural reaction to whenever anybody says anything, **I start to examine it myself: is this
true?**"* — and lands somewhere genuinely uncomfortable: *"**I don't really miss you, and I don't really
miss my kids, and I don't really miss my wife. It's not that I don't think about them, it's not that I
don't care** — but what *is* this idea of missing?"*

His model: missing is holding a memory of the past or an expectation of the future, and **comparing the
present against it unfavourably** — so the meal you're eating now tastes worse, the room you're in matters
less. Compounded by a property of memory he flags as strange: *"**by recalling the memory you change the
memory.** We romanticise ideas and we catastrophise negative things… That's why we always pine for the
good old days. **Things are never the way they were.**"*

**The discipline that follows**: be where you are. *"When I'm here, I want to be here."* Applied to work
(*"if I'm watching a movie with you I'm not thinking about my work, and at work I'm not thinking about
binging the Netflix series"*), and used to explain his own output: *"I can get more stuff done than most
people because **in that moment, that's all I'm doing.** I've optimised my mind, my work environment, my
tools, the palettes in my software toolset **for the singular purpose of doing this thing.**"*

**The conference observation** that generalises it: everyone at a multi-day event is on the phone to a
partner — *"and I'm like, **what about us in this room right now?** We're sitting here waiting for you to
rejoin the conversation."* His diagnosis: *"**it's reverse connection.** We're not connecting to the
people in front of us."*

## Key claims (2024-05-07)

- ★★ **The check-in call is often for you, not them.** Two costs: the absence itself, and the
  interruption. *"I'm interrupting when *I'm* free, not when *she's* free."*
- ★★ **Asynchronous communication as a form of respect** — it hands control of the timing to the receiver.
- ★★ **"Missing" is a comparison habit.** Holding a past or future reference point makes the present worse
  by definition. *"Instead of being grateful, being present to where you're at in the moment with the
  people you're sharing space with… **you're not really present, because we keep thinking about either
  the past or the future — neither of which we can have. We just have the moment.**"*
- ★ **Recalling a memory changes it.** Romanticised highs, catastrophised lows, and the resulting
  expectation trap: *"rarely does it ever live up to it."*
- ★★ **Single-tasking as the source of his output**, stated as mechanism rather than boast.
- ★★ **The don't-interrupt-a-thinking-person agreement**, a rare glimpse of an explicit household protocol
  (name-free): *"an agreement that I have with my wife… **when the other person's deep in thought, don't
  interrupt them.** It doesn't look like much, but they're working. You can tell because **my eyes are
  moving in a different place** — usually off into the horizon, because I'm processing an idea."* It runs
  both ways: *"sometimes I'll walk into the bedroom, I see her in the lotus position, it's very dark in the
  room — **I just gently close the door and moonwalk my way out of there.**"* And the family version:
  *"kids, no — don't talk to Mom right now, **she's finding that moment where she can get clarity in her
  mind.**"*
- ★ **His marriage runs on pragmatism, not sentiment** (self-reported, name-free): *"my wife is not one for
  much *let's do the goo-goo-gaga talk and tell each other how much we miss each other*. **If something
  interesting, exciting [or] relevant is happening, then we'll have a conversation.**"*
- **On compromise**, quoted approvingly from a board at the venue: *"**a good compromise is when neither
  party is happy.**"*
- **The irony he flags himself**: *"I know it's ironic because the company's called **The Futur**, but I'm
  trying to be very much in the present."*
