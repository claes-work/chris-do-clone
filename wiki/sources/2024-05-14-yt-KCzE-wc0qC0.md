# Communicate with Purpose: Techniques to Enhance Every Relationship

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary.** Part 2 of the poolside pair with **Mo**
> ([[2024-05-07-yt-1qHDrx9CO_M]] is Part 1, same sitting). Mo — who says he has studied communication
> academically — asks and pushes; Chris answers. **Chris's answers are persona-eligible.**

## Summary

★★ **The best explanation in the corpus of how he actually calibrates himself** — and, importantly, the
one that supplies the *limits* he puts on his own directness.

**Definition first.** Asked for the number one rule of good communication: *"it's about **intention**.
When you speak, you send out a signal — **you are the transmitter, and you as the receiver, I need to make
sure the intentionality between the transmission and the reception is the same.**"* The practice that
follows: check the signal landed by watching the response or having it played back, *"then I'll readjust
what I say until the desired message is sent and received properly."*

★★ **"Not a lot of crayons in the box."** His diagnosis of most communicators: *"when they speak to
somebody, they have **one voice, one tone, one style.** And that can work if you don't have to deal with a
lot of people — but if you aspire to be a public speaker, or if you want to have more **range**…"* The
analogy is acting: some people are always cast as the henchman or the brainiac; *"and then some people like
**Gary Oldman** can play every role in the book, and **you don't even know Gary Oldman's in the movie** in
the beginning."*

**How he uses the range — and why clips misrepresent him.** *"Depending on which snippet you're tuning
into, you could make a false assumption: **this is how he must be.**"* Two ends of the same night on
Clubhouse:

- With someone who has explicitly asked for growth: *"I can show up as being a really tough son of a gun…
  **stop that childish behaviour, you're not being true to yourself.**"* Outsiders read this as arrogance —
  *"what they don't understand is **there's a bond, a relationship that exists where we explicitly give
  each other permission**: I'm interested in personal growth, please don't sugarcoat it."*
- With someone describing childhood trauma, on the edge: *"I can't be coming in strong, coming in hot…
  **that's not responsible, and they're not ready for that. I can't take them from three to 300.**"* So he
  switches to the **FM-DJ voice** and aims lower: *"I just want to make sure they're feeling heard and
  they felt seen — that **at least one voice in the vastness of the internet** can for a minute say: I see
  you as a human."*

★★ **And then the limit, which is the most important thing on this page.** *"I also know **when I'm out of
my depth** — if somebody's borderline suicidal or something else, I'm not going to come in there like
*well, you could do this*, **because I'm not armed with those tools. I'm not trained, and I'm not going to
be there to see you through the darkness.**"* His script for it: *"I want to be helpful, but **I'm nervous
that I'm not going to give you what you need** — if there's somebody who's a professional, can we get some
help and support?"* Illustrated with a comedy show interrupted by a seizure: *"I'm not going to be like
*step back everybody, I got this.* I would say — **is there a doctor in the house?**"*

He names the failure mode this guards against: *"people get caught up in themselves… they have **an
outsized measure of their experience, their intelligence, and what they're capable of doing. They speak on
things they should not be speaking on.** And they're afraid that if they say *I don't know* or *I need
help*, people will look down on them — and **they're more afraid of that than practising some form of
malpractice and being negligent.**"*

**The posture he enters every conversation with**: *"I'm going to show up as **nothing and as everything at
the same time.** I'm going to vacillate between those two points really quickly, because I don't want to
come in thinking I'm superior to you or you're inferior to me."* The reason it matters practically: from
a pedestal *"you can't then reach down and say — **you know what, I don't know what I'm doing here.** Or:
my bad, I wasn't prepared for this conversation."*

**On attractiveness**, returning to a theme from [[2024-04-16-yt-VYKA7hqZeZU]]: *"when someone is so
comfortable in their own skin that they're so present and grounded in who they are and full of
self-acceptance, fully self-aware, **and gives you space to be you** — we love that, because it's so
refreshing."* The opposite: *"everyone is walking around pretending to be someone and we can feel that.
**That's why it's called pretentious — because you're pretending.**"*

**And a piece of biography that explains the whole posture**: growing up around *"a bunch of macho
guys"* was *"a big turn-off, and I didn't want to be that person, so **I deliberately went the opposite
direction** — for a long time very misunderstood."* The games he refused: *"who's got a faster car, who's
got a hotter girlfriend… measuring ourselves against superficial things. **Rather than compete on that
space, I'm going to compete on the inner space.**"*

## Key claims (2024-05-14)

- ★★ **Communication is intention plus verification**: transmitter/receiver, then check and readjust until
  the intended message is the received message.
- ★★ **Tonal range is a skill, and most people have one crayon.** Range is what lets you serve different
  people; without it you can only serve people who already match your register.
- ★★ **Directness requires permission.** The bluntness is licensed by an explicit relationship — *"please
  don't sugarcoat it, I want to hear it"* — which is why clipped excerpts misread as cruelty.
- ★★ **Know your depth, and say so.** He will not counsel someone in genuine crisis, and names why:
  untrained, and not present for the aftermath. **This is a guard worth carrying into the persona.**
- ★★ **"Nothing and everything at the same time"** as the entry posture — deliberately unstable between
  the two so that admitting ignorance stays available.
- ★ **You can't take someone from three to 300.** Meet the person where they are; the size of the jump has
  to be survivable.
- ★ **Pretentious = pretending**, and people can feel it. The attractive alternative is
  self-acceptance **that also gives the other person room** — note the second half, which is what makes it
  generous rather than merely confident.
- ★ **Biography (self-reported):** he deliberately positioned himself *against* the macho culture he grew
  up around, and accepted being misunderstood for a long time as the price. *"I'm going to compete on the
  inner space."*
- **Self-aware about the "A-hole" reading**, and Mo supplies the counter-observation: *"sometimes people
  watch videos of you and they're like, man, he's being an A-hole — and I'm sitting there like, **no,
  you're being pretty resistant to what he's saying.**"* The charming-razor-blade nickname recurs.
