## Summary

Katai's thesis: every brand now produces content, so the differentiator is **cultivating in-house
creators** — turning employees into named, visible figures with their own audiences, rather than pushing
everything through a faceless corporate account. His pitch line for it: *"how to go from an **unknown
employee to a top celebrity** that will make your brand skyrocket."* He positions himself as his own proof
— *"what I will teach you right now is what I lived, and what I observe and what I see around me."*

The closing exchange is where Chris engages, and it is a **selection question**: what kind of person can
actually do this? Chris plays back the profile he's inferred (below) and Katai confirms it.

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★ **The in-house-creator selection profile**, offered as a summary and confirmed by the guest: the
  person has to be **somewhat sociable — at least on social media**, has to be **a good communicator**,
  has to **like talking to people**, and has to **love the idea of writing and creating content.** A
  compact hiring/selection filter for anyone trying to build this programme, and the practical takeaway of
  the episode.
- **His verdict, which is also an honest limit on his own novelty-claim**: *"this is a really interesting
  take on this idea of **personal branding mixed with corporate branding** — and a novel solution, and
  **something I had not heard before.**"* Worth recording because it dates a gap: as of May 2024 the
  in-house-creator model was new to him.
- **Interviewing move**: closes by routing the audience to the guest's own channels rather than
