# LinkedIn Growth Hacks: Dominate the Platform in 2024

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary; only Chris Do's lines train the persona.** The guest is **Sam Brown**,
> a LinkedIn ghostwriter/consultant. **The LinkedIn playbook is his.** Chris interviews, and contributes
> one framework of his own plus the ratio The Futur actually runs.

## Summary

A platform-specific teach. Sam Brown's throughline is that the winners on LinkedIn — *"no matter what
they're doing, whether they're educating or sharing personal content"* — are *"providing something that is
actually **fun to interact with.**"* His own posture as a curator: *"**let me donate my time** and find the
very best things relative to the audience I want to serve,"* gathering ideas from books, films, YouTube
and walks around his neighbourhood.

His **three selling post types**, the practical core:

1. **The case study / triumph post** — a client's story arc; roughly **one in ten** of his posts, and
   *"my single favourite way to sell."*
2. **The testimonial post** — a mini case study: a pulled quote in italic over an image, plus the story in
   the caption.
3. **The straight sales post** — *"I'm taking on five clients in April; if you're a founder with an
   established business…"* — structured *"almost like a brochure or a landing page: this is what we're
   going to do, here's what you can expect,"* closing with two or three testimonials **cut down to the
   outcome only.**

His editing rule for testimonials is the sharpest thing in the episode: *"if I have a testimonial where
they go *Sam's great, I had such a lot of fun and the time flew by, and by the way I got a thousand
followers within a week* — **I'll probably just show *I got a thousand followers within a week of us
working together*, because the reader is thinking: what's in it for me. I don't care that Sam's a nice
guy.**"*

He also advises smaller creators to **skip the personal content** early and *"double down on educational
content only"* until people have a reason to care — which rhymes with Chris's own earn-the-right rule from
[[2024-03-29-yt-zCSjA-QoNiM]].

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★ **The 8-to-1 rule, and why it's tighter than the guest's 9-to-1.** Sam proposes 90% educational /
  inspiring to 10% sales. Chris: *"that is in alignment with us — **we use like an 8-to-1 rule**, because
  otherwise what happens is **you start to wear people down** and you're like: this guy is just pitching
  to me all the time, this is just a sales funnel for them — **and I don't want to be funneled**, to use
  the words of **Daniel Priestley**."*
- ★ **The three-way split inside the 90%**, which is his own refinement: *"the educational stuff could be
  the stuff that is **your authority.** So you're teaching people. **Some percentage of that should be
  inspirational stuff — more your personal story, things going on with your life — to give a more complete
  picture of who you are versus just the things that you do.**"* That is the four-components model of
  [[2024-04-16-yt-VYKA7hqZeZU]] expressed as a posting ratio.
- ★ **Permission to be direct in the 10%.** *"Sometimes it's okay in that 10% to be **very direct with your
  ask**: I can take on so many clients in the next number of months; if you're this kind of person who
  needs these kinds of results, here's what I do — contact me."* Consistent with the honest-scarcity
  material in `business` §44.
- **Interviewing move**: he plays the guest's own system back as a summary — *"wonderful, that was super
  clear. So you're saying the ratio is 90% educational/inspiring and 10% sales"* — before adding his own
  variant. The same restate-then-extend pattern seen across the corpus.
