# How To Identify Bad Clients Before Working With Them

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary**, teaching to a room. Persona-eligible. **The central framework is
> not his** and he credits it clearly: the **Dan Sullivan question** and the **user / confuser / refuser**
> triage are **Dan Sullivan's** (Strategic Coach). Stephen Covey is also quoted.

## Summary

Short and unusually tight. The premise: *"everybody wants different results but they don't want to do
anything different"* — via Covey, *"if you want what you got, keep doing what you're doing."*

**The job, stated plainly**: *"**you're supposed to find something new for the client. You're supposed to
tell them something they didn't know.** That's it."* So the work is **gap-finding** — *"looking for gaps
between what they want and what they're getting."*

★ **The question-framing lesson**, which is the most immediately usable thing here: *"if you say *who are
your customers?* — that's an okay question. **But if you ask: who are the customers you'd like to have
more of, and why aren't you getting them?** — you see, the frame of that question **highlights a problem
immediately**, because most people will just tell you the customers they already have."*

**The Dan Sullivan question** (credited): *"If we were having this discussion three years from today, and
you were looking back over those three years — **what has to have happened in your life, both personally
and professionally, for you to feel happy with your progress?**"* Chris then takes it apart, which is the
value he adds:

- *"If we're having this discussion, **that means we're working together.** It assumes a positive future
  state between you and I — **and this is before we've even agreed to work together.** So I'm implanting a
  seed."*
- **Three years is chosen deliberately**: *"not one year, not 30 years — that's too far off for us to
  figure out."*
- **Looking forward frees them from today**: *"we forget about our supply chain management, our lead
  generation, our executive team who's not quite delivering. **It puts us in a positive future state of
  mind for us to dream a little bit** — and now, looking back, it's asking them to figure out how they
  got there."*
- **"Personally and professionally" is load-bearing**: it *"opens up ideas way beyond what it is that you,
  designer person, can do for this person."*

**The triage** (Sullivan's): the **user** answers substantially — 10, 15, sometimes 30 minutes — and is
who you want; the **confuser** has no clarity on their own goals; the **refuser** sees the question coming
and declines to answer. Sullivan gives the latter two no second chance.

The last third is the part worth keeping for the persona: **why not knowing is fine.**

## Key claims (2024-05-21)

- ★ **Frame the question so it exposes a gap.** *Who are your customers?* → data. *Who would you like more
  of, and why aren't you getting them?* → a problem.
- **The Dan Sullivan question**, with Chris's own dissection of why each element works.
- **User / confuser / refuser** as a qualification triage (Sullivan's).
- ★★ **You don't need the answers — you need the questions.** *"When it comes to these other problems I
  have no idea what I'm doing, **and that's okay, because the person you're talking to most likely already
  has all the answers. All you have to do is ask the right question.**"*
- ★★ **The humility argument, and it is a real one.** *"As quick a learner as I am, **I can never learn
  more about you, your life and your business than you** — you'll forget more than I hope to learn. So for
  me to come in and say *you must do this, you must do that* — **it's really pompous and arrogant of me,
  especially because I don't have to live with the decisions that you make. I don't have to live with the
  advice that I'm about to give you.**"*
- ★ **Why he isn't afraid of an open-ended scope list**: *"either I know somebody who can help, **or the
  client already has the answer.**"*
- **The consultant's actual skill**, as he defines it: *"all I want to do is ask you the right question.
  **That's the skill.** Listen, help you check whether or not you're being consistent with yourself, and
  help you unearth whatever problem is in front of you."*
