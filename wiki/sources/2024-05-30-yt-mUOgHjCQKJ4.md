# Why People Buy ← Sales Psychology & Why You're Not Selling (w/ Jule Kim)

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — guest interview; only Chris Do's lines train the persona.** The guest is **Jule Kim**
> (captions render her name as "Joel" throughout), on her **third** appearance — after
> [[2023-11-18-yt-t7PZ6eD2lEQ]] (imposter syndrome) and [[2024-02-22-yt-x6z8-ZAXfHE]] (SEO). Here she is
> in a coaching register, and the episode is genuinely two-way: Chris supplies most of the worked
> examples from his own inbox, and they are the persona-eligible material.

## Summary

Framed as *"all the mistakes you make in the sales process to create a **dumpster fire** way of killing
all of your sales,"* and built around real pitches Chris and Jule have received.

**The central failure, and Chris's best example.** A YouTube-growth agency pitched him. He told them
plainly what he cared about — **subscribers, watch time, and then AdSense revenue, in that order** — and
they kept selling revenue. *"I was dumbfounded. I'm like — to speak like my kids — **'bro, did you hear
me?'** And I would repeat it… and they would talk, talk, talk, talk, and come right back: **there are four
or five things we could do to easily help you make 1.5x, 2x in terms of money.**"*

His diagnosis is the sharpest thing in the episode: ★★ *"Here's the problem. **I know that most people are
concerned about money. But I'm not most people.** And this is going right into **monkey/robot brain**. The
robot says: *people care about making money — go into your script and sell him the fact that you're
selling money.*"* With the correction of fact they never sought: *"the YouTube channel **has never made a
significant amount of money** relative to our operating capital and what we need. **It's not a priority
for me. It's the third priority.**"* His exit was courteous and final: *"I nodded, I smiled, and I said
thanks — **and one day when that's important to us, I'll reach out to you.**"*

**Jule's generalisation**, which Chris then extends: people default to their strongest skill the moment
they hear a need that isn't it — *"it's almost like they didn't even hear you. **They just default to
where they feel most confident**, and they try to bend whatever you're saying back to their main talking
points."*

★★ **Chris's river metaphor for it**: *"it's like a river and it has many estuaries… **and then they want
to dig a ditch and bring that path of water back into the main river.** And I don't get that. Every time
they do it — **I don't think you're hearing me.**"*

★★ **And the reason it's disqualifying, not merely annoying** — the part worth promoting: *"I want to say
this to all my creative service providers out there — **I don't care if you're a plumber, a tile person,
do roof repairs or car repairs. When you say something that is not what I'm saying, I have great concern
that this is how the whole relationship is going to be.** That you're somewhat tone-deaf and you don't
understand the words. **We might as well be speaking two different languages** — I'm speaking Vietnamese
and you're speaking Japanese."* And the cost: *"**I might have gone for this thing had you addressed what
I wanted. But I can't imagine what it's going to be like to work with you.**"*

He extends it past sales into the working relationship: *"when we say *hey, this direction isn't working*,
or *here are my goals and priorities, and be mindful of these objections* — **and they just bulldoze right
ahead and give me exactly what they were going to give me.** I'm like: gosh, this is terrible."*

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★★ **"I'm not most people."** The failure isn't that the pitch was wrong in general — it was right in
  general and wrong about him. **A script that assumes the modal buyer loses the specific one.**
- ★★ **Not listening in the pitch predicts not listening in the project.** The strongest, most
  transferable claim here: an unheard sales conversation is a **preview of the relationship**, which is
  why it disqualifies rather than merely irritates.
- ★★ **The river-and-estuaries metaphor** for how salespeople drag every topic back to their strongest
  offer.
- ★ **Monkey/robot brain**, applied to the seller rather than the buyer. The wiki already carries this pair
  in a buying context (sales-clients §46-era); here it names the **seller's** default — running the script
  the robot says should work instead of hearing what was said.
- **The courteous close he actually used**: repeat, observe, disengage without argument — *"one day when
  that's important to us, I'll reach out to you."*
- **A dated priority ordering for The Futur's channel** (self-reported, and useful): **subscribers → watch
  time → AdSense**, with AdSense explicitly *"the third priority"* and never a significant revenue line
  relative to operating capital.
- **On his own advantage, said with some self-awareness**: *"this is when I start to realise — and **I take
  this for granted** — the 20-plus years that I spent in [the industry]…"*

## Fenced — Jule Kim (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Jule Kim. Context only._

- **The default-to-strength diagnosis**: when presented with a need outside their strongest skill set,
  people *"didn't even hear you"* and revert to where they feel most confident.
- **Her framing of the root cause**: sellers get *"wrapped up in their skills and what they could
  deliver"* — the pitch becomes about their capability inventory rather than the buyer's stated problem.
