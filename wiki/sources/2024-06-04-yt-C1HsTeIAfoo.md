## Summary

**The reframe that opens it**, credited to **Ben Burns**: *"the question people seem to ask — and he says
it's the wrong question — is **how do I find better clients?** He says the better question is: **how do
better clients find you?**"*

**Why referrals need asking.** Waiting is *"a passive way to generate leads, because **you have no control
over it.**"* And the honest reason clients don't refer you unprompted: *"**when you're out of sight you're
out of mind** — are you sitting here thinking about your clients' lives right now? You're probably not. So
the reverse is true."* Plus a less comfortable reason: *"believe it or not, **some people are very
competitive and somewhat insecure about referring [you], because they want to keep you in their back
pocket.**"*

★★ **The three-step scaffold** (from his coach), and the principle behind it: *"**I don't want to just go
in for the hard ask.** If you met somebody on the street and said *do you want to give me $10,000, do you
want to sleep with me* — **that's too abrasive, it's too direct. You have to scaffold, build up towards
it.** You have to establish **need**, **desire**, then you can do the ask."* His worked script:

1. *"I hope you've enjoyed the work that we've done and feel like it's high-quality work."* — establishes
   satisfaction; wait for the yes.
2. *"I'm expanding my practice, I've recently added another designer."* — establishes **need**.
3. *"I'd love to do work with you or anybody you know who needs my services. **Is there somebody you know
   that I could reach out to?**"*

★ **The phrasing of step 3 is the whole trick**, and he says so: *"notice the way it's phrased — **is there
somebody that you know that I can reach out to**. Because you don't want to just say *would you mind
recommending me to some of your friends* — they get in their car, they think about you, they have dinner,
**and you're out.**"* Asking for a name transfers the next action to you.

★ **Timing**: *"the best time to do this I think is **before the job is over.**"*

**On networking**, which he dislikes as a construct: *"it seems kind of contrived, right? We're going to
throw a bunch of people in a room and ask them to somehow magically do business together."* Two moves he
offers instead: find the **connector** (*"they're like a matchmaker — they knew a little bit about a need
and a service and they're able to put you together"*), and change what you're doing in the room entirely
(below).

**Reciprocity** (Cialdini) closes it, with the Hare Krishna flower experiment as the illustration — the
book was expensive and heavy, so they switched to a flower, *"and it's just as effective; you will give
them money again."* Cialdini followed them and watched the recipients bin the flower — *"[they] would come
in, pick it out of the trash, give it to the next person."*

## Key claims (dated to the talk, Blind era; published 2024-06-04)

- ★ **"How do better clients find you?"** beats *"how do I find better clients?"* (Ben Burns).
- ★★ **The three-step scaffold**: satisfaction → need → ask for a **specific name**. Never the bare ask.
- ★ **Ask before the engagement ends**, while you are still in sight.
- ★★ **His networking method — the hidden-agenda problem, and the fix.** *"It feels like I have a hidden
  agenda. When I bump into Amy I'm thinking: **I need to ask for work, I wonder if they have work, I make
  websites and I've got to sell a website.** So what kind of real interaction am I going to have with
  Amy?"* Instead: *"I just want to have a genuine, real conversation and ask her about her challenges at
  work [and] in life — and **while I'm talking to her, I'm thinking: is there anything in there I can do to
  help? Do I know somebody who can help?**"* With a non-commercial example: he refers his chiropractor and
  acupuncturist to people with back pain — *"**I don't profit from that, but I'm going to connect them.
  So I'm building a real relationship.**"*
- ★ **The two silent qualifying questions** he runs during that conversation: *"**can they afford me, and
  do I like them?** If I don't like them I don't want to work with them — **I'm not going to ask them for
  the sale.**"*
- **Where buying actually happens**: trade shows and events where people are already in a buying state of
  mind; otherwise *"it's kind of hard."*
- **Reciprocity as the counter to "keep it in the family"** (Cialdini): when you genuinely help someone,
  they feel the imbalance and want to even it. His own dated example: *"my financial adviser — over years I
  paid him lots of money to handle our finances, so now when I talk to him about the business we do, **he
  refers me to his clients.** It's like we're just evening it out."*
- **Peer groups cluster**: *"if your client is a successful entrepreneur, chances are they know a lot of
  other successful entrepreneurs. **Successful people don't go and hang out in the high school** — they
  hang out and do business with other entrepreneurs. In fact they set up social clubs just to do that."*
