## Summary

A blunt talk on business fundamentals for creatives, from the coach Chris credits across the corpus.

**The recurring diagnosis**: creatives leave art school knowing art and not business, build to a ceiling
on *"sheer talent and hard work and willpower"* — and then *"the very things that got them there — the
willfulness, the determination, the working lots of hours — **are now working against them.**"*

**The financial literacy gate.** His first two questions of any new client: *"**do you have a P&L?**"* —
*"a lot of people I've asked that have made millions of dollars actually don't know what I'm talking
about"* — and *"**do you actualise jobs?**"*, i.e. do you track what a sold job actually cost you. The
distinction he hammers: *"the difference between talking to artists and talking to businessmen — **they
don't care what the gross sale is. All they care is what the net sale is.**"* Illustrated:
*"most people will say *Microsoft called* — and you say, how much is the job? **$400,000!** Everybody's
excited. **I say: well, how much are we going to make on that? Total silence.**"* And the consequence:
*"if you don't know that, **you don't know whether you can take the job.**"* — noting, *"as Chris has
learned, a lot of times you can take a **$50,000 job and make far more money as a percentage than a
half-a-million-dollar job.**"*

**On change**, via **Kahneman** (*Thinking, Fast and Slow*, unnamed but described — *"this guy won the
Nobel Prize, he's actually a psychologist"*): most of us run on **System 1**, making automatic decisions we
believe are considered. The Moses illusion is his demonstration (*"Moses led the animals two by two onto
the ark"* — it was Noah). *"People will always defer to the easiest way out… **so change is enormously
difficult.**"* Applied to his own clients: *"a lot of times people pay me very well, and for the first six
months every meeting is the same — **I make a suggestion, they tell me why they can't do that.** I'm sort
of like your college professor: **I get paid whether you take my advice or not.** … **If you're not willing
to change, then nobody can help you.**"*

**On selling**: *"the way you show you're a professional… **is how you ask questions and the questions you
ask. That's what demonstrates your expertise — not saying you're an expert.**"* Every meeting needs a
goal; *"the worst answer you can get on a sales call is **maybe**, because it wastes your time."* And the
free-work trap, stated absolutely: *"*I'll take this job for no money, because then the guy will come back
and give me more money* — **that's never going to happen. It never happens. Underline that.** You can't go
to a car dealer and say *you want $10,000 for that car, I'll pay you five and buy the next one at full
price.*"* Chris's gloss on the same trap: *"**the promise of work tomorrow for bills that need to be paid
today. It's the bait that traps you** — and sometimes we tell that lie to ourselves."*

**And the closing idea, which is the best thing on the page**: *"**stay out of the results.**"*

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★ **Naming the sales hang-up his audience actually has, and crediting the fix.** *"I want to share what
  Keir has helped me to do — and **I have some of the same hang-ups I think you have.** We have this image
  of what a salesperson does: it's very aggressive, it's a big turn-off, it's one of the more despised
  [roles] — the stereotypical car salesman. **And so if we ask, we feel like we're that person, and we
  don't want to be that person, because we're sensitive and very empathetic.** What Keir taught me was
  **you can ask and not be crass or rude about it.**"*
- ★ **His summary of the free-work trap** (above): *"the promise of work tomorrow for bills that need to be
  paid today — **it's the bait that traps you.**"*

## Fenced — Keir McLaren (Chris's business coach; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Keir McLaren. Context only. His fuller biography and toolkit are on
[[../entities/kier-mclaren]]._

- ★★ **Net, not gross.** The Microsoft/$400,000 example; *"do you actualise jobs?"*; a $50K job can beat a
  $500K job on percentage.
- ★★ **"If you're not willing to change, then nobody can help you."** With the professor framing — *"I get
  paid whether you take my advice or not."*
- ★★ **"Stay out of the results."** From his own therapy and Al-Anon background, offered with unusual
  candour: *"what you owe yourself in a meeting is to walk out saying **you presented yourself and your
  company in the best way. You have no control over the outcome. None.**"* Illustrated with the documentary
  *My Date with Drew* — the pursuer invents explanations for the silence, and the real reason turns out to
  be that the tape was chasing her around the world. *"**Don't try to imagine why you didn't get the job.
  You can't control that.**"*
- ★ **Expertise is demonstrated by the questions you ask**, not by claiming it — and he says he resisted
  doing this kind of talk for years *"because I have a real problem with people who pose as experts. **I
  don't think any of us know that much.**"*
- ★ **"Maybe" is the worst answer** on a sales call; a no lets you move on.
- ★ **Every meeting needs a goal**, and every question should serve the final ask: *"people's time is
  enormously important. Don't waste it."*
- ★ **Listen for what kind of client they'll be.** *"**Every encounter with a client is information** —
  but people are listening for what they want to hear."* Evasive, non-committal, can't-name-the-decision-
  maker are all data. And the pricing corollary: *"if there's something about it I want to do but I know
  there's pain and suffering, **I literally double the money.**"*
- ★ **Ask whether they're the decision maker**, because *"most of the people you work with, they're
  not."*
- **On agency website copy**: *"I can tell you what they all say — you're very creative, you're
  storytellers, you're great to work with, you're honest. **If Jesus came back, he'd be your best
  buddy.**"*
- **On money and meaning**: *"I don't work with people who simply want to make money, because **making
  money, to be honest with you, is not that hard. Making money and having a life and having it mean
  something — that part's pretty hard.**"* (Chris pushes back mildly: for some in the room, making money
  is hard too.)
