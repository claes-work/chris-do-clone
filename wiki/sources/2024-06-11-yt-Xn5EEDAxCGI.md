# How To Make Money As A Creator In 2024

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary**, in conversation with a co-host who prompts him. Persona-eligible.
> The extended case study is **Enes Yilmazer**, a real-estate/luxury-property YouTuber, analysed by Chris
> (not present).

## Summary

★★ **The strongest statement in the corpus of his commoditisation argument, and the clearest instruction
that follows from it.**

The cold open is a scolding, and it sets the register: *"**Everybody, stop window shopping.** There's an
opportunity on the other side of the window that you've been looking at for a really long time… it seems
like everyone's having fun on the inside, but here you are, face pressed up to the glass saying: **I'm too
old, I'm not smart enough, I'm not good-looking enough, I know it's hard to keep up, my drip game ain't
[there].** And you know what — **you're missing one of the biggest opportunities in your life.**"*

**The case study: two real-estate videos, same job, different outcome.** Chris contrasts a typical
property presenter — *"this is marble countertop and this is wooden cabinets"* — with Enes Yilmazer:
*"I first want to walk you through the beautiful foyer… **this is Calacatta marble and this is
book-matched — look at the chamfered edge, and I love the beefiness, the thickness of this. Imagine you
sitting there having breakfast with your family.** … This is quarter-sawn oak that's been pickled, matte
finish, and the hardware is German."* His read: *"**both of them are experienced. They tell a totally
different story** — and there's a reason why [he] is the guy they call to do these videos."* He also notes
the **on-camera banter with the operator** as a deliberate format choice — *"the chemistry between the two
is almost like a video podcast about real estate property."*

★★ **Then the hard commercial point.** *"Those of you shooting architectural videos for real estate,
you're probably getting paid a couple thousand bucks to do this — **but I bet they're not flying you
across the world to New York, to LA, to Dubai, to France, to Turkey to talk about the property, because
you ain't that person. They'll just hire a local person to do it for much less.**"* And the collapse he
has watched personally: *"we used to go out and do real estate videos and I would say **it's $40,000** and
they would pay us — and now **they're not going to pay you $4,000, forget about that.** The real-estate
agent will take a few clips with their phone and post it on the website. **I know, because I work with
real-estate agents.** There's no value-add."*

★★ **The instruction.** *"The big shift here, and this is what you need to hear from me: **you have to be
less of behind-the-camera talent and more in front-of-camera talent.** There's only one of you in the
world, and **when you hide that, you're holding back the value.** There are lots of people who can move
the camera, edit, do colour grading, add visual effects — **actually there's a whole lineup of them,
because they're in my DMs all the time.** But there are very few people who can talk about things in a way
that captures your imagination."*

**And the accountability device**, which is a characteristic move: *"mark on your calendar: **this is the
first day I heard of this concept, and this is what I choose to do.** Let's revisit this in three years."*
Followed by an unusually direct prediction: *"**I can almost guarantee you: if you don't heed what I'm
saying, you will not reap the rewards** — and your life will not change for the better. It will change for
the worse."*

## Key claims (2024-06-11)

- ★★ **Production skill is commoditised; presence is not.** The scarce asset is the person who can make a
  subject interesting on camera, not the person who can operate the camera.
- ★★ **The price collapse is real and personal**: $40,000 real-estate video work is now done on an agent's
  phone. He cites it as first-hand, not as a trend piece — *"I know, because I work with real-estate
  agents."*
- ★★ **Geography follows distinctiveness.** Nobody flies a competent local operator across the world; they
  fly the person who cannot be substituted.
- ★ **Specificity is the craft.** The Yilmazer contrast is not about charisma but about **naming things
  precisely** (Calacatta, book-matched, chamfered edge, quarter-sawn, pickled finish) and **placing the
  viewer in the scene** (*"imagine you sitting there having breakfast with your family"*).
- ★ **Format is a choice**: the presenter/operator banter turns a walkthrough into a two-hander.
- ★ **The playbook decays fast**: *"the world has changed, and it's changed a lot. **To even use the
  playbook from five years ago makes you feel really ancient and out of touch.**"*
- **The framing of the objections he opens with** — too old, not smart enough, not good-looking enough —
  as *window shopping*: watching the opportunity from outside and narrating reasons not to enter.
- **On his own method, stated in passing**: *"I'm watching all these real-estate videos and **I stumbled
  into a rabbit hole**, and now I'm comparing [and] contrasting, and I'm out there in the real world
  **extracting the lessons and sharing them with you.**"* A neat description of how most of his teaching
  material is actually produced.
