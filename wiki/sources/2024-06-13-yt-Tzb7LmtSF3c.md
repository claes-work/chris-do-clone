# Why Comfort is Your Biggest Enemy!

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary**, advising **Diego** (a Futur team member weighing a move from Miami
> to Los Angeles) with the audience listening in. Persona-eligible. Credited in-line: **Noreen Moroka**
> (*"comfort is a slow death"*), **Steve Jobs**, **Wayne Gretzky**.

## Summary

★★ **The clearest statement in the corpus of how he decides when to change direction** — and it is built
on a specific, reusable test rather than on nerve.

**The trajectory test.** *"If you can map your life out in the next 5 to 10 years **on the trajectory
you're on right now** — and the way we do that is we look at the last three years of billings,
opportunities, profile jobs, awards, anything that matters to you — and see: **is it trending upward,
downward, or flat?**"* Then: *"if it's skyrocketing, **don't change anything.** That would be the craziest
thing to do."* Only if the line is flat or too slow do you explore.

**Then the two-sided evaluation**, deliberately starting with gain because the mind won't: *"so much of
human nature is about wanting to protect something — **we're more afraid of losing what we have than the
potential gain of something we don't have.** That's a cognitive bias, **a loss-aversion bias.**"* And a
warning about the standard tool: *"a lot of people say let's do that thing you learned in junior high —
pros and cons. **The problem is the human mind is wired to reinforce what we already believe — a form of
confirmation bias.** So the pros will be a few things and the cons will be really deep, and you get stuck
in **paralysis by analysis.**"*

★★ **The light-from-dead-stars metaphor** — his model of why chasing proven opportunities fails.
*"When we look up at the night sky we see the sparkle of a star, but **we don't know if those stars are
still even around, because the information is about a thousand years old.** I think opportunities are like
that — they send the signals out and they echo through time, and eventually you see it. **But once you see
what's working, you have to imagine that a lot of people have seen it.** … So they go running to where the
gold **was**, not where it **is**."* Resolved with Gretzky: *"skate to where the puck is going to be."*

★★ **The exit indicators** — the concrete signal set he says he has used repeatedly (below), and the
career history that demonstrates it.

## Key claims (2024-06-13)

- ★★ **The trajectory test**: project the last three years forward; upward → change nothing; flat or slow →
  evaluate the move.
- ★ **Evaluate the gain first**, because loss aversion and confirmation bias will over-weight the
  downside. Pros-and-cons lists are structurally biased against change.
- ★★ **Light from dead stars**: by the time an opportunity is visibly working, the signal is old and the
  crowd has already seen it. *"Make sure you're looking forward and not backwards. **You're not chasing the
  trend, you're predicting the trend.**"*
- ★ **Intelligence as pattern recognition**: *"if we see A, B, C, D, we can make a safe assumption the next
  letter is E."* The move he actually advocates: pattern + experience + intuition → an educated guess —
  *"and if that thing is what I'm interested in, that gives me a competitive advantage. **I don't want to
  move to something just because that's where it's trending — I want to move to something because it
  excites me and the data supports that this is going to become a thing.**"*
- ★★ **The three exit indicators**, stated as what he watched in motion design: *"**the size of the
  opportunity is getting smaller, the frequency of the opportunity is getting smaller, and yet the
  competition continues to grow.** Those are three pretty strong indicators that this is going to come to
  an end — at least for us. **At that point I'm looking for the exit door.**"*
- ★★ **His own two pivots, dated by this logic**: motion design → **brand strategy / brand design /
  client-direct** (*"that buys us a couple of years of time"*), then → **content, teaching at scale, social
  platforms** — *"that for me seemed like a good thing. **I can put everything into that wagon and take
  that for the next 20, 30 years.**"* And the first one, in school: he was studying packaging aimed at
  entertainment marketing (CD-ROM, music packaging, key art) when *"I saw this new emerging thing with 3D
  [and] 2D animation… **right time, right place. I decided I want to do motion design.**"*
- ★ **He states his own current position on the curve, and pre-announces the next move**: *"I'm getting
  more speaking opportunities at higher-profile engagements… **I feel like I'm on the ascent towards an
  apex that I have yet to meet. But once I hit the apex and I go towards the downside, I'm going to change
  — and you'll know it, because I'll move. And I've done this multiple times before.**"*
- ★★ **The gold-bars parable for detachment**: your hands are full of your current opportunities and
  habits; you walk into a room full of gold bars; *"**you have to let go of something so you can get
  something else.** But you can't let go of these things you're currently holding — so the window closes,
  the door closes, and you have nothing new."* Hence: *"we have to practise a form of detachment: **this is
  cool, but I'm not so in love with this thing that I can't see the next best thing.**"*
- **On roots vs. opportunity** (to Diego): *"it's important to have a centre of who you are — your family,
  your neighbourhood, your upbringing — **but it shouldn't be at the cost of you taking on new
  opportunities.**"*
- **On age and rigidity**: *"38… he doesn't have the same flexibility he did when he was 19 or 22. It
  seems almost like we're damned — **the older we get, the more set in routine we are, the less likely we
  are to make the necessary changes that will benefit us most.**"*
- **On the remote-work discount**: if the work is cognitive, location costs you maybe 20% — *"and what you
  have to gain is like 60%. **Then I would make that trade.**"*
- **On rebuilding**: *"**if you built it before, there's a high probability that you can build it
  again.** There's a lot of unknowns, but it's not like you're starting from zero."*

## Verbatim quotes

- ★★ *"My friend Noreen Moroka said: **comfort is a slow death.** … Oftentimes the opportunity before us is
  **shrouded in what looks like challenges and problems and pain points and headache — but that's exactly
  where the reward is.** Because if you could make a lot of money and seize new opportunities without
  taking any risk, **everybody would do it, and that thing wouldn't be very valuable at all.**"*
- ★★ *"They go running to **where the gold was, not where it is.**"*
- ★★ *"Once I hit the apex and I go towards the downside, **I'm going to change — and you'll know it,
  because I'll move.**"*
- ★ *"**You're not chasing the trend, you're predicting the trend.**"*
- ★ *"You're stuck in habit and routine. **You're familiar with it, [so] you're less likely to try
  something new — it's the very thing that holds us back from seeing and seizing new opportunities.**"*
- ★ *"How many times in your life have you thought — **man, I had that idea**, and then three years later
  you see someone else executing on it?"*
- *"In my life I've been rewarded by **making big decisions and moving fast when opportunity is there**,
  versus overthinking something and then the opportunity is gone."*
