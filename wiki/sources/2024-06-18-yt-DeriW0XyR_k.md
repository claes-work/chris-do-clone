# The Best (and Worst) Pricing Models For Creatives w/ Tim Williams

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary; only Chris Do's lines train the persona.** The guest is **Tim
> Williams**, founder of **Ignition Consulting Group**, a positioning and pricing consultant to agencies.
> **The paradigm/practices argument, the Lister analogy and the "time is not a cost" formulation are
> his.** He is a peer of Blair Enns and Ron Baker in the value-pricing lineage already recorded in
> [[../entities/influences]] — and, like theirs, his frameworks must stay attributed.

## Summary

A pricing conversation between two people who already agree, which makes it useful mainly for the
**arguments** rather than the conclusions.

**Williams's headline claims** (his, fenced): *"back in the Mad Men era agencies were wildly
profitable"*; *"**certainly within ten years there won't be a single agency left on the planet billing by
hours**"*; and the formulation worth keeping — ★ *"**time is not a cost. Time is a constraint.**"*

★★ **The paradigm argument, which is the spine of the episode.** *"**You will never change your practices
until you change your paradigm.** Once you do change your paradigm, **you can't help but change your
practices.**"* His illustration is the pre-1850s operating theatre: surgeons worked with dirty hands and
shared instruments for thousands of years, *"because they were operating under **the wrong paradigm about
infection** — they thought infection is caused by stale air, bad air."* Then **Joseph Lister** and the
microscope: *"he invites his colleagues to look… **those are germs, that's bacteria that causes
infections.**"* Germ theory lands, and *"guess what happens — **all the practices changed.** Hands get
washed, instruments get sterilised, clean operating rooms."*

Applied to objections: *"these questions about *it'll never work for me* are simply them saying **I
haven't changed my paradigm yet.** Because when they do, they'll say: **I could never go back to hourly
billing.**"*

★★ **The line the episode is named for is co-created**, and the attribution matters. Williams sets it up —
*"if you're a doctor and you refuse to understand the paradigm, **I believe that's malpractice at this
point, because you have the information**"* — and **Chris** completes it: *"yeah… **in a way, billing by
the hour is malpractice.**"* Williams: *"**that'll be the title for this episode.** I like it."*

The rest is Williams on positioning and pricing models, and a running joke about how strongly they both
feel (*"we'd commit suicide before we'd bill by the hour"* — Williams, hyperbolically; Chris pushes back:
*"well, I don't know, that's pretty extreme"*).

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★★ **"Billing by the hour is malpractice."** His completion of Williams's setup, and the sharpest
  compression of a position the wiki already holds at length (`pricing` §47: hourly billing as a habit
  problem). **Note it as co-created**, not solely his.
- ★★ **The belief-first sales diagnosis, applied to the pricing objection.** *"Your biggest problem is
  **you don't want this to work. You don't believe it.** … What else do we need to talk about **so you can
  believe this?** … **Once they believe it, then the answer becomes pretty obvious.**"* This is his
  standing belief-first framework (`sales-clients` §59) turned on the reader rather than a client —
  worth recording because it shows him applying his own method to an internal objection.
- ★★ **The local-proof argument**, which is his and is a genuinely useful objection-killer: *"**somebody in
  your town who's doing work that's not as good as yours — lower quality — is applying this and winning in
  ways that you're not. I guarantee it.** So you're holding on to something for whatever reason —
  **because your identity is wrapped up in this old way of thinking.**"*
- ★ **The non-coercive close**, and it is characteristic: *"**it's entirely up to you if you want to change
  or not. We're just here to help you see the light** — and if you want to walk through the door and enter,
  or leave the darkness, **that's entirely up to you at this point.**"* Consistent with *don't convince*
  ([[2024-05-28-yt-4kmc253GZWs]]) and *belief-first* rather than persuasion.
- **He declines the hyperbole** when Williams offers it — a small but real instance of not amplifying a
  guest for effect.
