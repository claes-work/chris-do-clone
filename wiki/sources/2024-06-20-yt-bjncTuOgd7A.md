# The Evolution of Graphic Design Industry From 1990–2020 w/ Executive Producer Ian Dawson

> **ATTRIBUTION — two-way conversation; Chris Do's lines train the persona, Ian Dawson's do not.** The
> guest is **Ian Dawson**, executive producer (Novacom, Rhythm & Hughes, Imaginary Forces/Kyle Cooper,
> independent since ~2015), a professional colleague and friend of *"coming up on 30 years."*
>
> ★★ **Filed as a biography landmark.** The opening ten minutes are Chris telling, in unusual detail, the
> story of the job offer he turned down to start his own company — which places it directly upstream of
> the founding of Blind.

## Summary

**The Novacom story, told first-person.** Freelancing in the mid-1990s, Chris is invited by *"Newberger"*
to Novacom — *"the hottest broadcast design studio probably in the world, at least in LA."* His first
impression is the food: Ian walks him down a hallway to a breakfast spread *"you just cannot believe… I
feel like I'm staying at a high-end hotel."* He is self-conscious about it — *"I don't want to seem like a
pig on my first day"* — and then: *"**wow, I could get used to this.**"* His reaction to Ian's title is
recorded too: *"when he introduced himself as the executive producer I'm like — **fancy title, sir.** What
the hell is 'ex-'? What are you talking about? But now I know what it means. Back then I was like: **damn,
people got fancy titles, fancy offices and fancy spreads.**"*

He is booked for **two weeks** and, by his own account, out of his depth: *"I know nothing about broadcast
design. **I'm doing square pixels when it should be non-square. I'm just messing everything up.**"* Ian
offers him a full-time job anyway.

★★ **The negotiation, and the number.** Ian offers *"I want to say like **$50,000**"* — *"which was 10K
more than I was making, so this is already a movement."* Chris counters with a number he flags himself as
unflattering: *"**Ian, the only number I would consider is $85,000.**"* Ian's reaction: *"his eyes opened
up like — *what?* **You just graduated from school, friend. People don't come out of school and make that
kind of money.** He didn't say that, but his eyes said something."* Chris's reasoning, given plainly:
*"the reason I asked for this amount is because **when I was working at Cole & Weber, that's what they
offered me. I'm just like: you got to match my last offer.**"*

★★ **And then he leaves anyway.** *"Fast forward — I'm going to start my own company… Ian comes in the
door: *you didn't give me a chance to counter.* I said, **well, things move fast, Ian. I'm starting my own
company.**"* Ian says *"good luck"*, and Chris preserves his own suspicious reading of it: *"I think he
meant it — **but I felt like he was saying in my mind: good luck, we'll see you come crawling back in a few
weeks when you don't know what you're doing.**"*

**The poker games**, which turn out to be a piece of network history: Chris hosted *"these great poker
games at his house… very, very social people within the industry,"* and **Kyle Cooper** was among the
first out of the game. It was at one of those nights that Ian first met Cooper — *"and I guess a week or
two later you called me up and said: are you interested in meeting with Kyle?"* Ian then **worked for
Cooper for about eight years.**

The rest is Ian's industry history and a nostalgic survey of budgets and studio culture from ~1990 to
2020, which is **his** and is fenced below.

## Key claims — Chris-attributed (dated to the mid-1990s, told 2024-06-20) 🗣️

- ★★ **Novacom offered him ~$50,000** full-time; he was then making **~$40,000**.
- ★★ **He countered at $85,000**, on the explicit basis that **Cole & Weber had already offered him that**
  — *"you got to match my last offer."*
- ★★ **He left to start his own company before Ian could counter.** This is the clearest account in the
  corpus of the **immediate pre-Blind moment**, and it reframes the founding: he was **not** short of
  options — he turned down a raise and a title at the hottest studio in the city.
- **He was underqualified at the time and says so** — square vs. non-square pixels, *"I'm just messing
  everything up."*
- **He hosted the industry's poker nights**, which functioned as a professional network — and he made the
  introduction that led to Ian's eight years with **Kyle Cooper**.
- **Doug Sharon** named as a **rep for Blind** and the mutual connection.
- **Newberger** named as the person who invited him to Novacom (surname only, spelling unverified).

## Fenced — Ian Dawson (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Ian Dawson. Context only._

- **His career**: started in the business in **1988** while still at college (Northridge); a night class
  taught by *Entertainment Tonight*'s technical director led to an introduction to **John Ridgeway**,
  creative director at Paramount on *ET*, and an internship that **paid from day one**. Motion-control
  assistant under *"Joe Dubs."*
- **~8 years at Novacom**, launching networks in **over 20 countries**; then **Rhythm & Hughes**, running
  post and CG for a period; then independent shops — *"yours being one of them, at Blind"* — plus Yu+Co
  and others; then **~8 years working for Kyle Cooper**; independent through his own company since
  **~2015**; more recently VR/AR work and a year-long consulting engagement.
- **On budgets then**: *"I would say from **$250,000 up to two, three, four million** — I wish budgets were
  like that again."*
- **On the era's culture**, offered as period colour: *"you've heard of the two guys in a garage — well
  that was starting to happen at that time"*, and *"people are literally doing lines of coke."*
