# Why Branding Matters NOW More Than Ever (Personal & Business)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo.** Fully persona-eligible. Third parties (Apple, Microsoft, Steve Jobs)
> are analysed, not sources.

## Summary

Short and sharp, with one genuinely new claim and one very good worked example.

**The diagnostic question for whether you have a brand at all**: *"do people have **a preference for your
product or service over someone else's**?"* Set against the condition he thinks makes it necessary: *"we
live in a **highly, overly communicated world** and we have an abundance of choice."* His account of what
choice does to buying: *"as we make more money and have more choice, **the things that influence our
decisions are wildly different for each of us.** Some of you want a clear business outcome when you buy
something; **some of you want to feel like you belong.** So it's less about buying — **it's who you become
in the process, and the vote that you have with your money.**"*

★★ **The Apple/Microsoft store contrast**, which is the best-observed passage. He visits Apple stores
*"once every two and a half weeks"* and mostly buys nothing — *"I go there to watch people, I go there
just to put my hand on a device."* What he notices:

- *"**I've never been sold anything ever at an Apple store**, which is such an unusual experience. People
  say *hi, do you need help, do you need help* — that's all they ask, **and they don't even bother
  you.**"*
- **Who they hire**: *"they look like the people from the neighbourhood… **like they were hired from a
  United Colors of Benetton ad campaign.** People have afros, people have spots on their faces, people are
  albino — **they're all over the place and they don't have a very specific style or look.**"*
- **The Genius naming**: *"apple had a rather brilliant concept — they call it **the Genius**. It used to
  be for the genius behind the bar, and now **everyone is an Apple Genius. Everyone can help you.**"*
- **The operational punchline**: at a Microsoft store buying an Xbox controller he was *"fourth in the
  queue… there's nobody in here, but they've got **one person to ring you up.**"* Whereas *"**practically
  everybody inside the Apple store can ring you up, and the register is in their pocket** — they use an
  iPhone."* Hence: *"people are walking out with **multi-thousand-dollar purchases on the regular. That's
  how they can make so much money per square foot.**"* His own position is stated fairly: *"**I don't hate
  Microsoft. I just don't prefer them.**"*

★★ **Then the claim that makes this page worth promoting — the asymmetry of failure.**

## Key claims (2024-06-25)

- ★★ **A business brand can be rehabilitated; a personal brand often cannot.** *"**A business brand can be
  rehabilitated. Oftentimes a personal brand — once you f*** up, you're kind of done.**"* This is a real
  asymmetry the wiki does not yet record, and it is the strongest argument *against* the personal-brand
  advocacy he otherwise gives. **Promote it with that material, not instead of it.**
- ★★ **The structural comparison**, stated cleanly: *"most business brands are **scaled back** — their
  personality is tamped down a little, because it's collective and it's a lot of touchpoints. **A personal
  brand is one touchpoint: it's you.** A business brand has a logo; **you have a name, and your name is the
  most powerful part** — and you have a face. **A business has a place.**"*
- ★ **Everything on the face and body is signal**: *"tattoos, earrings, glasses, jewellery, cap, hairstyle,
  hair colour — **they all say something about you and the choices that you make.**"*
- ★ **The Steve Jobs aside**, which he uses to show that consistency beats polish: *"as much as I admire
  and respect Steve Jobs, **his fashion sense is terrible. The guy wore ill-fitting mom jeans.** Every time
  he walked on stage I snickered a little bit. **But who am I** — he's a revolutionary, a maverick changing
  industries. **He's got no time to wear proper jeans.**"*
- ★★ **The consistency test, and the harder question.** *"Act consistently with your worldview… **How many
  people can you spot from a mile away that aren't being themselves? They're fronting** — *I'm more
  successful than I am, I'm better looking than I am, my life is more together than it is.* And then we're
  shocked to discover they're getting a divorce — **I thought you had the picture-perfect marriage. It's
  perfect until you find out.** … So we can spot it. **The much harder question is: how many of you are
  guilty of it?**"*
- ★★ **The culpability distinction**, which is the sharpest line on the page: *"**you can be forgiven if
  you have low self-awareness** — *I don't even know what I'm doing, I don't know who I am, so I just show
  up whatever.* **But if you know, and you deliberately hide — that's creating a gap between you and the
  audience, the community and the clients you hope to connect with.**"*
- **What the Apple example is actually for**: *"the reason I bring this into sharp focus is because
  **you have to start thinking about who am I, how do I move around in this world, what are the conscious
  and unconscious decisions I'm making about how I interact with my clients — online, in person and over
  Zoom.**"*
