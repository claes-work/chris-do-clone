# 3 Traits of Irresistibly Magnetic People

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo.** Fully persona-eligible. He flags one element as **independently
> corroborated**: he asked his friend **Anneli** for her three traits ahead of a European tour and *"our
> list is **exactly the same**."*

## Summary

A tighter, better-argued restatement of the three traits from [[2024-04-16-yt-VYKA7hqZeZU]] — with **one
new precondition** and a **materially better definition of self-confidence**. Where the April video built
the framework by testing famous people, this one builds it by asking you to picture someone you're drawn
to and check the list against them.

★★ **The precondition, stated before the three traits and treated as non-negotiable: "magnetic people make
media."** He calls it **the 3M**. *"This could be written articles, writing books, taking photographs,
doing podcasts, making videos — and that might be **the first clue as to whether or not you're going to be
a magnetic person.** You have to commit to **publishing your thoughts in any form possible.** It doesn't
matter how you do it — through drawings, through graphics — but you have to communicate what's up here:
**share with people your story, how you frame the world, your opinion, your criticism. Whatever it is,
you must publish content.**"*

**Trait 1 — self-confidence, redefined against the obvious reading.** He spends most of his time on the
negative space. Not neediness: *"people who are really needy, who need you to reaffirm who they are,
they're insecure — **are those people attractive to you? … If anything, they suck up all the energy in the
room.**"* And explicitly not swagger: *"self-confidence isn't a person who walks in totally in shape,
square jaw, beautiful figure, bright-coloured suit, swinging the hair about — **and just says: look at me,
I'm more important than you. To me that's the opposite of self-confidence.**"* With a fair carve-out —
*"it's okay to wear your hair and heels and no socks; those are style decisions, no problem"* — before the
definition, which is the best in the corpus:

★★ *"**Self-confidence to me is a quiet love of oneself that creates space for other people to love
themselves too.**"*

**Trait 2 — high self-awareness**, with a sharper edge than the April version: *"they know who they are,
**they know and choose when they want to be disagreeable**, and they know how they're impacting you — and
**it is a conscious decision.**"* Then the reassurance: *"the good news is **you can work on both of these
things.** These are not things where I have to tell you: you have to grow taller."*

**Trait 3 — vulnerability**, which he again flags as the surprising one, and this time he explains the
dependency between all three: *"there's **a direct link** between self-confidence, self-awareness and
vulnerability — **they're kind of necessary ingredients.** If I am aware of what I like about myself **and
my shadow self**, and I'm able to show you it, **it's an act of courage** — and the demonstration is that
I'm being vulnerable. And **I can only be vulnerable if I feel safe and confident about who I am.**"*

His summary formulation: *"people who show up **100% who they are**, create space for others, **knowing and
choosing when to be what parts to whom — and being okay with all of it.**"*

## Key claims (2024-06-28)

- ★★ **"Magnetic people make media" (the 3M)** — publishing is a **precondition** for magnetism, not a
  consequence of it. New relative to the April framework.
- ★★ **Self-confidence = a quiet love of oneself that creates space for others.** Explicitly *not*
  attractiveness, dominance or self-promotion. Compare the April definition (*"a belief in your skills to
  solve a problem"*) — **these are two different definitions of the same word, three months apart, and
  both are his.** They are complementary rather than contradictory (one is about capability, one is about
  posture), but a synthesis pass should record both rather than pick.
- ★★ **The three traits are interdependent**: vulnerability requires safety, safety comes from
  self-confidence, and knowing what to reveal requires self-awareness. This dependency is the new
  contribution — the April video listed the traits, this one wires them together.
- ★ **Self-awareness includes choosing to be disagreeable** — deliberately, with knowledge of the effect.
- ★ **Both traits are trainable**, and he says so explicitly to head off the fatalistic reading.
- ★★ **The critical-thinking habit he demonstrates in passing**, which is worth extracting on its own:
  *"one of the things we do in critical thinking is **we come up with an idea and we try to disprove our
  own idea.** We check it immediately — **we don't wait for other people to tell us your logic is a little
  bit off.** You should **attack your own ideas with the same rigour you would a tweet that someone else
  posted.** Does this pass the smell test, the taste test?"*
- **The corroboration**, offered as evidence rather than decoration: he and **Anneli** independently
  produced identical lists.
