# 10x Your Revenue With This Sales Process w/ Daniel Priestley

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary; only Chris Do's lines train the persona.** **Daniel Priestley's
> FOURTH** attested appearance — see [[../entities/daniel-priestley]], where his frameworks are fenced.
> The sales process taught here is **his**.

## Summary

**The premise both agree on**, and Chris opens with it: *"most creative people start with the fact that
they love the work that they do — and **the cruel harsh reality is that to do the work that we love, we
have to win the work that we love.** A lot of creative people lean on the idea that **if they do great
work it'll sell itself.** There is no such thing as a super successful creative who isn't also good at
selling their work. **A successful creative business has a sales process attached to it.**"*

**Priestley's structure** (fenced): organise everything the prospect says into four buckets —
**current situation → desired situation → obstacle → criteria.** *"Sometimes I'm jumping around, but I'm
always organising it in my mind."* The discipline he adds is the useful part: ★ **minimum effective
dose** — *"you only need a **minimum effective dose** of information for *where are you now, where do you
want to be, and what's in the way.*"* Sometimes three minutes; sometimes thirty and still digging.

★★ **His signature move — "take it away from me."** Rather than arguing his own value, he hands the case
to the prospect: *"hey Chris, **you're a super smart guy, you're talented, you've got a 30-year background
with the business and there are amazing people who work there — why haven't you been able to do this on
your own?** I'm curious — why can't you just do this in house?"* And then: *"**they start making the case
that actually it's really hard for us to do this on our own.** I love to take it away from me."*

**The honesty moves he models**, which are the other keeper: naming his own pattern-matching out loud —
*"can I be cheeky? **I see so many people in your situation. I've got you pegged, I've got you in a box —
and I might be wrong, but I'm pretty sure**"* — and, when it isn't working, saying so: *"**hey, I know
this is frustrating, I'm asking all these questions and I'm still not getting it.** I'm not understanding
the key distinction — you keep saying there's this thing that you want, and to me it looks like you kind of
already have it."* Which is what finally unlocks the answer.

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★★ **The framing of the whole problem**: *"to do the work that we love, **we have to win the work that we
  love**"*, and the flat rejection of the meritocratic hope — *"**there is no such thing as a super
  successful creative who isn't also good at selling their work.**"*
- ★★ **The diagnosis of what creatives actually get wrong in discovery**, drawn from running live role
  plays: *"they ask questions that seemingly lead to **a bunch of dead ends** — they have no way of
  processing or organising [the answers], and that really **starts to erode trust, or it starts to
  irritate**, because [the client is thinking] *where are you going with this? We're not getting
  anywhere.*"* This is a genuinely useful failure mode: the problem is not asking questions, it is **having
  no structure to put the answers into.**
- ★ **He presses the guest for the honest version** of how deep discovery has to go, and gets both ends —
  three minutes on a pattern he recognises, thirty and still digging on one he doesn't.

## Fenced — Daniel Priestley (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Daniel Priestley. Full record on [[../entities/daniel-priestley]]._

- ★★ **Current situation → desired situation → obstacle → criteria** as the mental filing system for
  everything a prospect says.
- ★ **Minimum effective dose** of discovery — enough to understand, not more.
- ★★ **"Take it away from me"** — ask why they haven't solved it themselves, and let them build your case.
- ★ **Say when you're lost.** Naming the frustration in the room is what unlocks the distinction.
- **Genuine curiosity as the precondition**: *"it has to be backed up by — **I'm genuinely trying to
  understand the situation.**"*
