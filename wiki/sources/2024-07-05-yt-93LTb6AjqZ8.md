# You're Losing Millions by Ignoring This!

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo.** Fully persona-eligible. **Format note, dated:** he announces at the end
> that this is a deliberate experiment — *"we're trying something a little bit different… **we're doing
> solo episodes** — these are shorter, more contained, built around certain themes and questions"* — and
> asks for feedback on it. Worth recording as a **content-format change in July 2024**.

## Summary

★★ **The reframe that carries the whole piece, given in the first thirty seconds**: *"let's just **take
the label of content creation away** from this. We'll just call it **client acquisition.** Do you have a
client acquisition strategy? How do you plan on getting new clients?"*

**The cautionary tale.** *"When times are good you're busy feasting **and you're not planting any seeds in
the field** — so eventually your food stores, your warehouse, will run out and you'll go hungry."* His
diagnosis of the mechanism is unsentimental about his own peers: *"I have friends and I've seen this
happen to them time and time again. **They get fat — not literally, figuratively — and they get a little
bit lazy** because clients are coming in. They think they're the shiznit… **and they take their foot off
the gas pedal.**"*

★★ **And the claim he stakes hardest**: *"**and this always happens. It always happens. I can guarantee
you: your clients eventually change.** They become tired of you and they go elsewhere."* Scaled up: whole
agencies built around one automotive client, *"10, 20, 30 years later,"* losing the account or half of it
to *"some young up-and-coming competitor [who] figured out something you haven't, **because you've taken it
for granted.**"* His conclusion: *"if you know this is an inevitability, **then you need to prepare for
this.**"*

**Why content specifically.** He lists the alternatives fairly — in-person networking, dinner parties,
masterminds, *"old-school sales calls, knocking on doors"* — and then rules them out on one criterion:
*"all of those things take time and energy, but **those kinds of efforts are not scalable.** There are only
so many [of] you to go around, only so many meetings you can have."* Content is the exception: *"one thing
that has **infinite scale, infinite reach.**"*

**The gratitude argument**, and it is the most personal passage: *"back in my day **there was no social
media. The media was expensive to buy, the ability to reach people was questionable, and your ability to
track the results — the metrics — was impossible.** We took out old-school ads in magazines, in books and
directories, we sponsored shows and events — **I'm not sure if any of them ever resulted in one new
client.** So we should be grateful that we live in a time and space where you can quite literally
communicate to the world **at the amazing price of free.**"*

★★ **Then the part that saves it from being a pep talk** — what to do when content isn't working (below).

## Key claims (2024-07-05)

- ★★ **Content creation *is* client acquisition.** The relabel is the argument: if you resent it as
  content, ask instead whether you have a client-acquisition strategy at all.
- ★★ **Client loss is an inevitability, not a risk.** Prepare during the good times — *"working **in** your
  business and not **on** your business"* is the failure.
- ★ **Networking and outreach don't scale; content does.** Stated as the sole reason to prefer it.
- ★★ **Dread degrades the output, and the output then confirms the dread.** *"When you dread something it
  influences your state… you're going to give it some partial effort, **and you self-sabotage, and then
  the outcome is exactly predictable.** *Well, the content I wrote didn't get anybody excited, it didn't
  lead to any customers, Chris.* Well, yeah — **because you didn't care. You didn't put your love into it.
  You look tired and uninspired.**"*
- ★★ **"It's not that content doesn't work — it's that *your* content doesn't work."** The full form:
  *"it's not that the platform was broken; **it's that your content on that platform isn't working for the
  audience you're trying to attract.**"* Followed by the remedy, which he flags as self-serving and says
  anyway: *"**I hate to say this because it sounds self-serving, but you've got to learn a thing or two
  about storytelling. You've got to learn how to write.**"*
- ★★ **The hardest commitment, named as such**: *"you have to do **the most difficult thing that any
  business can do**, which is to say: **I'm going to be committed to this specific client. I'm going to
  niche down**, because I understand this client really well. **Most of us can't make that commitment —
  so we broadcast generic messages to generic people.** And when has that ever worked?"*
- ★ **More demand than capacity is what buys everything else**: *"we'd all be much more confident if we had
  more customers to serve than we had capacity to [serve]… **it would mean you could be choosier** — turn
  away work that doesn't fit your schedule, doesn't fit the creative brief, or doesn't pay what you want."*
- ★★ **How he actually used a hot streak**, self-reported: *"when we're hot — and there's a moment in time
  when you're hot — **you're able to find who you are, you can develop systems, you can hire the best
  people, and you can continue to raise your rate until you find whatever theoretical ceiling** you're
  going to find. **And you can only do that because you get to say no to everybody — and the best way to
  say no is to raise your price**, so only the ones who can afford you will say yes. **You put the decision
  in their court.**"*
