# How to Turn Job Loss into a Creative Career Opportunity w/ Matt Bretz

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary; only Chris Do's lines train the persona.** The guest is **Matt Bretz**
> — described by Chris as *"a friend, a client, and… just a person to wrap ideas with"* — a creative whose
> core capability is storytelling and who has worked across many channels. **His layoff experience and
> what he did about it are his.**

## Summary

A conversation about what actually happens after a senior creative loses a job, and it produces one
genuinely transferable rule.

★★ **Bretz's rule — "open every door."** When he announced the loss publicly, the responses sorted into
two kinds: the generic (*"I know you're going to be fine… let me know if there's something I can do"*) and
the specific (*"if you want to talk, I'm here"* / *"give me a call if you think there's something I can
do"*). His practice: *"**every single one of those people I have had coffee with, a call with, a meal
with — no matter how obvious or completely unobvious it was that they could do something for me from my
perspective.**"*

The payoff came from the least likely place: *"there was a **junior copywriter who worked with me years
ago** who said *let me know if you'd like to get on a call.* And I said okay. **In talking to them, I
learned about an opportunity they were working on that they were able to bring me in on.** And **I would
never have foreseen this person and this moment** as something new for me — **and I was just wrong.**"*
Hence: *"**take, open every door — because it will lead to the next door.**"*

**Chris's reflection** is the persona-eligible half, and it does two things: it names the *precondition*
Bretz is too modest to claim, and it supplies the counter-example.

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★★ **The humility precondition.** *"What I'm getting from what you're saying is **there's a humility to
  say: hey, this happened** — and not to hold it inside of you. And then the universe, or **the community
  that you've cultivated for the last x number of years or decades, will respond.**"*
- ★★ **He corrects the guest's own explanation.** Bretz attributes his response rate to being an
  extrovert; Chris says that isn't it: *"you say it's a more natural thing for an extrovert, and I think
  that's part of it — **but the real part of it is you've been a genuinely good human being. So when
  things happen to you, for you, with you, then you reap the seeds that you've planted.**"*
- ★★ **The counter-example, which is what makes it a claim rather than a platitude.** *"**Because if you
  were a jerk** — and I've seen this happen too. **We've had clients who were total bullies and did not
  treat us well, and eventually they got laid off or fired** for different reasons. **Everybody's like
  dancing on their grave.** You know — karma's a… you know how that works."* Stated from his own client
  history, not as a general moral.
- ★ **The mechanism, stated plainly**: *"**if you hold it in, no one knows.** It's a secret."* Community
  can only mobilise around information it has.

This is **karmic equity** (already on the content-strategy hub as *give-first*) observed from the
**receiving** end, years later — which the wiki does not currently have. The give-first material argues
what to do; this shows what it pays out, and when.
