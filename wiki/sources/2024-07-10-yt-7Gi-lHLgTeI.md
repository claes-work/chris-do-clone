# Introducing YouTube Memberships — Help Fund Our New Business Venture

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo, first person throughout.** Fully persona-eligible, and unusually
> operational: this is a **business-model announcement**, not a teach. Credited in-line: **Kevin Kelly**
> (*1,000 True Fans*) and **Anna Lappé** (*"every dollar you spend is a vote"*).
>
> ⚠️ **It is also an ask.** He says so — *"in a little bit I'm going to do something very rare: I'm going
> to ask you for something."* Record it as a **promotional/fundraising video**, not a neutral teach.

## Summary

★★ **The clearest statement in the corpus of The Futur's business-model problem, and of the pivot he wants
to make.**

**The dilemma, stated plainly.** *"I'm an educator at heart… **I want to release it all for free** for you
all the time — **but it's in direct opposition to me as an entrepreneur** and my ability to support and
grow my team and pay them a livable wage."* And the invisible cost: *"this is free for you to watch… **but
we pay people to help us produce.** Although you just see me on camera, there's lots of people behind the
camera."*

**The team, named** (a rare operational roll-call): **Diego and Mo** making content, editing and managing
channels; **Amy** handling brand-sponsorship opportunities *"and making sure we get the right ones that
are in line with what we do"*; **Rich** booking podcast guests and making strategic suggestions. Plus
writers, producers and editors.

**Why now**: *"**I'm a person who's not satisfied with stagnation, and we've hit some kind of ceiling
here.**"*

★★ **The metric he wants to be judged on.** *"Having a lot of subscribers or followers is nice, but many
people would refer to that as **a vanity metric**. The real metric that matters is **the value per fan**.
So if we have 100,000 people willing to support us… then we know **we really have 100,000 fans. We don't
really have two and a half million fans** — and I want to test if this model is true."*

**The sad story he tells against himself** — dated and specific: *"around **2019** I converted my entire
office space and my company to creating content 100% full-time,"* including *"a giant building… converted
into a dedicated event space where we can host workshops for over 100 people. **And we actually pulled it
off** — we invited none other than **Marty Neumeier**… to host a workshop, the first of its kind on brand
strategy. We had people flying from all over the world, including Australia and China."* Then: *"**that
was the first and last ever in-person experience we ran in our office space**, because the pandemic
happened… **so all this stuff that was being built up was just sitting there gathering dust and
cobwebs.**"*

**What the money is for**: a **hybrid learning space in Pasadena** — physical, open to some community
members, for podcasts with live audiences, *"experimental prototype workshops"*, exclusive dinners, and
**apprentices working with him in the studio.** His stated reason is an editorial observation, not a
preference: *"**the best-performing content on the channel involves real people working in a real space
working together.** … Some of our best content comes from **the tension that exists between me the teacher
and the student trying to figure out an idea together — and that friction is what creates the magical
moments**"* — naming *how to price a logo* and *tell me why I should buy this* as the exemplars.

**The offer**: two membership tiers — **$1/month** (a badge; *"no matter what your economic standing is in
the world, I think you can do this"*) and **$15/month** (unlisted, unreleased and unfiltered longer-format
content *"that should be courses that we charge money for"*). Free content continues; premium-destined
content gets a **48-hour free window** first *"so you have the opportunity to watch it for free."* The
arithmetic: **2.5M subscribers growing ~20,000/month**; *"if we got **less than 5% of you** to step up —
let's say 100,000 people — I would be able to pay my team a livable wage… and fund the creation of this new
hybrid learning space."*

★★ **The strategic point of the whole thing**: *"we will radically change the business model, **moving
away from courses to a subscription model.**"* And what that buys him: *"so that **we don't have to market
and create funnels, we don't have to create courses — we can just teach.** And that is where I'm in my
happy place. **That is me in my zone of genius.**"*

## Key claims (2024-07-10)

- ★★ **Value per fan > follower count.** The membership push is explicitly framed as **a test of whether
  the audience is real.**
- ★★ **The intended pivot: courses → subscription.** Stated motive is not margin but freedom from
  marketing and funnel-building.
- ★★ **Dated operational facts**: 2.5M subscribers, ~20K/month growth; two tiers at **$1** and **$15**; a
  **48-hour** free window before premium content moves behind the tier; the 2019 office-to-studio
  conversion; the Marty Neumeier brand-strategy workshop as **the first and only in-person event** before
  the pandemic; a planned **Pasadena** hybrid space; team members **Diego, Mo, Amy, Rich** named by role.
- ★ **Current revenue lines, listed by him**: digital courses and resources; group coaching (**the Pro
  Group** and **Accelerator**); public speaking; and **one-on-one consulting and coaching** — the last of
  which he flags as news to most viewers: *"**I'm rather reluctant to talk about it.**"*
- ★★ **1,000 True Fans** (Kevin Kelly, correctly credited as a Wired co-founder and executive editor) as
  the model: *"you don't need 100,000 followers… **you need 1,000 true fans** who are willing to step up."*
- ★ **The honest self-assessment about promotion**: *"we've been kind of keeping it secret **because I hate
  marketing and sales and I'm not very good at it, to be honest with you.** I want to create value for you
  and hope in some way you find your way to our website."* Followed by the correction he applies to
  himself: *"**every once in a while we have to let you know what we're doing** so that you can find ways
  to support us as we support you. **This is how societies are built: you get value, you give value.**"*
- ★ **The comparison he uses to justify the ask**, and it is pointed: *"you pay for a Netflix subscription,
  maybe Skillshare or MasterClass. **How many of those things that you paid for actually put money in your
  pocket** [and] left you feeling more confident, more capable and more able to do what it is that you do?
  **Chances are, probably not many.**"*
- **The mission restated in its sharpest form**: *"**disrupting the education industrial complex to make
  education affordable for everyone.**"* Compare *"private art school without the crippling debt"* in
  [[2024-04-16-yt-VYKA7hqZeZU]].
