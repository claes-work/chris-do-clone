# Are Your Beliefs Limiting Your Potential?

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary**, coaching a live room (participants **Alis**, **Kia**, and team
> member **Amy** volunteer their own fears). Persona-eligible; participants' statements are context.
> The triangle's **bidirectional** reading is credited to **Matthew S—** (surname garbled in captions),
> from a Clubhouse conversation.

## Summary

The stated subject is criticism; the actual subject is **the belief that prevents you publishing at all**.

**The origin of the inner critic**, as he frames it: *"in psychology [it's] the **inner critical voice**,
and the basic concept is **we tend to parent ourselves the way we were parented.** If you have loving,
supportive parents… you're going to tend to have that same inner dialogue. If you have parents who are
very belittling, who always diminish your efforts, **that's the voice that you carry around.**"*

★ **His stated belief about what would change everything**: *"each and every single one of you — your
career, your life, your happiness **would improve if you were freer to make the kinds of things that
scared you.**"* With the image he attributes vaguely: *"if you step outside your zone of comfort, **that
circle, that zone, expands to meet you.**"*

**The three fears the room produces**, and what he does with each:

1. **"I'll be judged for how I talk."** (A participant: *"people have a misconception about what I mean
   when I say I'm ghetto country."*) Chris asks for a 1–10 fear rating and the threshold at which it stops
   action — *"if I let it get to an eight then **I get paralysed**"* — then asks the diagnostic question:
   **is it real or perceived?** The answer, *"most of it is perception,"* is the hinge, and he generalises
   it: ★ *"a lot of us have the perception that we're getting negative feedback **even when in fact it's
   neutral to positive.** … We filter in neutral messages and **turn it and amplify it into something
   negative** — because perhaps the word, the cadence, or even the accent reminds us of **someone in our
   childhood who had a lot of power over us**: a teacher, a coach, your parents, a sibling. **Probably
   from the ages of three to nine.**"*
2. **"I don't want to be one of the loud people."** His read: *"we think they're sucking up all the oxygen
   and we don't want to be like them, **so therefore we don't act like them.** … And oddly enough, **either
   we don't say anything or we wind up rambling on** — so neither is good."* Because the nervousness
   itself produces the thing feared.
3. ★★ **"I have nothing to say that anybody's going to care about."** (Amy.) Chris tests it — *"how do you
   feel if I just say to you: **no one cares**?"* — *"I guess I'm fine with it, because I don't post."*
   His diagnosis: *"**I don't say anything, so no one can care; so I don't have to say anything, so no one
   will care.** … Often I find this to be true: **it's a pretty good self-defence mechanism.**"*

★★ **The triangle, and the fix.** Belief → action → result → which cements the belief. His illustration
with Amy: belief *"no one cares"* → action *"nothing"* → result *"nothing"* — *"and **that's the wonderful
thing about nothing: it gets nothing. It's safe. No negative feedback to deal with**"* → which confirms
*"no one cares."* Then the contribution he credits to **Matthew**: *"if we want to enact real change we
should look at this as **not a one-direction thing — it's bidirectional.** So actually **the results can
shape our action, which can then change our belief.**"*

Run backwards with Amy, live: desired result → *"help others"*; required action → *"write an article,
write a post"*; resulting belief → *"maybe it'll help somebody"* → **"someone cares"** → and then, *"when
you feel that someone cares, what will you do?"* — *"you're going to help more people."*

## Key claims (2024-07-16)

- ★★ **Run the triangle backwards.** You cannot argue yourself out of a belief; you can produce a result
  that makes the old belief untenable. Start from the **result you want**, derive the **smallest action**,
  and let the belief follow. (Credited in this form to Matthew.)
- ★★ **"I have nothing to say" is a self-defence mechanism**, not an assessment — and it is
  self-confirming, because silence generates no evidence against it.
- ★ **Most perceived criticism is amplified neutral feedback**, and the amplification usually traces to
  someone who held power over you between roughly ages three and nine.
- ★ **The fear-rating drill**: rate the fear 1–10, then name **the number at which you stop acting.** It
  converts a vague dread into a threshold you can observe.
- ★ **The avoidance paradox**: fear of becoming the rambling, loud person produces either silence or
  rambling — *"neither is good."*
- ★★ **The designer version, from twenty years of hiring**: *"designers that have come through our office
  for over two decades **have a very narrow window of things that they'll try.** If it's a style, an
  aesthetic or a typeface they're not comfortable with, **they won't do it — because they're afraid: if I
  do this, the whole thing's going to come apart, everybody's going to realise I'm a fraud and I only know
  how to do one thing. So I'm just going to stick to that one thing** — which then **says you only know
  how to do one thing. It perpetuates itself.**"*
- ★ **We are harsher to ourselves than about others**: *"whatever harsh things we might say about other
  people, believe it or not, **we say and use much harsher language when it comes to speaking to
  ourselves.**"*
