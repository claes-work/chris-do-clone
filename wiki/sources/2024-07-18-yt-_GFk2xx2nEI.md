# Originality, Pricing & Success — What Creatives Get Wrong

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary.** An **ADPList leadership series** interview hosted by **Felix**;
> Chris is the guest, so his answers dominate and are persona-eligible. Credited in-line: **Paul Rand**
> (*"don't try to be original, just try to be good"*).

## Summary

Three strands, and two of them are landmark-quality.

★★ **1. Originality.** *"**True originality is very, very, very rare — more rare than gold.**"* Via Paul
Rand: *"don't try to be original — **just try to be good, because being good is really difficult.**
Everybody's so fascinated by trying to be original. You know what — **if you do something that's good**,
and you've learned how to do this over time, **you might add to the canon** of what original content
there was."*

★★ **2. The story formula, and the argument that creativity lives in the variables.** His position is
counter-intuitive and he commits to it: *"there are formulas, and [the successful ones] follow the exact
formula. **I would argue the more creative they try to be — and not follow the formula — the less likely
it is to succeed.**"* The reasoning: *"if… over a thousand, 2,000, 10,000 years of human history **the
stories that endure have these common traits**, and the other stories we don't talk about anymore — then
**we're kind of foolish to say: well, after 10,000 years of experimentation, I'll be the one who changes
that formula.**"*

He backs it with his own experience of the alternative, from ArtCenter: *"art-house films… experimental
French *nouveau* whatever it is — and afterwards you're like: **I'm not sure what I was watching. There
were plot threads that went nowhere, characters I couldn't understand, an unsatisfying resolution — I
didn't see the character change, I didn't learn anything.**"*

★★ **And then the proof, worked live** — the same romantic-comedy structure with one variable swapped:
*Romeo and Juliet* (two lovers, families hate each other — *"well, in 2024 no one cares what their parents
think, so that story is not as relevant today"*) → they can't be together because **one of them is gay**
→ because they're **interracial** → because **she's a maid and he's rich** (*Maid in Manhattan*) → because
**he's a total loser** → because **she owns an independent bookstore and he owns the chain** (*You've Got
Mail*). *"It's literally the same story structure, but we're fascinated because **we're trying to figure
out: do they get together or not?**"*

★★ **The craft rule he draws from it**: *"the creativity comes in **changing the variables** — and when you
change one variable **you have to make sure there's a meaningful counterbalance to that.** If you just
change one thing and don't address the rest of the structure, **you're going to be ruined.**"*

★★ **3. The invisible work — and the timeline that finally dates his teaching career.** He describes
meeting a pastor of *"40-plus years"* who told him, *"modestly and matter-of-factly,"* that he could give
*"a TED-level talk, 18 minutes, on any subject you want, with about 30 minutes of preparation."* That
prompts the arithmetic: *"**I'm 10 years into my public speaking journey.** What would I be like in 30
years? … **I started at 42. What if I started at 32?** … I'd be at least two times better — but I don't
think it works on a linear scale. **I think I'd be like 4x better.**"*

Then the correction he gave his wife, and it is the best passage in the interview (below), including a
**dated fact the corpus has been circling for a long time**: *"what people don't know is **I've been doing
public speaking for 10 years, but I was teaching at that point probably for 13 years prior to that.** So
**I started teaching when I was 26 — I think five years out of school, 27.**"*

## Key claims (2024-07-18)

- ★★ **True originality is vanishingly rare; aim for good.** (Paul Rand.) Good, sustained, may itself
  become the canon.
- ★★ **Follow the story formula; innovate in the variables.** Deviating from a structure refined over
  millennia is arrogance, not creativity — and every changed variable needs a **counterbalance** elsewhere
  in the structure.
- ★★ **The rom-com proof**: six films, one structure, one swapped obstacle. A genuinely reusable teaching
  device.
- ★★ **The invisible work**, named as such: *"**this is the work that is invisible to everyone — the work
  that no one sees, notices or cares about, that you don't even talk about. You just do the work. You show
  up.**"* In his case: years with *"creative students from advertising, design and illustration"*, helping
  them *"manifest their vision"* and learning *"to react and respond to things in real time, to solve
  problems — conceptual problems, emotional problems."*
- ★★ **Dated career timeline (self-reported)**: started **teaching at ~26–27**, *"five years out of
  school"*; **~13 years of teaching before public speaking**; **~10 years of public speaking** as of 2024,
  begun at **42**. This reconciles several loose threads in the biography — the "15+ years teaching at
  ArtCenter" and the "YouTube at 42" facts now sit on one line.
- ★ **The cost, stated without varnish**: *"it is painful. I'm not going to delude you. **It's
  excruciating.** It's losing sleep at night thinking about the talk, walking around like a zombie trying
  to rehearse, **it's bombing on stage, it's having the cold sweats and an upset stomach — but that's the
  price of entry.**"*
- ★ **What he says he found on the other side**: *"**to find your voice** — to be able to move people
  physically, spiritually, emotionally with the power of your voice, and the energy, the vibration, the
  frequency that you send out."* (Note: the energy/frequency register already carries a
  balance-it guard in the system prompt.)

  wasn't.**"*
- ★★ *"She goes, *come on babe, give me the real answer.* I said: **no — it's the first step. You have to

- ★ *"If you're contemplating starting — **just start already.**"*
