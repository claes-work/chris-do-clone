# Avoiding the Clickbait Trap: How to Keep Your Audience's Trust

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo.** Persona-eligible. Part of the July 2024 short solo-episode format
> announced in [[2024-07-05-yt-93LTb6AjqZ8]]; ends with an explicit two-parter promise.

## Summary

★★ **The definition, and it is the useful part**: *"there's a nuance to this — **if the content actually
delivers on the promise or the hook, then it's not considered clickbait, no matter how sensational it
is.**"* So the test is **delivery**, not intensity.

**The supermarket illustration**: the tabloid *"mom has alien baby"* — *"you know in your heart that can't
be true, but it's so intriguing."* And the honest admission: *"we've all been there — **or at least I
have. Maybe I'm confessing something about myself.** I've gone down those rabbit holes… *tell me about
that child star* — **and 30 clicks later, nothing.**"* Then the consequence, in his own voice: *"**man,
you fooled me. And Mama didn't raise no fools.** So at some point I'm going to be very aware of **who you
are and how you show up online.**"*

He gives his own current examples — Tesla and Porsche content, and the Marvel Cinematic Universe — where
*"you won't believe what happened / Marvel makes major decision"* leads to ten minutes and no decision.
His verdict is unusually even-handed about the incentive: *"they're doing it for the views so they can
monetise their channel. **I get it. I understand — somebody somewhere needs to make a dollar.** … **But
every time they do that, they take away a little bit of my trust.**"*

★★ **The stake**: *"**the number one thing that I don't think you can afford to lose is trust.** That's
what brands are about. That's what **you** are about. So if you keep showing up one way, **eventually
people write you off — and it's going to be very difficult for you to regain that trust.**"* Hence:
*"there's a short-term gain, **but it comes at a long-term loss.**"*

**And the counterweight, which stops it being a purity lecture.** He names the opposite failure with equal
force: *"that's not to say you can't make sensational headlines — because we know **if you don't interrupt
someone's feed, they're not going to pay attention**, and all that hard work, all the research, all the
goodwill you put into the content **is not going to be seen. And that can be just as devastating as
clickbait content that doesn't work** — you put all this heart and energy into it and **no one shows up.
It's like crickets chirping in the field.**"* His example of permitted sensationalism: *"if you helped
someone generate 200,000 views — **for real, it's verifiable** — don't be afraid to say *this one idea
helped them get 200,000 views.* **Some people are too conservative**… it's not [clickbait] if it's the
truth."*

★ **A concrete AI workflow**, which is the practical takeaway: give a headline to a large language model,
say you're worried it's too clickbaity, and ask for *"10 alternates that vary [in] degree of clickbaitness
— **I don't even know that's a word, but GPT is so smart it will just know.**"* Then: *"**step back from
yourself and say: if I was consuming my own content, which one am I more likely to be attracted to** that
doesn't feel too scammy or [isn't] part of my brand voice?"*

## Key claims (2024-07-21)

- ★★ **Clickbait is a delivery failure, not a tone.** Sensational + delivered = fine. Sensational +
  undelivered = clickbait.
- ★★ **Trust is the asset you cannot afford to spend**, and it is spent in small increments that the
  spender doesn't notice.
- ★★ **Under-selling is also a failure.** *"If you don't interrupt someone's feed, they're not going to
  pay attention"* — and unseen good work is *"just as devastating."* This is the sentence that makes the
  page worth promoting: it's an anti-clickbait argument that **doesn't** counsel timidity.
- ★ **If it's true, say it boldly.** *"Some people are too conservative… **it's not [clickbait] if it's
  the truth, and the truth will set you free.**"*
- ★ **The AI headline-variation workflow** — generate a spectrum, then judge as your own audience.
- ★★ **The values test, delivered with a shrug that is entirely characteristic**: *"no matter what — if you
  get followers, if you get subscribers, or more money from AdSense — **if at the end of the day it goes
  against your core values, then maybe you don't have core values. There, I said it.**"*
- **On why people end up there**: unseen content *"will probably lead you to some dark alleys where you
  start to **hack your way through the algorithm.**"* The pressure is real and he names it rather than
  moralising.
