# Stop Hiding Who You Really Are: Be Authentic w/ Corey Poirier

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary; only Chris Do's lines train the persona.** The guest is **Corey
> Poirier**, a speaker, author and interviewer who has interviewed a very large number of public figures.
> **His observations and the people he cites are his.**

## Summary

A conversation about authenticity in public speaking, most of it Poirier's, built from his interview
back-catalogue.

★★ **His two opening claims** are the useful ones. First: *"**stop trying to become the next Tony
Robbins.**"* Second, a self-editing test that is genuinely reusable: *"**if you get bored after five
minutes — or two minutes, or three minutes — then you shouldn't expect the audience to sit there listening
to your one story for an hour.**"*

His definition: *"in a world where I feel everything is filtered, **being authentic to me is being
unfiltered.**"* And his diagnosis of the failure mode: *"**far too often speakers don't know how to engage
with the audience.**"*

**The evidence he offers is his interview log**, and it is specific rather than abstract:

- **Mark Victor Hansen** (*Chicken Soup for the Soul*) — authentic *"even [in] how he feels about business,
  how he feels about politics — an area where a lot of people won't go. **And I'm not even saying they
  should**, but I'm impressed by his ability to say: this is who I am, take me [as I am], **I don't make
  apologies.**"*
- **Tommy Chong** (Cheech & Chong), interviewed at 84 on the Sunset Strip: showed up *"wearing two
  different shoes"*, got lost in an alley, and immediately identified the producer's home town —
  *"**what part of Edmonton are you from?**"* Poirier's read on his promotional style: *"**I care about
  changing people's lives, allowing them to escape the crappy day they might have with my comedy — but I
  don't feel the need to sell you on it.** … **Disarming in a good way.**"*
- **Robin Sharma** (*The Monk Who Sold His Ferrari*, *The Leader Who Had No Title*) — *"sharing parts of
  his life that most people don't share."*

His own careful hedge, which is worth recording because it keeps the claim honest: *"**it's not to say
that it's the only way to do it or the best way** — it's just what I've noticed."*
