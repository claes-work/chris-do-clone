## Summary

A live listening drill rather than a lecture, which is what makes it useful.

**Her framing**: *"if your sales calls haven't been going very well — or maybe you've never had any sales
calls — **your listening skills can make or break the entire conversation.**"*

★★ **The drill.** Chris talks; a participant then has to answer three escalating questions:

1. **What was the main takeaway** from what he said?
2. **Which specific words or phrases** did he use? — with her constraint: *"**we don't want you to repeat
   everything like a parrot** — that's not the goal. But there does need to be **some level of you knowing
   which pieces to pull out of the conversation.**"*
3. ★★ **What was Chris experiencing while he was talking to you? What was he feeling?** — read live off
   his face, in front of him.

The separation she insists on is the pedagogical point: *"**today is not so much about the quality of your
response** — we'll get into that more on Sunday. **Today is just: how much are you actually picking up on
what he's sharing with you.**"* Comprehension first, response later.

**The scoring moment** is the best passage: after the participant's summary, Chris confirms it and then
re-states his own meaning in a compressed form — *"I noticed you're doing lots of things, I'm excited for
you, I just want to know what your plan is and if things are working out"* — and the participant's
takeaway, once stripped down, is *"you were interested and cared for me, and excited for me."* The gap
between the words said and the feeling received is the whole lesson.

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★★ **He compresses his own speech to reveal the signal underneath it** — *"I was trying to use fewer
  words, because all that means in essence [is] **you were interested and cared for me.**"* A live
  demonstration of intent-vs-transmission, which is the same framework he teaches himself in
  [[2024-05-14-yt-KCzE-wc0qC0]] (*"you are the transmitter… the intentionality between the transmission
  and the reception"*).
- ★ **He submits to being read**, and doesn't perform neutrality about it: asked what he is feeling, the
  room reads him as *"happy… now thoughtful… now he looks like he's evaluating you"* — and the running
  joke lands because it's true: *"**he can't hide, Chris.**"* Worth recording as a **voice/delivery**
  observation from outside: his face is legible, and he knows it.
- ★ **He normalises a non-native speaker losing a word mid-sentence**, unprompted: *"for those of you who
  are non-native speakers — **this happens to me too**, so I'm glad to put myself on the spot."* Small,
  and characteristic of how he manages a room.

- ★★ **The three-question listening drill** (takeaway → specific words → their emotional state), run live.
- ★★ **Comprehension before response.** Separating "did you hear it" from "did you answer well" is the
  structural move; most listening training conflates them.
- ★ **The parrot constraint**: mirroring everything is not listening; **selecting** what to pull out is.
- **The listening scorecard** as the instrument for all of the above.
- **The premise**: ghosting is downstream of a listening failure, not of a pitch failure.
