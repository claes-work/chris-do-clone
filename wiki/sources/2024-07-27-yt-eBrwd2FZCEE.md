## Fenced summary — Matt Essam (does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Matt Essam. Context only._

**Three pricing mistakes**, each introduced by its symptoms before it is named:

1. **Hourly/day-rate billing.** Symptoms: clients asking you to reduce days or hours; scope-creep
   arguments about amends; pressure to work faster; micromanagement (*"clients constantly breathing down my
   neck"*); a general feeling that your time isn't valued. His arguments against it: *"the faster you work
   the less you get paid"*; unbillable work (project management, research) is hard to charge for; a fixed
   ceiling of hours; and that an hour's value is *"quantified internally by that person"* rather than by
   any external measure.
2. **Undercharging.** Symptoms: a race to the bottom on price; nightmare clients who *"want a lot more than
   we feel they've paid for"*; a sense you can't do your best creative work; constant haggling. His claim:
   people undercharge hoping it wins the project, *"and in my experience the opposite is actually true"* —
   it attracts the wrong clients. He estimates *"like 80% of the creative industries are chronically
   undercharging."*
3. **Discounting.** Symptoms: clients who expect discounts and refer others who also expect them; feeling
   you're giving too much away. His mechanism: *"**something is worth what someone is willing to pay for
   it** — so if you allow them to pay that, in their mind **that is what it's worth.**"* It attracts
   *"bargain hunters."*

★ **His one genuinely clarifying distinction** (recorded because it is precise, **not** because it should
be promoted): **undercharging** is setting the price low yourself, invisibly, hoping to win;
**discounting** is lowering a stated price after the client pushes back. *"You don't have to discount to
undercharge."*

**Client case studies** (his): "Will" moving from £1,500–2,000 logos to £15K+ branding projects — *"but he
wasn't doing 10 times the work"*; "Alessandro" taking a budget from £12K to £25.5K in one conversation;
"Matt" winning a half-million-pound contract over 12 months. He frames the goal as closing the gap between
*"reality — how good you are"* and *"perception — how that client perceives those things."*
