# Dealing with Client Changes Like a PRO in 2024

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo.** Fully persona-eligible. The **Challenger Sale** is named as a source;
> the Audi story is his own.

## Summary

★★ **One of the most directly usable pieces on the sales-clients side of the corpus**, because it gives a
decision procedure *and* a script *and* the case where the answer is no.

**The opening reframe**, and it is blunt: *"you're in the service business, and the only way that you
have business is to be of service to your client. **They're not interfering with your art — you're
interfering with their money.** They afford you the life that you have… a lot of creative people
mistakenly believe themselves as artists, where you have a patron who just pays you to make whatever it is
that you make."*

★ **The invisible cost of a bad reaction**, which he says clients hide: *"they just say to themselves when
they get in the car: **never again.** And **whoever hired or recommended [you] — they're dead to me.** So
not only have you burned that relationship, **you burned the person who introduced you**, which is an
irresponsible thing to do."* Hence his own care with referrals — *"my reputation is on the line."*

**When to bend.** Clients with good taste who push you: *"they say — those ideas are great, **but how far
can we push it?** … **Those are dream relationships**, and many times they bear the best fruit, because
the client **gave you room to sort of hang yourself.**"*

★★ **When not to bend — the Audi A7 story.** Shooting the European debut of the A7 on a tight budget:
no techno-crane, no motion control, only a handheld jib. The precise, stable shot they needed was not
achievable. The footage came back *"kind of unusable"*, and the VFX supervisor had to **photo-map frames
onto a 3D CG car** (photogrammetry) to rescue it. His supervisor's verdict afterwards: *"it was **not
necessary** for us to shoot it on a jib arm if we couldn't use a techno-crane"* — they should have shot
**stills** instead, which are sharper and higher resolution than pulled video frames. The rule they wrote
from it: ★★ *"**when we know better — when we know this is going to lead to a bad result — do not agree,
do not comply.** Because at the end of the day they're not going to be happy, we're not going to be happy,
and **we're going to be eating it in the shorts**, spending all kinds of money to fix something we knew
was not going to work."*

★★ **And the honest boundary on that rule**: *"**there aren't that many cases** when you know that in fact
this is not going to work. **It's a technical thing**, and we as technical people in this situation know
better. **Most of the time you want to die on the hill for an aesthetic thing** and you believe your taste
is superior… **Aesthetics are subjective. No one has the ability to say this is always the best solution.
It's contextual.**"*

## Key claims (2024-08-04)

- ★★ **Be bamboo** — *"grows fast, very flexible, and super strong."* The governing metaphor.
- ★★ **"They're not interfering with your art — you're interfering with their money."**
- ★★ **Vendor → collaborator/consultant.** *"A vendor has no power in the relationship. **We need to shift
  our mindset from being a vendor to a collaborator or a consultant**, in which case they're paying you
  good money for your opinions and ideas and recommendations."*
- ★★ **The "you're the boss" script**, which he offers as the phrase that *"gets you out of a lot of
  trouble and gets you to have your way, and them thanking you for it"*:
  > *"**You're the boss. At the end of the day I will do exactly what you asked me to do — but hear me out
  > on this one thing. I'm concerned about X, because it would lead to Y. Having given you that
  > information, I'm relinquishing my responsibility. You need to make the decision that's best for
  > you.**"*
  His gloss: *"you have the power, I respect your authority — **but I have to voice something that I know
  is not going to work.**"*
- ★★ **The three-question triage**, to run *before* reacting emotionally:
  1. **Is the request reasonable and in scope?** → *"just say yes."*
  2. **Is it something you know technically leads to problems?** → *"stop, we have to have a serious
     conversation… it's not about subjectively me wanting X versus Y — **here's the problem, here's what's
     going to happen.**"*
  3. **Is it purely subjective?** — the test being, *"if you poll 10 creatives, 10 designers, 10
     directors, 10 editors and ask which one's best, you're not going to get the same answer"* → *"**maybe
     you just need to let that one go.**"*
- ★ **The Challenger Sale connection**, named: the aim is *"not to close a sale but **to get the clients to
  think: huh, I never thought of it that way before** — and to make them part of the decision-making
  process."*
- ★ **Referrals put your reputation in someone else's hands.** He vets who he refers to for that reason.
