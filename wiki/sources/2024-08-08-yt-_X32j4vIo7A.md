# Why You Should Start A Podcast To Grow Your Audience (2024-08-08)

> **Attribution:** a **solo monologue — Chris only, start to finish.** No guests, no co-hosts.
> Everything here is his except one credited idea.

## Summary

★★★★★ **Short and unusually high-yield.** It contains **a new detail in the much-told 2014 origin
story** — the specific offer that got him to say yes — a **candid account of resisting the podcast and
being wrong about why**, and the corpus's **best argument for podcasting**, built by comparison with
what people willingly pay to do on stage.

## Key claims (2024-08-08)

### ★★★★★ "I didn't want to do media at all"

> ★★★★★ *"Which reminds me — **why I didn't even want to do podcasts to begin with.** … **Actually, I
> don't want to do media at all.** I know it's really weird — you're listening to this, you see me
> everywhere, and you're like: **what, that guy doesn't want to do that? How could that be? That's so
> inconsistent with the way you're acting today.** Well — **sometimes we don't know what's good for us
> until it happens.**"*

★★★ **And the self-description, unprompted:** *"as a person who's **very reluctant to create content —
on YouTube, Instagram, every place. I'm just reluctant, I guess.**"*

### ★★★★★ The 2014 origin — with a detail the corpus did not have

> ★★★★★ *"Let me take you back to 2014. **My school friend Jose Caballer said: hey, let's go make some
> YouTube videos together — and I just did not want to do that.** But in that kind of **classic
> Godfather thing, HE MADE ME AN OFFER I COULD NOT REFUSE: HE SAID YOU GET TO SIT THERE AND SAY
> NOTHING, AND I WILL DO ALL THE TALKING.** And true to his word, he did all the talking and allowed
> me to say nothing. **But eventually I figured out: I WANT TO DO THIS THING. I CAN BE GOOD AT THIS
> THING IF I WORK AT IT.**"*

✅✅ **New.** The corpus records the 2014 start and Jose Caballer's role repeatedly (see
[[../entities/jose-caballer|his entity page]]), but **not the terms of the offer.** *"You get to sit
there and say nothing"* is the specific concession that got a reluctant person on camera — and it
explains the co-hosted format of the earliest episodes.

### ★★★★★ Why the podcast happened — and what he was wrong about

★★★★ **The request, and his refusal:** *"the audience who shows up says: **Chris, I can't watch a
YouTube video — can you make a podcast version of this?** And **I RESISTED FOR A LONG TIME.** I was
thinking: **why do you want to give us more work to do?** If it's literally taking the videos,
stripping out the audio and dumping it on a podcast — **why would I do that? Why would anyone
care?**"*

> ★★★★★ **What changed it, and he names the feeling:** *"**What I didn't realize — AND IT'S HUMBLING
> to think about it — is there are a lot of people who have TECHNOLOGICAL ISSUES, who don't have fast
> internet.** So there are people all over the world — say in the Philippines — **they would go to the
> library, they would DOWNLOAD the episode, and then they can listen to it. But YouTube didn't allow
> you to do that at that time.**"*

★★★ **The first version was deliberately minimal:** *"okay fine, **we'll do the MINIMUM VIABLE
PRODUCT** — just quickly translate YouTube episodes into podcasts."*

★★★★★ **Then the reframe:** *"**wait a minute — this is a COMPLETELY DIFFERENT MEDIUM. It's an
audio-only experience, and it's REALLY, REALLY INTIMATE. People are having your voice QUITE LITERALLY
IN THEIR HEAD, between their ears. YOU EXIST.** And **the relationship you have with someone on a
podcast is VASTLY DIFFERENT from the one you have with them on YouTube — THEY'RE SEEKING YOU OUT, and
they're SHARING A LITTLE BIT OF THEIR REALITY WITH YOU.**"*

★★★ **Credited:** *"another **SCOTT GALLOWAY** concept — he says **virtual reality is here, but it's
not happening in the eyes, it's happening in the ears.** … **that's what podcasts are: we're able to
experience real reality with some augmented version of reality, because we're hearing a story, we're
learning something WHILE WE'RE DOING SOMETHING ELSE.**"*

### ★★★★★ The argument — measured against what people pay to do on stage

★★★★ **His own unpaid-speaking period, itemised:** *"there was a period in my life when I wanted to do
public speaking — I wanted to practise, I wanted to teach, I wanted to build an audience. In the early
days **I didn't know what I was doing, so I'm not going to get paid. IN FACT I HAD TO PAY TO DO PUBLIC
SPEAKING.** What I mean is: **I worked on the deck for weeks, I wrote the material, I drove myself
there** — so it cost me time, money and resources — **and they didn't pay me anything. I PAID FOR MY
OWN PARKING. They didn't even reimburse me for my own parking.** … And then after I was done, they're
like: **don't let the door hit you on the way out.**"*

★★★★ **And what people willingly do for the same reach:** *"I know people on the speaking circuit who
have **taken a plane, stayed in a hotel — ALL AT THEIR OWN COST — so they can be in front of 200 or
300 people.** That's not even counting **the days and weeks of preparation**, the delivery, the
refinement."*

> ★★★★★ **The argument:** *"**If you create a podcast, you might not have more than 200 people
> downloading it — BUT YOU HAVE 200 PEOPLE SHOWING UP FOR YOU WEEK AFTER WEEK, listening intently,
> anxiously anticipating and consuming every word that you say.** So **YOU WOULD PAY to do it on a
> stage — BUT YOU'RE NOT WILLING TO INVEST THAT SAME TIME AND ENERGY INTO SOMETHING THAT YOU GET TO DO
> FOR ALMOST FREE.**"*

### ★★★★ The intimacy, and the brand tie

★★★★ *"People tell me this all the time and **it's a strange phenomenon.** They'll say: '**I went to
bed with you last night, with my wife**' — I'm like, **this is a little kinky, I didn't expect that** —
and they're like: no no, **we just listen to you.** And people say: **I dreamt about you.** Isn't that
wild — **the kind of intimate relationship you can have. IT'S NOT EXACTLY BIDIRECTIONAL, BUT IT'S
SUPER SCALABLE**, so that **YOUR VOICE IS IN THEIR HEAD.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"And when we say — **what do brands do? THEY OCCUPY SPACE IN YOUR HEAD.** And this is what I
> get to do with the people who show up and listen and consume our content."*

★★★ **The close:** *"**I love doing podcasts now. I actually look forward to [it].**"*
