# The Secret To Becoming Successful In Any Industry

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary**, teaching to a room. Persona-eligible. Heavily credited throughout:
> **Seth Godin** (the source claim), **Jason Fried & David Heinemeier Hansson** (*Rework*, 37signals),
> **Simon Sinek** (*The Infinite Game*), **Thomas J. Watson** (IBM), **Brian Tracy** (*The Power of
> Self-Confidence*). **The framework is assembled, not originated** — record it that way.

## Summary

Built around a line **Seth Godin** gave him as a guest on the show, which he says caught him off guard:
★★ *"What's the secret [to] innovation? … **Whoever fails the most wins.**"* Chris's first reaction is the
audience's: *"what if you fail — isn't it over?"* Godin's answer: *"**no, because what we're talking about
is the ability to play the game the longest.**"*

**His unpacking** is the contribution. The word *fail* triggers catastrophic images — *"bankruptcy, maybe
prison, foreclosure"* — so *"we do everything in our power to avoid failing."* The reframe requires
separating scale from kind: **small, survivable, learn-forward failures.**

★★ **The gambling arithmetic**, via *Rework*: don't make **one $100,000 gamble**; make **ten $10,000
gambles**, applying the learning forward each time — *"hopefully by the sixth time you fail, you've blown
$60K — and at that point you've gained all of what doesn't work."* The design implication he states as a
question to the room: *"**am I designing my company to fail in small iterative ways** so that I can learn
from my mistakes — and that I'm not making one giant gamble?"*

★ **The two-sided payoff**: *"if it fails and we've tried our best, then we know we don't have anything to
worry about. **If it succeeds, we disrupt ourselves.**"* **Netflix** given as the case — physical media →
streaming as an initial gamble with little content, now spawning *"an army of clones."* And the honest
limit he puts on his own example: *"**that will only last so long.** Let's see what the next arc of the
jump is — **I can't see it, because this is not my space.**"*

**Simon Sinek's infinite game**, as he retells it: Microsoft employees focused on outmanoeuvring
competitors; Apple employees, when told a rival's product was better, answered *"yeah, I think theirs is
better."* His conclusion: *"**Apple does not compete against other people — they compete against
themselves.** They tune out what other people are doing and they stay the course."*

★★ **The two supporting citations**, both dated to the talk:
- **Thomas J. Watson** (IBM founder), asked how to be successful: *"**double your failure rate.** If you
  want to be successful, **fail twice as fast.** On the far side of failure is success."*
- **Brian Tracy**, *The Power of Self-Confidence* — a study of self-made millionaires asked how many ideas
  they went through before the one that worked: *"**the median number was like 20-something.** … That
  should blow your mind — **these self-made millionaires failed 20-plus times on average** before they
  found the idea that worked."*

## Key claims (2024-08-11)

- ★★ **"Whoever fails the most wins"** — credited to Seth Godin, and glossed as *playing the game the
  longest*, not as recklessness.
- ★★ **Ten small gambles beat one large one**, with learning applied forward (*Rework*).
- ★★ **Design the organisation to fail small and often**, deliberately, as a cultural choice.
- ★ **Success and failure both resolve well** if the bets are sized right: information, or self-disruption.
- ★ **Compete against yourself** (Sinek's infinite game; the Apple/Microsoft contrast).
- ★ **Double your failure rate** (Watson) and **~20 ideas before the one that works** (Tracy).
- **The emotional obstacle named first**: the word *fail* carries catastrophic associations that don't
  match the actual stakes of an iterative bet — the reframe has to start there.
