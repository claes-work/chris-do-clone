# How Top Brands Create Products People Love

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary**, teaching to a room. Persona-eligible. **The spine of the argument is
> Marty Neumeier's** *The Designful Company* and he says so repeatedly — *"I got this information from the
> book"*, *"as explained in the book by Marty Neumeier."* **Herbert Simon**'s definition of design is also
> credited. Record the frame as Neumeier's; the teaching and examples are Chris's.

## Summary

★★ **The argument in one move: Six Sigma made everyone good, and being good stopped being an advantage.**

**The setup** — a Kelton research figure via Neumeier: *"**seven out of 10 Americans**, when asked why they
had to have a certain product"* — and he makes the room produce the answer by playing hangman with it. The
answer is **design**. His framing of why: *"we live in an age of abundance and **we're looking for
transcendent experiences — things that make us feel something.**"*

★ **The definition he wants to broaden**, credited to Nobel laureate **Herbert Simon**: design is
*"**everyone who takes a course of action to change an existing condition to an improved one.**"* His
gloss: *"most of us associate design with what it looks like — **but design is how it functions**"*, and it
applies to *"not just products but culture, community, the way we eat, the way we consume, the whole life
cycle."* Hence: *"it's not just for people in the visual arts — **and if we elevate our thinking beyond
what it looks like, maybe we can come upon a real solution.**"*

**Six Sigma, explained properly** (and he does explain it rather than gesture at it): invented by **Bill
Smith at Motorola**, championed by **GE**; *"Sigma is Greek… for standard deviation"*, and it runs
inversely — *"the higher the number, the fewer deviations."* The target, made tangible: *"defects per
million opportunities… **if you were to look in a small library, there would be like one misspelled
word.**"* The acronym: **DMAIC** — define, measure, analyse, improve, control — walked through with an
assembly-line example (hypothesise the break point, test it, and *"if that works, that becomes the new
standard"*). Its stated purpose: *"**the goal of Six Sigma is to make the customers happy.**"*

★★ **The Honda andon-cord story**, told for its counter-intuitive shape: every employee could *"pull a
chain to stop the entire assembly line."* They pulled it constantly, for weeks and months — *"and the CEO
was like: **oh my God, this is the worst idea I've ever employed.**"* Then: *"eventually they started to
root out all the problems, and they started to manufacture higher-quality products with fewer defects.
**This is how Honda went from being a laughed-at auto manufacturer to one of the world's leading
manufacturers known for quality.**"*

★★ **And the turn, which is the point of the whole talk**: *"Six Sigma has become the default standard of
how to run a company… **but here's the thing: we cannot innovate by making small refinements. We cannot
innovate by streamlining products.**"* The historical argument for why that changed: *"**in the age where
there were barriers to production — from factories and distribution and customer ignorance — making a
higher-quality product was all you needed for a competitive advantage. But today** we have to give
consumers **something radically different.**"*

**Samsung** is his chosen example of an unexpected innovator — *"a company you might not normally associate
with innovation… **they're taking over pretty much every display that you're using**"* — set against the
usual reverent list (Google, Tesla, Apple, Amazon).

## Key claims (2024-08-15)

- ★★ **Operational excellence is table stakes, not differentiation.** Six Sigma optimises defects out; it
  cannot produce the thing people *have to have*.
- ★★ **Refinement ≠ innovation.** *"We cannot innovate by making small refinements."*
- ★★ **The barriers that used to protect quality-as-advantage are gone** — production, distribution and
  customer ignorance. What's left is being **radically different**.
- ★ **Design is function and system, not appearance** (Herbert Simon) — and therefore not the exclusive
  property of visual artists.
- ★ **Abundance shifts the buying criterion to feeling**: transcendent experience, emotional connection,
  and — as the room supplies — **social status**, with buyers becoming *"unofficial brand ambassadors."*
- ★ **The Honda andon cord**: giving every worker the power to stop the line looks catastrophic for months
  and then compounds into a quality reputation. A patience-and-culture story, not a process story.
