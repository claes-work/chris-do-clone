# How To Use Scarcity in YOUR Marketing

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo.** Fully persona-eligible. He credits **Daniel Priestley** for the
> signal-then-sell sequence — *"this is a Daniel Priestley concept, by the way"* — and **Scott Galloway**
> for the luxury-brand framing. ★★ **The value here is the operational account of his own European tour**,
> which the corpus has referenced but never documented.

## Summary

The theory is familiar; **the practice is new and specific**.

★★ **The counter-intuitive core: undersell deliberately.** *"If you think you can sell 100 seats to a
workshop, **limit it to 60 or 70** — because it's better for you to sell out, have people on the wait
list, and say you've had to expand capacity."* Pushed further at the end: *"if you think you can do 40,
**sell 20.** If you think you can do 20, **sell 10.**"* And the reason, from the buyer's side, which is
the part most people miss: *"if someone thinks you're going to do a workshop for the same price and **10
people get to show up — I'm going to be one of 10, so the odds of you addressing my individual needs is
going to be 10 times better.** I think that's more valuable for me as an attendee."*

**Then Priestley's sequence** (credited): announce an *intention*, not a product — *"I'm thinking about
this; if you want to be first in line, add your name"* — and **make the queue visible**: *"there's 300 of
us in this room and only 60 tickets."* His own refinement, aimed at price: *"I like this a lot **especially
for mid- to high-ticket events**, because people can't always whip out their credit card… if it's in the
thousands, **give them time to prepare, to have a conversation with their partner.**"*

★★ **The European tour, documented.** Sparked by his friend **Anneli Hansen** asking him to come to
Stockholm — *"can we just chain a bunch of cities together so it would be worth your while?"* His answer
was not to book it but to test it: a poster asking *"I'm thinking about coming back to Europe to do a
workshop on one of two things — a business clinic or the personal branding workshop. **Are there people
interested, and where do you think we should go?**"* Because *"until they speak up, until they buy tickets,
**it's all speculative** — and one that could wind up being a financial disaster. You book the venue, you
make all your travel arrangements, and then **no one shows up. It's a blood bath.**"*

★★ **The principle he draws from it, and it is the best line in the video**: *"**we're going to design and
plan the event *with* people, not *for* them.** … A lot of corporations and event producers do not take
this into consideration. **After all, you exist to serve the community — and if the community is never
asked their opinion, you're just making a wild guess.**"* Plus the commitment mechanism: someone who
comments *"if you come to Paris, I will be there"* has *"the need to be consistent with themselves"* —
*"there's got to be some percentage higher that they're more likely to buy a ticket."*

**Then the onion**: poll the top six cities → add details (8-hour workshop, learning outcomes, price) →
*"are you all still interested? Comment with this keyword and I'll add you to the list."* — *"it's like
peeling back an onion, one layer at a time,"* so that by the time the sales page opens *"you'll hopefully
be **70, 80% sold just in the first 24 hours.**"*

★★ **What the tour actually taught him — the part he says they learned the hard way.**

- **Every city has a different mindset**, and running the identical workshop breaks: *"**two or three
  cities in we're like: oh my gosh, we're not reading the pulse of the city**, and I'm having to react in
  real time to adjust — when in fact I could have done some of this beforehand."* **Bucharest**: a
  post-communist, still-developing economy with *"really hard relationships with clients who don't want to
  pay them"* — *"totally different problems, totally different mindset."* **Warsaw** confounded his
  expectations in the other direction: *"I thought it's going to be a little tight — **but that room was so
  fun, they exploded, they were making all kinds of inappropriate jokes, and they drew me into a pool… and
  I swam right in there with them.**"*
- ★ **The prep method he now uses**: *"we used **ChatGPT** and asked: I'm going to be doing a workshop in
  Poland — **what are their belief systems around money? What are their client relationships like?** Use
  as up-to-date information as you can. **And it actually told us a lot**… which allowed me to adjust some
  of the prompts, so at least I was **going into the battlefield prepared.**"*
- ★ **Venue strategy**: partner with co-working and creative spaces — *"a lot of these spaces, that's what
  they've got: **they've got space and no humans**, and they want people in there, because otherwise
  they're still paying the exact same amount of money with or without you."* And treat them well:
  *"because we treat them with respect, we thank them, we mention them on social — they say: **next time,
  our space is yours.**"*
- ★ **You need local intermediaries**: *"to them we're an unknown entity, **so we need a middle person.**"*
  The named case is **Joanna Galvao**, who moved a workshop they had already sold tickets for **from
  Lisbon to Porto**: *"Chris, you don't want to be there, all the tourists go to Lisbon — **the design, the
  creative community, the pulse is in Porto.** … How many tickets have you sold? — Eight. — **I've just
  bought eight tickets. Cancel it.** And I did."*

★★ **And the story he chooses to end on — a workshop with two paying attendees.** *"Very early on, I'm
thinking like **2016**, the Futur's not even a real concept yet. Two people paid. **You know who showed
up? Rodrigo.** … He said: hey guys, we can cancel this, or we'll just do the workshop for two people. And
**they're both thrilled** — 'a workshop for two people, let's go!' So literally I spent hours with each of
them… **and you know how the rest of the story goes with Rodrigo. He's been to my house, we've travelled
the world together.**"*

## Key claims (2024-08-20)

- ★★ **Undersell capacity on purpose** — and note the *buyer-side* justification (better odds of being
  attended to), not just the seller-side scarcity effect.
- ★★ **Design *with* people, not *for* them.** Polling the audience is both research and
  **pre-commitment**.
- ★★ **The onion**: intention → interest → cities → details and price → sale, each layer more concrete.
- ★ **Signal-then-sell suits high-ticket** specifically, because expensive decisions need consultation
  time.
- ★★ **Localise or fail.** The same workshop does not transfer across cultures; prepare per city.
- ★ **AI as cultural pre-research** — a dated, concrete 2024 workflow.
- ★ **Space partnerships are a mutual-underutilisation trade**, and gratitude compounds into standing
  invitations.
- ★★ **The honest failure case**, given without spin: *"people sign up for things that are free… they see
  value and want to kick the tires, and **financially they're just not in a great place**, so they probably
  didn't have the intention to buy. **But that's okay, because no one's forced to buy anything.**"* Even
  after activating every channel — *"you've carpet-bombed everything"* — they may not come, *"and that's
  okay."*
- ★ **Small is not failure**: offer the buyers the choice to run it anyway, *"and I consider that a
  benefit to them."*
