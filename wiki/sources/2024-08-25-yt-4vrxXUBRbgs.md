## Summary

**The problem it addresses**: scarcity is obvious for events, but *"if I have a digital product the
quantity is unlimited… **you undermine the key principle of scarcity, because it's still available.**"*

★★ **The double cap — and a number that has changed.** Applied to the Pro Group: *"in no time will we ever
take **more than a thousand members** into the group — so there's a hard cap. But the second cap we add is
**each quarter we set aside a finite number. I believe it's 60 right now** — we only want to enrol **60
people per quarter.**"*

> ⚠️ **RECONCILIATION — the enrolment cadence changed.** [[2024-03-14-yt-0wse5TRJYHE]] (March 2024) has
> **30 per month**; this (August 2024) has **60 per quarter** — i.e. **20/month, a reduction of a third**,
> under the same 1,000 hard cap. **Record both with their dates**; do not overwrite the March figure. The
> direction of travel (tighter) is itself the interesting fact.

★ **The commitment rule, credited**: *"**the number is not important — the commitment to the number is
important.** That you mean what you say and you say what you mean. … **Do not get greedy. Do not try to
oversell it.**"* (Priestley's rule, and consistent with the honesty constraint already recorded in
`business` §44.)

★★ **The resale mechanic — new detail, and the psychology is the point.** When someone on the waitlist
offers to pay a premium to jump the queue: *"you can go to the group of 60 and say — **does anybody want to
sell their spot? Somebody wants to buy for $1,000.** They only paid $250… so they get their money back
**and an additional $1,000. You don't even need to keep the profit on this one.**"* Why he'd forgo the
margin: *"**now that person and everyone in the community realises the value of what they purchased — it's
worth even more than what they paid for it.** … Even though they literally didn't pay more, **they feel
like they got more.**"* His own label for it: *"this is like **black magic voodoo** kind of stuff."*

★ **The queue psychology**, stated plainly: *"have you ever waited in line at the movie theatre, or for a
ride at Disneyland, or for your place at a restaurant? **We're not going to leave that line, because as
soon as we leave, we look at the back of the line and we go back to square one.**"* Applied: a member who
wavers on their deposit gets told there is a waitlist, *"and that gives them **immediate FOMO**."*

★ **The disclosure discipline**: *"you want to let the market know what's happening and **be very
transparent about it.**"*

## Key claims (2024-08-25)

- ★★ **The double cap**: a hard ceiling (1,000) *and* a per-period intake (60/quarter). The second is what
  creates recurring scarcity for an otherwise unlimited product.
- ⚠️ **The intake rate changed** March → August 2024: 30/month → 60/quarter. **Both dated.**
- ★ **Commit to the number, whatever it is.** *"The number is not important; the commitment to the number
  is."*
- ★★ **The resale mechanic** as a value-signalling device, run at zero margin on purpose.
- ★ **Queue psychology**: leaving the line means returning to the back of it — which is why waitlists hold.
- ★ **Announce well in advance** (80–90 days for a quarterly window) and be transparent throughout.
- **An honest note on why the cap exists at all**: unlimited enrolment *"might impact the perception of
  the value — and it's being diluted — or maybe it's being too noisy for the people who buy the
  product."* The second reason is about **delivery quality**, not marketing.
