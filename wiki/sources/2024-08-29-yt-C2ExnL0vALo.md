# The Evolution of Design and Personal Branding with Paul Povolni

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do is the GUEST here**, interviewed by **Paul Povolni** (himself a
> first-generation immigrant, from Yugoslavia). **Chris's answers dominate and are persona-eligible**;
> Povolni's questions and his own biography are context.
>
> ★★ **Filed as a biography landmark.** It is an origin-story interview, and Chris tells it more
> continuously here than in most sources — including the **middle-child / caretaker** material he had
> disclosed via therapy only sixteen days earlier.

## Summary

**The framing question**: *"what is the origin of Chris Do?"* — and he asks how far back to go before
choosing to start at the beginning.

★★ **Refugee and outsider, stated as the first fact.** *"I'm a **first-generation immigrant slash
refugee**. My parents fled Vietnam in 1975, which is where I was born. And so for me, **I felt most of my
life as an outsider — sometimes an outsider in my own family, and outside in the real world.**"*

★★ **The middle-child account, given directly and unprompted** (and now corroborating
[[2024-08-13-yt-i10g2BVfWVI]] from a different angle): *"**I'm the middle child of three boys**, and it's
easy for the middle child to get lost in the mix. **I'm not the firstborn — I don't inherit the name
rights** and all that goes with it. **I'm not the baby, the darling who gets away with murder. I'm just the
person who's usually forgotten about.**"* And the therapeutic reading he now applies to it: *"as I've
learned in therapy, **the middle child tends to be the caretaker** — so we're really concerned about how
other people feel, and **that's how we feel like we can stand out.** … It wasn't until I went to therapy
that I learned that about myself, and **it helped me get a lot of clarity as to why in some of my previous
relationships I was so conflict-averse — I was so worried about their feelings that I put their feelings
ahead of mine.**"*

★★ **Asian-American academic expectations**, told with the joke intact: *"there's a lot of challenges being
an Asian-American… **you've got to do well in school — not just do okay.** *A* is just kind of average —
that's the joke inside the Asian community. **You have to get an A+, you have to get a 4-plus GPA.**"* And
his own diagnosis of why he didn't: *"**I think I'm smart, I just never was really engaged with what they
were teaching me.** … I could still, **with 50% of my brain paying attention to class, get A's and B's** —
but the other 50% was dreaming about design, about fashion, about hairstyles, about drawing comic
books."*

★★ **When the creative side got validated — and the reflex that blocked it.** Not until **senior year of
high school**: airbrushing, sculpting, a metal class *"so I can make things with my hands"*, the
**yearbook staff**, **designing the yearbook cover**, and **juried museum awards** for his artwork. Then
the moment: *"as my classmates and my instructor would say — **hey, have you considered a career in graphic
design?** — I'm like: **oh, I won't be broke.** I didn't say it out loud, but I [thought it]."*

**On the "nine lives" framing** he has used elsewhere, and on being a *geek* rather than a *nerd*: *"**I
think a nerd has no social skills.**"* (Povolni: *"I'm more of a fan."*)

**And the media thesis he opens the episode with**: ★ *"**every company today in the 21st century is a
media company — most of them just don't know it yet.**"* With the personal version of the same insight
from his commercial-production years: *"if we're in the business of making commercials, **we're going to
be screwed. I can see the writing on the wall.** … This is when **the label *graphic designer* doesn't fit
anymore.**"*

## Key claims (2024-08-29)

- ★★ **Outsider as the organising self-description** — in the world *and* in his own family.
- ★★ **Middle child of three boys**, and what that meant structurally: no name rights, no
  youngest-child licence, *"the person who's usually forgotten about."*
- ★★ **The caretaker pattern, sourced to therapy**, and its cost named in **previous relationships**, not
  just at work — this extends the disclosure in [[2024-08-13-yt-i10g2BVfWVI]], which located it in the
  company.
- ★★ **Academic pressure and disengagement**: A+ as the standard, and 50% attention sufficing for A's and
  B's while the other half drew comics and designed.
- ★★ **Senior year of high school** as the acceptance point — yearbook cover, juried museum awards — and
  the immediate financial fear (*"I won't be broke"*) that had to be overcome first.
- ★ **"Every company is a media company"** — his own framing, and the reason he left commercial
  production: *"I can see the writing on the wall."*
- ★ **The label stopped fitting**: *"this is when the label *graphic designer* doesn't fit anymore."*
- **Geek, not nerd** — *"a nerd has no social skills."* Small, but he draws the line deliberately.
