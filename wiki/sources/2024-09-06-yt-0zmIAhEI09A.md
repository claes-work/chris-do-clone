# How to Use Stories to Elevate Your Brand

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo** (the July-2024 short-form solo format). Persona-eligible. Credited
> in-line: **Michael Margolis**, **Joseph Campbell**, **Robert McKee**, and an "Eric" whose surname the
> captions lose.

## Summary

**Why stories stick, argued from memory rather than from craft.** *"There are two core components that
always exist when it comes to memory: **a strong emotion**, and **a story.**"* Test it, he says, on last
week: *"the only things you remember are the things that caused an emotional reaction. **The things where
you're kind of in a vegetative state — you're not going to be able to recall.**"* Hence: *"**storytelling
is so easy and giving lectures is so difficult**, because storytelling is the most natural thing — it's how
the human mind works."* Quoted (surname garbled): *"**stories are the operating system of the human
mind.**"*

★★ **His diagnosis of why adults lose it.** *"**As children we're so good at telling stories** — you'll
make up stories about how the dog ate your homework, about how that girl was really into you when she
really wasn't. And something happens when we get older: **we start to follow very specific rules about what
facts are**, and we don't get as excited to tell people how our day went — **we just tell them the
highlights, the bullet points.**"* Applied straight to the audience's complaint: *"*my social account is
dead, no one's engaging* — well, **you're all facts and bullet points at this point. No one cares, because
it doesn't remind them of anything.** … We're hungry to learn the facts, but in a day we'll forget it,
**because I felt nothing, and there's no story for me.**"*

The Aesop test seals it: you remember the tortoise and the hare decades later, *"but if I ask you what did
you learn in algebra, and who was your teacher, and what did they look like — **you probably can't
remember a single thing.** … The [teachers] I remember fondly **created a narrative around history or
science, and they gave me a frame to look at the world.**"*

★★ **The brand-equity arithmetic**, which is the most quotable thing here. Via **Michael Margolis**:
*"**a product without a story is a commodity; a product with a story is a brand.**"* Then he makes it
measurable: *"**the amount of money you're willing to pay over a cheaper alternative is the amount of brand
equity.** Two bottles of water: one sells for a dollar, one sells for $5. **The one that sells for $5 has
$4 of equity.**"* And the stakes: *"**a commodity is something that is easily replaced and usually sold at
the lowest price** — and most of us do not want to think of what we do as a commodity."*

**The structure, reduced to three words**: **character → want → obstacle.** The character must be
specific — *"when we look at movies that have **cardboard characters**, they're very empty… **we're looking
for a soul inside that character and we don't see it, because it's poorly designed.**"* The want creates
the tension (*"if they have no want, they're totally at peace with themselves — there's nothing left to
acquire, so there's no tension"*). The obstacle is invented. **Robert McKee**, credited: *"**no conflict,
no story.**"*

★ **The fun fact he saves for the end**: *"**Joseph Campbell mentored George Lucas** — and he said he's one
of his best students."* His point: *Star Wars* endures because *"George was such a great student. **When you
try to tell a story without the structure of what we've already learned works, it usually becomes very
forgettable** — it winds up becoming experimental art, experimental film. **More poetry than something we
can grab on to.**"*

**Then the application to the audience's actual work**, using a named colleague (**Rich**, who helps
authors and thought leaders launch podcasts): the narrower the ideal-client avatar, the better you know
their *want* — *"**not everybody wants to do a podcast.** Only a specific type of person wants to do it
professionally and cares enough to pay for it"* — and only then can you name the obstacle.

## Key claims (2024-09-06)

- ★★ **Memory requires emotion plus story**; bullet points are forgotten within a day.
- ★★ **Adults trade storytelling for factual reporting**, and then wonder why nobody engages.
- ★★ **Brand equity is the measurable price premium** — the $5 vs $1 water, and $4 of equity.
- ★★ **"A product without a story is a commodity; a product with a story is a brand"** (Margolis,
  credited).
- ★ **Character / want / obstacle**, with specificity as the quality bar and conflict as the engine
  (McKee).
- ★ **Structure beats experimentation** for anything meant to be remembered — the Campbell/Lucas point.
- ★ **The avatar has to be narrow enough to have a *want***; "people" is not an audience.
