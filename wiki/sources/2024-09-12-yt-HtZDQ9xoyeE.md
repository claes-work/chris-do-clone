# Build a Memorable Brand in Any Industry w/ Neel Dhingra

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary; only Chris Do's lines train the persona.** The guest is **Neel
> Dhingra**, a mortgage-industry creator whom Chris describes as *"my buddy… who has a very
> **non-traditional, non-conventional way of thinking**."* They met pre-pandemic.

> ⏸️ **Deferred from batch 133** (a 406KB all-large batch) and ingested here.

## Summary

★★ **The best-documented example in the corpus of someone using Chris himself as a ladder** — told to his
face, by the person who did it, with the tactic laid out step by step. That is what makes it worth a page.

**The play, in his own words**: he booked a **paid one-hour consult with Chris** — *"it was **a thousand
bucks** at the time… **I think since then it's gone up to 5K**"* — and then, having already paid:
*"I DM'd Chris and said: **hey, I booked a call with you, I've already paid for it. I'm actually going to be
in LA at this time with my media team — could we do the call as a one-hour podcast at your studio? We'll
come to you, make it easy.** And to my surprise you messaged back and said yeah, let's do it. I was like:
**oh, it worked. Let's go.**"*

★ **The conversion**: one hour, shot at the Santa Monica studio, *"which I then turned into **probably 20
pieces of micro content** plus an hour-long episode on my YouTube channel."*

★★ **And the compounding — the actual mechanism**: *"I got a lot of **brand lift** because — *hey Neel, how
are you sitting down with Chris Do? You're at The Futur in Santa Monica?* — I'm like: yeah man, **you should
be following me, you should be learning from me, I'm doing some big things.** … And then **I would leverage
that episode to get the next person, and the next person.** My previous guests included Chris Do — then I
did it with somebody else, and so on."* His summary of why it worked despite his own inexperience: *"**even
though I wasn't necessarily that good with my own content yet, I would leverage other people.**"* He repeated
the pattern across his industry — *"I would somehow buy their product or service"* to get the meeting.

★★ **The candour about the interview itself** is what elevates this from a growth-hack anecdote:
*"I remember watching back that interview — **my questions are terrible.** … I was rambling, no confidence
on camera."* And his conclusion: *"**how do you get to a good interview? Well, you do some freaking bad ones
first.**"* Plus the nerve required: *"**just jump in — what's the worst that's going to happen?**"*

## Chris Do's contribution (the persona-eligible material)

- ★★ **A clarification he stops the conversation to make**, and it is the most useful thing he says here.
  Dhingra recalls being scared because *"if you get a dumb interview, sometimes you'll actually tell them
  they're kind of dumb — **can you rephrase that question, I don't even understand what you said.**"*
  Chris interrupts to correct the record: ★★ *"**I just want everybody to know: when I ask somebody to
  rephrase, it's not me thinking you're dumb. It's just I want to make sure I understand the question.**"*
  Worth recording as a **voice/delivery** note — a move that reliably reads as contempt and isn't, and he
  knows it reads that way.
- ★ **He let it happen, and says so implicitly** — the paid consult converted into a podcast at his own
  studio, at the guest's request, with the guest's crew. A dated, concrete instance of the
  **give-first/access** posture the corpus documents in principle.
- **Dated fact**: his **one-on-one consult** was **~$1,000** at the time (pre-pandemic-ish) and is *"5K
  now"* — corroborating the one-on-one consulting line he mentions reluctantly in
  [[2024-07-10-yt-7Gi-lHLgTeI]], and sitting alongside the **$5,000/hr** self-report already on the pricing
  hub.

## Fenced — Neel Dhingra (guest; does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Neel Dhingra. Context only._

- ★★ **Buy access, then upgrade the format.** Pay for the consult, then ask to convert it into content —
  *"we'll come to you, make it easy."* The friction removal is the ask.
- ★★ **Borrowed authority, compounded.** One credible guest becomes the credential for booking the next.
- ★ **Volume over polish, knowingly**: bad interviews first; *"even though I wasn't that good with my own
  content yet, I would leverage other people."*
- **Repeatability**: he ran the same play on other leaders in his industry.
