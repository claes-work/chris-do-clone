# Forget What You Know: How To Create A Personal Brand

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do is the GUEST**, on **the 505 podcast**, re-published to his own channel. His
> answers dominate and are **persona-eligible**; the hosts' questions are context.
>
> ★★ **Biography landmark.** It is the most detailed account in the corpus of **how The Skool's on-camera
> personas were designed**, of **his actual role at Blind in the crossover years**, and of the
> **channel rename** — including a piece of advice he says he gives everyone.

## Summary

★★ **His initial refusal, quoted against himself.** When Jose Caballer pushed him to make YouTube content:
*"I'm like — **no, I'm not going to do that. It's stupid, dude. No one that's professional makes YouTube
content. It's all these wannabes, people who have never made anything with their life, and this is their
outlet.**"*

★★ **The personas were engineered, and he says so plainly.** *"Initially I'm putting on my producer-
director hat, which I'm used to doing… **I said okay, we need to figure out our personas** — and so **it
was crafted and engineered, very much so.** If you watch the old episodes you'll see: **oh, he is showing
up as somebody.**"* The casting logic is straight television: *"**in TV, chemistry is really important. We
don't want two of the same people.**"*

The contrast he built it on: *"Jose and I could not be more different. **I'm an INTJ, he's an ENFP** —
literally the opposite on the Myers-Briggs. I'm introverted, he's super extroverted, he's loud, he's
obnoxious, **he's Puerto Rican, I'm Vietnamese. We're both immigrants — that's about the similarity
there.**"* And the direction he gave: *"you lean more into that personality — **the hippie, everything
goes, let's drop some acid**, even if that's not what you're saying, you've got that vibe. **I'm a business
guy… and I need an excuse to wear suits.** I like my suits and I don't have enough opportunities to wear
them, **so I'll show up as a suit.**"* Reference: **The Odd Couple**.

★★ **And the crucial qualifier**, which is what keeps this from contradicting his authenticity doctrine:
*"**they are authentically us — just hyper versions of us.** And it was a thought, **but that's as far as
the thought went.** It wasn't like brand pillars and all that kind of stuff."*

★★ **His actual role at Blind during the crossover — "the Wolf."** *"The Blind side was really confused as
to what I was doing, because they wanted me involved in running the company, writing pitches, doing bids…
**but my team is very competent.** So I would be pulled in from time to time, **only as needed.**"* The
self-description: *"my role was kind of like **the Wolf from *Pulp Fiction*** — after you accidentally
shoot the brains out of the guy in the back seat and Bonnie's coming home, **you've got to call the
Wolf.** … **I don't mean to be curt — we've got to get stuff done. You want me here or not?**"* The
trigger, concretely: *"the executive producer would say — **I feel like this job is slipping away from us,
this $600,000 job might go bye-bye. It's time to call in Chris.** And I would step in, we'd do the call,
and I'm like: **okay, we got it back on the right path. You guys keep going. I'm out.**"*

★★ **The two-and-a-half-year turn.** *"Eventually — and when I say eventually, **two and a half years
later** — we're starting to make traction. **This is when the plot starts to thicken**, where the team could
say: oh, there's something here, **maybe we should get involved**, it looks like a lot of fun over there.
And **doing client work is fine, but it's not freeing. It's not this thing of self-expression.**"*

★★ **The business prenup**, given as standing advice: *"I always tell young people this — or anybody going
into business: **if you have a partner, work on the prenup before you go into business together.** What
does this look like if we were to separate, **so you don't have any fights later on?** And thankfully we
did that — because **when things got tense between the two of us, I had something to lean back on.** … And
**to his word, he's like: I'm cool** — even though that's not what he wanted, **he did agree to it.**"*

★ **The rename decision, and why.** *"You keep the product, I keep the audience — and the audience was on
YouTube. He's like: **do you want to use the name?** I said **it's probably better for me to start fresh,
so that people don't have the same association.**"*

★★ **The audience reaction, told without flattery.** *"For the first year **people were really unhappy with
me. *Why did you break us up?*** … It's like **Mom and Dad had a fight and now we the children have to be
the survivors of a divorced parenting.** They're like: *where is he? Bring him back.* And it's like — **it's
a new channel, bro. I'm running things now, and it's different.**"* With the honest concession: *"a lot of
people liked him, and they liked us. They were like: **you're better together.** And **for parts of it we
were better together** — but we had such different working styles."*

## Key claims (2024-09-15)

- ★★ **He thought YouTube was beneath professionals**, and says so in the words he used at the time.
- ★★ **The on-camera personas were deliberately cast** for TV-style chemistry — *"crafted and engineered"* —
  while remaining *"authentically us, just hyper versions of us."* **This is the single most important
  nuance for the persona**: designed presentation and authenticity are not in conflict for him, provided
  the exaggeration runs along true lines.
- ★★ **"The Wolf"** — his self-description of his role at Blind in that period: a fixer called in on
  slipping six-figure jobs, then out again. Includes a concrete $600,000 example.
- ★★ **Two and a half years before traction**, and the point at which the Blind team wanted in — because
  client work *"is not freeing, it's not this thing of self-expression."*
- ★★ **The business prenup**: agree the separation terms *before* you start. He credits it directly for
  the split going cleanly, and credits Jose for honouring it against his own preference.
- ★ **Starting fresh over inheriting the name** — a deliberate positioning choice, not a legal one.
- ★★ **A year of audience hostility** after the split, described with the divorce metaphor and without
  self-justification.
