## Summary

★★ **Chris opens by naming exactly what he admires**, and it is a craft observation rather than a
compliment: *"she knows **how to turn around when she's on the stage.** You might see her in the green
room and **she's super chill and she's talking about how she's an introvert and has certain anxiety — but
when she hits that [stage]…**"* — the transformation is the point. Worth recording because it is the same
thing he documents about **himself** (*"I'm a loud introvert"*, and the stage fear in
[[2024-03-28-yt-gacg2OqLd2k]]).

**Her origin story** (hers, fenced) is unusually specific about the arithmetic of risk:

- **The belief she had to break**: that starting a business required being *"set apart"* or already
  *"somebody"*. Her counter-thought at 25: *"**I actually think I could start a business.** With what? A
  camera. **Did I have a camera? No.** Did I have any skill or barometer of what I'd be able to do as a
  creative? **Not remotely.** But **I just saw what somebody did and I was like: I think I can do that.**"*
- **The financial reality, stated without softening**: working part-time at her father's church, just out
  of law school, husband on commission at a startup — *"**I want to say we were living paycheck to
  paycheck, but no, we weren't even doing that.** … Whenever that paycheck came in — **oh good, we can go
  to Taco Bell for dinner.**"*
- ★★ **The deadline as the mechanism**: she could return to law school **on scholarship** within a year.
  *"**You have one year to make this work.**"* A real, dated, revocable fallback — which is what made the
  bet finite rather than reckless.
- ★★ **Documenting the failure in advance**: *"I started a blog and I said — **I'm creating this blog
  because I'm documenting that I'm going to fail and I'm going to go back to law school.**"*
- **The outcome**: *"my first year of business **I made $100,000** — and to me, in my family, **being
  first-generation Latina, that was unheard of.** … 26 years old making $100,000. Game over."*
- ★ **The honesty about her own work at the time**: *"**my work did suck, and I knew enough about the art
  that I knew my art sucked — and yet I was able to sell what I was doing.**"* Which is why she gave the
  framework away openly on the blog, and why *"people were so upset."*
- **On the reaction**: *"there is no greater hater than a creator who is better than someone else [who]
  tears that person down… because they got something that **they believed they were deserving of.**"*
