# Ditch the Competition with Sales Strategies That WORK

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary**, in conversation with a co-host who feeds him the setups.
> Persona-eligible. The **accusation audit** is **Chris Voss's** and is referenced as something Chris has
> *taught* — record the technique as Voss's, the misuse critique as Chris's.

## Summary

★★ **The best thing in this source is a correction of a tactic he himself popularised.**

**The setup**: sellers open calls with a pre-emptive disarm — *"you might think that you're coming to this
call and I'm about to pitch the heck out of you, and **I just want you to know that that's not going to
happen.**"* That's an **accusation audit** (Voss), and it works: *"**you may buy yourself some
goodwill.**"*

★★ **And then they pitch anyway.** *"**But then what happened? You pitched them. You burn all of that
goodwill.**"* His escalation is the point: *"I think **you go beyond burning goodwill — you just lied.**
That's beyond goodwill."* The calibration he offers is precise: *"saying *I'll pick you up* and showing up
late — that's burning some goodwill. **Saying *I'll pick you up* and going to Disneyland and totally
spacing out on you — you just lied. You made a promise and you broke it immediately.**"*

★★ **Why it is disqualifying rather than merely rude** — and this is the sentence to keep: *"**how you do
anything is how you do everything.** So when you lied to me on the call — **theoretically this is the best
our relationship is ever going to be. We're still in the pre-honeymoon phase; it's courtship right now.
And if you're going to lie to me during the courtship, what's going to happen when I give you money and I
have no more leverage over you?**"*

★★ **The compounding argument**: *"**don't begin the relationship on a lie, because the lie compounds
itself — you have to keep lying to maintain the original lie.**"*

★★ **The resolution — the implicit agreement.** Rather than telling people to disarm better, he removes the
premise: *"there is **an implicit agreement between buyer and seller** to get on a call. **The buyer has a
problem and has money to spend; the seller has a solution and wants to receive the money.** This is
implicit. **So to say *I'm not going to sell you* is a strange thing — you don't even need to [say it].**"*
The honest version he sketches instead: establish whether it makes sense and feels good, *"**and then
perhaps we could proceed to working together.**"*

★ **His diagnosis of the underlying motive**, which is generous and damning at once: *"**people are so
intent on appearing not salesy that they become deceptive** — and then when that disconnect happens, as a
buyer I'm like: **you have an agenda, also known as an ulterior motive. And then I no longer feel like you
care about me and have my best interest at heart.** Of course I know you want to sell me something —
**that was kind of the premise of this call.**"*

★ **Evasiveness read from the political cycle**, offered as a live example: when a candidate is asked a
direct question and dodges, *"it just… doesn't feel right to us. **Just say: I have a plan, this is what it
is** — and be super direct. … **Our feelings towards them go way down** when they're evasive or tell us
something we feel is not the whole truth."*

★ **And the disguised version he calls out by name**: *"unless you cloaked it in one of those **coffee
chats** that I get **like a million requests for.**"*

## Key claims (2024-09-22)

- ★★ **The accusation audit is not a licence to pitch.** Using it and then pitching converts a goodwill
  purchase into a **broken promise**.
- ★★ **Goodwill-burning vs. lying is a difference in kind, not degree** — with the pick-you-up/Disneyland
  calibration to tell them apart.
- ★★ **The courtship argument**: the sales call is the best the relationship will ever be, and behaviour
  there predicts behaviour after the money changes hands and your leverage disappears.
- ★★ **"How you do anything is how you do everything."** (Note: he used the near-identical *"how you do one
  thing is how you do everything"* in [[2024-08-13-yt-i10g2BVfWVI]] about himself; here it's applied to a
  counterparty. **Same principle, two directions.**)
- ★★ **Lies compound** — maintaining one requires more.
- ★★ **The implicit agreement** dissolves the need for the disarm entirely: both parties already know why
  they're on the call, so pretending otherwise is the only false move available.
- ★ **Appearing non-salesy is the motive that produces deception** — the failure is well-intentioned,
  which is why it's common.
- ★ **Evasiveness is read as dishonesty**, whether or not it is.
