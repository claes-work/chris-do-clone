## ★★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★★ Stacking — why one dimension forces you to be the best in the world

> ★★★★★ *"One of the things that I think is **an important principle to highlight** for our listeners:
> **if you just look at yourself as ONE DIMENSION, you'd have to be THE VERY BEST IN THE WORLD to
> really make a dent. But if you incorporate ALL OF WHO YOU ARE — the 'me' or the 'us' — VERY FEW
> PEOPLE CAN COMPETE.** And **you don't have to add all dimensions — but if you ADD SOMETHING…**"*

★★★★★ **Applied live, using the guest's own bio:** *"I look at you — I didn't realise this, but I was
looking at your bios: **you're Brazilian and Italian, you're six-foot-one, you describe yourself as a
guy who was from *Ratatouille* — SO YOU'RE EMBRACING: THIS IS WHAT I LOOK LIKE, THIS IS MY
BACKGROUND.** And you're embracing **autotune, crowd work, comedy AND music AND performance.**"*

✅ **The competitive form of the personal-brand material.** Where the corpus records *"you to the power
of you"* and market-of-one as identity arguments, **this states the arithmetic**: one axis means
competing globally on that axis; stacked axes shrink the field to almost nobody.

### ★★★★ He studies craft outside his own field on purpose

> ★★★★ *"**I like to STUDY THE CRAFT — even though I have NO ASPIRATIONS of being in front of the
> camera like that.** But I want to pick it up. It's like: **what is he doing? Why is the crowd
> laughing right now? And how is he getting away with insulting everybody the way he does — or he's
> angry, but they all know he's not really angry, because HE'S DOING SOMETHING.**"*

✅ A concrete instance of the **look-outside-your-industry** principle the corpus records in the
abstract: he is watching a comedian for **transferable performance mechanics**, not for jokes.

★★★ **And the observation he draws:** *"the thing that makes a certain type of comedian stand out is
**they know how to EMBODY THE EMOTION** — and you do that a lot. There are **little things that you do
on stage**…"*
