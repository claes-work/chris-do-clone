## Fenced summary — Matt Essam (does NOT train the persona)

_Attributed to Matt Essam. Context only._

A **four-part framework** for a first call he calls the **"clarity call"** — *"I've heard it called a
discovery call before"* — capped at **15–20 minutes**, whose only job is *"to work out if you can help this
person and whether it'd be a good fit."* Offered with a downloadable script, and a pre-emptive defence of
scripts: *"a lot of people are averse to the idea of scripts because it feels a little bit robotic… **this
is more of a framework**, and it's important that you then **mould it to sound like you.**"*

1. **Control the frame** — tell them how the call will run. His analogy: a child's painting in a nursery
   versus a red square in a famous gallery — *"**the frame or the context in which you're viewing that
   thing**"* sets the valuation. *"**Whoever sets that frame controls the conversation.**"*
2. **Identify the gap** — current situation → goals → obstacles. Bring research: *"what I know about your
   company is… **what have I missed?**"* — which *"reinforces this idea of you leading the relationship,
   **you being a partner rather than just a worker.**"*
3. **Ask about goals broadly, not narrowly** — *"a lot of people… say *what are your goals with branding?*
   Well, most people don't know that, and **it really limits the frame.** Whereas if we go broader — *what
   are your general business goals?* — then we can figure out what this person wants **and whether our
   service fits into that picture or not.**"*
4. (The fourth part continues past the sampled section.)

His opening premise: *"if we don't have a process, **we're kind of at the mercy of our customer's buying
process.**"*
