# The New Approach to Personal Branding That Changes Everything (Forward Event)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo keynote**, delivered to a **mortgage-industry** audience at an event called
> **Forward**. Fully persona-eligible. Notable for being delivered to people **outside his usual
> profession**, which changes how he frames everything.

## Summary

★★ **The clearest statement of what he thinks personal branding is *not*** — given as the opening,
negatively, before he says what it is:

*"**Personal branding is not about how to get more clients.** It's not about a tricky way to leverage your
personality to get a sale. **It's not because it's buzzy or something that's on trend right now.**"* And the
diagnosis behind it: *"**the problem is most of us are building a barrier between our true selves and the
people who want to see who is that person behind there.**"*

★★ **The confession, which is the reason this keynote matters.** *"I'd argue I have **the best job in the
world**, because **I get to help people achieve their dreams and I get paid a lot of money to do that.**
What is better than that? And it fills me up with joy every single day."* — followed immediately by:
*"**And I also want to let you know: I'm extremely socially awkward, and I just pretend not to be most of
the time.** If you didn't know me, if you didn't see me, **I'll just be like this the whole time.**"*

★★ **And the life philosophy he built on top of it** — the single most economical explanation of his whole
career strategy: *"I have developed this life philosophy that **if I'm just good enough at creating content,
then I don't have to develop social skills.** And **this is why my personal brand is a loud introvert. I
want to be known before I have to walk into the room**, because I'm just super weird like that. So if I see
somebody and they smile at me, I'm like — *there's nobody behind me… okay, I guess we know each other kind
of.*"*

★ **The permission that follows**, addressed to a room he correctly reads as mostly extroverted: *"**if a
socially awkward person like myself can do this, I know for certain that you can do it** — you have the
resources, you have the personality type."* (He runs the room: *"introverts raise your hand — quietly,
gently"* — and notes with surprise that it's *"a lot more than I thought."*)

★★ **The thesis, stated as a heart-centred one and unusual in its bluntness**: *"I'm here mostly because
**I want to help you find your voice**, and it's a very **heart-centred** approach to what I'm doing —
because **I think we're all children in adult suits, and there's a lot of unresolved trauma.** And the
result of it is **we're afraid to do things**, from business to content creation and everything in between.
**So I want to help you to lean into more of you.**"*

**The origin, retold for a non-design audience**: *"social media and creating content was **the farthest
thing away from my mind**, and my good friend and former mentor told me: hey, let's go make content
together. So **in 2014 I made my first YouTube video** — we're coming up on 10 years here — **and I was 42
years old at that time.**"*

**On reading a room that isn't his**: he opens by noting how unlike a design audience they are — *"I come
from a design and branding community and **we're all really weird, so nobody ever talks to each other.**
I'm telling you the truth. But when I see you guys — *hey!* — I'm like: **you want to talk to me? Okay,
this is cool.**"* His hypothesis for the warmth, delivered lightly but with an edge: *"maybe **trauma bonds
people together** — and I know you're going through a tough time."* (Mortgage-industry audience, 2024.)

★ **The bit that shows the format work**: he had a British colleague (**Phil**) **freestyle-rap the
audience's own pain points** as a live demo of relevance — *"how many people in the mortgage space find
themselves looking to stand out from their competition… **put themselves in a marketplace of one**"* —
then punctures it: *"we'll have to forgive him, **he's British, so the timing was not quite correct.**"*

**Comedy note, recorded because it's characteristic**: a riff on ageing (*"Asian don't raisin… you know,
it's genetics"*, extended to other groups and then abandoned) followed by his own self-assessment — *"**I
woke up this morning [and] chose violence. I'm so sorry.**"*

## Key claims (2024-09-29)

- ★★ **Personal branding is not client acquisition, not a personality trick, and not a trend** — the three
  things it gets mistaken for, ruled out up front.
- ★★ **The barrier is the problem**: people build one between their true selves and the audience that wants
  to see behind it. (The same structure as the **authenticity gap** and *"the wall"* in
  [[2024-06-25-yt-EekKQvQUaUM]] and [[2024-08-13-yt-i10g2BVfWVI]].)
- ★★ **He is socially awkward and pretends not to be** — stated plainly, to a room, as the setup for the
  argument rather than as a confession seeking sympathy.
- ★★ **Content as a substitute for social skills**: *"if I'm just good enough at creating content, then I
  don't have to develop social skills… **I want to be known before I have to walk into the room.**"* This
  is the **strategic rationale for "loud introvert"**, which the corpus has held as a label without a
  reason.
- ★★ **"We're all children in adult suits"**, with unresolved trauma producing avoidance — *"we're afraid
  to do things, from business to content creation and everything in between."*
- ★ **The credential inverted**: if the awkward person can do it, the extroverts in the room have no
  excuse.
- **Origin, dated for a lay audience**: first YouTube video **2014**, aged **42**, at a former mentor's
  urging.
