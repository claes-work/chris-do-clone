# Are You Losing Deals Due to These Common Mistakes

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do primary.** A **live coaching roleplay** in a workshop: a participant runs a sales
> call against **Carlos** as the prospect, and Chris stops the tape repeatedly to correct. Persona-eligible;
> participant lines are context.
>
> ★★ **The format is the value** — these are micro-corrections made in real time, which the corpus mostly
> holds as principles rather than as coaching.

## Summary

★★ **"Use his language. Don't introduce a new word."** The central correction, and he catches it on a
single word. The participant asks *"is there a specific budget you guys are **comfortable** with?"* — and
Chris stops it: *"**did he use the word *comfortable*? Or what did he use?** When he used the word
**budget**…"* The fix: *"instead of saying *what budget would be comfortable for you*, what should you say?
**What budget will make sense to you.**"* Because *"make sense"* was the prospect's frame, not the seller's.

He is explicit that this is where he differs from the participant's instinct: *"**this is where I was
disagreeing with Carlos before — it's like: use his language. Don't introduce a new word.**"*

★★ **"He just gave you valuable information. Did you realise that?"** — his running refrain when a
participant lets a disclosure slide past. Worked example: the prospect names **30K** against a **40K**
proposal, *"so what's the difference between these two? **10K.**"* The gap is now a known, finite,
negotiable number — and most people miss that they've been handed it.

★★ **Address the energy shift, or ignore it — but decide.** When the prospect is rushing: *"**you can
address it or you can ignore it. What would you like to do?**"* The participant's attempt — *"if you don't
mind, can you slow down"* — gets corrected: *"**no, no, now you're telling him what to do.**"* Chris's
version: *"**Carlos, I might be getting this wrong — are you late for a meeting, or [is] something going
on? I'm sensing a different change in energy here.**"* And why it works: *"**then he's going to slow that
down. He will — and you'll see.**"*

★ **And the read if it doesn't work**: *"if he keeps going forward, **you don't really need to do this
anymore. It's not a good fit. He's decided you're not the guy.**"*

★★ **"Hold on — are you doing solutions already?"** The most-repeated correction, and the one he seems most
exercised by. The participant, hearing a budget objection, immediately offers *"can we adjust the scope to
match your budget?"* Chris stops it: *"**remember, we're not in solutions. We're just dealing with the
objection. See how fast you guys want to get straight to solutions?**"*

★ **The stall, handled**: *"let me think about it and I'll get back to you"* → *"**awesome. Out of
curiosity, what will you be thinking about?**"* — which produces the real answer (the numbers, and a
partner comparing another vendor).

★ **He narrates his own note-taking**: *"**I take notes for you guys.** If you wear the glasses and you look
at the board."* — the whiteboard is doing the teaching alongside the roleplay.

★ **And the encouragement, mid-correction**: *"**okay, you've made a lot of progress.** … Should we do this
one more time?"* — repetition inside the same session rather than a single graded attempt.

## Key claims (2024-10-03)

- ★★ **Mirror their exact words; never substitute your own.** *"Comfortable"* for *"makes sense"* is a small
  swap that changes whose frame the conversation runs in.
- ★★ **Notice when you've been given information.** A named number converts a vague objection into a
  measurable gap.
- ★★ **Do not jump to solutions while still handling an objection.** The most common reflex in the room.
- ★★ **Name a change in energy tentatively, not correctively** — *"I might be getting this wrong… I'm
  sensing a different change in energy"* beats *"can you slow down"*, which instructs the prospect.
- ★ **Non-response is data**: if naming the shift doesn't slow them, the decision is already made.
- ★ **Answer a stall with curiosity**, not pressure: *"out of curiosity, what will you be thinking about?"*
