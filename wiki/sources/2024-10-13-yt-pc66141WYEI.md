# Watch This Before Your Next Sales Call — 60 Minute Sales Crash Course

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do throughout.** Fully persona-eligible.
>
> ⚠️ **DATING TRAP — this is a COMPILATION, not a new recording.** Multiple distinct settings are cut
> together: a live workshop with a room, several one-to-one roleplays with different participants, a
> studio-shot five-mistakes lecture, and a coaching call taken from a car. **The 2024-10-13 date is the
> publication date only.** Several segments are recognisably older material (the *"$18,000 logo"* and the
> Ben Burns anchor story appear elsewhere in the corpus). **Date the ideas to their original telling where
> the corpus can establish it; treat 2024-10-13 as "still endorsed as of" — not as origin.**

## Summary

★★ **The densest single sales source in the corpus so far.** Its spine is one inversion, stated twice:
★★ *"**the harder you try to sell, the fewer clients you get**"* — because *"people who try to sell, who
try to close, who have a very clear agenda that they're not really trying to help me — **it pushes me away,
it makes me fearful, it makes me lose trust.**"*

★★ **The word swap he asks for outright:** *"I want you to **rethink the word sell**. It's four letters —
**let's just erase that word in your mind, and just help.** … If you help the other person, if in their
eyes you are perceived as somebody worthy of being trusted, they will not only **pay you more money**,
they'll **stop talking to other people**, and they'll give you **more creative latitude than you've ever
had in your life.**"* And the definition stack: *"selling is **not** pitching, it's not presenting, it's not
convincing, and it's not a form of manipulation. **The goal of sales is to inspire the prospect to make a
decision, not to tell them what to do.** You do this by helping them think through their challenges. **You
don't tell, you ask.**"* Compressed: *"selling is helping, it's about being curious, **selling is learning,
selling is an act of generosity.**"*

★ **Why the reframe is load-bearing rather than decorative:** *"if we walk around with this impression that
**to sell is to be like a snake**, then we're not going to want to do it, or we're going to have a bad
attitude towards doing it — and **if you hate doing something, you're not going to get good at it.**"* The
named alternative to *"Mr Jordan Belfort, The Wolf of Wall Street"* is *"more **permission-based**… **led by
curiosity and given in the spirit of generosity.**"*

### ★★ The two skills, and the listening technique

*"So that's a lot to remember in process — **serve, ask, listen, empathise and summarise.** [But] I just
want you to focus on **the two key skills.** You don't need to know anything else: **asking and
listening.**"* — *"they're **twins**."*

★★ **The counter-intuitive listening instruction**: *"instead of making and keeping eye contact — which is
what many people are told to do — what I would suggest is to **keep a notebook and write down what they're
saying.** This signals to the client: **I'm paying careful attention, almost as if I'm reporting whatever
it is you're saying. Your words matter so much I must describe them in a notebook — not leave it to chance
that my memory is going to correctly recall it.**"*

★★ **The tell for whether you actually listened**: *"when you ask a question and they give you an answer,
**the very next question should be a follow-up to the answer they gave you.** That's how you know you're
listening."* His example of the failure: *"unfortunately this is how most podcasters interview people —
**they have 75 questions and they literally just go from question to question.**"*

★★ **Playing it back, and why the fear of it is backwards**: *"we're afraid to play back what we heard…
because we're afraid we might get it wrong. **Actually, if you get something wrong, this is an opportunity
for the prospect to correct you**, so you're not building on assumptions that are wrong."* Form: *"**what I
heard you say was: you need X by Y, and you have a problem with Z. Is that correct?**"* — paraphrasing
*"even the language in which they used."*

★★ **And the reason it works emotionally**: *"**when you give voice to a feeling or frustration, you
diminish the power of that frustration. You remove the intensity of it.**"*

### ★★ The origin story he attaches to all of it

*"**I thought I was doing a great job until my coach told me: you're not doing a great job.** And I'm like,
what do you mean? And he told me — **how can you guess at what the client wants? Why don't you just ask
them what they want?** And after some practice we did it, and **it changed the entire trajectory of our
company** by learning this one basic technique."* The principle he draws: *"staying in the conversation,
**unafraid of it taking longer than you want** — because it's not about expediency, it's about
understanding. **I choose understanding over speed every single time, and it became our competitive
advantage.**"* Evidence: *"**45 minutes into a 15-minute call** we're deep into it… an hour and a half in,
[they say] thank you. **The battle was won here** — not in the pitch deck or the proposal."*

### ★★ Questions: what to ask and what makes a question bad

- ★★ **Question stems**: *"the best questions start with **what** — what opens the discussion, what remains
  neutral, what is unbiased. **How** questions focus on tactics, and unfortunately we're rushing to find the
  answer. **Why** questions sound accusatory if the tone isn't correct."*
- ★★ **A bad question is leading and binary** — his example: *"**would you like to eat spaghetti for
  dinner?** First of all you're assuming that I'm hungry, that I want to eat dinner, that I want to eat
  spaghetti, and **you're driving me down a chute** that says I must answer in the affirmative or I might
  risk alienating you."* The repair: *"**if you were to have dinner tonight, and hungry, what kind of food
  would you prefer?**"*
- ★ **And the flat contradiction of the classroom platitude**: *"not all questions are created equal,
  **despite what your third-grade teacher told you** — and there are such things as dumb questions. How do
  I know? **I get dumb questions all the time.**"*
- ★★ **His question bank** (borrowed, credited): *"what's on your mind"*, *"and what else"* (repeatedly),
  *"**what's the real challenge for you**"* — which he singles out: *"I love this question because it
  **supposes that there's an unreal or a fake challenge**, and often it's a smokescreen somebody presents to
  you as the real problem."* Plus *"what would be a big win for you"*, *"what's getting in your way"*,
  *"what keeps you up at night around this"*, *"**what is open for reimagining**"*, *"who else needs to be
  part of this discussion."*
- ★★ **The expert-mode compression**: *"when you start to develop the real skills, what you want to do is
  **reduce the hour-and-a-half question set down to two questions.** Take your two best questions and ask
  them up front: **why do you need a new logo? Why not do nothing at all? And why would you want to work
  with me?**"* *(He says "two" and gives three — recorded as spoken.)*

### ★★ Pricing moves demonstrated live

- ★★ **The hourly-rate symmetry-of-logic roleplay.** *"So you're saying you value this logo taking longer
  rather than shorter?"* → *"**charging by the hour punishes me for being good.**"* And the client-side
  version: *"most business people value time. **If I could do a logo for you right now and you love it in
  five minutes**, are you saying it's worth less than [$18,000], or is it worth more — **because I saved you
  a lot of time and we can go to market right now?**"* Closing move: *"that's worth $18,000 today. **Come
  back to me in a year, it'll be 26.**"*
- ★★ **The reframe that sounds like a contradiction and isn't:** *"here's the answer, and it's going to seem
  strangely hypocritical — **you should be selling time.** … **You've been measuring time in the wrong way.
  You're measuring the time in which you work on it, not the time for the client.**"* The illustration is
  an **electrician before a dinner party**: *"given the added urgency, the importance of who's coming to
  dinner, **the social standing I have with my guests** — all of a sudden my time becomes really important
  and I'm starting to stress out… **that's the time that matters.**"*
- ★★ **Anchoring, demonstrated on a live participant.** *"I do business coaching one-on-one — **I charge
  $1,000 an hour.** But I'm going to help you out: how much can you afford, realistically?"* → she says she
  can't. The reveal: *"**what I was trying to prove to you is: as soon as I say $1,000 an hour, what are
  you doing in your mind?**"* Her own account of the trap is the best part: *"if I know you're charging a
  thousand, that means you're worth… I'm thinking: **he must be worth a thousand, and I'm worth more than
  that** — that's why I can't say it, **because I don't want to go lower than a thousand.**"*
- ★★ **The Ben Burns anchor story — how he got out of one.** A prospect opened with *"I don't think we can
  afford you"* and *"10 is what he's got."* [[../entities/ben-burns]] said *"ask for 15."* *"And then I went
  home and I was thinking about it, and I'm like — **damn it, he just dropped an anchor on my face and I
  fell for it.** So I let it chill out, **the effects of the anchor wore off**, and I sent him an email:
  look, **I can help you find it for 10, but if you want me to do it, I can do it for 30.** See — **I ignore
  the anchor, I chop that thing off, I drop a new anchor: 30.**"* He then genuinely started writing a
  referral email — and the reply landed first: *"**Chris, we really thought about it, we totally want to
  work with you, we'll make the money work, let's get really creative on this.** I delete the email."*
- ★★ **Price bracketing**: *"use **big round numbers**, and give them a couple… **it could be 10,000, it
  could be 50, it could be 200. I don't know — let's get into this.** And **you're listening for the
  reaction. It's like sonar: you bounce the sound out, you see how far the wall is.** When they don't fall
  out of their chair at 200, **you know where your budget's coming in.**"*
- ★★ **Never justify the price.** *"**Justification is a sign of conceding the higher ground to your
  prospect.**"* His test is the **symmetry of logic**: *"go to a supermarket and ask why a can of beans
  costs 78 cents… go to the car wash and ask why it's $9. **If it's uncomfortable for you to ask, why do
  you think it's good for you to answer?**"* And the filter it doubles as: *"any prospect who immediately
  challenges me on the price and asks me to justify — **I already know it's going to be a long day.**"*
- ★★ **Raise prices on new clients, not existing ones.** *"It's difficult to raise your prices on repeat
  customers… **every time a new client comes in, you must raise your prices.** New client comes in, you
  raise your price; next month another one, another one — **this time next year you're charging double,
  triple, quadruple what you charge today.**"*
- ★ **Dated pricing data**: a stated coaching rate of **$1,000/hour**; a **$2,500/month minimum
  engagement** *"just for me to dedicate resources"*, *"up to 12,000 — **I have a client that pays me
  $12,000 a month**"*; and a roleplay range of **$18,000–$40,000** to build a personal brand to 100K
  followers from ~10K.

### ★★ Walking away, and the filter

★★ **Mistake four is having no filter**: *"anybody with a heartbeat and some money to spend, you'll take on
as a client."* His four conditions for accepting money: **can you deliver value to them; would it give you
joy; do you like this person; are the budget and timeline appropriate.** And the reading a client gives an
unfiltered vendor: *"**I have no standards, I'm not discriminating enough, and I'm probably not someone you
can trust.**"* With the market observation behind it: the most sought-after people *"are creating sometimes
artificial and real **filters or barriers** in order to work with them — and **it makes us want to work with
them even more.**"*

★★ **The £2,000 branding roleplay** shows the walk-away done warmly rather than coldly: *"I think I know
what you want, **I'm just not sure I'm the guy to do it for you** … I don't want to say no to you, because
**I like your vibe, I like your vision** — but I'm not sure this is going to work. **Is there anything else
I'm missing here?**"* Then, only after she confirms the budget is fixed: *"sounds like you can't do it —
**you probably don't need me.** … If you should talk to other people and you don't like what they do, feel
free to give me a ring. **The price is still the same**, but then I can help you."* The pivot that reopens
it is a question, not a discount: *"**just out of curiosity, what's motivating this conversation today?**"*
— which surfaces *"millions"* in lost opportunity, and then **Jim Rohn's minors and majors**: *"you don't
want to put a **major effort into a minor goal** — you don't want to spend millions of dollars to buy a
suit. **Conversely, you don't want to make a minor effort for a major goal.**"* Then the insurance question
(*"how much percentage would you spend?"* → 30% → *"30% of a million… I think that's 300,000. **Here's the
great news: I can help you for a lot less than that.**"*).

★★ **The $1,000 video roleplay** is the same machinery aimed at *declining*: he does the arithmetic **with**
the buyer (2–3 walk-ins/day × ~$100 average × 30 days = **$7,500/month**), then turns it around —
*"are you going to do a $1,000 video every month?"* — and finally uses the buyer's own spending as evidence:
*"**how much did that refrigeration unit cost you? How much is the AC?** … and **I saw the BMW parked
outside, the 5 series, the M5.** … **people tend to spend money on what they value.**"* His stated instinct:
*"when somebody says I don't have a lot of money to do something, **my first instinct is: let's not do it,
it's not important.**"* Ending with the walk-away extended to the whole market — *"I would encourage you not
to spend that money **with anybody**, not just me"* — and a Fiverr jab.

★★ **The unsell story, told as a real one.** A coaching client, now an employee: *"clients call me and they
just run over me. I don't feel like they respect me."* His instruction: *"find a client you absolutely do
not want to work with, that you would hate their guts… **when your client calls you, I want you to unsell
yourself. I want you to serve the client because you don't want to work with them. I want you to retreat
and see if they follow.**"* Outcome: she declined, diagnosed the problem anyway, offered other options —
and the client *"later called and said **I just recommended you to two friends**."* Her line: *"**Chris, for
the first time in a really long time, I felt respected. I felt wanted.**"* His diagnosis of the room:
*"**all of you are chasing your clients and trying to act fakely as a generous person, but you're acting in
your own self-interest. That's the problem.**"*

★★ **Retreat and follow**, stated as a general law: *"when you chase after someone and you chase after a
job, **their tendency is to want to run away.** It happens in dating, it happens in nature… **take a half
step back to see if the person's interested, and they naturally will follow.** … **We like to pursue
people, we don't like to be pursued.**"* Operationalised: *"**you should try to kill an engagement at least
three times.**"*

### ★ Other durable pieces

- ★★ **The three outcomes of any prospect interaction**: *"**one, they hire us** — which is what we always
  think we want. **Two, they hire somebody else. Three, they do nothing.** **Our only obligation is to be
  clear and provide them with information so they can make a decision.**"*
- ★★ **Don't fight the prospect** (the "Anthony" roleplay): *"**what happens when I push Anthony? He's going
  to push back much harder than I can handle.**"* Instead he asks which of the prospect's own priorities
  matters most, gets *"sales"*, and immediately concedes: *"sounds like you need sales help. **Maybe I'm not
  the right person.** … **Are we fighting? No.**"* The principle: *"**the only thing clients will do is what
  they already want to do** — so my job is to find out what they want to do."* With the trade-off named:
  *"the problem is, **if that's all you sell, you're screwed.**"* And the upside of the loss: *"he will not
  be a customer today. **I'm totally okay with that. He might tell three other people.**"*
- ★★ **Closing too early**, illustrated on himself: someone approached after a workshop with *"great
  workshop — **when are you going to hire me?**"* — *"whoa. **Take me out on a date first.** … I didn't even
  know I liked you, I didn't know I was hiring, and I don't even know what you're qualified for."* The read:
  *"it says **I'm desperate and I'm self-interested.**"*
- ★★ **Tone of voice.** He demonstrates fast talking on camera and then names what it produces: *"**why is
  Chris talking so fast at me right now? Is there something he wants? Is the house on fire? Is he desperate
  to get out of here?**"* The counter is **Chris Voss's late-night FM DJ voice** (*Never Split the
  Difference*) — *"speak in a slow, low tone, take lots of breaths"* — which he performs
  (*"you're cruising down the PCH…"*). Rule: *"if somebody's really angry and you speak to them in a slow,
  calm, deliberate voice, **it starts to calm them down**; and if you want somebody to get excited, **you
  speed up.**"*
- ★★ **Objection coaching, live** (a caller with two hours before a client call): **ask what the ask
  actually is, get them to say it in their own words**, then *"say it back to them — **here's what I heard**
  — so now they can even hear the absurdity of their request."* His absurdity handle: *"**you really want
  red apples in the middle of the desert?**"* → *"**how do you propose we do that?** How do we do that
  **and be sustainable?**"* Paired with genuine empathy work: *"ask them **what were the warning signs of
  the last vendor who burned you**"*, then remove each one specifically (meet the owner, visit the office,
  a phased engagement to mitigate risk). *"We're always on the same side."*
- ★ **Social proof is non-negotiable**: *"**people do not want to buy from people who have never
  demonstrated they can do this.** If you create logos and you don't show that you do logos at the highest
  level, **why would anybody hire you?**"*
- ★ **The give-first claim**, presented as someone else's and endorsed: *"**if you want to make a million
  dollars, give five years of value. Do not ask for a single thing.** … Every time you ask for something
  **you drop to the bottom again**, you start over, you're losing goodwill. **The more goodwill you create
  for other people, the bigger the ask can be.**"*
- ★ **A blunt qualification heuristic**, recorded as spoken: *"I know a client who cannot afford me —
  **because they're the owner and the founder.** If the owner and the founder calls me, **I know it's not
  going to work. Too small of a company.**" ⚠️ In tension with his own long-standing advocacy for small
  studios and solo creatives; flag rather than smooth over.
