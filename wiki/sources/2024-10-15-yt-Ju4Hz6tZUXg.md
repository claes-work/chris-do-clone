## ★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★ The founder-vs-company following argument, worked live

He builds it from the room rather than asserting it — first asking what corporate accounts actually
use social *for*, then:

> ★★★★ *"**Who typically has more followers — the corporate brand, or the CEO or the founder? By a
> wide margin, right?** If you look at **Tesla** and you look at **Elon Musk** — it's not even
> close. **This is shocking to me.** If you look at **Apple** and **Tim Cook** — **who's not even the
> founder** — Tim Cook has **way more followers than Apple.** You look at **Virgin** versus
> **Richard Branson** — totally different."*

> ⚠️ ★★★★ **And he immediately bounds the claim**: *"**it seems to hold true IF it's an interesting
> CEO.**"*

✅ **The qualifier is the part worth keeping.** The corpus holds the personal-brand-beats-corporate
thesis in several places (`tim cook` **7**, `richard branson` **4**); **what it does not hold is him
narrowing it.** The Tim Cook example is chosen deliberately — *"not even the founder"* — to show the
effect is not about founding, and then the *"interesting CEO"* condition concedes the effect is not
universal either.

⚠️ **Do not repeat the follower comparison without the qualifier**; on this source the claim is
conditional, not general.
