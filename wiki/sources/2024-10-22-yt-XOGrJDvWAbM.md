# Chris Do Immigrant Journey Interview w/ Arjun Dhingra (2024-10-22)

> **Attribution:** ✅ **CHRIS IS THE SUBJECT.** The host interviews him about his own life; the
> answers are his throughout. The host's framing and his own immigrant background are `[HOST]` and
> are not persona data.

## Summary

★★★★★ **A landmark biographical source.** The fullest first-person account in the corpus of **what
the refugee background actually left him with**, delivered without romance — plus **attachment as the
mechanism of being stuck**, another attribution to his business mentor, and two more instances of him
**flagging a borrowing he cannot cite.**

## Key claims (2024-10-22)

### ★★★★★ "I'm a first generation immigrant, so I wasn't born here"

> ★★★★★ *"**I'm a FIRST-generation immigrant, so I wasn't born here.** I'm still figuring out my way,
> my own identity. And **when you flee a country due to war, civil war, and you flee communism and you
> start over, I think there are CERTAIN TRAUMAS THAT ARE JUST INHERITED IN THE BODY — that I don't
> learn about until much later, after talking to a therapist — that MY PARENTS FEEL.**"*
>
> ★★★★★ *"**I can't imagine what it's like to drop everything — your identity, your language, your
> culture — and start over somewhere else where EVERYTHING IS STACKED AGAINST YOU.** And so that's
> held in their bodies, as my parents. **BUT THEY SHIELDED US FROM IT** — but there are little
> behaviours that, once you look back on your life, you see happen all the time."*

✅ **Note the care:** the trauma is attributed to **his parents**, the shielding is credited to them,
and his own knowledge of it is dated to therapy *"much later."*

### ★★★★★ The grades standard — the sharpest telling in the corpus

> ★★★★★ *"The stereotype of the tiger mom, high expectations of Asian children to do well in school —
> **it's a given. IF YOU GET AN A, THAT'S CALLED AVERAGE. You need to get like an A-plus.** … I
> remember very clearly **THE FEAR OF GOD WAS PUT IN ME** going to school: **if you don't get an A
> you're not doing well; if you get a B it's acceptable; BUT IF YOU GET A C, DON'T EVEN BOTHER COMING
> HOME.**"*
>
> ★★★★★ *"I remember talking to my friends who are Caucasian, who grew up here multigenerational —
> **they're like: I got a C, I'm getting a new BMX bike.** I'm like: **LIFE IS SO UNFAIR. Here I am
> getting A's, A-minuses — and it's not something you get praise thrown upon you. THERE'S NO REWARD.
> IT'S JUST LACK OF PUNISHMENT — IS ALL THAT IS.**"*

✅ **Sharpens the existing *"A is just kind of average"* record** with the BMX comparison and, above
all, **the reward structure named**: *"it's just lack of punishment."*

### ★★★★★ Hardship as an inherited capability — stated without romance

> ★★★★★ *"When you grow up with war, or as a refugee, and all the hardships — **IN THE MOMENT IT
> DOESN'T FEEL LIKE IT'S A GIFT. BUT IT ACTUALLY IS a gift, because YOU LEARN TO ADAPT TO SURVIVE OUT
> OF NECESSITY.** And those skills, those gifts given as a child, carry into adulthood — because if
> you're living somewhere very challenging mentally, physically or economically, **you learn to adapt
> and you learn to make do, so THAT BECOMES THE NORMAL, THAT'S THE BASELINE. So as you progress in
> life, IT SEEMS LIKE NOTHING IS EVER AS BAD AS IT USED TO BE.**"*

★★★★ **The frame he puts around it**, relayed from a same-day guest: *"the question he asks is: **are
we capable of change? All of us are capable of change WHEN OUR SURVIVAL IS DEPENDENT ON IT.**"* ⚠️
**That question is the other speaker's** (Erwin McManus, named), not Chris's.

★★★★★ **The philosophical position he attaches:** *"it's a **Buddhist idea** — **we should NOT try to
escape or run away from pain or challenge, because LIVING THE EXISTENCE IS PAINFUL.** Once we embrace
that and stop trying to avoid our problems, I think we'll become much more happy and at peace. **So I
just accept everything, good or bad.** It's really hot outside — but that's just the way it is. **And
it's OUR THINKING of it, THE STORY THAT WE TELL OURSELVES, that frames it as good or bad.**"*

★★★★★ **Dated to his twenties, and explicitly stoic:** *"**once I'm in my early to mid twenties I've
developed a different mindset: I'M IN CONTROL OF MY DESTINY, MY FATE — how hard I work, where I want
to go to school.**"* → *"at this point **it's just work. It's not horrible work — it's JOY.** It's not
exuberant, euphoric joy — **I try to rein in the emotions, to be AS STOIC AS POSSIBLE.**"* ✅ Consistent
with the Spock material in [[../persona/biography|biography]].

### ★★★★★ Attachment is what makes you stuck

> ★★★★★ *"**What makes us stuck is ATTACHMENT. We don't like problems — BUT WE ARE MORE AFRAID OF
> CHANGE AND THE UNKNOWN THAN WE ARE OF OUR PROBLEMS.** So when there's a better solution — because
> it's unknown, unpredictable, no guarantees — **WE'LL STICK WITH THE DEVIL THAT WE KNOW.** And we
> become really attached to these things that define us, this identity, and we carry it with us —
> **and it's a very heavy burden to continue to carry.**"*

★★★★★ **The worked example, which is the best part:** *"if you grew up looking and sounding a certain
way, somebody might be picking on you — and then you have that thing. **Even though it didn't serve
you, IT PROTECTED YOU for a little bit — but you still carry: I'M GOING TO BE PICKED ON FOR THE REST OF
MY LIFE.** And **you can be a captain of industry, a city council person, chairperson of the board —
and you still [think]: 'are you slighting me right now?' It's like: NOBODY'S PICKING A FIGHT WITH YOU
ANYMORE. THAT WAS IN THE PAST.**"*

> ★★★★★ **Credited to his mentor:** *"**something I learned from MY BUSINESS MENTOR was that
> oftentimes WE ARE RESPONDING TO THINGS THAT ARE NOT HERE RIGHT NOW.** So if your wife, your husband,
> your children say something to you, **you're recalling a memory from the past — and it's not even
> serving you any more, but you hold on to it so tightly.**"*

⚠️ **He says "my business mentor" and does NOT name him here.** The corpus establishes that role as
[[../entities/kier-mclaren|Kier McLaren]], but **this source does not say so** — record the
attribution as he gave it.

★★★★★ **The unburdening argument:** *"**your body and your mind, your spirit and your soul CAN ONLY
HOLD SO MUCH.** And in order for you to receive new things, new gifts, **you have to UNBURDEN
yourself, UNENCUMBER yourself, and get rid of the weight — so that you can actually have the STRENGTH
TO CARRY SOMETHING ELSE.**"*

### ★★★★★ Blame goes both ways

> ★★★★★ *"**The only person you have to blame for the things that you don't have — OR THE THINGS THAT
> YOU HAVE — is yourself. BLAME GOES BOTH WAYS.** When you achieve something it's: it's my credit, I
> should receive the accolades. But when things don't go your way **it's always somebody else's fault
> — somebody didn't open the door for you, the man is trying to hold you down. IT'S ALL YOU.**"*

★★★★ **And the two-step he attaches:** *"**you can achieve as much as you want in your life — you just
have to be WILLING TO SEE IT, and then TO WORK TOWARDS IT.** You have to **see the goal clearly** —
and once you have clarity in the vision, **then you can actually take steps towards it.**"*

### ★★★★ Two borrowings, one flagged as uncitable

- ★★★★ *"There's a phrase — **I forget who. I SHOULD BE ABLE TO CITE THIS QUOTE** — it's like: **DO
  LESS BUT BETTER.** Everybody's trying to do more, **but doing more means you're not doing as good a
  job.** So if you could just do less but better, I think everything works out."*
  ✅ **A third instance in this run of him flagging an uncited borrowing** rather than absorbing it.
- ★★★★ *"There's a **BRUCE LEE** quote that **I always come back to, AS A MARTIAL ARTIST**: **'human
  beings are all merely works of progress, who mistakenly think from time to time that they are
  done.'**"* ✅ *"As a martial artist"* is a small biographical self-description.
