# One LinkedIn Post That Changed My Business Forever w/ Avital

> **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary.** The guest is **Avital** (first name from the video title; surname not
> stated on air, `attribution: uncertain`), a London-born, Israel-based brand strategist who runs an agency
> branding *"thought leaders and small businesses."* **Her story and her frameworks are hers** and are
> fenced below. Chris's own material — including two pieces of **notable biography** and a
> **self-implicating anecdote he does not dispute** — comes first.
>
> Note on how the episode came about, told openly on air: she cold-DM'd him on Instagram, and he had
> **forgotten why the call was happening** — *"in full transparency I forgot why we're having this call…
> **I literally had to reach out and say: hey, I'm sorry, how do we know each other again?**"* He then uses
> that as the proof of her pitch: *"**it's not easy to do. The pitch, the proof, the way you show up, the
> language — it was very precise.**"*

## Summary — Chris's material

★★★ **Biography, stated plainly while explaining why her confidence is alien to him.** Asked why a
networking reception would frighten him, he answers: *"because **I'm an introvert, I'm socially awkward**…
my dad is not a doctor, my mom [is not] in investment banking — **there's the pedigree there. And we're
refugees from Vietnam, so I've never really felt kind of at home until more recently, and it's been a
challenge of mine.**"* Then the concrete version: ★★ *"the first thing in my mind is **where's the exit,
and how do I get back to my hotel room — because I don't want to do any of this.**"*

> ★★★ **"I've never really felt kind of at home until more recently"** is new language in the corpus for
> the refugee/outsider material, and it dates the change to recent years. It belongs beside the
> *"loud introvert"* rationale from [[2024-09-29-yt-rl69VQbs3gA]] and the outsider/middle-child account in
> `persona/biography.md`.

★ **A second, smaller confirmation**: the guest refers to a month-long trip *"back to where you were born —
in Colombia?"* and is corrected mid-sentence to **Vietnam**.

★★ **The line he draws from her story** — his own framing, not hers: *"the difference between successful
people and struggling people is: **successful people take life's bumps and they turn them into gold**, and
the struggling people look at the bumps and **keep tripping over the same bump over and over again for a
really long time.** … Most of your bad experiences can be turned into wonderful learning opportunities —
**you can take straw or hay and turn it into gold.**"*

★★ **He reads her as the live proof of Priestley's key-person-of-influence argument**, and says so
explicitly to the audience: *"she's **the living embodiment of what Daniel Priestley talks about**… **she
doesn't play small, she plays big**, and she knows how to tell her story in a way that makes her separate
from everyone else in the room. **She knows how to be different — that's about branding. Different is
better than better.**"* With the instruction attached: *"all of you sitting back having all these excuses
about *I'm not ready, only if this happened then I'll be ready* — **take a note from this conversation.**
Now, **she's coming with a thousand watts of energy. Most of us aren't like that — forget about that part.
Just look at the structure of what she's done.**"*

★ **Cross-reference to his own current teaching**: *"incredible synchronicity — **I just spoke to my Pro
Group yesterday about the courage to ask**, and **if you don't ask, the answer is always no.** Most people
get stuck at that idea — **the fear of rejection and the self-story that brings.**"*

★★ **On upsell funnels, unusually harsh.** Hearing that her paid consult ended with a pop-up offering to
extend for more money: *"I suspect this is probably how a lot of people run their businesses —
**everything that you buy is a funnel to buy something else, and something more expensive**, and at a
certain point you get caught up in all that stuff. **I find that those practices are despicable.**"*

★★ **On what to do when a post takes off** — his own framing of the question, then his rule: *"**not
everything you do is going to be fire. But if there's fire — fires need things. They need fuel and they
need oxygen.**"* And the failure mode: *"a lot of creators **hit and run** — they make the post and they
just leave, and they're expecting all this engagement and **they don't do anything with it.**"* His
practical version: *"that's not to say you need to be online 24/7 answering every single comment, **but
there should be a little window** — maybe an hour after you post, go in and check, give feedback, answer
questions, **so people know that a human, a real person, is addressing whatever their concerns are.**"*
The reason it compounds: *"if you don't comment back and don't answer any questions, **it becomes a
pattern** — and eventually people start to check out."*

### ★★★ The self-implicating anecdote — recorded because he lets it stand

The guest raises it herself, unprompted, while arguing for giving people grace: *"you shared a story a while
ago about an interaction with someone where **you really let them down. They caught you on a bad day** and
they weren't getting to their question, and you were like: **if you're a real fan you would have read this
about me — you're clearly not a real fan.** And it ended badly, and they wrote a comment: **met Chris, and
he wasn't so great. Really a disappointment when you meet your fans in real life.**"*

★★★ **Chris does not dispute it or explain it away.** He answers by endorsing her conclusion about how such
things should be handled: *"although you were really disappointed and frustrated with the result of that
call, **you gave grace** — because you say to yourself: **we all have bad days, we're not always perfect,
and we still believe in the goodness of people.** This could be the one time in which they effed up, and
that's okay. **We're not letting them off the hook, but we don't need to burn their whole house down
because of one instance.**"*

> ★★★ **This corroborates, from the outside, the self-description he gives two days later in
> [[2024-10-31-yt-U1anI0b1nvI]]**: *"more often than not I usually destroy people"*, *"I am abrasive, I am
> opinionated"*, *"I want to go for impact, not for feelings."* **Keep the pair together** — one is his
> account of himself, the other is a documented instance with a cost. Neither cancels the other.

★★ **On what an audience is for**: *"social media attention can be great — **but what do you do with this
fame or this audience that you have? Your whole point should be to help people** — to serve your clients,
or to serve people in your network. **If I can use this platform to connect you to someone in my network,
then I've done a good thing with my fame or the reach that I have.**"*

★ **Voice, and a long comic runner.** The episode ends in an extended riff on shared food and double-dipping
— *"**this is not even American, this is my Asian germophobic thing**"* — including the detail that at a
dinner party he placed a shared spoon centrally *"and I watched each person, and as soon as someone
double-dipped **I just stopped going. I was like: yo, observe protocol. Even the 25-year-old kid is
observing protocol.**"* Then: *"**I didn't even share food with my kids.** … The only person I share food
with is my wife, and that's it."* Asked whether he consulted his sons about the rule: *"**no, because I'm
an authoritarian**"* — immediately walked back as a joke — *"no, we just say: hygiene. **I don't know where
everybody's mouth has been.**"* He turns it into a call to action: *"just write in the comments: **dip or
double dip.**"* And closes: *"even though it ended kind of weird."*

★ **Closing generosity**, worth recording as voice: *"I'm impressed by your worldview… and **I have yet a
lot to learn — even though I'm 20 years your senior, I have a lot to learn from you.**"*

## ⚠️ FENCED — the guest's story and frameworks, attributed to her

- **The viral post.** She paid **$250** for a **15-minute** consult with a personal-branding figure; it was
  cancelled five minutes before, rescheduled, opened with the consultant's own story, addressed a question
  she hadn't asked, produced a pop-up offering **$150 for another 15 minutes**, and ended with a request for
  a recommendation. Months later she wrote it up on LinkedIn — *"really short to the point, **I didn't tag
  anyone, I did no image**"* — and it reached **2.7 million impressions**, 1,000+ comments, ~4,000 likes,
  ~2,500 new followers. **She named no one, then or since**: *"the amount of DMs being like *who was this* —
  **we're here to take that person down. That's not what it's about.** … I don't think they were frauds, I
  don't think they're a bad person. **It was a bad day.**"*
- **What she did with it** (her answer to Chris's fire/oxygen question): replied to **every comment**,
  looked up each commenter, followed up by DM, and took **40–50 calls in two weeks**; three podcast
  invitations, three paid consults, and three branding leads followed. Then she deliberately posted twice
  more — once about getting a friend a job (*"**true magic happens when you make a difference offline to
  real people**"*), once about the substrate underneath a viral moment (*"**is there a call to action on
  their LinkedIn banner? Is the about section making sense with the post you've read?**"*).
- ★ **Her networking doctrine**, against the usual advice: *"people think the idea of a networking event is
  to network the room, to master the room, to leave with a ton of contacts… **the idea is to make one solid
  connection**, and leave knowing that when you add that person on LinkedIn and when you email, **they're
  going to email you back.**"*
- ★ **The outsider advantage**, which Chris names for her: she used being visibly foreign as licence not to
  know who anyone was — *"**could you just remind me in what capacity you were working for him?**"* — where
  the same position usually produces *"I'm an outsider, I have no right to be here, I'm going to feel really
  small."*
- **"If you never ask, the answer will always be no"** — her stated motto, demonstrated by asking (at 19,
  as an unpaid intern) to attend a donors' reception she wasn't allowed at, and later by accepting a US
  speaking invitation on three conditions including *"I'm bringing my baby, and I need you to help me find a
  babysitter."*
- **Her business model and the word she wants used more**: **eight clients a year, two per quarter**, at a
  premium — against the *"how do you scale, how do you make money while you sleep"* pressure. *"**We don't
  hear the word enough: that it is enough.** … **There is a time for everything**, and maybe my thirties
  won't be that — but there will be time afterwards, and I'm okay with that."* Boundaries: no work
  **3:30–7pm**, and **25 hours** of no technology from Friday to Saturday evening as an orthodox Jew.
- **The brand-strategy questionnaire** she inherited from a friend's late friend, then refined by asking
  every client three months later *"**was anything missing?**"* — *"**no one ever says to be obsessive about
  the feedback, but that is how you grow.**"*
- ★ **On parenting**: normalise failure at the dinner table (*"tell me something that was great, tell me
  something that really didn't work out, and how would you do it differently"*), and stand behind the child
  on the stairs **without letting them know you're there.** Her line, which Chris calls *"a bar"*:
  ★ *"**failure is not only okay — it's the making of you.**"*

_Also referenced:_ *"**don't post and ghost**"* is credited by the guest to a previous Futur guest,
**"Alec"** — surname not given, `attribution: uncertain` — *"his whole thing is growing on LinkedIn not by
creating posts but by commenting."*
