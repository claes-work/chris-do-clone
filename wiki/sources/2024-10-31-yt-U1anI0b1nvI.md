## Summary

### ★★★ The thesis, and the frame he chose for it

He opens by placing himself as a heretic. His grandmother is *"a deeply religious Catholic woman… the kind
who would call you on a Sunday night and say **I didn't see you at church**"*; he grew up in the Catholic
church and went to Bible school. *"So father forgive me for what I'm about to do. I'm going to talk about
the church — **not the church you're thinking of. The Church of personal branding**, and how the vast
majority of folks usher and pray to a false god… **I'm here as the blasphemer, the one who was excised from
the church, to tell you: everything you were taught about personal branding is wrong and untrue.** You can
think of me as **the anti-Chris.**"* Later, plainly: *"**In the Church of personal branding I am the
blasphemer. I am the Antichrist**"* — with the disclaimer *"I'm not trying to put myself in a messiah
complex, I'm just saying **I'm the anti-church.**"*

★★★ **The thesis itself**, quoted back to him by Mo from a previous recording and confirmed: *"**personal
branding for me is a process of discovering who you are, and getting in touch with that, and healing from
trauma that we don't even understand we have.**"*

★★★ **And its hardest consequence, stated first**: *"**I don't think we should pursue personal branding if
you believe the thesis is to get clients — at all. This is the wrong thing to do.**"* His objection to the
alternative: *"almost everybody I know posts content on social media as **a thinly disguised advertisement**
for their business… and it makes us all cringe. **Even if you lead with 80% value and at that last little
20% you ask me for something, you kind of burn all the goodwill.**"* His replacement: *"**generosity
marketing** — you just give lots and lots of value, and **when you're ready to ask, just ask.** You don't
have to worry about hiding it in something. **We don't have to use this Trojan Horse concept.**"*

★★ **What the "experts" are actually selling**: *"personal branding has become a **cultural zeitgeist**…
a **lightning-rod word** that if you use, all of a sudden everything you say has more gravity. **Few of us
can build a brand; all of us have a personal brand.** So they're using this new label to **put lipstick on
a pig** — they're teaching you marketing funnels, sales strategies, positioning. And I say it's all
hogwash, **because who the hell are you?** … **You're just packaging things up. Call it packaging — I'm
okay with that.**"*

### ★★★ The origin story: 2014, the Art Center auditorium

*"I'm at Art Center, it is **2014**, I'm sitting in the **LA Times Auditorium**… somebody sitting in front
of me, a student — and at that time I was already teaching — they turned around: *oh, you're one of the
professors here?*"* They were talking about a designer. *"They're like: **do you know who he is?** I said
no. And she was flabbergasted — **come on, stop fronting, everybody knows who Aaron is.**"* He went home and
Googled it: **Aaron Draplin**, fresh off a TED talk in Portland.

★★★ **The moment he says set his direction**, and he names the reference: *"this is like a scene from
*When Harry Met Sally* — **I'll have what she's having.** … **I want to do what he's doing, so that people
are talking in the theatre saying: *you don't know Chris Do? You should be ashamed of yourself. Stop
fronting, everybody knows him.*"*

★ **He resists the obvious reading of it.** Asked whether he wanted that reverence: *"it was **nothing more
than curiosity** — here's a name I've not heard before… **I'm a person who's really driven by my
curiosity.** I've done well in life by following my curiosity wherever it leads. I go to the nth degree —
★★ **people are surface swimmers; I'm a dolphin. I'm a deep diver.** I'm going to go to the bottom of the
ocean: what's down there, what do I need to know about this thing."* And: *"**I'm not looking for anyone to
revere me. I don't want to be put on that kind of status.**"*

★★ **The context that makes it strange**, in his own accounting: by 2014 he was **19 years into his
business** (*"1995 to 2014"*), teaching, judging shows, *"I've won the Emmy already — in fact **our agency
has won two Emmys**, a bunch of Clios, Addys, BDA Awards, Telly Awards, so many awards. **The awards were
like: what's the point? Now we can just apply and we'll just get an award, because our work was really
good.**"* But: ★★ *"**that was for the work, not for me.** It was almost always for the work — *oh, Chris
Do and Blind team does X, Y and Z for X client, achieved certain results.* Cool. **It was always result and
work driven.**"*

★ **On being called a design celebrity**: *"a couple of years into my journey of making content people
started to introduce me as a **design celebrity**, and it made me feel **really uncomfortable**. I am not a
design celebrity — **I don't even know that such a thing exists.**"*

### ★★ The Draplin / Sagmeister comparison

He built a presentation for the Futur Pro community *"six or seven years ago"* (≈2017–18) on *"two people in
the design community with equally really strong personal brands **who could not be more different**."*

| **Aaron Draplin** | **Stefan Sagmeister** |
|---|---|
| Opens with *"my name is **Aaron James Draplin**"* — ★ *"who tells you their middle name? Nobody tells you their middle name"* | *"Austrian, very proper designer, whose work is exhibited in museums, has written many books… **part of that elite design class**"* |
| *"I've never won an award — **look it up. Never tried.** No professional accolades. **Google that.**"* | Judges award shows, has won all the awards |
| *"There's exactly one employee — it's me — and **I don't have to wear pants at work**"* (the reveal: he wears shorts) | Sabbaticals, deep ideas, art history and philosophy |

★★ **The conclusion he drew**: *"**they're probably not sharing the same fan base, but they're both powerful
people.** We knew who they were, we knew what they stood for, **we knew what they stood against.**"* And the
detail that dates his own position: *"at that time they were the monsters on social media — Instagram,
probably 100,000-plus. **And there I am with my measly 15,000 followers**, thinking: how does one attain
that kind of height?"*

★ **Why Draplin's method worked**: *"he talks about himself and **integrates his whole life, his whole
being, into the work that he does** — and it was so refreshing, because **designers are very snooty, very
proper, especially the ones who speak on stages.**"* He *"gets some boos and hisses here and there, **but he
don't care** — he goes: *oh, tough crowd huh? All right, all right.* **He just embraces it. And that's what
I learned.**"*

★ **Hasan Minhaj's *Homecoming King*** as the second model, with the honest caveat attached: *"who's been
through a turbulent year for some of the things he's talked about that were somewhat fabricated — **but
what I saw** when he did his comedy special was a really beautiful thing. A brown man… **mixing English with
his native language, celebrating his whole culture.** … **He embraced all of it. His identity.**"* The
generalisation: *"the more we lean into our ethnicity, our culture, our idiosyncratic nature, the more
people will love us. **And it's not even about that — because we need to love us.**"*

### ★★★ The disagreement, on record

His counter-evidence: *"guys are attracted to success — **the trappings, the symbolisms, the signals.** If

famous**… *if you pay me 200 grand I'll show up to your event and take pictures with whoever you want* —

soul.**"*

★★ **The MrBeast example**, used carefully: *"the biggest creator on YouTube… **we know a personality, we

★★★ **The headline claim, which he flags as the headline**: *"here's your headline for the title: **I think
98% of creators that you see on the internet are all fake. They're all fake. They're all building a lie.

lying, for fraud — or being sued in court.** Because **people feel the need to put on a certain persona to
be accepted, to be liked, to be respected and admired. And I'm just sick of it.**"* His example, unnamed:
*"certain health and fitness gurus who purportedly sold their companies for a certain dollar amount… **they

★ **The 2% exception**: *"there's a 2% there that I'm like — **you're real. And I like you.** But **they're

### ★★★ Vulnerability — the distinction he insists on

Mo raises a creator who publicly withdrew from vulnerability after it was *"weaponised"* against her.
★★★ *"What this person did was **show up and present their vulnerability as a means to get more of an
audience. Wrong. Wrong already.** … That's like saying *poor me, I've struggled so much, don't you feel so
bad for me.* It's — **I hate to say it — pity porn.**"*

★★★ **The version he means instead**: *"**I've accepted that I have certain shortcomings. I've healed from
it — and more importantly, I've grown from it.** I've understood why these things that are so ugly, that
most people don't like, **have made me who I am.**"* Illustrated with his own material: *"I grew up
**relatively poor as an immigrant** until my parents worked their way up to the upper middle class, and **I
had a strange relationship with money** — and I talk about this. And I talk about **being bullied, about
being singled out, and being thrown racial slurs from people who didn't know me at all because they thought
it was fun.** **I don't use it to say: won't you feel sorry for me.**"* Because: *"**I've learned to lean
into the things that make me different, and to embrace the entire story — not just the part I think you
want to hear.** Sometimes I talk about **being a good father, sometimes about being a bad father; a good
husband, a bad husband.** All that makes me me."*

★★★ **And the flat rule**: *"**vulnerability is not a thing you try. It's either you're vulnerable or you're
not.**"* Plus the cost he says must be accepted up front: *"**in order to do so you have to be willing to
alienate people** — because when you reveal yourself, people don't like it. **They just don't like it.**"*
The quote he reaches for (he attributes it to Kurt Cobain): *"**I'd rather be hated for the person I am than
loved for the person that I'm not.**"*

### ★★★ Praise vs. criticism — the inversion

★★★ *"Here's another Antichrist statement: **you should not seek praise. You should seek constructive
criticism. You should be sad when people praise you, and you should be joyful and delighted when people
give you solid constructive criticism.**"*

The reasoning: *"when people say *you did a really good job*… **what you're saying is: I don't know my own
inherent goodness. I did not know I did a good job. I'm waiting for someone else to tell me.** So once
again **we've given the controls of our emotional regulation to some other person.**"*

★★★ **And the sharpest form of it**: *"when people say **I'm proud of you**, what people don't realise is
they're subconsciously, unwittingly saying: **I have power over you** — and me being proud of you is
something that you should like."* Parents are the near-exception (*"they brought you into this world"*) —
*"but **I still find it problematic that parents say it. Why are you proud of me right now? Because I did
something that you wanted me to do.** … **What is the inverse?** *When you don't do the things I want, I am
not proud of you.* **That's a nasty codependent relationship** — you don't notice it when you're young, and
then you get older and think: **wow, I've been doing a lot of things for reasons other than internal.**"*

★★ *"Are **pride, love, joy, generosity** like **little pieces of candy to be doled out from time to
time**? **Aren't you deserving of love, joy and pride by merely existing?**"*

★★★ **What he says he tells his sons** — the passage the interviewer becomes emotional over, because he
adopted it with his own children: *"from a very young age I would tell my children, especially the ones
that weren't so sure of themselves: **I'm proud of you. I love you. There's nothing that you ever could do
that would ever change that. No matter what you did — even if you committed a crime, the most heinous
crime, you murdered somebody — I still would love you. Do not ever allow anyone else to tell you otherwise.
You don't have to do anything to deserve this. You don't have to do anything to earn this. You get it just
by existing.**"* Mo reports having said it to his own children every night for six or seven months, until
*"my son can recite it from memory."* Chris's reading of why it made him cry: ★★ *"**there's a child inside
of you that needed to hear it too. We all wish that our parents were better. It's too late — but we can
parent ourselves differently.**"* And on children generally: *"these very young, fertile minds, **these
gardens — the seeds you plant. You can put weeds in, or you can grow vegetables and fruit and plant a mighty
oak tree in there.**"*

★★ **Feedback, by contrast, must be earned.** *"Unlike the love, the generosity, the joy and someone feeling
proud of you — **you have to earn the feedback.** … I don't care enough about you to take out my time and
put my intellectual ideas and creativity into solving a problem. **People pay me for this stuff.** People
ask me all the time: can you look at my logo, can you look at my website? **I don't have time. You haven't
earned that from me.**"*

★★ **Why praise from the uninformed is worse than useless**: *"there are people out there **whose lives are
a mess, who are financially broken, working through their own trauma, and they don't have any design
sensibility** — and they say *that was a good piece of design*, and you're like *oh my god, you just made my
day, I'm on cloud nine.* **So the opinion of an uninformed person matters to you.** … **It's like a drug —
the more you chase it, the less effective it is; the more you need it.** You become **a very needy person
whose emotional wellbeing is regulated by the random comments of strangers.**"* Then the line the episode
opens on: ★★ *"**you some kind of compliment ho? You turning tricks on the street to get a compliment?
That's what you become.**"*

★★★ **The boat parable**, which is the cleanest statement of the whole argument: *"you have to cross the
English Channel, you're a first-time boat builder and I'm a master boat builder. You ask me to inspect it.
**I say: yep, looks pretty good** — in my mind: *you ain't going to make it, you're going to die on the
ocean.* You're like: thanks, I have all the confidence in the world… and then **you drown and you die.**
The other scenario: **this boat will capsize, you've got to redo this and this part** — and you're like:
oh my god, so much work, I just want to get on the water… **and you know what you're thinking? *F you, I
just want you to praise me.*** When I present that example, **nobody wants the first of the two options.
But that's how you act.**"* ★★★ *"**You can seek empty praise, or you can hear the hard things to help you
grow. You can't have both.**"*

★★ **The "ask-hole"** (he flags it as not his term): *"people in my inner circle — you ask me for my
opinion, you tell me about your idea, and they tell you why it doesn't work, and **what do you do? You
reject it, you feel attacked, you feel hurt.** … **You've become an ask-hole: you ask for advice, you just
don't take it, because the feedback doesn't align with you wanting praise. This is a sick relationship.**"*
And the professional analogy: *"we pay consultants thousands, hundreds of thousands, millions of dollars to
go in and do what — **to praise us? Absolutely effing not.** You've wasted your money."*

★ **He also draws the limit on his own argument.** Asked about dreamers whose good ideas get dismissed:
*"**radical ideas are hardly ever welcomed** — we want more of the same, just a little bit better."* So:
*"**be careful who you're seeking opinions from** — people who can give you money, people who've been there
before, people with expertise… **I'm not saying take every piece of feedback as gospel.**"* His example is
**Ring on *Shark Tank***: *"they proceeded just as normal **because they knew who they were. They weren't
seeking external validation** — they were looking for feedback to make it better, or mentorship, or money.
They got none. **So they went back and did what they needed to do.**"* And his own version: *"lots of people
tell me *the Futur's stupid, social media is dumb, why are you giving away content* — I'm like: **smell you
later. I know what I'm doing.**"*

### ★★★ The warning he attaches to all of it

★★★ *"**Don't do this without guidance. Don't do this without doing the inner work first**, because you're
too raw and vulnerable in a way that's **not the right kind of vulnerability** we're talking about — you're
so sensitive to the feedback that **you're not quite ready.** Some people have social anxiety even in
writing a comment — **their hands are literally shaking** as they're trying to write out the comment.
**That person is not ready to do this deep dive.**"* Asked directly whether he underestimates how differently
others would experience this, he answers with a method rather than reassurance: *"**find somebody your soul
connects with**, someone you think is steps ahead of you on that journey of self-acceptance, self-awareness
and self-love — **and pour into them, and drown out the noise.** … It doesn't need to be me."*

★★ **On the crowd**: *"**the world conspires to tear each other apart.** We're so unhappy with our own lives
that **when somebody else finds inner peace, success or joy, we have to create a new narrative that
something is wrong with them** — because it allows us to continue living in the lie we tell ourselves."*
And his own filter: *"I have **active filters** that I put out… so I know **who's on Team Do and who's never
been on Team Do**, who's just hanging around trying to take stuff. **And when they reveal themselves to me,
I thank them for doing that**, because I don't want to waste more of my energy, generosity and friendship on
those people."*

★★★ **The reframe of the wound**, which closes the argument: *"the wound we're revealing to the world is:
**I care about your freaking opinion. My emotional wellbeing rises and crashes on your opinion of me. I seek
your validation to affirm my goodness.** That's the wound. **We paint a giant target on our head and say:
please attack me here** — and when they hit us we're like *why does it hurt so much?* **Because you told
them.**"* Versus: *"once you heal from that enemy within — **that's not a wound, that's a shield. It's
callous. The scab has turned into a scar. How can you hurt me?**"* He cites *"an African proverb: **if there
is no enemy within, the enemy outside can do us no harm.**"*

★★ **And the thing he says actually repels him**: *"**it's not the ugliness that drives me away** — because
I think we're all ugly in a way, and who the hell am I to say my ugliness is better than your ugliness.
**What really kills me is them pretending that no one can see it, and trying to fool me.**"* With the
preceding line: *"first of all, **you're not that good at hiding it, because I can already see it** — and
despite seeing it, **I accept you and you don't need to change. But I'm irritated that you keep trying to
pretend.**"*

★★ ***The Sixth Sense* line**, which connects directly to the *"children in adult suits"* thesis from the
Forward keynote a month earlier: *"Haley Joel looks right at us… *I see dead people.* So I'm looking around
the world and I'm like: **I just see fake people. I see children who are trapped in adult bodies who've
never had the kind of emotional healing that they need.**"* And the Maslow framing: *"the top is
**self-actualisation** — your full potential. But if you're staying at the bottom… **well, who the heck are
you?**"* Preceded by: *"**from the minute you were born you began to die.** … At what point do you want to
show up as you, **fully realised, as who you are?**"*
