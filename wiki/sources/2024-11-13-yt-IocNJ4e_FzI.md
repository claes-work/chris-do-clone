# Why Your Social Media Strategy Is FAILING (w/ Brendan Kane)

> **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary.** The guest is **Brendan Kane**, author of *One Million Followers* and
> *The Guide to Going Viral*, running a strategy firm built on format analysis. **His model, his case
> studies and his numbers are his** and are fenced below. Chris's own material — which includes a
> **values-over-performance decision** worth promoting and several **dated internal Futur benchmarks** —

## Summary — Chris's material

★★★ **The standout: he identifies exactly why his content underperforms on a platform, and then declines
to fix it.** He raises a genuine puzzle — *"people who **reshare our exact content get more views than I
do**, which is really weird. Or sometimes they apply some crazy-ass art filters on it and those get a lot
of views."* Kane offers explanations. And then Chris supplies the real one himself:

> ★★★ *"**I know what it is that they did, by the way — but I couldn't do it. That's the problem.** The
> video has a description of me like *coach owns somebody*, or *sales expert owns fool or bad client*.
> **I could never do that, because I don't want to describe myself as ever owning somebody.** And so that
> was highly charged emotionally… **we just called it something much simpler.**"*

That is a **deliberate, named trade of performance for values** — not a failure of knowledge. Kane's reply
concedes the point and reframes it as a lesson about visual hierarchy rather than about the word *owns*.
**This is prime `persona/beliefs.md` material**, and it belongs beside his standing objections to
manipulation marketing and clickbait.

★★ **His own read on his best-performing format** — the answer to *"I think I found my jam"*: *"I feel that
**our best content is almost always with me live, with some level of studio audience** — it happens because
**they bring a little bit more spice out of me**, and I like that, and I'm more alive, I feel I have to be
on point. Number two: **if I have a piece of paper and a marker with the live audience**, those videos tend
to do really well — **not millions of views, but hundreds of thousands.**"*

★★ **On thumbnails, where he claims a trained instinct**: *"you can literally **A/B test thumbnails built
into the YouTube platform**… and over time, if you're paying attention, **there is a certain predictability
to which ones will do well. I can almost, without running the test, tell you this one will do better than
the others** — every once in a while I'm surprised."* And the design doctrine attached: ★★ *"**your frame
shouldn't be a replication or repeat of what you say in the title** — it's like **a little mini story**.
There's a little tension and storytelling happening within that."* His example is a MrBeast thumbnail:
*"a giant blender and a Lamborghini in it — **that's not what's happening, but it tells you about what's
going to happen.**"*

★ **He does most of the summarising**, and the teach-backs are compact enough to quote: *"**formats trump

## ⚠️ FENCED — Kane's model, attributed to him

- ★★ **The algorithm is not your enemy**: platforms *"make money by serving ads"*, so the algorithm exists
  *"solely to find the content that can grab and hold attention as long as possible at scale."* Therefore
  *"**they want to partner with us. It's not about trying to beat the algorithm** — this isn't like Netflix,
  which invests billions in original content; **they rely on us as content creators to be the fuel.**"*
- ★★ **"It's not the content, it's the context."** His term for the variables is **performance drivers**
  (upward and downward).
- ★★ **The generalist principle**: *"how do we make your content interesting to the average person **while
  not losing the subtext of our core audience**?"* Examples: a finance YouTuber's *"how I bought a Tesla for
  $78 a month"* (*"if he'd said *the top five principles I've learned in leasing a Tesla* it would have got
  10,000 views instead of 9 million"*); a clinical psychologist's **visual metaphor** format (an overflowing
  wastepaper basket for emotional overwhelm — 12M views); a luxury realtor touring a *"$250 million ranch."*
- ★★ **The arithmetic he uses to sell it**: 10,000 views × 50% core audience = 5,000; 1,000,000 views × 10%
  core = 100,000. *"**If your audience is big enough, the percentage of those people who are going to be
  your customer will be bigger than 100% of your niche audience.**"*
- ★★ **Format, not trend.** A trend *"lasts a few weeks — the ice bucket challenge"*, and *"**99% of people
  who do that don't break through; we only see the people at the top.**"* A format is structural: *"every
  movie the past 50 years follows the three-act structure — **Spielberg isn't reinventing it.**"* His
  library: **~220 catalogued formats, adding 3–5 a week**, from *"about 10,000 hours of research."*
  *"**Mastery of a single format** — the minute you master a single format **you can print money.**"*
  Named formats: *is it worth it*, *two characters one light bulb*, *man on the street*, *visual metaphor*,
  *walking listicles*.
- ★★ **Gold / silver / bronze** — his analysis method: take one creator using one format, pull **10 top
  performers**, **10–20 average**, and the **underperformers**, then run **qualitative** (not quantitative)
  comparison. *"Most people spend their time on quantitative analysis… **it doesn't uncover the why.**"*
  The consumer version he gives Chris: **play the high performer and your own side by side on one screen**
  — *"if you're really honest with yourself, you will see the differences."*
- ★★ **Start with the hook, not the question.** From 220 interviews produced for a broadcast journalist
  moving to digital: *"**we're no longer going to craft questions — we're going to start with what is the
  hook.** I want to look at the outcome I'm looking for and then reverse back."* Ideation is regimented: a
  spreadsheet of **~50 hooks**, ranked 1–5, narrowed to 5–10.
- ★ **On a well-known podcast's process**: dissecting *"like 500 interviews"* per guest for emotional
  triggers; A/B testing *"a hundred variations of thumbnails and headlines"* on the Facebook ad platform,
  sometimes against a coming-soon page.
- ★★ **Social media is one-to-one, not one-to-many** — the idea he credits to working with a musician around
  **2010–11**: *"most people are communicating on social media as if you're standing on a stage speaking to
  hundreds of thousands… **but that really [hurts] performance, because you're not creating that personal
  connection.**"*
- ★ **The case study**: a leather craftsman stuck at **2,000 followers** making *"commercials"*, redesigned
  around an **"is it worth it"** format (buy a luxury handbag, deconstruct it on camera, rule on the price)
  → **2.5M followers, 200M+ organic views**, site traffic from **10K to 100K** visitors, with **no calls to
  action** — *"he just has a link in his bio."* Kane's framing: *"**the goal of social media is not to sell
  your product, it's to get people to know, like and trust you.**"*
- ★ **Business terms, dated**: engagements *"start at 10,000 and can go all the way up to 200,000"*, with
  some clients *"paying us six figures a month"*; a **120-day / one-million-view guarantee** (*"we'll keep
  working with you until you do"*); milestone awards issued at 1M through 1–2B views, *"probably given to
  over 100 clients."*
- ★ **Personal vs. company accounts**: *"more times than not **a personal brand will drive the business**…
  the minute you have a brand account, **the mindset comes in: I'm creating a commercial and my account is a
  website.**"* Counterexamples he allows: a language-learning app's TikTok, and one very large personal
  brand that *"doesn't spend any money on advertising."*
- ★ **The stadium reframe** (he flags it as borrowed, source unknown): an *"underperforming"* 60,000-view
  video is **a full stadium of people**. Chris's response is to check the arithmetic — *"60,000 in a stadium
  would be a really big stadium"* — and then agree: *"I don't take it for granted at all."*
