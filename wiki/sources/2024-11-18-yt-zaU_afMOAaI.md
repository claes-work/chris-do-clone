### ★★ Other Chris material

- ★★ **Generosity before the ask, restated**: *"I tell people **your content sucks**… you're pretty much
  following the advertising model and **you're pitching to me nonstop.** Maybe the first thing you need to
  do is **show up for the people, do something that feels like an act of generosity — and then eventually
  you can ask them for the sale.**"*
- ★★ **Against platitudes**: *"if you're saying everything that everyone's saying, **what's the point of
  listening to you?** If you start opening up with platitudes — these empty statements, these truisms that
  everybody knows are true and good — **why would anyone care?** So you kind of have to **zig or zag when
  everyone else is going one direction**, and have a perspective, a point of view."*
- ★★ **Against repurposing other people's thinking**: *"if you're sharing other people's content and other
  people's ideas, **it begs the question: what are your ideas? Why should I follow you? Why should I give up
  some of my time and my social feed** for things you're just going to repurpose and regurgitate?"* With the
  concession that it is a general condition, not a LinkedIn one: *"**original thought is actually hard to
  come by, and people like shortcuts.**"*
- ★★ **On AI-generated engagement, dated and specific**: *"there are a lot of **cookie-cutter AI chat
  responses** to posts, and it annoys me a little bit. **I'm appreciative of the engagement, but at the same
  time it's fake engagement**, and I'm trying to find the real people so I can answer them. And **sometimes
  I'm fooled for a beat** — I'm like: *is that really a person?* No, it's not."* His hope: *"the algorithm
  gods… **can shift the dynamic to counterbalance the amount of content that's proliferating by faceless,
  thoughtless machines.**"* He cites Instagram weighting originators over resharers as the model — *"without
  those people there's nothing for us to share."*
- ★ **On cold DMs, from the receiving end**: *"I'm jaded… when people say *I promise you I'm not going to
  spam you or sell you anything*, **I actually know they are going to sell me something eventually.** And I
  can tell by the way they ask questions — *Chris, what are you working on right now?* — **I know where this
  is going. You're prospecting me right now.**"*
- ★ **Company pages, his prior position** (which the guest argues against): *"you're right about reach and
  engagement on company pages — **I tell people it's dead, don't even bother.**"* He updates it only
  partially, accepting her *"weirdly you boost each other"* argument rather than the reach claim.
- ★★ **Dated team and personal facts**: *"we don't have that many people working for us… **many of my staff
  don't like being on social media** — it's not a regular habit for them, especially LinkedIn, so **we have
  fairly low engagement for my own team.**"* He puts the headcount at **eight employees**. And, in the memes
  discussion: ★ *"I like to share **dad jokes**, which makes my children cringe… my community is like: yeah,
  **because I'm a dad, I'm 52** — this is kind of what makes me laugh, and it's so bad it's good."*
  (**Age 52 as of October 2024**, consistent with the corpus.)
- ★ **Voice — the closing riff.** He tells her she has a second career in voiceover: *"even when you said
  some like *here, shots fired*, I'm like — **it's still pretty soothing. It's like a bullet coated in
  Teflon: it's going to just go right through the body and it's all good.**"*

## ⚠️ FENCED — the guest's material, attributed to her

- ★★ **Her central contrarian claim**: *"there is **this undercurrent of don't be salesy on LinkedIn**… we
  got so focused on being of service that **we forget to talk about what we do, the problems we solve, and
  how our business literally exists to solve problems for other people.** … **We've just made sales a dirty
  word.**"*
- ★ **Why B2B is late**: the pre-internet culture of *"the little black book… **keeping secrets** — you
  didn't want your competitors to know who you were working with"* never updated, *"and now we're asking
  them to throw that all out of the window."*
- ★★ **The search bar over the feed** — the tactic Chris singles out as new to the show: *"most people don't
  use the search bar as part of their LinkedIn strategy. **It is the most underrated feature**… because
  they're too busy scrolling, and **when people are scrolling, LinkedIn's job is to keep you distracted.**
  Your job is to put the blinkers on."* Filter by geography, industry, company size; connect strategically
  and the search algorithm starts suggesting more of the same. Her verdict on the paid tool: *"I'm not a fan
  of **Sales Navigator** for most people — **it's like giving a Ferrari to a learner driver.**"*
- ★ **Find the micro-influencer first**: *"find a much smaller creator — **the mini Chris Do of your ideal
  client in that industry** — and go and see who's hanging out with them, and hang out there."*
- ★ **Ten accounts, not a thousand**: *"I've always scratched my head about these people trying to connect
  with a thousand people, **burning 999 relationships to try and get one meeting.**"* Her alternative:
  a list of ten, one question per accepted connection, *"and **don't be the person at the party who spends
  their whole time talking about themselves.**"* Her own openers: *"what's your favourite part about your
  job?"*, *"what do you like least?"*, *"what's the hardest part of LinkedIn for you?"* — *"**if there's no
  problem, there's no sale.**"* And the timescale: *"most selling I've done in my career took **18 months**,
  sometimes up to five years."*
- ★★ **"Page advocacy"** — her signature idea, and the inversion of employee advocacy: use the **company
  page** to amplify the people, not the reverse. The company page comments on and reposts the individual's
  posts; *"**the company page is Michelle J Raymond's biggest fan.**"* Rationale: a Google search for the
  business surfaces the LinkedIn page early (*"we can piggyback off their reputation"*), and *"**is it going
  to be a ghost town** — which is a poor reflection — **or is it going to roll out the red carpet?**"*
- ★★ **The cautionary tale behind it, told against herself**: *"once upon a time **I stole 5,000 followers
  from the company that I worked for.**"* She built a niche community on her personal account while employed;
  the employer had no page activity and no other creators; *"**the whole community went with me into my next
  job… and they got left with nothing.**"* Two lessons, one per side: individuals should build a personal
  brand because *"you're not going to have your job forever"*; businesses that neglect their page discover
  *"**hey, all our leads dried up — what happened?**"*
- ★ **Onboarding reluctant employees**, which she frames as an empathy problem: *"nine out of ten people
  never want to show that they're available… **LinkedIn is intimidating. It's a platform that scares
  people**"* — CEOs included. Her ladder: like a teammate's post → repost → comment → eventually one post a
  week. *"**Repost from an algorithm perspective is not great — but it's that step and that action that's a
  giant leap for some people.**"* And the fear she says is actually operating: *"if I stuff this up, am I
  going to lose my job? That might mean in the US I'm going to lose my healthcare benefits, my house, my
  car… **it really is tied to their income, which is super scary.**"* Page advocacy is her workaround —
  *"they're happy **as long as it doesn't have their name on it.**"*
- ★ **The RATE framework** for company-page posts: **R**elevant (*"can we stop just posting content because
  we want to say something"*), **A**uthentic (*"if I could stamp out one thing… **it would be stock
  images**"*), **T**hought leadership, **E**mpathy.
- ★ **Thought leadership, defined** via a friend's line: *"**have thoughts, be a leader.**"* Plus:
  *"sometimes going first… sometimes going contrarian — **the herd's going this way and you're prepared to
  die on that hill and stand by yourself over here.**"*
- ★★ **The energy test**, her closing argument: *"what's the energy that you're sending out? **If it is a
  tick-and-flick — we've done our quota for this week — the person on the other side feels that**… it feels
  like you've just been slimed on."* Versus one good post made *"in service of the person on the other
  side."* Then the tough-love version: ★ *"**if you wouldn't stop for it, stop complaining about your
  impressions and your reach.** You can bang on about your corporate guidelines, and then **you don't get to
  whinge** about how things aren't working out on a social platform."* And the AI caricature that opens the
  episode: *"*hey ChatGPT, could you please write me five LinkedIn posts with lots of rocket emojis and
  overused words* — **that's why content doesn't work.**"*
- ★ **The 2025 prediction** (recorded Oct 2024): the dark path is *"mountains and mountains more of carbon
  copy clones, unoriginal, templated content"*; the hoped-for path is that LinkedIn's stated preference for
  **knowledge and advice for one specific audience** rewards people who narrow. *"I hope that **community**
  becomes a buzzword next year… **community is the thing that outplays any algorithm.**"*
- ★ **Niching felt wrong even to her**: *"the biggest thing I struggled with… **being one thing to such a
  specific audience felt like I could never work with anyone else ever again.**"* Her resolution was two
  brands — one for what she's known for, one for everything she does. *"It's like a giant game of **Where's
  Wally** on LinkedIn — **too many people are the crowd.**"*
