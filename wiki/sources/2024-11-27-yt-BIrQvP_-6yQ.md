## What Essam said — HIS material, recorded so it can be recognised, not used

**"The realignment process."** A recurring 45–60 minute call with existing clients, positioned monthly or
quarterly, ideally built into the relationship at the start. Stated purpose: *"more predictability and
consistency in their revenue and increase their profits **without having to sign a single new client.**"*
He gives away a ClickUp template with **no email gate** (*"I'm just giving this to you for free"*).

**The four-part call structure:**

1. **Preframe** — state the intention: delivery to standard, expectations met, *"and this is the important
   part — **an opportunity for us to discover any new requirements or specific challenges and realign on
   our goals.**"* His stated source: *"I learned this early on in some sales training I went through, and
   I've been **mentored by some very successful entrepreneurs, some of whom have appeared on this
   channel.**"* ⚠️ *He does not name them; do not infer.*
2. **Goals and objectives** — start with the project (*"if this project was a complete success, how would
   you know? What are some of the things you would see or hear?"*), then widen: *"**how does this project
   relate to the wider goals of the business or the department?**"* Against the objection *"I'm not a
   business consultant"*: *"you're not talking about the goals to solve every goal — **you're understanding
   how your skill set fits into the bigger picture.**"* Closes with the playback technique above.
3. **Satisfaction** — *"on a scale of 1 to 10, how satisfied were you… do you feel like those projects
   helped you move closer to these goals?"* His case study: a client asked the satisfaction question about a
   **new supplier** the buyer had switched to, *"and it revealed a lot of holes… the client admitted they
   were trying to do things more cost-effectively but it had backfired"* — and the work came back.
4. **Opportunity** — *"challenges"* deliberately reframed as *"opportunities"* because *"it feels a little
   bit weird… asking them about their challenges."* Questions: *"is there anything you'd like to change or
   improve moving forwards?"*, *"**if you had a magic wand**, is there anything you'd change about the way we
   work together?"*, *"is there anything that's **less than perfect** right now?"* Then the close:
   *"**would it be okay if I came back with some suggestions?**"*

**His claims about results**: *"retainers that were sometimes **double or even triple** what that client
was already paying"*, price increases, and referrals.

**His definition of a retainer** — the one genuinely distinct idea here: *"my clients often tell me they
just want more retainers… **retainers are simply someone solving a problem consistently. That's all it
is.**"* Worked example: a branding studio with no recurring revenue found that *"how that brand guideline
was being implemented"* was the unsolved problem, and built a monthly review-and-consultancy package plus a
print-supply relationship out of it.

**His closing instruction**, which is also the giveaway that this is a different practitioner's voice:
*"change them slightly so that they're in your language, **so you don't sound like me.**"*
