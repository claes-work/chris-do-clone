## ★★★ Chris's material

### The pivot away from client work was the guest's idea

★★★ **A named employee is credited with one of the company's defining strategic turns**, with the
arithmetic attached: *"you've been pretty instrumental in shaping a couple of big seismic changes within my
own thinking… I remember one right now, because you're like: **Chris, what if we just focus on doing just
the Futur? No more Blind service work.** And it took me a beat. I'm like: **okay, let's try.**"*

★★★ **The maths he ran, stated plainly:** *"when we book a **$600,000 job**, you're not keeping $600,000 —
**you might keep $150[K]** — but then **the rest of the year you're just burning that 150 down to nothing**,
because we're not doing another project. **You're just keeping the machine going.** At a point you do get
caught up in just **the cycle of sustaining yourself.**"* Against that: *"if we launched an educational
product — because it isn't super expensive for us to produce — **we could make 100,000 or 200,000, but
almost all of it would be profit.** And the good news is **it's IP.** It hopefully is **a perennial
seller**, and it can generate long-term revenue — **you're building a library of stuff.**"* His verdict on
the effect: *"that **surprisingly just messed my brain up in the very best ways.**"*

> ★★★ **This is the clearest account in the corpus of *why* the agency became a media company**, and it
> credits the reframing to an employee rather than to himself. It belongs on [[../entities/the-futur]] and
> [[../entities/blind]] both.

### ★★★ "When you open the gate, the chickens run"

On giving the team creative freedom and what it cost him: *"when we worked for someone — agencies —
**there's always a master, there's always a client.** And then we started doing content for ourselves, which
was radically different. I remember **slowly inviting each and every one of you into the circle to taste the
freedom.** … ★★★ **What we realised is: when you open the gate, the chickens run. They don't want to go back
in the cage.**"* And the observation that it was unanimous: *"despite investing almost all of your creative
energy up until this point in developing a professional [reel] to do work for clients — **to the man, to the
human, you're like: we don't want to go back.** Even if it means this is weird and undefined and **it's not
as sexy, there aren't big brands attached to it.**"*

★ He turns the consequence into the joke against himself: *"so what you're saying is, **had I kept a tight
rope on you, given strict deadlines and kept being the taskmaster, you might not have tasted the
freedom**… **honey, I screwed up. I messed up the company by giving everybody so much freedom.**"*

### ★★★ On people leaving — and on the ones who never speak to him again

*"Part of running a company is **like being a parent** — and I want the best for the people that come in and
out of our company. **Sometimes the best means they need to be somewhere else**, and often that means I have
to ask them to leave, and they wind up doing great things."*

On the resignation conversation itself: *"anybody ever has that meeting with me — *I need to talk to you* —
**I already know what's coming**… so I try to recognise the moment and say: **hey, whatever you're going to
say, it's going to be okay, and there can be no such thing as bad news.**"* His actual reply at the time:
*"I'm so happy for you. I've been wondering when you're going to have this conversation with me… **my
biggest question was: why so long? Why did it take you so long?**"*

★★★ **And then the part that is genuinely exposed**: *"sometimes people leave, or are asked to leave, and
**I don't ever talk to them again — and it makes me wonder a lot about: was there more in the relationship
than I thought, or was there less?** Because I thought we worked together, and all things come to an end —
but **unless I betrayed you, or was disloyal, or did something unethical, why shouldn't we have a
relationship?** But often times that's not the case at all."* Against which he sets the counter-example:
*"people always wondered *what happened to Matthew* — **I just had a sandwich or taco with him the other
day. You guys relax.**"*

### ★★ The Futur's content operating model, stated as a contrast

Comparing himself to the guest's meticulous approach and to a well-known podcaster's: ★★★ *"there's a lot of
planning, a lot of research, a lot of A/B testing for titles and thumbnails before anything is made, and I
admire the amount of energy and focus and **intentionality** you put behind your things… **We're a volume
kind of operation. I have an idea, we just make it. There's not a lot of planning or thinking — things go
out, we adjust on the fly. That's kind of our style.**"* And why the guest's approach demands the opposite:
*"if a video doesn't drop for a month or every other month, **it's got to work** — otherwise you're going to
wait another three months."*

★★ **A third statement of the same benchmark**: *"for us **a good video would get like 30,000 views**"* —
consistent with the 30–40K *"broken through"* threshold in [[2024-11-13-yt-IocNJ4e_FzI]]. And the
self-assessment attached: ★★ *"**I always believe what we make is kind of boring.** It's educational
content — unless you're running a business and trying to sort out one problem we're talking about, no one
else would be watching it. **So it's always to my surprise that many years later we have a couple million
subs, and I don't even know who's here. Why are you guys here?**"*

### ★★ Wealth, defined

Re-using **Peter Drucker** (*"in business all profit comes from risk — the more risk you take the more
profit you **have** to make; that's a potential, not a guarantee"*), he lands on: ★★★ *"so you bought
yourself a lot of freedom — and **I think that's the real meaning of wealth: not that you have a lot of
money, but the freedom to do what you want.** Now you can say **I can earn as much as I want, but I can do
it on my own timetable.**"*

### ★★ The warning he gives the guest

When the guest floats dropping his manufacturing partners and taking the whole margin: *"there is one
potential pitfall, which is **this beautiful lifestyle business you've been able to build for yourself will
most likely be going away** — because the minute you start taking on inventory, you're putting money into
it, there's fulfilment, there's customer service. **Now it's a business.** And **because I know you as this
ethical person, you can't just ship it and abandon it** if it doesn't feel right anymore. **You're kind of
stuck with it for a while.**"*

## ⚠️ FENCED — the guest's material, attributed to him

- **The trajectory**: Art Center **2006** (Chris's student) → intern → freelance → **Blind creative director
  for ~a decade** → **The Futur from 2016** → left ≈**2021** → YouTube + **industrial product design**
  (desk accessories, keyboards).
- **The channel**: started **early 2019** while still at The Futur. A home-office remodel video hit
  **~100,000 views in a week** and *"probably three months later it might have reached **a million views**"*
  — against a Futur benchmark he found bewildering: *"all of the stuff we were doing at the Futur I felt was
  so high value — **I would pay for this information** — and the viewership was nowhere near what I was
  getting on this one single video."* Follow-ups *"all hit a few million views each"*: *"okay, **this isn't
  just a fluke — there's a formula here.**"*
- **The economics of leaving**: his stated goal was only to match his salary; *"I think that **first year I
  was making four times my salary in revenue**."* Cadence then: ~**one video a month, each with a sponsor**.
  Cadence now: ~**one every three months**, because *"my time is spent behind the scenes working and
  documenting"* — *"it's a lot slower, but **I kind of like it. I don't feel stressed to constantly feed
  this content machine.**"*
- **Production reality**: a 10-minute video takes *"**at least a month, probably three**"*; the product-design
  videos document *"**six months to a year**"* of manufacturing. He manages the waiting by running several
  projects *"in a little bit of a relay race"*, which keeps him from losing steam.
- **Temperament**: *"**I'm a very risk-averse person**… it felt very calculated."* And the reframe he uses:
  *"**fear and excitement feel the same in your body** — how you look at it determines which one you're
  actually feeling."* His metaphor for entering unfamiliar territory: **a dark room with a flashlight** —
  *"you might bump your knee on something, and that might hurt, but **you know that it's there**."*
- **How the product work started**: a desk-accessory company that had sponsored his channel for ~three years
  invited him to co-develop; a **two-year** process produced a note-taking kit. *"They valued my opinion and
  perspective"* despite no industrial-design experience — *"**I was just a sponge in that situation.**"*
- **The deal structure**: he enters **as a designer**, sometimes with narrow scope (materials, colourways),
  sometimes *"carte blanche"*; *"where there's moments of a lot more authorship or **I'm taking a lot more
  risk, that's where I would get more of the pie**"*, typically **a royalty percentage** that continues
  across production runs. He is now weighing going direct — *"**could I just take the whole pie?**"* — and
  looking at **3PL** fulfilment to avoid the parts he doesn't want.
- **On planning**: *"**we have to put that insane amount of planning into it because it's very expensive to
  make a mistake** — but probably more so, **it's professionally very embarrassing.**"*
- **On the surprise**: *"the biggest thing that surprised me… was **all the things I did after were things I
  hadn't done before**. None of these things were planned — **I barely know what I'm doing next week. I
  don't have a five-year plan.** I kind of just listen to my intuition and let my curiosity and passion pull
  me as hard as possible."* His only stated goal on leaving was *"**to become a better storyteller**"* — and
  a no-code platform then asked him to direct a documentary series.
- ★ **The line from the cold open** — *"**hard work and preparation increases the surface area of luck**"* —
  is repeated by Chris later as something *"people tell me"*; ⚠️ **origin unattributed by either speaker.**
