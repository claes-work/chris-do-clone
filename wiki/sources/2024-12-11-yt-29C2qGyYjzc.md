## ★★ Chris's material

★★★ **On AI-generated content — the sharpest line, and it is a moral argument rather than a quality one**:
*"your comment about AI — **if we use AI for everything, then what do we need you for?** … **AI can make
things better than you. But you are not better.** And at some point the name of the game for me is: **it's a
game of continuous personal development and growth.** So **if you don't write, you're not really thinking**,
you're not helping others — you're using robots to do this."* With the market note attached: *"I know
there's a whole industry designed to do content for you… **this would be the polar opposite of that
approach, and I like that.**"*

★★ **On AI comments — a concrete, slightly petty tactic he admits to**: *"here's what I sometimes do —
**I just type in the word *bot* and a question mark. And they never reply.**"* And the pattern-recognition
that triggers it: *"you can tell there's a pattern, a way the machine thinks. And if you see one you're
like: okay, that's cool… then you see the next comment is very similar, **eerily similar** to the previous
one, and then **you see 10 of them come in.**"* This extends the *"fake engagement"* position from
[[2024-11-18-yt-zaU_afMOAaI]] from complaint into behaviour.

★★ **On why the work doesn't feel like work** — said to the guest, about them both: *"when I help people
**I just feel so alive.** So **this is not about work for me** — and I assume it's not about work for you.
**It's about making that deeper impact.**"*

★ **His recurring interviewer move**, in a new phrasing: *"**I'm going to channel our audience here**"* —
then he voices the objection: *"great idea, drive engagement in the comments — **but that also means your
original post has to be quite interesting and full of value.** I've seen people use this strategy… and then
I read their post and I'm like: ★ **this post sucks.**"*

## ⚠️ FENCED — the guest's material, attributed to him

**Platform state, dated December 2024** (useful as a time capsule, and explicitly hedged by him):

- LinkedIn *"has crossed a billion users, and **less than 2% are posting**"* — *"it's the **silent
  community**: a whole lot of consumers, not a whole lot of creators."*
- Video is being pushed hard (*"an entire new video, TikTok-style feed in the app"*), but his read is
  measured: *"**is video taking over, or is video just coming?** I feel like it's the other option."*
  Reasoning: *"**you can't change 20 years of user behaviour with one update**"*, and LinkedIn *"is actually
  21 years old — **the oldest social media platform we're currently using.**"*
- ★ **His counter-intuitive data point**: *"**I still get more reach on my text-only posts** than I do on my
  image posts."* His explanation is oversupply: *"everyone's been told *post a selfie, it's going to give
  you more reach* — **it's not, if the selfie's irrelevant.**"*
- ★ **What he says the algorithm actually scores**: not likes, but **comments and dwell time**. Hence the
  target of *"people staying 30, 40 seconds — sometimes 3, 4 minutes"* under a post.
- ★ **On the reported reach decline**: *"last year there were 50,000 people on your post and each saw it
  **twice** — 100,000 views. This year there's still 50,000 people, **they only see it once.** So **your
  audience really isn't smaller** — it's the way LinkedIn is distributing the content."*
- ★ **Niche now travels on video where it never did in text**: he cites a network-engineering creator posting
  *"super duper granular"* material to seven-figure impressions — *"back in the day on LinkedIn that would
  have never gone viral."*

**His tactics:**

- ★★ **Pinned author comments.** *"**You never end your post with the post.**"* He self-comments (which pins
  by default) with behind-the-scenes material, extra POV, or an inverted take on the post's argument.
  *"**Real value on LinkedIn is determined in the comments underneath the post.**"*
- ★★ **The one-line hook rule.** LinkedIn shows three lines before *see more*; if the first sentence wraps to
  two lines, the third is an empty space and the second sentence never appears. So: **hook as a one-liner →
  blank line → a second line that works in unison with it.** His test: *"**if your readers could only read
  the first three lines, what would you want them to know?**"* And his objection to clickbait here is
  mechanical, not moral: *"your audience has no idea what they're going to read about… **they might not even
  click *see more*.**"*
- ★★ **Sign-posting** — *"quite literally put several sign posts in every single post"*, reminding the reader
  how you know what you know. His diagnosis of the generic educational post: *"as a CEO you're expected to be
  on 24/7, so here are seven science-based tactics… **whose post is that? Anyone in the world could have
  literally published that post.** … **We forgot to tell them how we know this.**"*
- ★★ **Kill the niche in the hook.** For case studies: *"treat the first three lines… **as an educational
  post, not a case-study post**"* — because *"simply by stating the industry in the hook you're going to
  lose people."* Lead with the relatable lesson, then get specific once they're in.
- ★★ **End with an easy question.** *"The psychology will always be: **don't make me think.** If I'm
  investing 20, 30 seconds reading your post, **don't make me invest another 30 or 40 thinking about what
  I'm going to comment.**"* So: yes/no questions, specific metrics, fast answers — *"**above or below your
  goals this month?**"* — which then *"pushes me as the commenter to justify myself."*
- ★★ **The engagement hour.** *"I post at 12:30 and I will **100% of the time stay at least until 1:30**,
  religiously… people can count on me to respond."* Plus *"a 50-minute commenting spree in the morning"* and
  one or two 15-minute sprees later. His advice to employed people: *"put those 30 minutes **in your work
  calendar** — it makes a world of a difference."* And the boundary: *"**if you let LinkedIn bleed into your
  life and into your business it's going to be overwhelming.** Be strict with yourself."*
- ★ **No sales posts, ever.** *"I've **never written a sales post in my life**, never written a case-study
  post, never written a testimonial post — **and I don't need to sell.** How do I prove that? **Because
  we're booked out six months in advance.**"* The mechanism he credits is cumulative sign-posting rather
  than selling: *"the trust gets built and the trust stays."*
- ★ **On his own situation**, offered when Chris asks about burnout: *"**I can retire today.** I literally
  could retire today, but **I choose to work more because I'm aiming for a bigger impact.**"* And on not
  getting tired of it: *"I've literally been in parks and theme parks, I've travelled, I've played, I've
  watched cartoons, I've eaten great food — **how do I get tired of that?**"*
- ★ **Tooling**: he names a LinkedIn writing tool he also mentioned in the first episode, used for reading-time
  estimates.
