# Lessons Every Creator Must Know About Growth w/ Colin & Samir

> **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary, two guests.** **Colin and Samir** run a creator-economy show, have been on
> YouTube ~13 years, and built and sold a lacrosse media company. **Their frameworks, history and numbers
> are theirs** and are fenced below. Chris is mostly in interviewer mode — but three of his own
> contributions are new to the corpus, including **the clearest statement he has made about how he works
> with other people.**
>
> Recorded in **Venice** (Los Angeles), not the usual studio.

## ★★★ Chris's material

### The collaboration self-diagnosis

★★★ **The most useful thing he says all episode**, offered while asking how a 13-year partnership survives:
*"I'm of the mindset — **I'm a good captain, I'm a good soldier. I'm not a good captain-soldier.** I can
lead, I can follow, **but one of us has to lead and one of us has to follow.** It works better for me —
**I'll do everything you say.** But when it comes to — and maybe because of my age — **this co-creation
where everything is shared, let's hold hands throughout the whole process: it doesn't work for my
brain.**"*

★★★ **And the flat warning that precedes it**: *"**that is one thing I always tell people: don't get into a
partnership with anybody**, because it's just so difficult. It really is — because **half the time you're
explaining why you want to do something, versus just doing what it is that you need to do.** … **Every time
I see somebody who's made it work, it's a miracle for me.**"* He invites the counter-evidence honestly:
*"feel free to air any dirty laundry if you wish."*

> ★★ **Worth holding beside** [[2024-10-05-yt-XhxS8c_8-SQ]]-era material on the Jose Caballer partnership
> and **the business prenup** ([[../entities/the-skool]]). The prenup advice and *"don't get into a
> partnership with anybody"* are the same lesson stated at two different temperatures, ~20 years after the
> fact.

### ★★ Naming his own cultural frame — and then asking about someone else's

Contrasting two default responses to success, and flagging his own generalisation as he makes it: *"**too
broad of strokes** — everybody white American: *I'm grateful, everything is great, if it ends tomorrow
[fine]*. **Asian immigrant American: like, oh my God, I'm a disappointment to my family, you got to go.**"*
He then turns it into a genuine question for the Indian-American guest: *"**I just want to know what the
Indian-American experience is.** When you said you're sitting at the dinner table and you're talking about
money, **give us a little glimpse into the kinds of conversations.**"*

> ★★ Characteristic of the interviewing style already documented: he states a crude version of a pattern,
> **labels it crude**, and uses it to open a more specific question rather than to close one.

### ★ Smaller items

- ★ **On being on camera**, agreeing with the guests: *"I can't agree more about the benefits of getting on
  camera and learning how to be on camera — **to be present, to command attention, to be able to articulate
  your ideas without stammering your way through it.**"* And the general principle he draws from their
  early reps: *"**at first you suck** — but if you keep doing it you make small improvements, and eventually
  you get pretty good at it, **or at least comfortable enough doing it.**"*
- ★ **He asks directly about criticism**, a recurring move: *"what do the critics say about you? How do you
  respond? The trolls?"* — which produces the guests' admission that *"industry plants"* and *"a little
  corporate"* land because *"it makes me think we're being inauthentic… **I don't want to be in a format
  where I have to examine myself and go: am I being inauthentic in this context.**"*
- ★ **A live packaging exercise**: he offers one of his own old videos (*"the best burger in Los Angeles"*)
  for retitling, and the guests diagnose it — *"**really no curiosity**"*, and the surrounding videos in the
  feed matter as much as the title itself.

## ⚠️ FENCED — the guests' material, attributed to them

- **The origin**: a **Lacrosse Network** built ~2010–11 and run *"like a TV channel"* (*"I studied the surf
  channel, the Tennis Channel — every day we have a new show"*), initially licensing another creator's
  documentaries on a revenue split. When they ran out of programming a colleague pushed them on camera;
  the first videos were *"shot into a MacBook and uploaded **without editing**."* Sold at **23**, after
  *"three years"* of making no money — *"experiencing really hard times and then an incredible success and
  validation is a pretty powerful thing to experience with someone."*
- ★★ **Their platform insight, dated to that era**: *"**YouTube is not creating content *for* an audience —
  it's creating content *with* an audience.**"* The comparison: if you could tell an ESPN host what to do
  tomorrow and they did it, *"you'd be like: whoa, I have a say in this network."*
- ★★ **The two questions**: *"**who is this for, and why would they watch?** … Those are the two questions
  you have to [answer] before you do anything."* Their own confession is the useful part: years into
  YouTube *"we didn't ask that question — we were just like, *these are cool videos*, and actually **they
  were for us**, not empathetic towards a broader audience."*
- ★★ **Their three tracked rules**: (1) **click-through** on title and thumbnail, then **first 7-second**
  and **first 30-second** retention — *"I need to now respect your time… I need to give you a return on
  that investment"*; (2) **average view duration**; (3) **do you want more stuff like this**.
- ★★ **On titles**: **juxtaposition** as the engine (*"New Yorker tries LA pizza"*, *"I tried public sleep
  pods"* — *"**sleep and public are words that don't typically go together**"*), and specificity over
  abstraction — *"it doesn't really tell me what's going to happen if I invest my time in this."*
- ★★ **Creative vs. Creator**, their distinction: *"**a creative does a creative act and produces creative
  work. A Creator is a distribution platform.** Those are different art forms — **and you can be both.**"*
  Chris's reaction: *"I like that, it's more inclusive."*
- ★★ **On partnership**, which is the direct answer to Chris's warning: shared taste built over years;
  deferring based on *"who cares more about having it the way that they see it"*; a lopsided gratitude
  (*"everything was already the icing on top"*); and *"when I put my one in and my creative partner puts in
  the other one, **for some reason it equals three.**"* Against which they set a borrowed corporate maxim —
  **Reed Hastings**' *"companies are not families, they're sports teams; there's a depth chart"* — and note
  that **between the two of them** they operate like a family anyway.
- ★★ **On fear**, the cold-open line: *"**fear is a really interesting collaborator in the creative
  process**"* — but *"there's a tipping point of the scarcity mindset that pushes you over to a place where
  you're not creating as authentically as you want to be."* And the sharper form: *"**when we found success,
  the fear of losing it I find to be more stifling and paralysing than the fear when you're trying to build
  something for the first time.**"*
- ★ **On what changed public perception of the profession**: a headline nine-figure-adjacent platform deal
  — *"**that's possible? You can get a $60 million deal with Spotify when you make YouTube videos?** That's
  when people started taking it really seriously."*
- ★ **On MrBeast**, asked directly: they know him personally, don't believe the *"this guy's evil"* framing,
  acknowledge *"there's stuff that happened"*, and hope *"he does right the ship."* ⚠️ **Their view, not
  Chris's** — and it sits **against** Chris's own colder take in [[2024-10-31-yt-U1anI0b1nvI]] (*"I don't
  know who Jimmy is… I'm not shocked"*). **Record both; do not merge.**
- ★ **Their one-word answers on what's next**: *"**doing less**"* / *"**education**"* (courses), and
  *"**interest**"* — *"as a creative, **your personal interest is a threat to you at all times.** When you
  find something that works… where our interests go is a potential threat to the company."*
