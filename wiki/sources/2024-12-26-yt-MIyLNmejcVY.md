# How to Hit 5-Figure Success as a Creative Entrepreneur (with Michelangelo)

> **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary coaching interview.** The guest is **Michelangelo** (formerly Michael Angelo
> Gomez; he legally changed his name in **2023**), **26**, owner of a San Antonio video production company,
> ~8–10 years in video. **His numbers and history are his** and are fenced below. Chris is interviewing and
> coaching, and contributes **two strong, quotable principles** plus a notable piece of praise.

## ★★★ Chris's material

★★★ **The line that carries the episode**, offered when the guest describes learning to delegate:
*"the key thing that most solopreneurs don't understand is **the act of delegation and training people.** I
keep telling people: ★★★ **being an entrepreneur is synonymous with being an educator.** **You have to teach
people what you do — because if you can't, it all hinges on you.**"*

★★★ **And the standard he sets from it**: *"if you have sick days — I mean, we don't have to go as dire as
dying — but **if you can't physically work, what happens to the entire company?** And you have to prepare
for that. ★★★ **A healthy business survives without its owner.**"*

★★ **Then he stress-tests it on the spot**, which is what makes it useful rather than aphoristic:
*"**are you at that stage right now?** Tomorrow, if you're hit by a truck — heaven forbid — **is the company
in a place where it can continue to go on without you**, that your wife can somewhat remotely manage? **Are
you there yet?**"* The guest answers honestly (*"no… I want to say like **20%** there"*), and Chris takes
the number rather than softening it: *"okay, so **a ways to go still.**"*

★★★ **On honouring a bad quote** — praise, then the underlying rule, credited. The guest describes
fulfilling an early booking he had come to regret. Chris: *"**you did the honourable thing.** … **You made a
commitment, and despite looking back on it and thinking *this is a terrible commitment*, you're going to go
through with it — because that's what people do: they honour their word.**"* Then the rule, attributed:
*"I remember talking to **Jonathan Stark** about this. He said: **if you quote something and you decide
later that you undercharged them — suck it up. Let that be a painful lesson for you.**"*

★ **On catching someone mid-arc**, and he refuses to predict the ending: *"you're still very early on in
your entrepreneurial journey, and it's awesome for us to be able to **catch you on the train as you're
heading to wherever you're going.** … Sometimes you start off with a bang and you actually go bigger…
**or some unforeseeable thing happens and you have to go back to the drawing board and fix a few things.**"*

★ **A one-line endorsement he gives twice**, when the guest reaches a business conclusion himself:
*"**spoken now like a true entrepreneur.**"*

## Key claims (2024-12-26)

- ★★★ **Entrepreneurship is a form of teaching** — the constraint on growth is whether your skills can be
  transferred, not whether they're good.
- ★★★ **A healthy business survives without its owner**, and the hit-by-a-truck question is the test.
- ★★★ **A bad quote is honoured, not renegotiated** — and the loss is the tuition (credited to Jonathan
  Stark).
- ★ **Trajectories are not predictions** — he explicitly leaves both outcomes open for the guest.

## ⚠️ FENCED — the guest's material, attributed to him

- **The name**: originally **Michael Angelo Gomez**; a hiring manager remembered his application because of
  the middle name, so he used it professionally for years and **legally changed it in 2023** on getting
  married. *"I used to call it a stage name until I was told not to call it that."*
- ★ **The Thumbtack economics**, which are the most concrete numbers in the episode: leads cost **$10–$30**
  (he averages **$20**); he needed **~8 leads to book one job**, so ≈**$400 customer-acquisition cost** per
  gig; typical jobs ran **$1,000–$2,000**; and for years he charged **$35/hour** *"that was me and my
  equipment — which was extremely low."* Competing on the platform required *"being very quick at
  responding… and priced competitively."*
- ★ **The pivot to Google Ads**, scaled to **~$2,000/month**: *"why didn't I consistently just keep adding
  more to it until I found the upper limit? **You have to do what's working and double down on it.**"* His
  complaint about the channel: *"**Google makes it very difficult for you to be a manual Google Ads
  person** — their back end and UI is extremely complicated… **Google wants to push you towards smart
  ads**"*, and *"you lose a lot of money on smart ads."* His reasoning for not mastering it: *"I had to pick
  up a lot of skills in videography, sales, portfolio and websites, client experience — **one thing I don't
  want to pick up skills on is Google Ads.**"*
- ★ **His four-stage arc**, as he frames it: salaried job → **freelance outside the 40–50 hour week** →
  **"evolve" — get serious and seek mentorship** → **go full-time.** His self-criticism at stage three is
  the useful part: *"**I avoided learning sales for the longest time. I avoided project management. I
  avoided client experience. I avoided all the things that weren't videography and photography** — so **you
  have to evolve eventually into a business owner, and the longer you avoid that, the longer you're not
  going to go full-time.**"*
- ★ **On the market**: *"I'm not trying to dog on San Antonio, but **it's not Austin**"*; and more generally
  *"New York, Atlanta, California — **you throw a stone and you'll hit like 30 videographers.**"*
- ★ **On replication**: *"you can't do that forever — **you have to find a way to replicate your skills and
  your processes, and make it official by teaching other people how to do what you do**, and keep that same
  level of consistency and quality."* Progress so far: a full-time editor hired, **a creative producer
  next**, and ~**20%** of the way to owner-independence.
- ★ **His advice to peers**: *"**don't try to reinvent the wheel.** Video has been around for a hundred
  years… why are you trying to figure out how to start a business on your own?"* And on ego: *"a lot of them
  struggle with **putting their own ego aside** — they'll be scared of clients, scared of asking too much
  for a job, or **scared of just asking questions in general.**"*
- ★ **The opening line**, on the salaried era: *"I was working 50 hours a week, **I was like a glorified
  secretary.** I was doing everything but what I wanted to do. **It was like hell on Earth** — but it was
  totally worth it in the end."*
