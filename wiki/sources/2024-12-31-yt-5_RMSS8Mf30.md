## ★★★ The Four Agreements (his own framework)

He introduces it with the borrowing acknowledged: *"I wrote this little document, it's very little, and we
call **The Four Agreements**. **It's a play on the same idea, but it's a different idea.**"*

**1. ★★★ You must do good work.** *"There's a lot of people who look at the concepts we teach, **haven't put
in the energy and effort to do good work — and so they're just living on top of a lie.**"* The standard he
names is Gladwell's: *"**the 10,000 hours of deliberate, intentional practice.**"* His aside on asking a
room about it: *"does anybody not do good work, who's so new into their field, or **pretending** right now?
Then no one will raise their hand — **but I'm sure there's one or two people. There always is.**"*

**2. ★★★ You charge a fair price.** *"People are like: well, what is fair? That's a complicated word. So
what you have to do is **just give more value than what you charge. That's it.** If you charge $100, all it
has to be is **$101.**"*

> ★★★ **And the exchange that follows is the best pricing moment in the batch.** *"What's the maximum that
> you can charge? Is there a maximum?"* — **"It's limitless, everybody. It is only limited by the value that
> you can generate.** … If you do good work, if you're really good at what you do — **what's the theoretic
> limit? I don't know. I haven't found it.**"* Someone objects that the client's valuation is the limit; he
> declines the objection: *"no, no — **I can charge whatever. If they don't value it, that's a whole other
> story.** I'm just saying: **if you pay me a million dollars, I give you a million plus.** The more I can
> give, **the better I can sleep at night.**"*

**3. ★★★ We act in service of others.** *"This one's really tricky… **if we serve others ahead of our need
to make money, we can be honourable in how we run our business.**"* The operative claim underneath it is
sharper than it first sounds: ★★★ *"**very rarely does the client have a problem that you can solve.** If
you accept that, **you'll take away most of the pressure in the buy-sell cycle.**"* And the obligation that
follows: *"when you hear a problem that is not to your benefit, **you must recommend** [elsewhere]… *it's
my recommendation, based on what I've learned from you today, that you pursue this other option.
Unfortunately I don't do that. I can help find you somebody.*"*

**4. ★★★ Always act with integrity.** *"That means **you're willing to do the difficult things.** That's
it."*

## ★★★ "We did that" — the admission

Setting up the critique of advertising-as-content, he indicts The Futur's own beginning rather than an
anonymous other:

> ★★★ *"I say that also because **we did that at the beginning of our content strategy.** So if you're
> there, **circa January 2014** — I'm **a babe in the woods**, I don't know what I'm doing, and I'm posting
> content, and **almost all of it is: we tease the course material such that you would be left wondering
> *oh, I need to learn this, therefore I must buy this from you.*** Can anybody predict what happened then
> with the content, and how the audience felt?"* — the room answers, and he confirms: ★★★ *"**a sales
> funnel. It is a sales funnel. Absolutely right.**"*

His general form of the test, aimed at the room: *"reflect on the content you post on a regular basis. **How
much of that content is selling and positioning you as an expert? How much of it is you promoting something
where you're going to extract more value than what you're giving into the world?** … There's a
disproportionate amount of content that is **thinly disguised as advertising**… **yes, that bothers me a
bit.**"*

★ **On why advertising fails as content**: *"almost no one wants to open up their email to read an ad or be
sold to. Almost no one looks through a magazine or watches TV **to see the ads**."* With the exception noted
precisely: *"there's always a few weirdos — **the ad people**, who show up to watch the ads because **it's
part of the tradecraft.**"*

## ★★★ Jung, the Persona, and why "normal" is the problem

★★★ **The frame**, offered with unusual epistemic humility (see the honesty marker below): *"I came upon
**Carl Jung**… there's this thing called **the Shadow**. And Jung, unlike Freud, has a different relationship
with the ego. **The ego regulates between the Shadow** — the things you have anxiety about, that make you
feel some sense of shame or guilt — **and the Persona**, and **the Persona is who you show up in the world
[as] to be accepted.**"*

★★★ **The consequence**: *"**most of us live in the Persona and we don't even know it.** We dress a certain
way, we speak a certain way, we pursue a professional career **because someone told us that's what you do to
be accepted.**"* And he makes the stakes real rather than dismissing them: *"**to not be accepted by
society, to not feel like you have a tribe, is a very scary thing** — you feel really isolated and alone,
and **as social creatures we need to feel connected.**"*

★★★ **Then the turn, which is the sharpest version of this argument in the corpus**: *"if you look up the
word **normal** in the dictionary, it kind of means **average**. … We're okay saying **I want to be
normal.** I don't think we're okay with saying **I want to be average.** **Anybody here want to be average?**
But that's what we're doing — **we're showing up to blend into being average.**"*

> ★★ **Compare** [[2024-11-24-yt-JUueoUqV9eQ]]: *"if normal is bad, should you do that?"* — same target,
> reached by a moral route there and an **etymological/psychological** route here. **The Jung frame is new
> to the corpus.**

★★★ **And the correction he adds to the standard advice**: *"to be a thought leader, to be a 1%-er
according to **Mark Manson**, **you have to be a contrarian** — you have to go against what everybody
believes."* Then: *"okay — and a contrarian, you could say *well, it's a flat Earth, Chris.* **So we have to
add one more word: you have to be a *correct* contrarian.** Where everyone goes this way, you're going to go
this way — **but you have to be right. And that's the difficult part.**"*

## ★★★ Why people run out of content

★★★ *"How many of you have been struggling to write consistently good posts and stories? Probably everybody
in the room — **because you don't know what defines your character.**"* His prediction of what they do
instead: *"you follow some social influencer [who] says *use this headline, use this hack, 10 content
ideas* — so **you're creating pieces of content that [aren't] tied into the larger version of who you are.**
They're like **standalone episodes that don't tie back to this universe that you're building around you.**"*
The failure mode: *"it might work at the beginning — you get a hit, you get a hit, and you get a dud, and
you're like: **who am I writing for? What's the story I'm trying to tell?**"* His conclusion: *"**in your
world, as the main character in your universe, you have to have a pretty clearly defined character.**"*

> ⚠️ **Synthesis flag.** This is **character-driven content-strategy language** of the kind that pass 14
> deliberately did **not** re-add to the persona (the character/want/obstacle material). This source states
> it independently, in his own words, in a live setting. **Revisit that decision at the next pass with this
> source in hand** — it may change the call, or it may confirm it. Either way, decide explicitly rather than
> by omission.

## ★★★ Money, impact and freedom

The exchange opens the video and recurs: *"what is one thing you want more of right now? … **Money.**"*

- ★★★ *"**Money is not what you really want.** You want to do something that, by doing that thing, **people
  value so much they give you money.** **Money is the result, not the pursuit itself** — because I've seen
  lots of people pursue money and **they're neither happy nor rich.** It's kind of weird, right."*
- ★★★ *"I believe this: **money is a metric that is measuring impact.** So **the more impact you can make in
  life, the more money you will get.** So then the real question is: **how can you create this impact?**"*
- ★★★ *"**What money does is it buys your freedom. That's the whole point of money.** … To be rich [is] that
  **you can afford to spend time with whoever you want, for as long as you want, doing whatever it is you
  want.** … **Sometimes we think we need more money, but what we want is more freedom** — and not [just]
  freedom, but **freedom to do what gives us joy.**"*

> ✅ **Corroborates** [[2024-12-05-yt-t7exym6wXBY]] three weeks earlier: *"the real meaning of wealth: not
> that you have a lot of money, but the freedom to do what you want."* **Two independent statements, same
> position.**

★★ **The diagnosis he attaches to it**, offered without softening: *"if you're **unclear about the impact
you want to make** in the world, there's a good chance **you're not going to make a lot of money, and you're
going to have less freedom.**"* And the trap he acknowledges is real: *"it's hard for me to tell you this,
because you're looking at your primary need, your first order — **I've got to feed myself** — so what you
do is **the very thing that is not going to help you grow your authority. That's the problem.**"* His own
frustration with the loop: *"everywhere I go I'm like — **work on who you are, tell that story** — and then
people go right back to: *here's what I do, I need a client.*"*

★★ **On scammers, defined economically rather than morally**: *"the problem with some of these **scammer
influencer people** is **they take more money than the value they give.**"*

## ★★ Other keepers

- ★★★ **The honesty marker at the top**, which is characteristic and worth having in `voice.md`:
  *"**I'm borrowing concepts that I barely understand, but I package them in a way that I think might help
  you apply it** — so **if there's anybody who knows much more than the surface level of this, I welcome
  them to join in on the conversation.**"*
- ★★ **Teaching as a development stage**: *"**if you can teach what you're doing while you're doing it,
  you've advanced to the next level.** Very few people know what they're doing [and] how to do it — **even
  fewer people can teach others how.** So **teaching is a critical part of your own development.**"*
- ★★ **"Why not me?"** — his stated method for evaluating whether something unprecedented is worth trying:
  *"whenever we ask a question like that, we search our mind for what company has been able to pull that
  off. **If we can find zero companies, then we say probably not.** But if we find a handful — even just one
  — where it's possible, maybe not probable… **I live in the state of mind where I think if it's remotely
  possible for anybody, the first thing is: why not me?** And **I'll be the first. Or if I'm the second,
  that's fine too.**"*
- ★★ **Personal brand outperforms company brand**, with the evidence he uses: *"look up on Twitter how many
  followers **Apple** has versus **Tim Cook**… it's **disproportionate.** Tim Cook has way more — as does
  **Elon Musk versus Tesla**, as does **Richard Branson versus Virgin.**"* The conclusion: *"**people who
  have mastered building their personal brand have created outsized influence that their company benefits
  from, directly and indirectly** — so **if you focus all your energies on marketing and doing more
  advertising, you might be using your money not as smartly as you could.**"*
