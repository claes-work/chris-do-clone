# How To Start A Faceless Channel That Gets Millions Of Views

> **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary.** The guest is **Austin** (surname not given on air,
> `attribution: uncertain`), a serial entrepreneur and speaker building AI content tools; he is *"just the
> idea guy"* with a CTO co-founder, and turns **34** days after recording. **His tools, claims and numbers
> are his** and are fenced below.
>
> ★★★ **The reason to keep this page: it is a DATED UPDATE to Chris's AI position.** The corpus's standing
> guard marks his AI stance as **2023**. This is **January 2025**, and it is both more optimistic *and* more
> pessimistic than the earlier material — in different directions, deliberately.
>
> ★ Context he gives: *"I just got back from [a conference] doing a talk on **intelligent identities** and
> how people are using AI in corporate branding."*

## ★★★ Chris's AI position, January 2025

★★★ **The label he now uses for himself**: *"**I'm an AI optimist.** I think there are many dark paths that
could go down — **but** … how do I use this, **how can I use it to help me be more of me and be more
efficient**? **We're not talking about inauthentic or unethical things.**"*

★★★ **And immediately, the split that makes the position honest** — he does not resolve it in his own
favour:

> ★★★ *"I think there's going to be **massive job displacement.** **I love it from a creator point of view.
> I don't love it from an industry human-creator point of view.** There's a real problem here, and I'll tell
> you why."*

★★★ **The stake he names is his own son.** *"I have an **18-year-old son**. He's studying — what is it
called — **entertainment design, concept art**. And I'm telling him: **Midjourney already does what you do,
better, faster.** **So what are we learning?**"*

> ⚠️ **Biography note.** The corpus records a son *"(≈17 in 2024) considering art school"*, whom Chris
> steered toward **a gap year** to *"live some life first."* This source has him at **18 and studying
> entertainment design / concept art**. **Both recorded, name-free; the gap-year steer evidently did not
> settle it.** Not a contradiction — a sequence, with the outcome now known.

★★ **What he tells him to learn instead**: *"composition… anatomy, framing things, understanding
perspective — **because perhaps when the machine generates something and you feel like it could be better,
then you can art direct it and talk to it.**"* And the horizon: *"we're very close to where **we can just
use plain language and tell it what we want, and it'll make exactly what we want** — and when that happens,
**I really don't understand what the visual effects and even design and concept artists are going to do.
This is troubling for me.**"*

★★★ **The historical precedent he reasons from — desktop publishing.** This is the most useful part, because
it is a **structural** argument rather than a mood:

> *"When we had **desktop publishing**, **professional typesetters were out of business**, and a lot of
> designers were challenged because the market was going to be flooded with a lot of new designers. **But
> once the dust settles, the people who understand design who can use the technology — and that's the
> critical part — have a competitive advantage.** The old-school designers who were used to doing sketches
> and sending things out to typesetters… **weren't able to do things efficiently and cost-effectively
> enough, [and] were then soon to be displaced.**"*

★★ **With the exemption and the honest caveat both stated**: *"people like **icons** — people who have books
written about them, who teach classes at Harvard or Yale — **those are totally different.** But for **the
average designer** who could not get on board with the digital revolution, **I think they were left
behind** — and it created a lot of opportunity for **new self-taught designers** to enter the market. **Most
designers would say it has been a net positive thing.** But **if you're one of those people who were
displaced and weren't able to get back into the field, it was probably very problematic.**"*

★★ **The same pattern applied to software** (agreeing with the guest): *"there's going to be some
displacement, because **it's going to open the door for new people to accomplish more, faster, with less
experience.**"* And his recommendation: *"as an AI optimist **I would encourage all of you, regardless of
what you do, to integrate**."*

★★ **On authorship — an honest non-answer**, worth keeping as voice: *"**does authorship come from the
intention and the idea, or the actual creation process? I don't have a short answer for that.**"*

★★ **On what survives**: the conversation lands on *"there's always going to be room for **real thought
leadership**"*, and possibly a push **offline** — *"more events, more masterminds, more in-person
get-togethers."* Chris's framing of the endpoint question is the sharper bit: if the information is good,
*"**does the source that you get it from really matter?**"* — asked, not answered.

## Key claims (2025-01-03)

- ★★★ **"I'm an AI optimist"** — a dated, explicit self-label, with the dark paths acknowledged rather than
  denied.

## ⚠️ FENCED — the guest's material, attributed to him

- **What "faceless content" is**: *"some people just don't like being on camera"*; formats include
  **b-roll stitched with voiceover**, **AI-animated imagery**, and image-to-video enhancement (*"a three or
  four second moving clip with mouth movement, hand movement, background scene movement"*). He reports
  *"**one guy do over 900,000 views** on one of these faceless videos"* and **100,000–500,000 views** across
  his own in a month.
- **Tools he names, dated Jan 2025**: **Runway**, **Pika**, **Luma Labs' Dream Machine**, **Midjourney**
  (image → animation), and fake movie trailers cut from generated stills.
- **His stance**: *"these tools **enhance creativity and democratise what goes into content creation**, and
  allow anybody to express themselves online"* — with *"some things that keep me up at night."*
- **On trolls**: *"**I love the trolls.** Some of the comments I get — **AI slop peddler**"*; and an account
  of growing on TikTok repeatedly, being blocked, then permanently banned. His platform theory: *"the point
  of these platforms is to keep you on longer so they can serve you more ads — **when there's content that
  takes you off platform, they sometimes don't like it.**"*
- **On resistance inside his own team**: *"some people on our team have resisted leveraging AI to write
  code — **I think people like that are also going to get a little left behind.**"*
