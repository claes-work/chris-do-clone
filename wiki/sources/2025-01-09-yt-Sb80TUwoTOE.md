# How To Create the Life You've Always Wanted (With Jodie Cook)

> **ATTRIBUTION — guest-primary, second appearance.** The guest is **Jodie Cook** — a Forbes contributor,
> children's-book author, former social-media agency owner (sold), now running a coaching software product,
> and a competitive powerlifter. **Her six-step success system and her history are hers** and are fenced
> below. ⏸️ Deferred from batch 143; ingested here.
>
> ★★★ **Kept for Chris's half**, which is unusually personal: **step one of his own "success system"**, the
> **fullest telling of the public-speaking origin**, and **two new biographical items.**

## ★★★ Chris's material

### ★★★ Step one: good things are on the opposite side of discomfort

Asked for his own success system, he answers with characteristic hedging and then gives something precise:
*"**I think step one for my success formula — I have no idea what the other steps are — but step one is…**"*

> ★★★ *"I realised this in my life that **good things are on the opposite side of discomfort.** So whenever
> somebody says something to me that — **I have a physiological reaction to it, like *oh, I don't want to do
> that*** — there's a moment that switches over: **wait a minute, chill out for a second. What is this idea,
> and why are you reacting this way?**"*

★ **And the honest qualifier**: *"**I don't always say yes right away** — but **it starts to eat away at
me.**"*

### ★★★ The public-speaking origin, told with the body in it

The corpus already records that his coach pushed him into speaking. This is the fullest version, and the
physical reaction is the new part:

★★★ *"**I'm a reluctant public speaker.** The moment that was the catalyst for all this is that **my
business coach at that time said: *stop being the world's best kept secret. You have things to offer, and
I'm sure people want to hear from you.*** And as an **introvert, socially awkward kid**, I'm like:
**everything I don't want to do is packaged up in what you just asked me to do.** But he said go do it."*

★★★ *"**When [Kier] says you should do public speaking, I'm like — I wanted to vomit.** But then I let the
words sit in there. And **what he's done in the past is always wanted the best for me — so he wouldn't send
me to a death squad.** He would want me to do something **because he sees something in me that I have yet to
recognise.** **So that discomfort is the thing that I need to get over** — and then there's a lot of other
steps, but **I know that's the beginning to really good things.**"*

★★ **The rule the guest extracts and he accepts**: that this is repeatable — *"every time someone you trust
suggests something"* that produces the recoil, the recoil is the signal.

### ★★★ Biography — two new items

- ★★★ **The raised-hand story, and his brother's challenge.** In a room of 30–40 people asked whether they
  would give their own life [to end an entire population's suffering], *"**I think I was one of two people
  who raised my hand — and my brother looked at me like: *are you trying to get on your high horse to get
  credit with this room?*"*** His answer: *"**I love my life, I think my life is important — but it's not
  enough to love it more than an entire… human suffering.** And **I was curious why everybody's not raising
  their hands. It kind of bothered me.**"* The argument continued afterwards, *"because he was questioning
  my motives — as to if it was true, or it's virtue signalling. **Of course I didn't know that word back
  then.**"* *(Brother unnamed per policy; he does not name him either.)*
- ★ **Afraid of heights** — *"**I'm afraid of heights to a degree — a lot more when I was a kid.** When I
  look over and I look down I get the weird heebie-jeebies… **am I going to fall? No, I'm safe.**"* And what
  he gets from the view anyway: *"**clarity and calmness**, and the sense that **there's a lot of us out
  there — and we're kind of reducing people down to whole groups versus individually.**"*

### ★★★ "Last rep syndrome" — the psychology with physical evidence

> ★★★ *"When I exercise — I have a **home gym** — I'll say to myself: *I'm not feeling so strong today, I
> don't have a lot of energy, I'll just do five.* And when I get to four, **the fifth one is so hard.** But
> sometimes: *man, I'm going to crush today, I'm going to do eight* — and then **you do eight and it's not a
> problem. Same weight.** **The psychology of it really does affect you. You commit to something, and then
> your body responds accordingly.**"*

**Worth promoting because it is a mindset claim with a controlled observation attached** — same load, same
body, different declared intention, different outcome. (The guest names it *"last rep syndrome."*)

### ★★ Other keepers

- ★★ **On children's books — and a lesson for creators.** *"Children's books **aren't for children** —
  they're **adults who are trying to send a message back in time to their younger self**… **Shel
  Silverstein** — *The Missing Piece*, *The Giving Tree* — **so deeply therapeutic.**"* Then the
  application: ★★★ *"**content creators tend to overcomplicate their messages and put too much in** — when
  in fact **if you could just reduce it down to the point where a seven- or nine-year-old could read it and
  learn a life lesson, I think you've done something.**"*
  ✅ **Same doctrine as** *"pretend you're talking to a five-year-old"* in [[2025-01-16-yt-SsuiKyAwDhM]].
- ★★ **Art from a dark place — an opinion he hedges as he gives it.** *"It's a strange thing for me to say,
  but **I think art is better when it comes from a deeply emotional place — mostly a dark place.** So when
  people get successful and happy and their relationships are stable, the art… — **this is probably
  correlation and not causal** — but people are like: *now that they're happy, watch how the songs
  suffer.*"* **Recorded with his own causal caveat intact.**
- ★★ **Reading faces**: *"if you're going to be in business for any period of time, **you either learn it or
  you lose and you're out of business.** … **Luckily for me I don't have too many voices in my head and I'm
  trying my best to be present** — I actually do see them. If you smile a lot you're going to have lines
  here… **so before we even talk, I feel like you must like life, you must like to laugh.**"*
- ★★ **Being the odd one out**: *"**being the odd one out — and getting comfortable with being the odd one
  out — is a really good thing.**"* (Engaging the guest's reframe: *"the alternative is that no one looks,
  because you're unremarkable."*)
- ★ **The status joke, and it lands on the domestic**: *"to me **everyone just talks down to me** the whole
  time — it's like **you're just called Dad**, or my husband at home. But in the world I'm like: *hey, I have
  some information I'd like to share with you* — *we're so excited to see you.* **And when I come home:
  *hey, can you take out the trash?* — yeah, okay, sure.** And then other countries are like: *what, you're
  asking Chris Do to take out the trash? He's far too important for that.*"*

## ⚠️ FENCED — the guest's material, attributed to her

- **Her six-step success system**: research → make a plan from it → **split the plan into daily repeatable
  blocks** (*"30 minutes a day"*; for a book, *"**90 minutes every single day, without fail**"*, tracked on a
  spreadsheet where she **coloured in a cell per day so as not to skip one**) → **visualise** (*"shut my
  eyes, imagine it coming true, feeling it with every part of my body"* — she **wrote herself a pretend
  cheque** for the agency sale, dated and signed) → **persist** (*"absolutely relentless — bang on all the
  doors"*).
- ★ **Her diagnostic use of it**, which Chris seizes on: *"now that I know what my success system might be,
  **where did I deviate from this?**"* — his gloss: *"**that's the beautiful part** — *I used too much egg in
  the batter, or I didn't let it rest long enough* — **go back to where you messed up, fix that part, and
  move forward.**"*
- **How she became a Forbes contributor**: met the editor at a Forbes 30-under-30 event (she was there
  because she'd made the list, for co-writing **children's books giving 6–9 year olds entrepreneurial role
  models**), then *"**followed her up and followed her up and followed her up until she let me be a
  contributor.**"*
- **On validating before building**: use a **waiting list** — *"if enough people join, you know a certain
  proportion will buy; **if the waiting list doesn't take off, don't build the actual thing.**"*
- **On customer service as a transferred strength**: moving from an agency (clients worth thousands) to
  **$99/month** software, she feared *"agency baggage… I'm going to over-service"* — *"but really **that's a
  massive strength. If you can apply agency care to a SaaS product, you're just winning.**"*
- **On preparing for a big podcast**: after studying other guests and thinking *"maybe I need to be like
  that to succeed"*, she concluded *"**just be more you**"* — and prepared by **reading her own website**
  rather than more episodes.
- **Powerlifting**: trains for one-rep maxes in gyms oriented to volume, which makes her conspicuous —
  the origin of the odd-one-out exchange.
