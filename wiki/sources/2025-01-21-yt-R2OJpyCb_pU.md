## ⚠️ FENCED — the guest's material, attributed to him

- ★★ **His visibility argument**, which he **credits onward** (to a friend; name caption-garbled and **not
  asserted**): *"**opportunity goes to people who are the most visible — not necessarily the people who
  quote-unquote deserve it.**"* Illustrated with the promotion everyone recognises: *"**she's not actually
  good at her job, but she's good at making herself visible to execs.**"* Then the bridge to his offer:
  *"**you need to build a personal brand, because visibility is sort of oxygen for opportunity.**"*
- ★ **And he describes the persuasion sequence openly**, which is the interesting part: establish the
  uncomfortable truth → *"that opens the door to them [asking] **what should I do about it?**"* → *"**that's
  when I can open the door to the other thing**"* → *"**once you've got them to buy into that, that opens
  the door to your offer. You just sort of lead them there, logically.**"* He also flags the discomfort
  deliberately: *"it's with a wink, it's still safe — **but there might be a moment or two where you're
  going to feel a little uncomfortable.**"*
- **His career turn**: after ~10 years committed to design, *"**I think I'm a good designer but I'm not
  great — whereas when it comes to marketing I think I'm naturally very good at it**"*, so he switched
  majors three years in. *"I feel like I got two degrees in one."*
- **What he took from P&G**: *"**fundamentally, marketing is applied psychology** — if you understand how
  the human brain works, then you understand how to fit into that."*
- ★ **The attribution problem that pushed him to YouTube** — and it is a genuinely good statement of a
  common frustration: *"you did good work, but… *tell me how you did it* — well, **I didn't really do it, I
  was part of the team**… there were 100 people that worked on this. **I was just executing what the
  creative director gave me.** So can I really say that was mine? **I'm proud of it, but it's tough.**"*
  Hence: *"nobody cared about me or knew who I was — **I felt very invisible**… **130 million views later,
  now that's a thing I can hang my hat on.**"* With the irony he notes himself: *"**I feel like the other
  stuff I've done in my career is actually more interesting and cool**, but the accepted belief among my
  audience is that the YouTube channel [is the achievement]."*
- ★ **On what he was missing when self-taught**: *"I was **not even sniffing the opportunities that I
  wanted**, and I had to admit to myself **it's because I wasn't good enough**… I was missing **the
  fundamentals**. I could do flashy trendy stuff."* On the formal programme: *"**that first year you don't
  touch a computer at all**"* — which he concludes was *"exactly what I needed."* He is careful not to
  generalise: *"**I don't want to knock self-taught people, because there's some amazing ones.**"*
- ★ **A detail he volunteers about Chris**: *"**I did not know that you were Chris from Blind until like a
  year ago.**"*
