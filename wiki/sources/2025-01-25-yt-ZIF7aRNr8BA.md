# Don't Lower Your Price. Do THIS Instead.

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do, live to a workshop room**, taking answers from named participants. Fully
> persona-eligible.
>
> ★★★ **A landmark, and it substantially DEEPENS the Four Agreements** promoted in pass 15 from
> [[2024-12-31-yt-5_RMSS8Mf30]]. That source stated the four. **This one reframes them as a gate**, supplies
> the **ethics defence** they answer, adds a **duress exception** that limits his own model, and names
> **which agreement actually gets broken.**

## ★★★ The ethics defence — what the Four Agreements are actually for

He opens with the accusation rather than the doctrine: *"a lot of you will hear the concepts we share so
that you can charge more… and some of you come back to me in the comments and say: **is that ethical to do?
How come you can just keep telling people to raise your rates, raise your rates, charge double — and still
be okay with that?**"* His summary of how it reads: *"**they think I'm like Satan incarnate.**"*

★★★ **His answer has two parts.** First the belief: *"I have this strange belief that **money is an
infinitely renewable resource — like water, like oxygen.** All I have to do is figure out what I can do to
**serve people**, and they will give me that money, and **there's no shortage of it. I live in a state of
abundance.**"*

★★★ **And then the assumption that does the real work** — this is the load-bearing sentence: *"what they
don't understand is **I have an assumption, and the assumption is: you're good at what you do.** And **I
can't go in there and try to fix all those problems** — **that was supposed to be the job of the university
you went to, or the person you [studied under].**"*

> ★★★ **THE GATE — new, and it changes the standing of the framework.** *"I recently ran a sales workshop
> and I said: **before we can do any of the training that I share with you, we must agree to these things —
> and you have to explicitly say *I agree, I commit.* Otherwise everything else I'm going to teach you is
> not good, because you're not a good person and I don't want to teach you anything.**"*
>
> The Four Agreements are therefore **a precondition for being taught**, not a values appendix. Pass 15
> recorded them as a framework; **they are a consent gate.**

## ★★★ Agreement 1 — you must do good work (deepened)

**The bar**: *"have you done your **10,000 hours of intentional practice**? And **if you haven't, go do
that.**"*

★★★ **The failure mode, named precisely**: *"there are people who are really good at picking up tidbits from
videos — **they read a book or two and they're really good at regurgitating the information they've
learned**… you can walk around and talk about something **on a superficial, surface layer and sound really
smart to someone who doesn't know** — and then **you sell that thing.**"*

★★ **The consequence ladder he walks the room down**: no repeat work or referrals → *"you feel stressed
out"* → *"you will embarrass yourself"* → *"the clients will feel really bad about giving you money"* → they
tell other people → they want their money back → *"**they sue you** — for damages, for waste of time, **for
fraud**"* → and *"they can go onto websites and **post negative reviews in ways that will ruin your
reputation.**"*

★★ **Why reputation is the asset**: *"we only have one — and **good news travels fast, bad news travels
faster.**"* Plus a credited maxim: *"this is what **Professor Errol Gar[r]on** would say — **when you do a
good job they tell one person; when you do a bad job they tell 10 people.**"* ⚠️ *Name caption-garbled; not
asserted.*

★★★ **And the argument that isn't about you**: *"**it doesn't just hurt you — it hurts everyone who calls
themselves the same thing that you call yourself.**"*

★★★ **The live poll, and what he does with the answer.** He asks how many have actually met the bar: *"a
show of hands…"* — *"okay, this is good, and I think that's a pretty fair assessment of this room. **I count
less than 30% of the room. This is wonderful — I love that we can have an honest conversation with each
other.**"* ★ Followed by: *"**don't compare your beginning with someone else's middle or ending** — but
that's what we do."*

## ★★★ Agreement 2 — always deliver more value than price (deepened)

*"No matter what somebody pays you, **you will always deliver more value than what you're paid.**"* The
principle, source forgotten and said so: *"I forget who said this, but — **when value exceeds price, they
buy.**"* His proof is the room itself: *"all of you here are evidence — especially if you had to pay for
this — **that the perceived value you got from coming here exceeded the price. Otherwise you would not
buy.**"*

> ★★★ **THE DURESS EXCEPTION — a real limit on his own model, stated by him.** *"This is true with any
> purchase you make **unless it was done under duress.** For example, if your car broke down on the side of
> the road in the desert, middle of the night, **the tow truck driver can charge you whatever they want.**
> You probably will **not** feel the value exceeds the price — **even though you literally will, because
> you're paying** — but **you might feel cheated about it, because you're under stress or duress.**"*
>
> **Worth promoting precisely because it's a caveat against his own framework**: value-exceeds-price
> explains ordinary purchases, not coerced ones.

★★★ **And the consequence**: *"as long as we deliver more value than price, **what is the limit on what we
can charge?** **You can put in whatever number you want. There's zero judgment — because it's a deal you
make with yourself.**"* (The same *limitless ceiling* as 12-31, now framed as a self-agreement.)

★★★ **"You are in business to sell money."** *"There's an expression: **you are in business to sell money.
When you sell money, people buy non-stop.** So what we have to do is **figure out what money is for the
buyer** — and **each buyer has a different definition of money** and value."*

★★★ **How to price a logo — ask, don't explain.** *"**I don't do any explaining. I'm just asking them
questions: what's it worth to you?**"* The worked example: a blank t-shirt is worth ~$5; with a good logo
it's worth more — *"how many shirts can you sell? **Two million.** At how much profit? **A dollar.** So
you're going to increase your profit by **$2 million.** **Yes — that's how much it's worth to them.**"*
Then: ★★★ *"**when the client tells you what it's worth to them, now you know some percentage of that price
will feel really fair.**"*

## ★★★ Agreement 3 — serve others ahead of your need to make money (and it's the one people break)

★★★ **The image he uses**, which is the strongest in the talk: *"in the military they form a very tight
bond — **they will literally die for each other.** So **when a grenade rolls into a tent, they're all
fighting to see who can jump on it to save their nine friends** — that they have **no blood relationship
to.** **That's what it means to serve others.**"*

★★★ **The link to integrity**: *"**if you serve others ahead of your need to make money, you will then be
selling with integrity** — because sometimes serving others means: *I don't think I'm a good fit for you.
I think there are people who do this a lot better than I do.* … **I need to refer them, even though it's
going to hurt me financially.**"*

★ **He asks the room whether they actually do it, and takes the honest answer**: *"do you always serve your
clients ahead of your need to make money?"* — *"**Not always. That's the truth.**"*

★★★ **THE DIAGNOSIS — rule 3 is the one that gets broken.** *"We can say this logically and agree to it in
our mind, but **more often than not, the rule we're breaking is rule number three.**"* His demonstration is
the hammer: *"**if the only tool you have is a hammer, every problem looks like a nail**… you approach
everything with one attack. **You basically have one solution for all problems.**"* Drawn on the board: many
different problems, one hammer — *"you know what you need? **This.** You know what you need? **This.** …
**Is that how you serve others? Clearly not.**"*

★★ **The concrete version**, using two participants who both sell ads: *"what if I don't need ads? **What
are you supposed to do then?**"* — and the correct answer, given by the participant: *"**what you need is
not what I provide, but I know someone who can meet that need.**"* Chris's script: *"**I think you need a
really awesome logo identity based on our conversation, or really high-end photography. What I do is help
you run ads. I don't think we're ready to work with each other.**"*

★★★ **And what people actually do instead**: *"**we actively do the opposite. We try to convince them that
they don't need that — they need this.** So you're already screwed. **We've broken the Four Agreements.**"*

## ★★ Agreement 4 — integrity

*"The last one's not that tricky: **you always act with integrity — and acting with integrity means you're
willing to do things even though they're difficult and painful.**"*

★ **How he tests all four in practice**: *"if we can do that, we have no problem — **we can get into sales
roleplay right now**… and then all of a sudden I'm like: **are you serving me right now, with that question,
with your agenda? You've never asked me what I value. How do you know the price you're charging is fair to
me?**"*

## ★★★ The value conversation — solutions-then-price, inverted

★★ **The default he's replacing**: *"typically we talk about **solutions** — *I'll build your website, I'll
make you a logo, I'll create an ad campaign, targeting, retargeting, an email funnel* — and then **we talk
about price.**"* Credited: *"it's written very clearly in **Blair [Enns]'s book.**"*

★★★ **The sequence he runs instead**, live:
1. **The goal** — *"we want to make more revenue."*
2. **The mechanism** — *"increase **awareness** (a.k.a. impressions) and increase **conversions**"*, plus
   copywriting and the offer.
3. **The measure** — *"how will you measure success? … we need **2× to 10×** on awareness."*
4. **The reality check on conversion** — *"what's the average conversion? … a fraction. **Let's say 1%.
   Maybe 2% if you're really good. 4% — you're a banger.**"*
5. **The arithmetic, in the client's mouth** — *"if you were able to create **two million impressions** at
   **1% conversion**, **what value is that to your company?**"* — *"**$400k.**"* — *"so you're saying if a
   company did all this for you, **you would generate $400,000 of additional new revenue that you didn't
   have previously.** … **Are you sure?**"*
6. ★★★ **The ask** — *"then you say: **what percentage are you willing to invest for that?**"*

★★★ **And the design principle behind it**: *"you should **design the entire conversation around assessing
their needs, their wants, their fears, the dream outcome, the reason why they wanted it in the first
place** — and you should have **a business conversation, a strategic conversation**, such that you **help
them understand what the real problem is and what might actually solve it.**"*

> ⚠️ **A crude formulation, recorded as spoken.** Explaining that value-based pricing is bounded by who you
> talk to: *"value-based pricing means whatever the person is worth. **All I do is change the standard of
> friends or business people that I'm interested in talking to.** … If I'm **slumming it out on Skid Row**,
> the value is less than zero — **I'm going to get stabbed, they're going to take my money.** But if I hang
> around Wall Street, or people with multi-million, multi-billion dollar companies, **the value is very
> high.**" **The Skid Row line is glib about homelessness.** Recorded because it is what he said and because
> the underlying point (your ceiling is set by whose problems you choose to solve) is real — **but it is not
> persona-safe as phrased, and should not be reproduced.**

★ **On who needs this most**: salespeople, *"because **they're seeing a lot of at-bats** — if they can close
at a higher rate for a higher amount, it's worth a lot of money."*
