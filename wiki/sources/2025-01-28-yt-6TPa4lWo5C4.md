## What Essam said — HIS material, recorded to be recognised, not used

**"The partnership playbook."** The reframe: stop thinking **one-to-one** (agency → prospect) and think
**one-to-many** (agency → **partner** → many prospects). *"A partner is somebody who can get you in front of
multiple prospects."* His pitch for it: ~*"30 minutes a day"* for *"a handful of warm leads every single
week"*, versus cold outreach where *"you send thousands and thousands of emails to hopefully get one or two
responses."*

**His three partnership types** — ⚠️ **credited by him to Daniel Priestley**: *"I learned this from **Daniel
Priestley**, who has been on this channel… he talks about this in a slightly different way, but this is my
interpretation."*

1. **Distribution partnership** — someone who *"already has the attention and the trust of our ideal
   clients"* puts you in front of their audience. (Product analogy: Nike shoes sold through Walmart and shoe
   outlets, not only Nike stores.) *"For creative business owners this most commonly works around
   **content**."*
2. **Delivery partnership** — you are part of a larger delivery: a specialist video studio delivering inside
   a bigger agency's campaign. He says he did this himself: *"when I was first running my design studio I
   didn't have enough work directly from clients, so I used to go into other agencies and almost freelance
   for them."* He rates this **the easiest to obtain.**
3. **Brand partnership** — association: Michael Jordan/Nike and the Air Jordans; *"nowadays… they want George
   Clooney to partner with an espresso."*

★ **His ranking of difficulty**: delivery (easiest) → distribution (*"a lot easier than most people think,
because content creators always need content"*) → brand (hardest). **And the ultimate is all three at
once** — which is what he has here.

★ **His critique of referrals**, which is the setup for the whole model: *"referrals are great, but they're
**unpredictable** — and often you're **trapped in this price cage**, because somebody refers you knowing how
much they paid for that particular thing, and then they want to pay the exact same price. **So it's very
difficult to raise your prices.**"*
