# Stop Losing Clients—Avoid These 5 Things

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo, scripted to camera.** *"Welcome to a **super short episode**."* Fully
> persona-eligible. Compact (6KB) and unusually clean — five named failure modes, no guest, no ad read.
>
> **Why it matters despite the length:** the corpus is rich on *winning* clients and thin on *keeping* them.
> This is a dedicated retention list, and three of the five items are new framings.

## Summary — the five ways creatives lose clients

**The framing**: *"it's really hard to get a client in the first place, so **you need to treat them like the
gold that they are** — especially when you're young and you feel like **you're God's gift to whatever it is
that you do**, then you can take it for granted and think: *well, I'm the gift, and they should be so
grateful to work with me.*"*

### 1. ★★★ Not being responsive

*"When they ask you for something and it takes **hours, days, weeks** for you to respond — for whatever
reason — **that should never happen.**"* He immediately bounds it: *"I don't mean to say you're on their
beck and call and there's a hotline to your personal phone — but **you should have systems in place.**"*

★★★ **The holding reply, which is the transferable bit**: *"sometimes when you don't have a really good
answer, **the best response is just to say: *received. We are processing. I will get back to you as soon as
I have a good answer for you.*"***

★★★ **And he uses it as a hiring filter on himself**: *"**responsiveness is something I use to measure
whether or not I'm going to hire somebody at the very early stages. If you're not responsive, you're not
going to get the [work].**"*

### 2. ★★★ Being completely inflexible

*"It is a natural thing for creative people to feel like: **I have the ideas, you hired me, I'm the expert,
so let me do my thing and don't mess it up.**"*

★★★ **The insight is that the attitude leaks even when you say nothing**: *"sometimes **even though you're
not literally doing anything**, [it] can be felt — and people are very sensitive to these kinds of attitude.
**You give them a weird look, you side-eye them, you roll your eyes, you let out [a sigh]** — and they start
to feel that, and **they feel like they're being punished for trying to contribute to the project.**"*

★★★ **The test he applies instead of difficulty** — this is the keeper: *"sometimes when you're working with
really good clients they will ask you to do something that's **really difficult** — and **I don't try to
measure if it's difficult** [and decide from that]. **I always ask myself: is that a better idea? If it's a
better idea, I do it — and I'm grateful for it.**"*

★★ **Why it pays**: *"then you are **inviting them to feel like they're a collaborator** — and **no one wants
to feel like they're just money.** They want to feel like they've had a hand in it, big and small."*

### 3. ★★★ Leaving them wondering what happens next

*"When you onboard a client and **the feelings are high**, you need to make sure you have **a very clear
process** — and **don't leave them asking *what happens next.*** **You should take the initiative:** *thank
you for signing the purchase order, we're going to send you an invoice, and here's what's going to happen
next.*"*

★★★ **Why a process reads as competence**: *"**only people [who] have done things many times have a system
in place** — it makes it feel like you're an experienced professional and **they're in good hands.**"*

★★★ **And the reframe of what you're actually selling**: *"**the whole point of it is: you're selling not
what you do — you're selling peace of mind.** Having a clearly defined structure and process and system in
place — *here's what happens next* — will help you communicate that **in literal terms and figurative
terms.**"*

### 4. ★★★ Expecting the client to have your language — and the false yes

*"Your clients hired you **because you are the professional** — [don't] expect them to have the language to
so carefully articulate the things that you understand in the language that you know. **There's no point to
hire you if they can do that.**"*

**So**: *"make it feel **safe** for them to say, **in non-technical terms, using non-jargon,
non-specific language**, what they want — and for you to then dialogue with them, **ask them deep follow-up
questions**… and then **play back**: *here's what I heard you say, here's the objective that you want, and
this is how we will do it. Do you have any questions?*"*

> ★★★ **THE FALSE YES — the sharpest idea in the episode.** *"Sometimes in you describing what you're going
> to do, **you'll default to language that you understand.** It'll make you feel really smart and feel like
> you're an expert — and **you intimidate the client to the point in which they just *go along with it*
> versus *agreeing to it*, which is a very different thing. You get a false yes from them.**"*
>
> **And the bill arrives later**: *"you do the thing, and then you show them the work, and you're so excited
> about showing the work — and they have a reaction you never want to hear: ***oh, that's not what I was
> expecting when you said you're going to do X.***"*

★ The guard: *"continue to ask them: **are we on the same page? Do you understand?**"*

### 5. ★★ Taking the relationship for granted

*"Usually it's like three, four, five months into the relationship — you've fallen into a rhythm, everybody's
doing what they need to do. **Don't forget to call up your client** to go over progress and just dialogue
with them about their impressions… or future things that might be coming down the pipeline."*

★★ **The cadence he names**: *"maybe in the beginning **a couple of times a week**, then **once every other
week**, then **once a month** — **but probably not less than once a month.**"* And the content: *"give them
valuable information, insights, **analytical data** that's driving some of the things you're doing —
**that reminds them that they're making progress towards their goal.**"*

★★★ **The failure mode this actually guards against, which is about your own team**: *"**sometimes the team
that you put in place will not tell you they're having challenges with a client until it's too late** —
until the client says: *you know what, I don't want to work with you anymore. It started out great, but
your team isn't getting it.* **And now that bridge is broken, and there's no way you can get back
across.**"*

## Key claims (2025-01-30)

- ★★★ **Responsiveness is a qualifier, and he applies it when hiring** — a holding reply beats silence.
- ★★★ **Attitude is transmitted non-verbally**; the eye-roll punishes the client for contributing.
- ★★★ **"Is it a better idea?" replaces "is it difficult?"** as the decision rule on client input.
- ★★★ **You are selling peace of mind**, and a visible process is the evidence.
- ★★★ **The false yes**: jargon buys compliance, not agreement — and the cost lands at presentation.
- ★★ **Check in on a decaying cadence, never less than monthly** — partly because **your own team won't
  escalate in time.**
