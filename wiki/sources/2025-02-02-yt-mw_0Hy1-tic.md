## ★★★ Three origin stories the corpus did not have

### 1. ★★★ How he met Kier McLaren — by asking an awkward question at lunch

*"I'm having lunch with a friend at **Fritto Misto**, an Italian pasta place, and they were talking about
how great things were and how much their business had grown. And I'm sitting there thinking: ★★★ **is it
rude for me to ask who is this person, and can I get their information?** You know — **your friend is
sharing something really great about their lives, and the first thing you think is: how can I benefit from
your greatness?**"*

He asks anyway. *"And this person — his name is **Hugh** — thinks for a minute, then he paused. ★ **I'm
really good at detecting pauses.**"*

> ★★ **This is the origin of a 13-year coaching relationship that the corpus credits with more or less
> everything he knows about business** — and it began with a question he was embarrassed to ask.

### 2. ★★★ The first break — the Adobe After Effects CD-ROM, at ~22

*"**I don't want to tell you there was some kind of grand plan** — in fact I think **if you try to find the
plan, the answer that you seek will be in front of you and you won't see it.**"*

> ★★★ *"The first big breakthrough for me was when an instructor said: **hey, we'd like to include some of
> your work on the After Effects CD-ROM. Adobe just purchased it and they need some student work to
> showcase.** And **I was initially resistant** — because **they give you a dollar to buy the rights to your
> work.** **I was a little offended**, even though I was only like **22** at that time. It just felt strange
> to me. But I said: whatever. So I do that."*

★★ **The irony is the point**: the thing he nearly refused on principle became the first break.

### 3. ★★★ The Venice house — and the junior-high dream it came from

*"I guess it was like **1998, 1999**, just a few years after graduating from school. First of all, ★★★ **I
paid off my student loans within the first six months.** I'm receiving checks — ★★★ **one single check more
than I thought I would make in a year.** Things are going well."*

> ★★★ *"And then my wife and I decide we need to move to the west side. And on our way to meet a real estate
> broker to look at a studio space, **we see a house for sale in Venice Beach — literally one and a half
> blocks away from the ocean.** ★★★ **A dream that I had when I was in junior high**, watching
> [a show where] twenty-somethings **had an agency and they lived on the beach.** I was like: **that's the
> life. That's the life I want.**"*

> ✅ **This completes an item from [[2024-12-22-yt-vCXPvDF1Sfg]]**, where he says only *"the first house I
> ever bought was just down the street from here"* in Venice. **Now dated (~1998–99), motivated (a
> junior-high dream), and preceded by the student-loan payoff.**

## ★★★ The price game

★★★ **The frame, and he flags the word choice deliberately**: *"here's how I look at the **game of finance
and value** — and **I deliberately chose that word *game*, because for me it's just a game.** I just keep
asking myself: ★★★ **how ridiculous would it be for me to just charge this outrageous amount of money —
just a stupid amount? Who would ever say yes to that? Let's try. Let's just try.**"*

★★★ **The Ben Burns story, as the worked example.** *"[[../entities/ben-burns|Ben Burns]] is someone I've
coached — I found him randomly off the internet, on Facebook, and **he was suffering.** When I did the math
with him, **the amount of work he was doing relative to how much he was being paid was less than minimum
wage.**"* Then the escalation: *"**I'm going to charge 18 grand. I'm going to charge 30 grand.** And we just
do this over and over again."*

★★★ **The game he plays with his own team.** *"Eventually I hire Ben — he comes to work for me — and whenever
we get a new lead in… there was a **logo job for an East Coast telecom company.** They're not ginormous, but
they do probably close to **$100 million in revenue**, and they want a new logo. So we sit around — ★★★
**this game I love to play with my people: *what do you think the price should be?*** Ben sitting across
from me, and **they're kind of scared of me — they don't want to say a low price, because it'll be
embarrassing.**"*

★★★ **And the argument that justifies the whole exercise — the comp.** *"Every once in a while **they say
yes** — which is fantastic for me, because **somebody just set the new benchmark.**"* The analogy: *"when a
home sells for a record, **it sets the new standard for the neighbourhood** — a **comparable**. **We already
instinctively understand this concept.**"*

> ★★★ *"**The question for you is: why can't you be the comp that sets the new standard?**"*

## ★★★ "I don't think you want more clients"

> ★★★ *"**I don't think you want more clients. I think you want higher quality clients.** What you want is
> **more opportunities to decide.** When you level up and you become more attractive, **higher-profile
> clients will find you.**"*

★★ **And the diagnosis behind it**: *"in my experience working with a lot of creatives, **the quality of
their work is not the problem.** Normally what's difficult is **most people make it so hard for you to learn
about that** — think about what label you're putting around your work, [and] about who you [are]."*

## ★★ Other keepers

- ★★ **On the Emmy, deflating it**: *"the first one you win and you're like: **okay, nothing changes.** You
  win another one — nothing changes. **So it doesn't feel great.** Winning the Emmy was **just an award that
  my friends or cousins would know about. That's all that really did for me. Didn't change my life at
  all.**"* (Priestley: *"you looked good in the suit."*)
- ★★★ **The shorts experiment, run as an experiment**: someone told him his content would work as shorts, so
  *"I call up my team… **I need you guys to cut some shorts for me and just pump them out for like 30 days —
  let's see what happens.** We release one — **nothing happened. Nothing happened. Nothing happened.** And
  then one day I'm on the YouTube backend, the dashboard, and I look at this video…"* — a clip with **Mo**,
  described as *"a great guy, an Egyptian-American guy, he likes to argue, he's super smart."*
- ★★ **You get what you pursue** — and Priestley teases him for it: *"**you just get more of what you pursue
  in your life** — so **whatever you think about the most is what you're going to get.**"* — *"that is such
  an LA thing to say."* Chris doesn't back off: *"I keep thinking things are always going to work out, I'm
  always going to be paid more — so **I start practising a lot of positive self-talk. And I'm the product of
  that. My own design.**"*
- ★★ **The reciprocity network**, named with self-deprecation: *"they become part of the unofficial — **I
  know it's a dorky name — international travel posse** — where **we just help each other, because we feel
  the desire to build genuine human relationships.** … What they do for me is they help me do stuff and
  **they really look after me** — so what do I do? ★★ **I try to reciprocate 10×.** I'll sit down, I'll
  design a business model with them, I'll refer clients, I'll do the stuff that would cost them **tens of
  thousands of dollars** to get from me — **and I'm staying up at night thinking about them.**"* With the
  hedge he supplies himself: *"**I know that sounds utopian, like la-la kumbaya kind of talk.**"*
- ★★ **On asking**: *"it seems like **every time I've asked for something, things happen** — but **you have
  to be bold enough to ask for it.**"*
