# Why Creators Should Use AI in Their Workflow (Right Now)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do live with a room**, taking answers from named participants and one colleague
> (**Dave**) who contributes a worked AI example. Fully persona-eligible; Dave's contribution is marked.
>
> ★★★ **A landmark for two reasons**: the **remix doctrine with hard numbers attached**, and a **dated,
> two-sided AI workflow position** that is practical rather than philosophical.

## ★★★ The three-year rule

Stated flatly in the cold open: *"**every three years or so, if you haven't changed your business model and
what you do — or evolved it — you're falling behind.** I don't mean that to scare you. **It's just a reality
dose right there.** The future keeps evolving. **If you don't evolve with it** — that's the real truth."*

> ✅ **This is the general form of the pivots the corpus already documents** — Blind → The Futur, courses →
> subscription, and the memberships launch. Promote it **with** them.

## ★★★ The remix doctrine — one video, three uploads, ~74 million views

He walks the room through The Futur's own lifetime YouTube graph (*"the first two years it's kind of…
horrific"*), then the spike:

| Upload | Format | Views (as stated) |
|---|---|---|
| The pricing / Nike-logo argument | **regular long-form** | *"**4 million plus**"* |
| The same video, **cropped** to vertical | **Shorts** | *"**over 40 million**"* |
| The same video again, **full-frame** | **Shorts** | *"**30 million**"* |

★★★ **How the second one happened** — a friend told him to recut existing content for Shorts: *"I released
this video and **for the first time in my life I have a video that goes thermonuclear.** Literally within
days it's getting millions of views. I'm so happy — for once I can say: ***Mom, I made it. Mom, I did
it.***"* He is candid that the first attempt was lazy: *"we just took the video and **we're really lazy, we
just did like that** — **that's not native.**"*

★★★ **And how the third one happened — he copied someone copying him**: *"then we saw **someone else took
the same video and made it full-frame.** So I called up [my team] and said: **how come we don't have a
full-frame one? Do you see this video that they took from our video? Go do the same thing.**"*

> ★★★ **The lesson, and the industry he takes it from**: *"sometimes we feel like **we just need to make new
> content all the time** — when **some of our best content is what we should be making *versions* of**, onto
> different formats: a podcast, a live thing, **interpretive dance**, whatever. **You just keep making
> money.** And you know where this model comes from? **The music industry. You remix it, you sample the best
> hit, and you make more hits.** We love hearing the same thing, watching the same movie over and over —
> **that's why they do sequels and prequels and series and spin-offs.**"*

★ With the joke that makes the point: *"**if I did the Japanese version of it, that would be another popular
video of the same video.**"* And the honest aside about scale: *"**some angry people in there**, because
you're reaching an audience you hadn't reached before."*

## ★★★ Social capital *is* capital — a position he argues against the room

*"Have you heard this before — ***followers don't equal dollars.* Your followers ain't worth anything.** How
many people believe followers is a vanity metric to fulfil your own ego? I've heard [that] a lot."*

> ★★★ *"**I don't believe it.**"*

★★ **His rebuttal**: *"when they tell you *followers don't equal dollars*, **it's because they haven't made
any money — their followers haven't been big enough for it to make a difference.**"*

★★★ **The reframe**: *"**having a large following does equal money** — because **what brands want is to work
with people who have an engaged audience and show up authentically as themselves.** ★★★ **You are the new
platform for people to give money to.**"*

★★★ **And the disclosure, with a condition attached**: *"I've done brand deals where **I get to do a very
little amount of work for over six figures** — just for showing up and saying *hey, I love this product, I
think you will too.* **That's literally it.** ★★★ **My condition is: I must love the product before I
started talking to you.** That's it — **otherwise I cannot authentically say I love this product.**"*

★ **On AdSense**: *"we release [full workshops] on YouTube for free — now we make a little bit of money
because YouTube gives us AdSense, which is **really truly passive income**, because **I don't have to do
anything but click one button that says *monetize*.**"*

## ★★★ The AI position — combine tools, don't pick one

He first has the room exhaust the obvious uses (copywriting, image generation, generative fill, audio
enhance, text-to-speech, brainstorming, summarising, transcription, editing), then asks the real question:

> ★★★ *"Is any one of these things game-changing — that's going to shatter your life and make you a
> superpowered human? **No.** **What if we combine them together?** Starts to get kind of interesting."*

★★★ **The creativity principle underneath it**: *"here's the thing we know about creativity — maybe
[a thing] by itself isn't that cool, but **[that thing] carved out of ice in a lava pit** might be cool.
**If you just take discrete elements and mix them together, all of a sudden that mixture becomes really
powerful.**"*

★★★ **The non-partisan tool position**, which is unusual and worth keeping: *"**there's no AI engine that's
bad** — it's just **trained on models that work or don't work for you. But you don't have to be stuck to
one.**"* His stated division of labour: one model *"can search the internet"*, the other *"understands
symbolism and metaphor — **you can tell it very little and it would understand it**"* — so *"**he ping-pongs
back and forth.**"*

★★ **The handover use case** — contributed by **Dave**, and the best concrete example on the page: stuck for
**two hours** explaining a metaphor to one model, he copied *"the entire conversation from start to finish"*
into the other and asked it to **analyse where they had been talking past each other** — *"where was it
understanding what I was saying, where was it not, where were we fighting, where were we hitting walls"* —
then: *"**can you now use that as instructions, and not do that from now on** — continue the conversation
pretending that you're [the first model]."* Result: *"**I finished the whole thing in half an hour.**"*

★ **Chris's gloss, which is also the joke**: *"I have two boys — it's like: **tell me why the other kid
isn't doing well, and can you not do that?** And the other one's like: *I'd love to, Dad.* **Oh my God, if
my real children worked like that it'd be amazing.** … **These two children that we're using can do that for
us.**"*

## ★★ A live product-validation, run in front of the room

Asked what digital products are worth building, he demonstrates rather than answers — interrogating a
podcaster in the room: *"what's the worst part of producing a podcast?"* → editing → *"after you edit, what's
the next worst part?"* → QA → *"after that?"* → getting listeners.

★★ **Then he corrects the premise from his own supply chain**: *"a buddy of mine is a professional podcast
producer… **the easiest part of it, believe it or not, is the editing.** It follows a very specific formula
once you know the voice of the podcaster. **The worst part is coming up with a good title, writing a
description for the blog post, writing social media assets, and writing a LinkedIn post.**"* And the quality
problem: *"when I look at the title I'm like: **who came up with this crappy name? This is not even about
this video.**"*

★★ **The offer he tests, live**: *"would it be valuable to you if someone **wrote a robot** to listen to the
episode, come up with a really great title, write the blog post that summarises it, write the email with
high-level summaries and actionable items **that takes only three minutes to read**, write five tweets and
one LinkedIn post — automatically? What do you think?"* — *"**Yes please.**"* — *"**How much do you
think…**"*

> ★★ **Recorded as a method, not a product**: he validates by finding the **expensive-but-unloved** step in
> someone's existing workflow, not by asking what they want.
