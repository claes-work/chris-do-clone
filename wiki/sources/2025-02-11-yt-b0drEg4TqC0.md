# Charge What You're Worth: A Designer's Guide to Premium Pricing

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo**, opening what he describes as **a course** (*"in this course I'm going to
> focus mostly on mindset"*). Fully persona-eligible. ⏸️ Deferred from batch 147; ingested here.
>
> ★★★ **A landmark.** It contains **the corpus's clearest mechanical explanation of why creatives
> under-price** — three linked frameworks (**run-from-pain**, **the self-concept band**, **the guilt of being
> paid for what you'd pay to do**) — plus an admission that the failure mode **still happens to him.**

## ★★ The self-description he opens with

*"Hi everybody, my name is Chris Do. **I'm a loud introvert, a recovering graphic designer, a serial
entrepreneur, and an above-average student but a first-class troublemaker.**"* Followed by the standing
mission (*"teach a billion people how to make a living doing what they love"*) and:

★★ *"**Failure is a tuition you pay for success** — and instead of trying to avoid failure we should embrace
it as part of our growth process. **It's a clear sign that we're moving in the right direction, that we're
going out of our comfort zone.**"*

## ★★★ Framework 1 — you either run from pain or run toward life

Credited: *"I was at a church — and I haven't gone to church in a really long time — and I heard **Erwin
McManus**, somebody I met on the speaking circuit. He's a very powerful speaker."*

> ★★★ *"He said: **we basically are grouped into two types of people. One type of person runs FROM pain, and
> one type of person runs TOWARDS love — or runs towards life and living. And they're drastically
> different.** **So you have to decide which type of person you are.**"*

★★★ **Chris's application**, which is the useful part: *"most of us might not put ourselves in that category
— that we're running from pain — **but when we start to break it down we'll start to see certain patterns of
behaviour that will clue us in.**"* The patterns he names:

- **Grew up without means** → *"money was a very powerful thing… **the more money you had, the happier you
  were.** We put markers of success and self-worth in how much money we're able to command — **and that's a
  lot of pressure to be holding on to yourself.**"*
- **Didn't get enough love and affirmation** → *"we're **constantly seeking others to affirm us.**"*
- **Didn't get enough attention** → *"we're constantly trying to organise ourselves and our relationships
  **around people who give us attention.**"*

★★★ **And the commercial consequence, which is the whole argument**: *"all these things create **a state of
fear, a state of lack and neediness** — that is **actually the very thing that will push away great
relationships, and therefore great clients, from us.**"*

## ★★★ Framework 2 — the self-concept, and the ±10–15% band

Credited to **Brian Tracy** (*"a book called *The Psychology of Selling*, or sales psychology, or something
like that"*): *"a very profound discovery in psychology and psychotherapy called **the self-concept** —
**who you think you are and what makes you you.**"*

★★★ **The mechanism, worked through concretely.** Suppose you inherited a belief that *"to make it in life is
to earn **$100,000**"* — *"so you go to work, you show up, you start to work towards achieving this goal,
and **as soon as you hit the $100,000… you start to shift into cruise control.** You don't show up for any
more meetings or calls, and **your calendar is all of a sudden booked up with other things.** And the funny
thing is, **when the year starts over again you start at zero** — and so you're grinding, you're making
calls."*

> ★★★ **The band**: *"give or take about **10 or 15%, we move to whatever belief we hold to be true.** So if
> you make **115,000 you'll start to chill out** and you go back to $100,000. **If you miss all your goals
> and you're at 60,000** and you've set your self-concept to be worth 100,000, **you'll burn the midnight
> oil, you'll take on another job, you'll pick up the phone and reach out to more people** — until you get
> closer to that **85 to 100** mark, **and then you'll chill out.**"*

★★★ **The conclusion**: *"**what we think of ourselves, what we think we're capable of, really will largely
determine the kind of life that we have, the relationships we have, and ultimately how much we're
worth.**"*

## ★★★ Framework 3 — why creatives feel guilty charging at all

★★★ **The analogy is the clearest statement of this mechanism in the corpus**, and he makes it about
himself: *"if you asked me what I enjoy doing in my free time, I'd tell you: **I like watching mixed martial
arts, I like reading comic books, and sometimes I play video games.** And if you asked **how much do I have
to pay you to do that** — I would think **that's the strangest concept. Why would anyone want to pay me for
reading comics, watching MMA or playing video games?** And **I would feel a certain amount of embarrassment
and guilt** for someone to pay me to do that — **because I actively and happily *pay* to be able to do
this.** I buy comics. I pay for the pay-per-views. I'll go to the venues. I buy video games."*

> ★★★ *"So now when we reverse that psychology and say **someone will pay us to do something that we love to
> do — it feels really wrong.**"*

★★ Applied: *"whether you're a designer, illustrator, animator, editor, social media marketer — **there's a
good chance you're doing this because you love it**, because you have a passion for it… **and it feels wrong
to charge for it.**"*

## ★★★ The failure sequence — negotiating against yourself

★★★ **The frame**: *"here comes the real battle, and it's **the internal battle** — it's the hardest to
overcome. **The greatest enemy you ever face on the battlefield is the one that exists in your mind, not the
opponent standing in front of you.**"*

★★★ **And he puts himself inside it, in the present tense**: *"this has happened to all of us, and
**happens to me to this day**, where I think: *well, I don't know… **$5,000*** — and I think: ***God, that
sounds like a lot of money. Why would anyone want to pay me that amount? This is so easy for me, it's like
something I would do like breathing. Why don't I charge them 2,500? It'll be an easy yes for them.***"*

★★★ **Then the trap springs**: *"so we float a number by them — *I'm thinking $2,500.* And **what does the
client do? They don't say yes** — **because they're business people, and business people know that you're
not supposed to take the offer, you're supposed to negotiate down.** So now **you've pre-negotiated against
yourself, and now the other person, rightfully so, is negotiating against you.**"* They counter at 2K —
*"and **because you are afraid of conflict and friction**, you say: yeah, that works."*

★★★ **The cost is not the money — it's the work**: *"in the back of your mind **you feel a little
resentful**… and **if your state is a negative one, one that is resentful and there's bitterness in your
heart, then you're going to do the work and it's going to affect it.** It's going to affect **the words that
you write** if you're an email marketer, **the logo that you design** — because you're like: ***you know
what, I'm tired of working on this*** — when **if you worked on it a little bit more you could get it just
right.** And you deliver it, and the client's like: *oh… okay, thanks.* **And this cycle just repeats itself
over and over again.**"*

## ★★★ Whole vs. hole

> ★★★ *"You're trying to be **a whole human — 100% complete, devoid of gaps** — **not a human that has a
> *hole* that needs to be filled.** Those people tend to be very needy, and they're looking for fulfilment
> and acceptance and love from others — **and it's a never-ending cycle.**"*

★★ **And the transmission**: *"**the most unhealthy relationship that we have with ourselves transmits and
translates into our relationship with others**, where **we self-sabotage — we lower our rates because we
think that's what people are willing to pay.**"*

## ★★★ The fix — move the spotlight off yourself

★★★ **The diagnosis**: *"when we think about pricing we think about **what it costs *us* to make
something** — how much time, energy and effort, our level of experience. **So it becomes a very
self-centred approach. It becomes a very egocentric way of looking at things.**"*

★★★ **The replacement, stated as a sequence**: *"the simple way is to be **very empathetic and very
generous**, and ask the other person: ***what is it that you're trying to achieve by doing this thing?***
… And **the other person will happily tell us — because no one's ever asked them before.**"* → *"it'd be
great if I could achieve a higher click-through rate… a higher open rate… increase the conversion"* →
*"**well, what will this do for your business?**"* → *"it'll make us **$10,000 more a month**"* → ★★★
*"**great — so what are you willing to spend to achieve that result?**"*

★★★ **What has actually changed**: *"now **we're learning to speak the language of business.** We've moved
the focus away from ourselves and we're **shining that spotlight on the other person — which is where it
belongs.**"* And: *"don't think about what it's going to cost you to make — **the client doesn't care. They
only care about what you're going to be able to do for them.**"*

★★ **A structural benefit he notes**: *"this removes a lot of the friction, because **you cannot land on a
price or a solution that isn't relevant to your client** — because you spent all this time talking to
them."*

★★ **The objection he pre-empts** (a recurring pattern of his): *"you might be thinking: ***great Chris,
easy for you to say. You have awards, you've been doing this a long time. What am I supposed to do — I'm
still starting out, I'm still struggling, I don't have consistent lead flow?***"* His answer: *"**the first
step to doing anything is first committing your mind and your heart to saying: this is possible.** We have
to change **the self-concept, the internal self-dialogue, the self-talk.**"*
