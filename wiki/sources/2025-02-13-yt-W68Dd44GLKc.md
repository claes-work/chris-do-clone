# Travel Changes You (If You Let It): A Call To Adventure

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo, storytelling.** Compact (9.4KB) and unusually warm. Fully persona-eligible.
> Other trip participants are context, not persona data.
>
> ★★★ **Kept for two things**: a **named framework new to the corpus (7 hours / 11 touch points / 4
> locations)** which he explicitly **treats as a hypothesis he runs experiments on**, and a **biographical
> thread** — the Canadian fishing trips — that the corpus did not have.

## ★★★ The 7-11-4 rule — and he tests it rather than repeating it

Credited: *"if you're not familiar with this concept, it's called the **ZMOT — the zero moment of truth**,
published by Google, who did this study about all the things that people do before they make a purchase."*

> ★★★ *"Somebody needs to spend **at least 7 hours** getting to know you, across **11 touch points**, across
> **4 different spaces or places**, to really get to know you. **That's where we stop becoming strangers,
> and it's the beginning of friendship.** Seven hours, 11 touch points, four locations."*

★★★ **What makes this worth promoting is that he holds it as a claim to be tested, not a fact to be
recited**: *"I've been thinking about this — **is this a theory? Is there something more to this?** And **at
every opportunity I've been able to do this, I've tried my best — for whatever group I'm meeting with — to
change the location three more times.** And **something does happen. People walk away feeling more
connected, more themselves, just less alone.**"*

★★ **The trip as a deliberate execution of it** — he counts the locations: a Cambodian-Vietnamese restaurant
(*"Michelin-star level… but really down to earth, eating family style with the lazy Susan… an outrageously
crowded table because there's too many humans at one table"*) → a small airport → a **two-prop plane** → a
**water taxi** → a **floating lodge**. *"**And that was just day one, guys. That's just day one.**"* Then
swapping boats, trading stories, *"bump into one another in the dry room getting on our wet gear"* — and
post-trip, a **fireside chat in Vancouver** where the group sat in the front row and *"they were obnoxiously
loud, clapping, applauding, standing up — **like, guys, you're embarrassing me, sit down.**"*

## ★★★ The call to adventure — his own hero's journey, run as an experiment

★★ **The structure first**, credited: **Joseph Campbell's** hero's journey — ordinary world → call to
adventure → refusal → mentor → crossing the threshold → trials → *"the relapse and the resurrection"* →
return with a lesson. *"If you look at the story structure of **Star Wars** it's literally this — and the
reason it's not a coincidence is because Campbell said **George Lucas was an amazing student of his.**"*

★★★ **Then he puts himself in it**: *"about **two years ago I put out a call to adventure** — **who would
like to go fishing with me in the Canadian wilderness?**"* — and ★★ **only one person answered** (**Paul**,
*"from the south, so he has his kind of Southern gentlemanly ways"*), *"two complete strangers, but we kind
of sort of know each other in the way that anyone knows each other on the internet."*

★★★ **A year later, the same call**: *"to my surprise, this time around a whole bunch of people expressed
interest… **19 strangers and myself. 19 creatives** embark in the Canadian wilderness."* (September, the
month before recording.)

★ **He notes the incongruity himself**: *"people are thinking: ***Chris likes to be outdoors? How's his
sensitive skin going to handle this?*** And number two, *he likes fishing — isn't that barbaric?* **But all
those things are true. I do love fishing in the outdoors.**"*

## ★★★ Hosting, and what changed

★★★ *"Unlike previous times, **I was here as a host, not as one to participate** in the lodge. First and
foremost, **my goal for this whole thing was to make sure each person experienced the adventure that I got
to experience.**"*

★★★ **His kickoff line to the group**, which he flags as risky as he says it: *"**if you open your heart and
you're full of curiosity, and if you let what happens happen and you don't try to control it, you will not
be the same person that came to this trip.** And that's a bold thing for me to say. **I almost sound so
sappy** — but that was the call to adventure."*

★★ **On why being there beats seeing it**: *"photos do not do a place like this justice… **when you see it
with your own eyes in stereoscopic vision, and you feel the atmosphere and the sun on your face and the
crispness of the water in the morning air — it does something to you.**"*

## ★★★ Phil, the phone, and the jackpot

The story he chooses to tell, and it is a story about reframing:

Excitement in another boat — he assumes a big fish. *"He goes: **oh my God. My phone is at the bottom of
this ocean.** … *I was so excited about getting this weird upside-down shot of you guys in your boat, I was
holding it upside down, it slipped right out of my hand and straight into the water.*"*

★★★ **And the turn, within seconds**: *"I said I'm so sorry to hear that — trying to express deep empathy and
condolences for losing a **$1,000 iPhone**. **He goes: *you know what — it was a lesson.*** And what's the
lesson? ***To be fully present in the moment. I wasn't present, obviously — and I'm grateful that this
happened now.***"* Chris: *"**what an incredible transformation** — that 30 seconds ago it's *oh no, I just
lost my phone*, [and] by the time we came up to you, **it was a reminder to be present.**"*

★★ **The payoff**: a $10-per-person jackpot (19 people, *"so there's $190 in the pot"*) for the biggest
fish, led all week by another participant by *"half a pound here, half a pound there"* — and on the
**second-to-last day**, *"**Phil of all people** pulls in a salmon that's half a pound bigger and **he wins
the jackpot.** And we're all so happy — **it just could not have gone to a better person.**"*

## ★★ Other keepers

- ★★★ **Safety produces disclosure**: *"many of them **had talents that no one had ever seen, and they'd
  never dared to share it with anybody.** But **when you feel safe and you feel really connected, I think
  your guard goes down and you let a little bit more of yourself out.**"*
  > ⚠️ **Hold against, don't merge with,** *"I like to create **unsafe** space — in the friction is where the
  > real learning happens"* ([[2025-01-16-yt-SsuiKyAwDhM]]). **Different objects**: intellectual friction in
  > a teaching room vs. relational safety on a trip. Both are his; **record both, note the distinction.**
- ★★ **The gratitude correction**, which is characteristic: *"people would come up and thank me for
  **allowing** them to go on a trip like this. I said: ***allow you? You'll pay the money, you get to go.
  That's how this works.***"*
- ★★ **The paradox**, ✅ the same doctrine as *"good things are on the opposite side of discomfort"*
  ([[2025-01-09-yt-Sb80TUwoTOE]]): *"**some things that look like scary, challenging things — on the
  opposite side will feel like the best decision you've ever made.**"*
- ★★ **The teacher's multiplier**: *"my feeling as a teacher is: **I share something with someone, and they
  share it with two more people, and they share it with two more people — and that's the power of education
  and storytelling.**"*
- ★★ **The verdict, and it's the best line in the episode**: *"though I probably had **one of the worst
  fishing trips on a *how much fish did you catch* level, I had one of the best fishing trips I've ever had
  in my life** — getting to share that with 19 strangers who became friends."*
- ★ **A live offer**: DM him to join a waiting list for future trips — *"if you're ready to go on adventure,
  to go totally outside of your comfort zone and **do something weird with some wackos**, let me know."*
