# Why Your Brand Is Failing Without a Story (REFRAME Conference)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo on stage**, working from a flip pad and taking the session live from the
> floor. Audience members are context, not persona data. ⏸️ Deferred from batch 148; ingested here.
>
> ★★★ **A landmark.** New: **"build a world, not a narrative"**, **the Daywalker self-concept**, **thoughts
> on trial**, **the dark-thought triage**, and a **live commodity-rescue** that proves the talk's thesis on
> a real profession. Plus a **second attestation of "unsafe space."**

## ★★★ The thesis — no story equals a commodity

★★★ *"**A product or service or an organisation without a story is a commodity.** So **no story equals a
commodity** — and a commodity is something that's **easily replaced**, and usually **whatever is more
convenient and cheapest is what is purchased.**"*

## ★★★ Build a world, not a narrative

★★★ *"**Each time you create a story it's a brand-new act of creation. It's very difficult to do this.**
**What if, instead of creating a narrative, you created a world?** … Figure out how you want to package it
up **so that people ask you questions about it** — **that becomes the springboard for all of your ideas.**"*

> ★★★ **New to the corpus, and it solves a problem the existing storytelling material doesn't address**: a
> narrative has to be re-made each time; a **world** generates its own prompts. Note the success criterion
> is **questions**, not comprehension.

★★ **And the naming problem he flags for the room**: *"**your name is your brand** — so how can you use your
name as a way to create a brand? Some of you have that problem, because you're **Johnson Associates** or
whatever it is."*

## ★★★ "I create unsafe space" — second attestation

*"I know in today's time and space it's really important to create **safe space**… well, **I like to tell
people I create *unsafe* space, because it's in the friction that we get to the truth** and we get to some
point of clarity — **either for you, or for me, or for all of us.**"*

> ✅ **Second independent statement in four weeks** (cf. [[2025-01-16-yt-SsuiKyAwDhM]]). **This is a settled
> teaching doctrine, not an ad-lib.** ⚠️ Still to be held against *"when you feel safe… your guard goes
> down"* from [[2025-02-13-yt-W68Dd44GLKc]] — **different objects**, both his.

★★ **His stated format preference, corroborating the Kane episode**: *"**my happy place isn't doing
keynotes**, even though I literally will make hundreds of slides for keynotes. It's **just to have
conversations with people and to have a little fun together** — and **I'm in my happy place if I have a piece
of paper and a marker.**"*

## ★★★ The Daywalker — his own hybrid self-concept

★★★ **The doubt that produced it**: *"a couple of years ago I ran into a group of **left-brainers** — people
who work in the mortgage space, real estate, loan officers — and **I started to doubt myself. What am I
doing here? Why are they even listening to me right now?**"*

★★★ **The resolution**: *"and then I realised, going back to those origins — **I think I'm half accountant /
business person, and half crazy creative person.** So depending on who I'm speaking to, I tell people:
**I help left-brainers think right.**"*

> ★★★ **And the metaphor, which is the keeper**: *"I'm also a big nerd about pop culture. Do you know who
> **Blade** is? Wesley Snipes' character — he's half [vampire] and half human, so his line in the movie
> which I love a lot is: ***I have all the strengths of the vampire and none of their weaknesses.*** He's
> called a **Daywalker.** **A hybrid. Someone who travels two worlds. And sometimes I think of myself like
> that.**"*

★ **The origin joke behind it**: *"in a different life **I would have been an accountant** if I'd listened
to my aptitude test in high school — but instead **I decided to go down this dark path called design.**"*

## ★★★ The live coaching — a working example of his method

A participant (**Corey**) volunteers: *"how do we get out of our own way? I feel like **so many of us feel
like we don't belong in these spaces.**"* What follows is the clearest procedural record of his coaching in
the corpus:

1. *"**What do you think's in your way?**"* → *"Me."* → *"**That's a lot of self-awareness. That's
   great.**"*
2. *"Where are you trying to get to?"* → *"a more successful place"* → ★★★ *"**A more successful place is the
   place that you're in right now. Does it not feel successful?**"*
3. ★★★ **The single question that does the work**: *"are you operating **in feeling, or based on fact**, that
   these people are more successful than you?"* → *"absolutely feeling"* → *"so this is an **unvalidated**
   [belief] that you have."*
4. ★★★ **He asks permission before pushing** — consistent with the documented *directness requires
   permission*: *"**I don't want to invalidate you, but I'm going to challenge your thinking. Is that
   okay?**"*
5. ★★★ **The arithmetic rebuttal**: *"if you were to write down on a piece of paper how much money you're
   making — **do you think everyone is making so much more than you? It can't be true.** Either **half the
   room is rich and half the room is poor**, or **that cannot be true.**"*
6. ★★ **Look behind you**: *"people often say **there's always people more successful than them** — but
   **what they never do is look behind you. Look how many people are behind you.**"* With *"comparison is a
   thief of joy"* cited.
7. ★★★ **The inner-critic instrument.** He cites *"a book called **Conquering Your Inner Critical Voice**…
   by a couple of doctors, **Dr Firestone**[s] I think — a husband-and-wife team"*, then turns her own words
   back: *"when you said ***I want to get out of my own way, my problem is me*** — **that's pretty violent
   language.** … **Does that sound like a loving, supportive voice inside your head?**"* And the close:
   ★★★ *"**Do you have any children? … Would you ever say that to your child? Never? Why wouldn't you?**"*
8. ★★★ **On stopping inner work when it hurts**: *"have you done any kind of inner work?"* → *"some, until it
   gets uncomfortable"* → ★★★ *"**oh — that's the way to do that kind of work. It's supposed to be
   uncomfortable from the beginning.** … **Why do you want to pull out before it gets uncomfortable?**"*

★★★ **And the guard he puts on himself, in public**: *"I usually ask **if there's a therapist in the
room** — is there a therapist in the room? Anybody, please save me. … **Let's not go so deep, because I can
get us in trouble and I won't know how to get us out of it.**"* Plus the honest aftermath: *"**I feel like I
just opened the wound and just left you there.**"*

> ✅ **This is the *know your depth* guard, executed live.** The corpus records it as a principle; here it is
> in operation, including the discomfort of having gone slightly too far.

★★★ **The generalisation he draws**: *"I'm fascinated by philosophy, therapy and all these kinds of things —
but I just find that **we're all adults with a broken child inside. And the minute we can come in peace with
that child — to love on that child, to respect and honour that child — wonderful, amazing things can
happen.**"* ✅ Same family as *"children in adult suits"* / *"children trapped in adult bodies."*

## ★★★ Two portable instruments

**1. ★★★ Put your thoughts on trial.** *"When you have a thought, a feeling — **I think you should do what
therapists do: make a case FOR and AGAINST this thing. Put it on trial. Put your thoughts and feelings on
trial in a court of law — and you are the lawyer and the judge for both.** … **What evidence do you have
that to be true? What evidence do you have that it's false?**"*

> ⚠️⚠️ **COLLISION FLAGGED — and it resolves in nobody's favour.** [[2024-10-27-yt--wh61BGjNNs]] (Matt Essam,
> 8th instance, **2024-10-27** — *earlier*) contains a **court-of-law exercise**: *"I always like to imagine
> that I'm in a court of law and someone has brought this accusation to me… **is there anything the defendant
> might say to that?**"*
>
> **Do not treat either as derived from the other.** Chris attributes his explicitly to *"what therapists
> do"* — it is standard **cognitive-behavioural** practice (evidence-for / evidence-against), and Essam
> credits **Byron Katie** for his adjacent material. **Two people independently using a common therapeutic
> instrument.** Record Chris's version under his name; **do not flag it as a leak**, and do not strip it.

**2. ★★★ The dark-thought triage.** *"I always think about this when I have a negative or dark thought. I ask
myself: **if it were true, would it make me feel better? Is there anything I can do about it? And if I can't
do anything about it, I don't want to think about it anymore.** **I only want to change or work on the
things that I have a possibility that I can change.**"*

## ★★★ The bookkeeper rebrand — the thesis proved on a live profession

A participant (**Patty**) asks him to *"rebrand the word **bookkeeper**."* He refuses to start with the
answer: ★★★ *"**before we can rebrand, we need to know what is the public perception** of what bookkeepers
are [and] what they do."*

★★ **He also negotiates the definition first.** Patty: *"branding is public or common perception of a
word."* Chris: *"do we accept that definition? … if you read the books on branding, it's this **gut
feeling** — it's a **reputation**, it's **an idea shared by enough people.**"* And he checks whose
perception is being reported: *"is that **the public perception**, or is that **your** perception?"*

**What the room supplies**: *"it's just data entry"* · *"easy — anyone can do it"* · *"there's no value in
providing bookkeeping services"* · *"low cost"* · *"low skill"* · *"purely admin."*

> ★★★ **And he lands the cold-open thesis on it**: *"**so it's a commodity, right?**"*

★★ **The aside that widens it**: *"here's the funny thing — **there are a lot of professions right now that
some of these things could be said about as well, because we're in a time of massive change.**"*

★★★ **The move to the fix, with a constraint**: *"how do we change this? **What is it that we'd like to move
it to — and we have to be truthful here, not wishful thinking.**"* The room's honest answer: *"it's a unique
skill — **they don't know how hard it is.**"*

★★★ **Which produces the real lesson, drawn out of a participant (Wendy) rather than asserted**: *"**the key
to communication is talking in a way that the person you're communicating with understands you** — **not
getting them to come to the way we communicate.**"* Chris's restatement: ★★★ *"**communication isn't about
what I want to say — it's about how I can most effectively deliver the thing that would make sense to both
of us. You have to find that common language.**"* And the diagnosis: ★★ *"**we assume they're as nerdy about
what it is that we're nerdy about, so we force it upon them.**"*

★★ **His own worked example, which is also a household detail**: *"my wife does this to me — although I'm an
entrepreneur, **I don't care about the money at all.** She's like: *hey, can we look at the balance sheet* —
**oh, please shoot me now.** … **All I ask her to do is: faster or slower. Step on the gas — you can spend
more money — or pump the brakes a little bit. That's all I care about.**"* Then the instruction: *"**if
you're working with an entrepreneur, maybe that's how you need to talk to them.**"*
