# 7 Habits of Millionaires — That Most People Learn Too Late

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo, scripted listicle.** No guests. Fully persona-eligible.
> Format caution: a listicle recycles doctrine by design. **Most of this is already in the corpus** —
> kept for **three genuinely new items** and **one attribution finding** that only becomes visible when
> this page is read against [[2025-02-20-yt-e2zox3-QL5Q]], ingested in the same batch.

The frame he opens with, and it is a claim about himself: *"these are the **exact same habits I used as an
introverted teenager** to transform into an award-winning designer and to build a multi-million-dollar
business."*

## ★★★ Habit 7 — innovation is messy and wasteful (NEW to the corpus)

The corpus has plenty on experimentation; it has **not** had this argument, which is a hard
incompatibility claim rather than a preference:

> ★★★ *"Everybody says of course we want to be innovative… **conversely they say we need to be really
> efficient. Those are two opposing ideas. They don't live in the same universe.** **You cannot be
> simultaneously innovative and efficient.**"*

★★★ **The failure mode he names**: *"most companies **prematurely decide they're in an efficiency-building
mode** — they try to cut cost, systematize everything, create processes… and then **they get t-boned on the
freeway of innovation**, and their business is no longer viable in the marketplace."*

★★ **The mitigation**, credited to *"the book **Rework**… this chapter on **making small bets versus one big
gamble**"*: take *"one large project… and chop it up into 10 smaller projects."* Worked: *"you take a
**$100,000 budget** and instead of launching one gigantic video or one gigantic refresh of an app or
website, you'd say **what is one idea we can try this month that we can spend $10,000 on**?"* ★★ And the
reason the waste is the point: *"you'll probably make lots of mistakes on the way — **by the time you hit
mistake seven or eight you've learned a ton**, so it informs each new initiative."*

> ⚠️ **A variance in his own stated interval, recorded not resolved.** Here: *"this is exactly how I was able
> to **reinvent our creative agency every 3 to 5 years** to stay relevant… and be able to make **over $80
> million.**"* Eleven days earlier ([[2025-02-07-yt-Hmpf-q2IGQQ]]) he states it as *"**every three years or
> so**, if you haven't changed your business model… you're falling behind."* **Same doctrine, different
> stated period.** Treat the interval as approximate in both directions; do not harden either number.

## ★★★ Habit 3 — talk to strangers, and the unofficial-ambassador practice (NEW)

★★ **The self-diagnosis**, unusually blunt: *"**you add a healthy dose of introversion, social awkwardness
and lack of social skills, and it can create a pretty nasty recipe.** How do I know about this? **Cuz this
was me. This is my story.** I'm the type of person who, when clients came by, **I would just hide in my
room** — and my executive producer would say *hey, we're going to have a client dinner* that I was paying
for, and I'm like **I don't want to go, you go.**"*

★★★ **The intervention, and who made it** — his business coach **Kier McLaren**: *"**who is the
relationship with — is it with you, the creatives, or the producer?**"* Chris: *"I know the answer to that.
**It's definitely not with me** — they didn't even know that I… they've been working with a creative team
who went out to dinner with them, and the executive producer who's in their ear all the time."*

> ★★★ **The rule he extracts, which is the load-bearing line of the habit**: *"**whoever is closest to the
> client makes the most amount of money.**"* And the consequence: *"everybody takes a little bit of the
> money away from the budget as it moves down the chain — **this is why the people who actually do the work
> have the least amount of money and the lowest amount of profit.**"*

★★★ **The practice — new to the corpus, and characteristically small.** At a fast-casual restaurant with a
bottleneck queue and a confusing menu he began helping strangers order: *"**are you guys new in town?**…
everything you order is going to be great, I come here all the time — **pick a protein, pick a veg, pick a
side and you're going to be great.**"* He does it for *"every single guest"*: *"**I became an unofficial,
unpaid ambassador for this cafe.**"*

★★ **And the payoff, which he is careful NOT to inflate**: the manager (**John**) became a friend, mentioned
a rebrand was being considered, and introduced him to the **chief marketing officer** — *"we had a whole
dialogue, we went through what they were doing. ★★ **It didn't go beyond that.**"* Then the honest moral:
*"it's really interesting that **you can start off doing something super innocent, to be of service to
people, and it builds natural connection that can open doors you didn't even know existed.**"*

★ **He explicitly refuses the usual word**: *"**I don't even want to use the word networking** — but to be
able to build real relationships with people."*

★★ **The instruction**: *"**go talk to somebody you've never met with no other agenda except to help them**,
or to just start up small talk… **be the unofficial ambassador to some place you feel comfortable with.**"*

## ★★ Habit 4 — be a forever student, and what it does to how you're perceived

★★★ **The intentionality clause is what makes this more than a platitude**: *"**I don't consume information
to say that I've done something.** It's not a thing I do on a checklist. It's not something I do at a dinner
party to tell people how educated I am **with pinky raised in the air.** **What I know comes from the
materials I've absorbed with the intentionality that I'm going to teach or talk about this** at some point
in the near future — **I need to get it out of my system**, and the more times I share it, the more likely
it is to stick."*

★★★ **Then the effect, and he flags the blur himself rather than enjoying it**: *"people start looking at
you a little bit differently — *wow, you have deep thoughts, a wealth of knowledge and experience* — and
they just assume you've lived all these adventures. **But you know what — I've read most of them. I've lived
some of them.** **The lines start to blur**, because in my command of the information it becomes part of my
experience and my identity."*

★★ **Why he goes outside his field**: *"once we achieve some level of quote-unquote mastery… **expand that so
we can tie different ideas together**… when I enter a conversation with a business person in an industry I
know nothing about, **it's much easier for me to find the common ground and tie things together for them.
This is how you become a strategic thinker.**"*

★ *"**The more I learn, the more I know what I don't know** — it's the problem of the ignorant people who
assume they already know everything."*

## ★★★ Habit 5 — questions > answers, and the attribution finding

★★ **The schooling critique**: *"we grow up in a system where the rules have been clearly defined [and] the
answers are more important than questions. In fact when a teacher gives you instructions and you raise your
hand — *Miss Smith, I have a question about this* — eventually Miss Smith gets so agitated: **no more
questions, Chris, let's get into the quiz.** ★ **They shouldn't do that, but sometimes that happens.**"*

★★ **The mechanic analogy** for answering the wrong question: you tell the shop *"XYZ is wrong"*, they fix
exactly that, the problem returns — *"**you told me this is what you wanted to do, so I did exactly what you
wanted.**"* Hence: *"**slow down, be curious a little bit longer, be slower to give answers.**"*

> ★★★ *"**We need to shift away from being a problem solver to a problem seeker.**"* And the money line
> attached to it: *"**the reason people pay me so much money for my time is because I help them get clarity
> on what their problem is by asking better questions.**"*

> ⚠️⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION FINDING — do not record problem-seeker as his own coinage.** **Here it is uncredited**,
> presented as his habit. **Two days later**, in [[2025-02-20-yt-e2zox3-QL5Q]], he credits it explicitly:
> *"when I was having a conversation with **Brian Collins**, he says designers need to move from being
> problem solvers to problem seekers."* **The credit exists in his own mouth, in the same week.** Record the
> idea as **Brian Collins's, adopted and propagated by Chris Do** — and treat the uncredited listicle
> version as compression for format, **not** as a claim of authorship.

> ⚠️ **A quotation he attributes twice and gets wrong twice.** Here: *"**I think it was [name garbled] who
> said there's no such thing as the right answer to the wrong question.**"* In the 02-20 course, the same
> line: *"**I forget who said this.**"* **He does not reliably know the source and says so.** The attribution
> is **not asserted** by this page.

## ★★ Habits 1, 2, 6 — the familiar three, with what is worth keeping

- **Habit 1, bias toward action.** ★★ *"the ones who are going to succeed the most have **the greatest
  propensity towards taking action**… often what happens is **we're waiting for complete sets of
  instructions before we take action**."* ★★★ The image is the good part: *"**they are stuck at home plate —
  actually they're stuck in the dugout. They never even step on the plate and take a swing**, because
  they're so fearful they're going to waste time and resources **that they actually waste time and resources
  not doing anything.**"* ★★ *"**you don't read a book on how to play basketball, you just start playing**"*
  — then a coach makes small adjustments. ★★ *"**it's either I win or I learn something.**"* ★ Plus a
  loosely-quoted *"it is better to regret the things you did and not the things you did not do"* —
  **unattributed, and not asserted here.**
- **Habit 2, treat your time like gold** — *"**precious, non-renewable and perishable.**"* ★★★ The
  disarming interrogation of the delegation excuse: *"are you literally **the world's best designer**? …the
  best in your **country**? …your **state**? …your **city**? **So you're not the best — and therefore you're
  limiting your growth by acting as if no one else could do this at your level or even better.**"*
  ✅ **A-players hire A+ players** is already corpus doctrine; here it carries the Silicon-Valley framing
  and a personal end-state: *"I hire designers who **care more about the design than I do**, so **I rest
  really easy at night**… someone in charge who cares about it **to a degree above my own level of quality
  control.**"* ★★★ **And he pre-empts the obvious objection about himself**: *"people wonder, *well Chris,
  easy for you to say because you're now successful and you have the resources* — **I'm successful and I
  have the resources BECAUSE I made this decision at the beginning and not after the fact.**"* ★★ The
  contrast: *"people who don't have a healthy mindset say **one day I'll be able to afford to hire someone**
  [vs] **I will make sacrifices so I can hire the people so that I can have the money to hire more
  people**… they don't look at people as an expense, they look at them as **an ROI**."*
- **Habit 6, feedback is a gift.** ★★★ The definition is the keeper, because it excludes most of what
  people call feedback: *"**real feedback isn't *this is dumb* or *that's great* — those are just opinions,
  those are very subjective. Real feedback is thoughtful, intentional communication as to what's not working
  and what can be improved. It takes a real skilled person to be able to do this.**"* ★★★ And the guard he
  attaches to his own aphorism: *"**as I say, feedback is a gift — not everybody's giving gifts that you
  want. So be careful of who you seek feedback from.**"* ★★ On why people avoid it: *"**we're so in love
  with our own ideas at this point, we're so deep in the process, we can't bear to change anything**"* —
  and then post-launch someone says *I wouldn't have phrased it that way*: *"**well, where were you? They
  weren't there because you weren't seeking the feedback.**"*
