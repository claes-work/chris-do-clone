## ★★★ Three frameworks new to the corpus

### 1. ★★★ Pi-shaped, not T-shaped

*"What's really important is for you to get that **T-skill** first — really deep on one vertical. And you
know what builds confidence? **Doing the same thing over and over again. It's called drills.** ★★★ **Drills
before skills.**"* Then the extension: *"and I've learned this recently — **instead of the T-shape you can
have the pi-shape, which is two verticals with a crossbar. So you can go deep on two things** — and you can
do that usually **when you're a little bit older.**"*

★★ **With the age-gate he knows is provocative**: *"people think *I'm a polymath, I'm a multi-hyphenate.*
**Are you?** If you're 40 or 50 and you have a master's degree in multiple programs — yeah, maybe. **But
you're 18. Don't tell me you're multi-hyphenate, because I just don't think you've been alive long
enough.** ★ **I know that's ageist. I'm just going to throw it out there.**"*

★★★ **The food-court argument against going broad** — the sharpest version of this in the corpus: *"you sell
pizza and you're not that good — the crust isn't good, the sauce isn't good — so you're like *let me add
tacos*, and that doesn't work, so you add chicken wings. **Now you compete with the chicken wing place, you
compete with the taqueria, AND the places that make pizza better than you.**"* ★★★ *"**You've unwittingly
invited all the people in those other categories to compete with you too.**"*

### 2. ★★★ Read to teach — the three levels

> ★★★ *"Whatever your favourite book was — **could you teach it today without any preparation?** Some of you
> can. **Most of you cannot.**"*

★★★ **The story that produced it**, and it is at his own team's expense: he bought copies of **Blair Enns's
*Win Without Pitching Manifesto*** for his management team and gave them a week. *"So you read it? Yep, yep,
read it. **Okay — what's it about?** They're like, *I… what do you mean?* **Give me a summary, tell me a big
idea.** ★★★ **None of them in the room could tell me anything about the book.** *Tell me one vague memory of
the book.*"*

★★★ **The levels**: *"most people **read a book to finish the book, not to actually learn.** You're already
in **level two** if you read the book because you want to be able to apply it. But I'm going to tell you
**read the book on level three, which is read it to teach it. Your intentionality affects how you read the
book.**"*

★★★ **The compounding version, which he flags as too simple to be adopted**: *"the hack is **so dumb-stupid
simple that most of you will not do it.** I've got a theory that some of the people you really admire… will
**read a book over and over again until they know that book like it's their book — and then they read four
more versions of that book.** **By the time you read five books on one topic from five different people**,
you can feel pretty confident that you know something — and **if you start to synthesise those five ideas
and add your own stories on top, you'd be up here instead of me.**"*

★★ **The etymological support**, which he had just read: *"the phrase **well read** today means reading a
ton of books — but **it used to mean reading a handful of books really, really well.** That's what *well
read* used to mean to the ancients."*

★ **The running joke of the night**, from a friend named **Jose**: *Win Without Pitching* is *"a **two-poop
book** — you could read it in two poops"* — after which the room measures everything in poops
(*"how long have you been in business?" "8,000 poops"*). ★ Recorded because it is genuinely characteristic
of how he plays a room, not because it is doctrine.

### 3. ★★★ The question formula — character, want, obstacle

> ★★★ *"There's this formula for story: **a character wants something, and there's an obstacle.** That's all
> stories. … **So YOU are the character.** Just say **I'm this, I want this, and this is what's standing in
> my way** — ★★★ **if you can just say this, 99% of the problems [are] baked into your question.**"*

★★★ *"**A well-constructed question is at least 50% of the answer.**"* And the observation that follows:
*"once we were able to get the question on the board, **the answer will be evident to almost everybody
except for the person asking it.**"*

★★★ **He then runs it live for ~20 minutes on an architect, and the transcript is a much better record of
his teaching manner than any description**: the opening answer is *"I am a designer and I want to find
interesting scenarios where I can help people through design"* → *"**that's a generic question, bro.**"* →
*"I'm mostly a spatial designer, architecture"* → *"**why don't you say I'm an architect? Why you got to make
it so complicated?**"* → *"I'm not registered but—"* → ★★★ *"**I'm not going to call the board.**"* Then:
*"**we banned the word *interesting*, it doesn't mean anything**"*; *"tell me what you want in **basic baby
fifth-grade language**"*; and, quoting his own son's malapropism, ★★ *"**be pacific** — be very specific."*
Landing at *"**I want to design cool hotels, 100-plus rooms**"* and the obstacle *"you don't have a
licence"* → ★★★ *"**hey — go get your licence. Next.**"*

> ★★ **He narrates his own impatience while doing it**, which is the honest part: *"**there's a reason why I
> only do these talks once a year. You guys test my patience here.**"* / *"**what the heck is wrong with you
> guys?**"* / *"it's going to take us **half an hour** to get a person's question out."*
> ⚠️ **Directly relevant to the unresolved *"I am abrasive"* vs *"I recede into the background"* tension —
> this is the abrasive register, on stage, with the room laughing along.**

★★★ **And when he can't fix it himself, he hands the microphone to the room**: *"**who here paid attention
to English class?** … **raise your hand if you're really good at English and understand how to rephrase this
question.**"* Then accepts an audience correction that reframes the whole problem — *"the hospitality clients
aren't the clients; **the clients would be the developer** trying to raise investment money"* — with *"okay,
**you know your business.**"*

★★★ **The content version of the same formula**: *"if you write your next LinkedIn post [or] Instagram story
with **I wanted this, but I couldn't do it because this happened, but what I learned was this** — **your
story will be 10× better than it is today.**"* Plus the step he says almost everyone omits: ★★★ *"**add at
the end a reflective question** — *tell me about a time when you experienced this* — and then **they start
answering. This is the beginning of dialogue, and the algorithm loves engagement.**"* ★★ And for
non-business stories: *"if you read a lot of my posts on LinkedIn **it's actually not business related at
all, it's a very personal story** — but **you build a bridge to the business lesson.** ★★ **That's the gift
of creativity: you bend the story to this thing and you make it work.**"*

## ★★★ Pricing, positioning, and the client relationship

- ★★★ **Value-based pricing is optional — he talks a man OUT of it.** *"Chris, I struggle with value-based
  pricing." "**Okay, I'll help you right now: you don't need to do it.**"* The substitute is a **flat
  price**: given a stated range of *"50 to 150 grand"* skewed to the low end, *"**why don't you just tell
  every client it's $100,000? If all your clients paid you 100 grand, would you be happy with that?**"* —
  *"really happy"* — *"**you don't need value-based pricing.**"* ★★★ The reasoning: *"**take out all the
  variability**… some jobs I'll make less than I'm supposed to, **but I'll make a lot more than the jobs
  I'm getting.**"* ★★ And the consequence he predicts: *"they'll have a new problem — **now they want to do
  value-based pricing, but they've made so much money in the meantime**… **and they may never want to go to
  the next level, and it's totally okay.**"*
  > ⚠️ **Contains an off-colour analogy about his wife's appearance**, used to argue that you needn't chase
  > a status marker you don't want. **Recorded as existing; NOT reproduced here and marked
  > do-not-reproduce** — same handling as other flagged phrasings in the corpus.
- ★★★ **The strategist test, and it is an accusation**: *"**do you care about your client's real success?**
  … if they say *I need a new logo*, you have to say **what will that do for your business — because as much
  as I'd like to take your money, how will it impact anything? ★★★ And if they can't [answer], you should
  not take their money.**"* Then: *"you say *we're strategists* because **you think you can charge more when
  you don't understand strategy** and **you actually don't care if their business succeeds or not**… **you
  just like that term because it sounds better than the term you're using.**"*
- ★★★ **Advice for juniors that contradicts the usual upsell**: *"if you're more junior, **be happy to work
  with clients who love what you do, and just charge enough money that you feel really excited to do the
  work.**"* Worked live: *"*I hate my client.* **What if we charge twice as much?** *Well… they're mildly
  annoying.* **What if you charge four times as much?** *Oh, I think I'd like the work.* ★★★ **Is it them,
  or is it you? Because you were afraid to ask for the amount of money that would bring joy to you.**"*
  ★★★ **And the defence against the obvious objection**: *"you're like *that's so capitalistic* — **it's
  really not**, because somebody worked really hard to make that money and they know they have a boring job
  to give you… **we take it for granted that money is easy to get. It's not.** ★★★ **When someone gives you
  that amount of money, that's a vote for you.**"*
- ★★★ **The 30-year proof of relationship over transaction**: *"some of the clients I worked with **20, 30
  years ago** will call me out of the blue and say *Chris, I love where you're going with your life, can we
  do something together?* … **that seed planted 30 years ago was handled in such a way that they felt
  positive residual energy.** ★★★ **How many of your clients would call you 30 years later?**"*
- ★★★ **Flaky clients — a complete, usable script.** *"Number one: **charge more.** Number two: **have
  penalties for missing deadlines.** Those are just **healthy boundaries** to have."* Then the grace-period
  language: *"*as we previously talked about, you have this much time and we're over that time. **This is a
  grace period now**, and if you go beyond that we have one or two options: **I'm going to kill the job, send
  you an invoice for the work that was done**, and we'll pick it up — **but it won't be the same price,
  because we're starting up again** — **or you're going to have to pay this amount, because I can't have my
  team idling by.*** ★★★ **It's like leaving the car running. We're not going nowhere.**"* ★★ And the exit
  line for stalled projects: *"**it feels like this is no longer a priority to you. Let me know if that
  changes** — and move on with your life."*
- ★★★ **Permission to change your mind**, credited to **Tony Robbins**: *"the number one thing that drives
  human decisions is **the need to be consistent with ourselves** — and **I'm stuck here just like you are,
  it's a human thing.**"* The illustration is deliberately absurd: you promised a 4 o'clock pickup, then
  realise it's your anniversary — *"**what do we do? We go pick the person up at 4:00 and we get
  divorced.**"* ★★★ The rule: *"**when doing what you said you're going to do hurts more people than it
  helps, change your mind.**"* ★★ And the reciprocity: *"**you do this, [which] means your clients can do
  this.**"*

## ★★★ Freelancer vs. independent business owner

★★★ He stops the host mid-question to define terms: *"**are we using the same terminology when you describe
a freelancer? I'm afraid we're not.**"* His definition: *"a freelancer **sells time, usually for a boss**,
cannot hire other people to do the work for them, often has to show up on site… **they say when, where and
how much, and then you get booked. That's it.**"* Polls the room — almost nobody qualifies. ★★★ *"**You guys
are independent business owners who just happen to have one employee: you.**"*

★★ **The etymology**, credited to **Jonathan Stark**: *"the word freelancer comes from **a lancer who used to
sell the services — you're a mercenary in medieval times.** ★ **You're a sellsword, in Game of Thrones
terminology.**"*

> ★★★ **And then the reframe that lands the whole exchange.** To *"we feel like they're hiring us to bend
> over backwards"*: *"**when you're an independent business owner you get to dictate the terms of how you
> want to work.** When you accept terms you're not happy with, **you bent yourself over backwards. Nobody
> bent you** — **because you don't have boundaries, you don't have clear definition of scope, and you
> accepted it because you're afraid to have that conversation, because you're afraid of some tension.**"*

★★★ **The counter-intuitive claim attached**: *"**if you're brave enough to have that tension, people
respect you more. It's really weird.** We think *no, no friction* — okay, and then the person walks away
like **I'm not sure they understand how this stuff works.**"* ★★ With the positive case: *"when they say
*here's how most clients work with us*… **you feel relieved. You've done this before.**"*

## ★★★ Portfolio, and the philosophy underneath it

- ★★★ *"**A portfolio is only as good as the person who's looking at it.**"* — *"if I come in with a
  portfolio of album art or music videos and the person is in the finance space, **what are you showing me
  this for?**"* ★★ And a subtlety worth keeping: showing too much can price you out **before price is
  mentioned** — *"they'd literally say **we don't want all that, that's too much for what we have** —
  **we're not even telling you the price**, they just look at it like **we can't afford that.**"*
- ★★★ *"**People don't care about what you do until they know what it could do for them.**"* (He flags his
  own uncertainty about the source: *"I'm not sure if this is a phrase I've heard or I just invented it."*)
  ★★★ Then the method: *"**look at your customer, be super specific, understand their wants, needs, hopes,
  dreams, fears — and ask yourself: do I solve any of those problems with this work?**"*
- ★★★ **The line that generalises it**: *"**the work is evidence of a philosophy. So if the philosophy and
  what you do isn't correct, it doesn't matter what the evidence looks like.**"*
- ★★★ **Four pieces in a FedEx box** — how he got the ad-agency job, via a classmate copywriter who needed
  an art-director partner: *"I hadn't finished school, I didn't study advertising, **I didn't even know what
  to send**… **these are four pieces I think are most aligned with advertising. I didn't send them 30 pieces,
  I sent four pieces**… **and I was thinking I'm never going to get this job.**"* They hired him. ★★★ *"**You
  don't need a lot if it's really good. And if it's not good, then get good — I don't need the volume of
  it.**"*
- ★★ **On his own book, asked what he'd update in *Pocket Full of Do***: *"**I can't answer that question. I
  just stay present, look forward. I don't look backwards.** ★★★ **If I spent any time re-reading my own book
  I'd probably just cry** — *I wasn't good enough, why did I phrase it that way?* **I'm just ready to write
  the next book.**"* ★★ With the same move applied to portfolios: *"you look at your student portfolio —
  **what idiot made that work? Let me spend a lot of time looking at that, that'll build up my confidence, I'm
  pretty sure of it.** … **You're a learning, evolving machine. That was just an artifact of your
  intelligence, your creativity, your curiosity at a moment in time.**"* ★ And what he's writing next:
  *"I'll write a book on sales, I'll write a book on **personal branding — which is literally what I'm
  working on right now.**"*

## ★★★ Niche, confidence, and the thing that is actually hard

★★★ **The formula**, credited: *"I was reading **Michael [Port]'s** book ***Book Yourself Solid***, and he
wrote that **your niche is the market you want to serve, plus your passion.** That's it. It didn't have to
be that complicated."*

> ⚠️ **RECURRING ERROR, third instance — the corpus has already quarantined this.** He says *"Michael…
> Porter"*. The author of *Book Yourself Solid* is **Michael PORT**. **Not Michael Porter** (the Harvard
> strategist). **Record the correction; do not propagate the misattribution.**

★★★ **The live niche coaching with Amy**, a photographer, is a good worked example: *"dramatic portraits for
commercial brands"* → *"**that's a pretty meaningless sentence**… **you're like basically anybody who has
money.**"* → passion is music → *"what kind of music?"* → jazz → ★★ *"**jazz? You're going to be broke.**"*
→ rock → ★★★ *"**so: I take behind-the-scenes photos for rock bands that tour the world. It's not that hard,
right?**"*

> ★★★ **And then the real diagnosis, which is the most quotable thing in the section**: *"**you know what's
> really hard? Your attachment to your identity, and your resistance to change and commit. That's all it
> is.** You get past that, everything's super easy."*

★★★ **On why his advice sounds easy**: *"I know it's frustrating from your point of view because I've heard
*you make it sound so easy.* **It IS really freaking easy.** So then I have to figure out — **it's not easy
for you because there's something holding you back**… **and then if we find out what the limiting belief is
and I tell you what it is, you still don't want to let it go.**"* ★★★ *"**I coach lots of people at scale and
individually, and people just don't want to change. That's the bottom line.**"* ★★★ With the image: *"as
broke and frustrated and stressed out as they are — **if I say just walk through that door and there's a pot
of gold, everything will be solved: *no, I like my misery.*** **That's how it feels for me. I just don't
understand it.**"*

★★★ **Where confidence actually comes from**: *"**the confidence comes from this belief that you can solve
problems** — **then the confidence precedes your ability to demonstrate it.** Because **when you can
demonstrate it, what is confidence for?** I've won 55 tennis championships — **well of course you're
confident.**"* ★★★ And the framing he brought back from the fishing trip: *"**faith is acting despite having
evidence** [i.e. without it]. I'm not necessarily talking about God — **do you have faith in you? Do you
believe in you? Because if you don't believe in you, I don't believe in you.**"*

## ★★★ Entrepreneurship is not for everyone — and the passion soapbox

> ★★★ *"**Not a popular answer: not everybody who wants to be an entrepreneur IS an entrepreneur.**
> Entrepreneurs like risk. Entrepreneurs are totally comfortable with the unknown. **Entrepreneurs just
> figure the s— out.** And if you're not that person — **I know it's sexy on Instagram to talk about your
> entrepreneurial journey — but you're not made for it.** A lot of people I know are not made for it…
> ★★★ **so be an employee. It's totally okay. Nothing wrong with that.**"*

★★ **The test he offers**: *"**does not knowing where the next job comes from bring me excitement?**"*
★★ **And the spending pattern he attributes to real entrepreneurs**: *"they will spend crazy amounts of money
because in their mind, **if I spend $10,000 or $50,000 for a mastermind, they KNOW they'll get $51,000
back.** **They go into these programs knowing this in their heart — and then they can execute.**"*

★★★ **The passion soapbox, which he announces as a soapbox**: *"*Chris, I'm just not that passionate about
what I do* — **I'm like: get out. This was not meant for you.** You tried… **you told someone you're going to
be an NBA player, it's not working, you're not doing it. You're not a standup comic. It's not working. Get
out, do something different.**"* ★★★ His definition: *"**passion is something that you would do for free,
something that you obsess over, something where when you do it you lose track of time.**"*

★★★ **He corrects the host on The Futur's own mission, live** — and the omitted clause is the whole point:
*"when you told everybody our mission, **there was an important component missing** — it's **to help a
billion people make a living doing what they love, WITHOUT LOSING THEIR SOUL.** ★★★ **The love part is
really important.**"* ✅ Consistent with [[2025-05-31-yt-RCocxOMQD3M]].

★★★ **The mortality frame**: *"let's just assume we don't have that much time left. **How are you going to
spend the rest of your days — because every day you have one less day. We're all in states of dying from the
moment we're born.** So accept that part."*

> ★★★ **THE PARABLE — new to the corpus, and the best-constructed thing he says all night.** *"Imagine
> you're the byproduct of **some super alien species**, that you were sent to Earth **as the last of your
> kind**, and you were **raised by human parents who taught you about human limitations** — but **your
> parents knew that being on Earth would give you an advantage**, and you had all these gifts. And then,
> because you're raised by humans with human limitations, **you're socialised to believe certain things —
> and the thing that kills you the most: you're socialised to be normal.**"*
>
> ★★★ **The turn**: *"**normal just means average. The norm is average.** Who here grew up thinking *you
> know what I'm going to be? I'm going to be average*? **So somehow we've made being average good.**"*
> ★★★ **And the proof he offers, from the critique room**: *"the deepest insult you can have for a creative
> — especially if I was your teacher — was for me to say **your work is average. That would cut a hole
> through your soul.** ★★★ **To say it's bad would be better than to say it's average. To say I don't
> understand it would be better than average. To say it's weird would be better than average — yet somehow
> we want to be average.**"*
> ★★★ **The close**: *"**all those gifts are waiting for you to unearth — but you have to remember: you're
> not a human, you're superhuman.** And if this sounds familiar, you might think *maybe I'm Superman or
> Superwoman* — **it's a little different than that, but I believe that.**"*

★★★ **And the personal application, which dates the whole arc**: *"for a long time I believed **I need to be
an entrepreneur, I'm going to make commercials and music videos for a living, this is going to be freaking
awesome** — **and I wasn't wholly fulfilled.** Then I was like **I'm going to teach** — **I was financially
fulfilled** [but]… **it wasn't until I started to bring all the things I forgot about, the things that I
love** — **and I put it in a place where people can see it, that's the important part — then I found my
passion.**"* The inventory he gives: *"business concepts, learning and reading, teaching, running a
business, [being] a designer, motion designer, client work, **mixed martial arts and skateboarding**,
**weird typographic oddities**, **comic books and narrative**, **I'm a nut for movies** — and **I like to
draw diagrams and weird stuff.** **Now you can see, if you look at our content — duh.**"* ★★★ *"**I'm not a
young man anymore, but I'm so energised by what I'm doing I just can't wait for the next day** — **and the
fact that I get paid to do it is just a bonus.**"* ★★ And the closing rule: *"**follow your highest passion,
without expectations, to the highest of your ability, and you will live well** — **without expectations of
financial gain or anything else.** And **every time I've done that in my life, things have worked out pretty
freaking good.**"*
