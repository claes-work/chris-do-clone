## What it is (recorded, not endorsed)

A screen-share teaching video: **five obstacles to a sale**, which Essam credits to **Zig Ziglar's**
*Secrets of Closing the Sale* — **need, desire (want), trust, money, urgency** — and how to address each on
social media. In outline, and **all in his words, as his framework**:

1. **Need** — swap talking about *services* for talking about *symptoms*; interview a past client you
   enjoyed working with to learn their actual day-to-day. He cites an unattributed marketing maxim (*"I
   can't remember, so apologies to whoever came up with this"*) that **when you can articulate someone's
   challenges better than they can, they unconsciously give you permission to solve them.**
2. **Desire** — be specific about the outcome, not generic (*"more clients, more money"* wins nobody).
   Worked through a client in **audio branding for sports clubs**, whose real desire was match-day
   atmosphere and season-ticket renewals.
3. **Trust** — the **prospect pathway**: cold prospects are risk-averse, so lead with a free
   **irresistible offer**, then **qualify** (on revenue, team size, current marketing spend — *not* on
   budget), then a **logical next step** priced at roughly a tenth of the core project (*"if your average
   project is like 30 grand, your logical next step would be something like three grand"*).
4. **Money** — the qualification question he recommends: *"what would you need to know about this potential
   client **if you were only going to get paid when you helped them achieve the result they want?**"*
5. **Urgency** — four authentic mechanisms: **announced price increases** (which he prefers over discounts,
   because discounts train the buyer to expect them), **communicated capacity**, **time-limited bonuses**,
   and **cost of inaction (COI)** as the counterpart to ROI.

**His own credits within the video** — recorded so that no idea here is ever mistaken for Chris's or for
this repo's: **Zig Ziglar** (the five obstacles), **Daniel Priestley** and *Oversubscribed* (supply–demand
tension; *"people buy what other people want"*), and **Alex Hormozi** (calculating the gap between current
and target earnings as *"what you're currently paying to not know how to reach your goal"*).
