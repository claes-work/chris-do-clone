## ★★★ Chris's succession theory — an original argument, and a criticism he owns

He asks Caleb to *"put on your content strategist hat and be as objective as you can"* about a phenomenon he
has noticed himself: *"there was a moment when **Gary was the only talk in town** — everything he did was
exploding… **I barely see him on any of my social feeds anymore**, and despite his large following, **some
of his videos get less views than ours** — and **we only have 2.6 million followers ourselves.**"*

★★ **A dated self-reported figure for The Futur: ~2.6M followers (March 2025).**

Then he offers his own account rather than just asking for one:

> ★★★ *"**Gary is a first mover in many different things** — being a professional person, an entrepreneur
> who's successful, creating content on YouTube **before it was in vogue to do so.** He created all the
> space, and **when there's no competition you can dominate.**"*

★★★ **The audience-ageing argument**: *"he was speaking to a very specific, I think **mostly
male-oriented demographic** — **these young hustlers who are going to grind all the time**… *I'll swap, I'll
go and sell, I'll flip this and make some money* — him doing the garage sales. **But your audience
eventually grows up, and you grow out of it.**"*

> ⚠️★★★ **THE CRITICISM, WHICH HE FLAGS AS A CRITICISM**: *"**one of my criticisms of Gary**, despite all
> we've just been talking about, is **he was giving more or less similar advice to someone at a certain
> level in their life** — it wasn't like *I, as a multi-millionaire business person, am going to learn from
> [it]*… **it was kind of very rudimentary stuff, which is good for a broad audience.**"*
>
> **Handling**: this is **Chris Do's dated opinion about a named living person**, offered with an explicit
> acknowledgement of that person's achievements in the same breath. **Record it as his opinion, attributed
> and dated. Do not present it as a finding, and do not strip the qualification he attached to it.**

★★★ **The succession**: *"then comes in this new guy with a crazy handlebar mustache… **and really gives
high-level, really good business stuff that's highly actionable** for people anywhere from **the $100,000
to maybe $10 million range**… so **he starts to dominate.** ★★★ **And I think maybe the universe can only
have so many players in one category** — so now **Alex is just crushing.**"*

★★★ **And the part that makes it a theory rather than a compliment**: *"**one day somebody else will take
it from Alex** — the next version, the next iteration of it."*

★★ **Caleb's counter-theory, which Chris accepts and extends** — that creators drift **from tactics to
mindset** as they mature, because *"the tactics are very important, but **if the individual doesn't have the
correct mindset they will never actually put those tactics into place.**"* ⚠️ **Caleb's framing.** Chris's
extension **is his own**: *"you can talk tactics till you're blue in the face, **but if somebody doesn't
believe it's going to work they'll never take action** — so the bigger play is to understand what the
limiting beliefs are and help them overcome that. ★★★ **But then what happens is you start to drift away
from this core audience of young entrepreneurs who want more tactics** — **so you have to choose.**"*

## ★★★ Be the same person online and offline — and why it matters for HIM specifically

Caleb raises consistency as a lesson; **Chris's version of it is the keeper**, because he applies it to his
own worst reviews:

> ★★★ *"**Be you — whether you like you or not.** Just be you, because eventually the world will find out.
> … **I'm trying my best to be me, and sometimes me on camera is not somebody you like** — and **I don't
> want you to come up to me in real life [and say] *no, you're a prick.*** ★★★ **Well, I was a prick on
> camera too. So there you go. I'm just going to be consistent all the way through.**"*

✅✅ **This is the same doctrine as the pass-16 abrasive/warm reconciliation, arrived at from the other
direction.** There he explained *why* people misread him; here he explains *why he will not adjust for it*.
**Consistency is the resolution, not likeability.**

★★★ **The cost he names, from inside the industry**: *"here's the sad part — **as I'm in more creator
circles**: *oh my God, that person is nothing like how they appear.* **They talk one game, they play a
different game. They have one personality, they have a different personality — and it's really jarring.**
Hence that phrase: ★★★ **be careful of meeting your heroes, because they're bound to let you down.**"*

> ⚠️ **Read against [[2024-08-13-yt-i10g2BVfWVI]]**, where **he is the hero who let someone down** — a fan
> published *"be careful of your heroes when you meet them… Chris is a jerk"*, and **he accepted it
> unhedged**: *"I was a jerk — and I chose to be a jerk in that moment."* **The two must travel together;
> the phrase is not one he gets to use from outside.**

★★ ✅ **And the everyday version, corroborating *"I cannot have bad days"*** (2024-08-13): *"**I always tell
my wife: I've got to be the best me, the me-est me I can be** — because sometimes I go out and I look like a
scrub, and somebody's like *yeah, didn't shower and shave today, did you?* **You're right, I didn't — and
I'm going to hide for a little bit.** ★★ **People want you to show up the way that you've been showing
up.**"*

## ★★★ Managing creatives vs. left-brainers

Responding to Caleb's plan to teach creative-team management:

> ★★★ *"I've managed a creative company for 20-some-odd years doing production services, and **it's
> different when you're dealing with left-brainers — they understand rules on how to move forward, it's a
> lot easier to deal with them.** When you're dealing with **individual creative spirits and beings** it's
> **a lot harder to manage**: **you want to challenge them, you want to be clear — but you also don't want
> to be Draconian**, because then ★★★ **they stop functioning as creative people and they start turning in
> work instead.**"*

✅ **The management-side corollary of the Daywalker self-concept** (*"I help left-brainers think right"*,
[[2025-02-16-yt--3B7cbOOis4]]) — the same two-populations distinction, applied to running a team.

## ★★ Chris's own additions in the recaps

- ★★★ **On price objections, stated as settled doctrine**: *"**I have always said this: they will find the
  money somehow. It's never about the price — it's because they didn't want to work with you.**"*
  ✅ Consistent with the pricing hub, and a compact form worth keeping.
- ★★ **On Hormozi's book craft**: *"he says in his book **I write this for a gym rat who doesn't want to
  read books** — large-point type, phrases, **Sharpie-marker cartoon drawings** — so that you can read it.
  **There's a genius in that approach.**"*
- ★★ **On depth vs. talking about it**: *"go deep, **show** the people versus **talk about** what it is that
  you do — that's how you build credibility, **plus it's way more entertaining to watch, because everyone
  can talk about something.**"*
- ★★ **On platform-native content**: *"if that's the only thing you can do, then do that — but **if you want
  to grow on specific platforms and they're important to you, you have to spend the time and energy to do it
  in the way that the platform and the people who show up understand it.**"*
- ★★ **On compounding a team**: *"**if your team improves by 20%, multiply it across every person that works
  for you** — those are gigantic numbers. **And it's not always linear; sometimes it's exponential**, because
  there's a compounded effect happening across multiple teams."*
