# If You're Creative But Lazy, Please Watch This!

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo, scripted listicle.** No guests. Fully persona-eligible.
> **Eight rules, framed by a self-description that is the most useful thing on the page**: *"this is coming
> from a **self-described hardest-working lazy person you've ever met.**"*

## ★★★ The self-description, twice stated

*"**I often describe myself as the hardest-working lazy person I know — because I don't want to do all the
work myself.** I enjoy collaborating with other people who are highly specialized in what they do."*

★★★ **And the honesty about his own ceiling that goes with it**: *"**I'm a pretty good animator — not the
best, not even close. I'm a pretty good illustrator — not even close to being the best.** So when I get to
work with the best illustrators, animators, designers, typographers, **I'm having a great time, because they
inspire me and they allow the work to be better than what I could have done by myself.**"*

## The eight rules

### 1. ★★★ Take the smallest viable action

*"When you're looking at some big gargantuan task it seems overwhelming and **you're going to be stuck in
paralysis by analysis**… **chunk it down to the smallest viable action.**"*

★★ **The Terry Crews story he leans on** (from a fitness-magazine interview, publication *not* asserted):
*"in the article he said **I hate to work out. I don't know what's up with the people who love to work
out**"* — then the ratchet: put on the workout clothes at breakfast → *"I'm kind of silly in my workout
clothes, what should I do?"* → drive to the gym → sit in the parking lot → *"I'm already here, **I'll just
work out for five minutes, let's just do arms**"* → an hour and a half later, *"okay, I did something good
today."*

★★★ **And he applies it to himself, unflatteringly**: *"**I personally do not like to exercise. I don't want
to send out invoices, I don't want to build proposals or bids.** So I think: **what is the very first, most
innocent thing I can do?** **Showing up is 90% of the battle**, as they say — **so just figure out ways to
trick yourself into showing up.**"*

### 2. ★★★ Attach the reward to the painful thing — the 90/10 bargain

*"**If it's all work and no play it's a pretty dull life.**"* The diet version: be disciplined all week,
then *"**I want to get a double scoop of ice cream, and it's okay, because I've done the really hard
work**"* — *"**you're rewiring your brain to say: when I do this, I get to have that.**"*

★★★ **His own, concrete and self-incriminating**: *"**I love to do social media** — I'm on Instagram, TikTok,
LinkedIn, I'm everywhere in the comments — **and I realise: oh my God, half my day is gone.** So I make a
deal with myself. **The bargain is: do 90 minutes of focused work — a work sprint — and then after 90
minutes, give yourself 10 minutes to check the socials.**"*

★★★ **The double payoff he claims**: *"**I enjoy the hard work more** — and get this: **when I'm on that
10-minute break doing my socials, I enjoy that even more**, because **I know I'm not going to go down that
slippery slope where three hours disappear.** … **When I've done this, I can work all day long.**"*

★★ **And the honest note about restarting**: *"**when I'm not in the rhythm of working it's very hard to get
that motor started** — if you go on vacation and come back after three weeks, work feels like such a drag."*

### 3. ★★★ Learn how to hire others

*"**If you're a talented creative person and you don't want to work that much, you have to trade something**
… **so if you have more money than time, you spend your money and you hire other people.**"* Framed as
protective, not just economic: *"this is very important in terms of maintaining your **spiritual and creative
energy, mental health** — and to grow your business at the same time."*

★★★ **The objection and his standard rebuttal** — *"but no one else can do this better than I could"*:
*"**so you're telling me that in your town, you're the very best that can do this thing you're trying to hold
on to?**"* Then the audit: *"**it's usually a lie that we tell ourselves.** First of all, **you've not met
everybody in your town. You've not even tried** — you didn't put out a job posting, you didn't go to a job
fair, you're not teaching at a school where you can scout new talent. **You're just telling yourself I'm the
very best at this because you're reluctant to hire people.**"*

✅ **A softer, more evidential version of the same interrogation as *"are you the best in the world? the
country? the state? the city?"*** ([[2025-02-18-yt-uOWi13jSAPA]]) — same doctrine, different pressure.

### 4. ★★ Automate — and he means the unglamorous kind

*"**I'm not talking about using AI workflows or anything.** When you're doing something over and over again,
**that's an opportunity to figure out a way that's more efficient.**"* His examples are deliberately
old-fashioned: **Photoshop actions** (*"that's old school, man — you can do that"*), **keyboard text
replacement** for repeated client replies, **reusable Illustrator asset libraries** for mockups, and
**cleaned-up, tiled texture maps** so *"every time you need a stone texture, wood texture, something with a
little grit — it's available for you."*

★★★ **The payoff, with a personal claim attached**: *"it takes a little time and energy up front but **it
pays dividends every single time you use it.** **This is also why I spend an inordinate amount of time
cutting out objects** — so every time I need a telephone, that telephone is ready to go. ★★★ **This is how
I'm able to design keynotes in a day, or a few hours, where it will take people weeks.**"*

### 5. ★★★ Systematize and productize — with the objection answered by Jiro

*"**Remove as many variables from your creative process as possible.**"* The method: *"**make a recipe** —
**what is your creative recipe? What are the steps one through n that you always have to do?**"* (e.g. brand
voice and tone → mood board and reference → …), *"and once you have that documented, **I would share this
with people.**"*

★★ **Credited to Paula Scher** — her onboarding exercise: she gives a new client *"a book of beautiful
logos"* and three sticky notes, *"put it next to three logos you really like — **they can spend five minutes
or three hours, it doesn't matter.**"* Why: *"**from them picking three things she gets insight into their
preferences, their taste, and how far she can push them** — so **she's not wasting time exploring logos that
she loves but that they would hate.**"*

★★★ **He states the obvious objection himself** — *"Chris, I'm making this all too formulaic, too cookie
cutter, I'm a real-life creative human being"* — and answers it with ***Jiro Dreams of Sushi***: *"a
beautiful documentary about this man in Japan who goes through a very rigorous approach to preparing sushi
**exactly the same way**… **for him the highest art form was to be able to do something at a very high,
consistent level for as long as humanly possible.**"* With the detail that makes it land: the son, *"an
acclaimed chef himself, is just **massaging octopus for like six hours**, and his dad touches it: **needs
more time. Needs more time.**"*

★★★ **And the conversion of tedium into positioning** — *"**my first reaction might be like yours: this
would be so dull.** But **the reward of doing something over and over again is you get really good at it —
and then you can train people to help you, and you get the acclaim that comes with it. You become the
world's fill-in-the-blank.** ★★★ **This is how you go from *a* designer to *THE* designer** — instead of a
web developer, **the** web developer."*

### 6. ★★★ Hire a coach — with a caveat that is the sharpest line on the page

*"Why do people hire trainers? **You just want to switch your brain off.** You don't want to know what you
have to eat, whether it's leg day or arm day, the right reps, the right form, the right sequence."* →
*"**we could bumble around the gym not getting results, or we can pay someone who's been there.**"*

★★ **His own case, consistent with the corpus**: *"**I can't tell you how much of a difference it made when
I hired my first business coach and I worked with him for 13 years.** He changed everything about how I
think about business, how I do sales — **and I have grown exponentially because he was able to help me.**"*

> ★★★ **THE CAVEAT**: *"**save your money if your intention is to hire someone and then not do what they
> tell you to do, or to question everything they tell you to do.** This is a waste of money — **so now not
> only are you working harder, but you're wasting money to boot.** You've accomplished nothing. ★★★ **Now
> you're just dumb and busy.** **So if you want to be smart and lazy: hire someone, trust them, do what they
> tell you to do.**"*

✅ **The compressed form of the mentorship doctrine** from [[2025-02-23-yt-hC2_b-y6zuc]] — *"I don't know why
anybody pays someone money and time to get advice and then not do the advice."*

### 7. ★★★ Be a franchisee — new to the corpus, and counter-cultural for him

*"**Perhaps the hardest one to get your head wrapped around.** McDonald's has created a beautiful system…
**you don't want to invent a new McDonald's — so you become a franchisee.** You pay them a certain amount,
you use **their branding, their research**, they send you the components, **and you're able to make money
this way. So you do the labour part and don't have to invent the system.**"*

★★★ **Applied to knowledge work**: *"people in the coaching space **don't want to develop their own sales or
marketing program — so they find one they love, that they benefited from, and they become a franchisee.**
**They license the system, the name, the procedures**… **you're using your money to save yourself the entire
process of writing the material, creating a system, testing it, prototyping it, fixing it.**"* Why it works:
*"**you're leveraging the equity built into that name, and you're bringing customers to you just because of
name recognition.**"*

> ★★★ **And then he excludes himself, explicitly and at his own expense.** *"If you're saying *but Chris,
> I'm trying to build my own system, my own unique formula* — **I say congratulations, friend. This video is
> not for you.** Remember: **you're supposed to be lazy. You can't be not-lazy and lazy at the same time.**
> So **I applaud you for doing that — I'm doing that. I don't want to license someone else's program — but
> I'm in the grind, I'm working 14, sometimes 16 hours a day.**"*
>
> ✅ **Notable on two counts.** (a) A personal-brand teacher recommending that some people **rent someone
> else's brand and system** instead of building one. (b) **A dated self-report on his own hours** —
> *"14, sometimes 16 hours a day"* — which sits **against** the entire premise of the video and which he
> volunteers rather than hides.

### 8. ★★ Activate "Big Mo" — momentum

*"I'm not talking about my friend Mo, **I'm talking about momentum.**"* — *"once you **prime the pump** you
get the benefit of **the compound effect**… **momentum loves action.**"* The well metaphor: *"it's a lot of
effort to create enough pressure to drive the water up the pipe — **but once you're there, it's a very little
amount of effort for it to continue to flow.**"* Closed with **Newton's first law**: *"a body at rest tends
to stay at rest, and a body in motion tends to stay in motion **unless acted upon by a different force.**"*
