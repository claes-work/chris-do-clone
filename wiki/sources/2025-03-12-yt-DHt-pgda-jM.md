# Why Micromanagement Destroys Teams (And What Works Instead)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo, short and unusually dense (7KB).** No guests. Fully persona-eligible.
> ★★★ **Kept for a framework new to the corpus (the black sheep and the family roles), a second Kier
> McLaren maxim, and — quietly — an on-air confirmation of McLaren's death in Chris's own words.**

## ★★★ General or soldier — never both

✅ **The second dated telling of the captain/soldier problem**, in different words and with a sharper
diagnosis of *why* people misread leaders:

*"**It's commonly misinterpreted that when someone is in a leadership or executive role they're not good
listeners or good followers** — and that might be the case with many folks. **What happens is people get
really intimidated around them and they don't offer up new ideas or suggestions.**"*

> ★★★ *"**I can play two roles: I could be the general, or I can be the soldier.** The general leads, has a
> plan; the soldier executes the plan. ★★★ **Where I'm really uncomfortable in all of this is being a
> general-soldier.**"*

★★★ **The generational observation attached to it**, which he hedges rather than sharpens: *"a lot of —
especially younger types of people — **they like to make decisions together; everything's a co-creation,
everything's a collaboration. And there's a wonderful time and place for that.** But **when we're launching
new products and ideas I don't want it to be groupthink. I don't want it to be a series of compromises,
because no one's ever happy.**"* ✅ **Second attestation in eight days of *"a compromise is when no one's
happy"*** ([[2025-03-04-yt-2dY6MsSedKg]]) — **now settled phrasing, not an ad-lib.**

★★★ **What he actually wants, stated as a protocol**: *"**I like clarity and definition and clear
boundaries.** *Oh, you want to run this project? You take ownership over it… **tell me what my role is** and
I will do everything I can.* Or you can say *Chris, what do you want to do?* — and I want to say what I want
to do, **and then I expect the same: I'm going to fall in line and do exactly what you want to do.**"*

★★ **And the self-aware disclaimer**: *"**it might seem like someone so strongly opinionated as myself
wouldn't want to follow — and in fact I really do**, when I know someone's in charge."* With the sailing
example: *"**I don't know how to drive a speedboat**, so when I'm on that boat there's a captain — **tell me
where to pull the rope, tell me what to do, I'll scrub the deck. I'm super happy.**"*

## ★★★ The black sheep, and the family-roles framework (new to the corpus)

**Source, and it is a personal one**: *"I found a long time ago when **my wife and I were in a relationship
workshop** — they said that **the family usually has about five roles**, and they created avatars, the lion
or something. **I don't remember any of that** — but what I do remember and really stuck with me is that
**every family has a black sheep.**"*

> ★★★ *"**The black sheep is the saddest character, the most misunderstood and hated character within the
> family** — typically the rebel, the one who goes punk rock, gets a tattoo and the ear piercing, and seems
> to not do anything the family thinks is good. ★★★ **But the black sheep is a critical role to play,
> because what they do is they bring new ideas.**"*

★★★ **The island parable he uses to make the case** — the black sheep says *"the water source we all depend
on is drying up, we should get off this island"*, and is treated as *"casting doubt on the tribe leader…
they don't listen to this person. **But a few people go along with the black sheep**, and the black sheep
leaves and finds new, more plentiful, prosperous land… ★★★ **and then the old tribe dies.**"*

★★★ **And then he does the harder half — he defends the person the black sheep despises**: *"likewise, the
chief, the head of the family, winds up becoming **the historian — the person who preserves ideas, cultures
and customs.** Now **the black sheep looks at the chief saying *you're old-fashioned, you're out of touch*
— but what they don't realise is they bring, in corporate terminology, **institutional knowledge**: **we do
these things because this is how people don't die.** We learned that we don't eat these fruits, we stay away
from these animals."*

> ★★★ **The rule the whole thing exists to produce**: *"**what's unhealthy with families and with
> corporations is when we don't share those roles — when we don't move them around.** … **We trade roles and
> responsibilities, but we don't merge them together — because that's where it gets really, really
> messy.**"*

✅ **Note the structural symmetry with the general/soldier point**: both say *play a defined role, swap roles
over time, never occupy two at once.*

## ★★★ Kier McLaren, a second maxim — and a confirmation of his death

> ★★★ *"Something I learned from my mentor — **God rest his soul, Kier McLaren** — he would say something
> like: **you can tell me WHAT you want, you can tell me HOW to do it, but you can't tell me both.**"*

★★★ **Chris's gloss**: *"that kind of summarizes my whole feeling about it — **allow me the autonomy to
figure it out; or you lead, and I'll just follow what you do.**"*

> ⚠️✅ **A quiet but important record.** *"God rest his soul"* is **Chris's own on-air confirmation of Kier
> McLaren's death**, said in passing in March 2025 — corroborating the *"he since passed away"* statement of
> 2025-02-02 recorded on [[../entities/kier-mclaren]]. **Still no date is given and none is inferred.**

★★ **The domestic example he uses, told against himself**: *"my wife will often ask me *hey, I need you to
fix the computer*… **but she doesn't leave. She's not there to learn.** *You want to try this? What if we
click on that?* — and at a certain point **I went myself** [i.e. lost his patience]. ★★★ **If you knew how
to fix it, why don't you fix it? If you want me to fix it — let me fix it.**"*

## ★★ Let the person who knows, know

★★★ *"When I'm in a new town at a new restaurant and somebody is local, **I just let them order. Don't even
ask me. I put my entire faith and my health in your hands — you know what you're doing.**"* The contrast:
*"I always find it confusing when someone who's **never been to a town, never been to that restaurant,
never eaten this type of food** says *well, I'd like the wonton soup please* — **they go with the familiar,
and not what the person who's there would recommend.**"*

★★ **The self-check he offers**: *"if you find yourself in a situation like that, **stop and check in with
yourself: what am I doing here? I clearly don't want to lead — but I'm not letting the leader lead.**"*
★★★ And the precise thing he objects to: *"**what I don't like is someone to tell me *I want you to follow
my directions* — and then ask me *what would you like to do?*.**"*
