# Win More Clients With This Simple Pitch Hack

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do live with a small room**, workshopping participants' pitches. Participants are
> named in passing and are **context only**. ⚠️ **The closing ~40 seconds are spoken by someone else** — a
> colleague reflecting on Chris's advice in the third person (*"if you come here and Chris gives you a
> straight-shot answer…"*). **That passage is marked and fenced below; it is NOT Chris.**
>
> ★★★ **A landmark for one reason above the rest**: the **Kier McLaren pitch story told with numbers** —
> a **win-rate**, a **revenue figure**, and a **doubling** — which the corpus has previously had only in
> qualitative form.

## ★★★ The formula — "You know how…"

**The diagnosis first.** He polls the room for job titles — *"fractional CMO," "multimedia designer,"
"graphic designer," "teacher," "UX researcher," "motion designer," "brand designer"* — and takes each apart.
*"What's the problem with **multimedia designer**?"* — *"real broad."* — *"**Everything. That's the
problem.**"*

> ★★★ *"**If I don't understand what you do, I don't know how to say you can help me — therefore I don't
> know how to give you money.** Pretty straightforward. **Confusion and complexity are the enemy of
> understanding and retention:** ★★★ **if I don't understand what you do, I cannot remember what you do —
> and then I can't tell other people what you do.**"*

★★ **And the charge underneath it**: *"we're asking the other person who potentially can give us work **to
do a lot of thinking on our [behalf]**"* — *"**you guys love abstraction.**"* Plus the framing he opens
with: *"the mistake most people make on the elevator pitch is **it's just all you, and people tune out.**
**We have to go from *me* to *you*.**"*

### The formula, and the live rebuild

1. **Who do you help?** → then ★★★ **add an adjective**: not *"high school students"* but *"**creative**
   high school students."*
2. **What is a big problem, frustration or headache they have — that you solve?** → and crucially,
   ★★★ *"**what's the NEGATIVE version of that?**"* (*"give them purpose"* → *"**feel lost and
   directionless**"*).
3. **What is the result you produce?**

> ★★★ **The template**: *"**You know how [adjective] [avatar] [problem / how it feels]… I help them
> [result].**"*

**Worked live on a teacher.** Before: *"I teach digital multimedia design."* After:

> ★★★ *"**You know how creative high school students don't know what career direction to go, and feel lost
> and directionless? I help them find a career in art and help them get into the university of their
> choice.**"* — *"Oh, how do you do that?"* — *"**Well, I teach digital media design.**"*

★★★ **The mechanic that makes it work, and he makes the room feel it**: *"**you see, it's a conversation,
not a monologue.** … **you have to PAUSE. It's a conversation, we need to converse with each other.**"* He
then role-plays the payoff: *"I actually have a high school student myself — how do you help them?"* …
*"**I have a bunch of friends that need your help.**"*

> ★★★ **The compression rule, stated twice**: *"**One avatar, one problem, one result.**"* — *"**Do not
> confuse people by using complicated language or adding a lot of commas.**"*

★★ **On why the biography doesn't belong there**: *"you [don't] need to tell them all of your life story…
*I was born on a cold winter night, December 12th, I remember it clearly* — **nobody cares. It's too late.**
★★★ **They WILL care as soon as you tell them what's in it for them.**"*

★★ **And a memory constraint worth keeping**: *"**we only listen to like six or seven pitches** — not a lot,
[and it's] very hard to remember."*

> ⚠️⚠️ **FOURTH RECORDED INSTANCE OF THE SAME MISATTRIBUTION.** *"There's a book — **I think his name is
> Michael Porter** — it's called *Book Yourself Solid*… **he wants to kill the elevator pitch.**"*
> **The author is MICHAEL PORT.** The corpus has now caught this error **four times** on record
> (2025-02-23, and three earlier instances). **The correction is recorded; the error is not propagated.**
> ✅ The substance he attributes is right: Port offers versions at *"10 seconds, 30 seconds, a minute, five
> minutes and 10 minutes."*

## ★★★ The Kier McLaren pitch story — now with numbers

The corpus has this story qualitatively. **This telling supplies the figures.**

*"I worked with my business coach for 13 years — **he's passed away since.** The thing he told me to do
**that allowed me to double my revenue in one year — actually less than a year**…"*

> ⚠️✅ ***"He's passed away since"* is the THIRD on-air confirmation of Kier McLaren's death**, after
> 2025-02-02 (*"he since passed away"*) and 2025-03-12 (*"God rest his soul"*). **Three passing references
> across six weeks, all past tense. Still no date given, and none inferred.**

★★★ **The setup, and Chris's own starting position**: *"he says *how do you pitch new business?* — we work
for ad agencies. I told him. He says: **okay, that's not very good.** I said **I thought it was great** — at
this point **I'm doing $2.1 million in revenue every year.** He goes: **when do you ask the client a
question?** ★★★ **I thought I was supposed to just pitch.**"* His actual behaviour: *"at the end they ask
*what's your take on it* — and **what I do immediately is I start pitching my ideas**: we could do this, what
if we lens this, what if we cast these people, what if we grade it this way."*

★★★ **The coaching, and Chris's resistance to it**: *"he didn't literally say this, but he's like: **idiot,
how do you know what somebody wants without asking them? *What do you want? What inspired you? How will you
decide who you want to work with?*** ★★★ **And I said to him: Kier, I can't ask those kind of questions.
That's rude.** He goes: **do you want to try it or not?**"*

> ★★★ **THE NUMBERS — new to the corpus.** *"I tried it, and I swear to God — **two calls later I'm winning
> pitches left and right.** ★★★ **We went from winning 20% of the new business pitches to 75% plus.**"*
> `[self-reported]`

★★★ **The before-state, stated in emotional terms he doesn't usually use**: *"I went from feeling **really
depressed and demoralized** because we're only winning one out of five pitches… **we were leaving so much
money on the table with brands I wanted to work with** — to a point where **I felt like there's no pitch I
could not win.**"* And the scaling: *"**then I taught my team how to ask questions, and they were winning so
much I didn't go on the pitches anymore.**"*

★★★ **The counterfactual he runs on himself**: *"every time he told me to do something, **I just did it.**
**Imagine if Kier told me *ask your clients questions* and I waited 10 years to ask that question. Would I
be here? Would I still be in business? I don't think so.** ★ **I'd be in rehab or something… develop a
drinking habit.**"*

> ★★★ **And the most honest passage on the page — what losing pitches actually felt like**: *"**it sucks to
> pitch after work that you really want, for budgets you can only dream of, and for four out of five of
> them to say *no, not today, not you, someone else*** — and then you're like **why not me?** ★★★ **You know
> how I would console myself before I hired Kier? I'd say: at least we were considered. Look at all these
> amazing companies I admire — we're pitching against them.** ★★★ **That self-talk can only last so long
> before you want to take desperate measures.**"*

## ★★ Why people don't follow the formula

*"As long as I've been teaching — **20-plus years now** — **creative people don't like to follow formulas.**
They just would not follow formulas. Why? I do not know. ★★ **They like to just struggle and be poor.** If I
told you *do these next five things in this exact sequence and you'll be a millionaire*, they're like
**I want to do these two, but not those three.**"*

★★ **The Alex Hormozi anecdote he uses as corroboration** (recorded as **Chris relaying a third party**, not
as Hormozi's verified words): at a conference, asked by *"these young kids… *I want to become rich, Alex*"*,
Hormozi says *"I'll tell you what to do and you tell me if you're going to do it — **but I guarantee you if
you do it you'll make a million dollars**: **post content for a year, never pitch a single thing, do it
consistently every single day.**"* Response: *"**I don't want to do it.**"* Chris: *"**so you're not willing
to do [that] for one year to make a million dollars?**"*

## ★★ You cannot see your own problem

Asked how to find the one thing holding you back: *"**it's very difficult for you to be in your world and to
be able to see your own thing.** That's why **consultants and coaches and preachers, ministers, therapists,
friends, instructors** help you to see what you cannot see. **If you have high self-awareness and you sit
and think a lot about who you are, you can probably see it yourself** — [but] **it's very difficult,
especially if you've not been coached or trained for any period of time.**"*

★★ **With the commercially candid coda**: *"**which is good news for people like me — cuz I can see it. I
can see it almost instantly.**"*

## ⚠️ NOT CHRIS — the closing reflection

The final ~40 seconds are spoken by **another person in the room**, reflecting on Chris's method in the
third person. **Fenced; not persona data.** Recorded because it is a genuinely good observation about how
his advice is received:

> *"Just because your problem is not special, it doesn't mean that you're not special… if Chris gives you a
> straight-shot answer, what happens to a lot of us is we start going *okay, but here's why what you're
> saying doesn't apply to my situation*… **you are special, you are valuable — but your problems may not
> be.**"*

✅ **It rhymes with Chris's own *"a business problem is a business problem"*** ([[2025-02-26-yt-HtkujhkfHOM]])
— but **the words are not his and must not be attributed to him.**
