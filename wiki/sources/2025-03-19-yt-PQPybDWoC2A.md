> **Andrea Sampson** is a speaker coach and founder of Talk Boutique / Thought Leader Academy, formerly SVP

## ✅✅ The Carl Jung line — and why it matters

He brings it in himself, having found it that morning: *"I came across this [post] on my Instagram today —
and **how wonderful and serendipitous** is it that I see this today of all days. It's a quote from **Carl
Jung**: ★★★ **'To be normal is the ultimate aim of the unsuccessful.'**"* — *"**I love that.**"* ★ With a
small, honest hedge about attribution: *"**I hope you did say this**, because it's so brilliant."*

> ✅✅ **This sits directly on top of doctrine the corpus already holds.** *"Normal just means average… **to
> say it's bad would be better than to say it's average**"* (the superhuman parable,
> [[2025-02-23-yt-hC2_b-y6zuc]]) and the **Jung Persona/Shadow material already in `mindset` §75**. **This is
> the sentence his whole normal-is-average position has been circling** — and he encountered it *after*
> teaching the position, not before. **Promote it as a late-found articulation, not as its source.**

## ★★★ "What makes you weird as a kid" — credited, and he flags his own edit

★★★ *"This is my take on something that a friend of mine says or writes about — his name is **James
Victore**. He says **what makes you weird as a kid** — ★★★ **and I alter it a little bit — will make you
wonderful as an adult.** **We need to lean into that weirdness.**"*

> ✅ **Good attribution behaviour worth recording as behaviour**: he names the source, then **explicitly
> flags that the version he uses is his own alteration.** Contrast this with the *Book Yourself Solid*
> misattribution he has now made four times — **when he knows a source, he is careful with it.**

★★ **And the argument he attaches**: *"leaning into the things that make us different **when we're in a
society that wants to make us all the same** — **yes, it's problematic. And so that's why it's such an
unusual concept.**"*

## ★★★ How he interviews — the clearest external testimony in the corpus

The guest's closing, unprompted: *"Chris, this has been an absolute joy. ★★★ **You are an incredible
question asker** — and just very insightful."*

**What that actually looks like in the transcript**, and all three are recurring behaviours:

1. ★★★ **He plays the answer back to check it.** *"I want to say it to you **to see if I got this part
   right**"* → then a full restatement of her point, ending *"**is that about right?**"* He does this
   repeatedly, and it is how the episode's clearest passages get produced.
2. ★★★ **He asks for a role-play rather than a description.** *"**Let's role play a little bit** — just
   pretend I'm that person. **I'm the absent-minded professor**, I've got the two PhDs, I sit down with you
   — **what are some of the questions you might ask me** so that I can start to unearth these stories?"*
   ✅ The same instinct as his live-coaching and live-audit habits elsewhere in the corpus.
3. ★★ **He stops a story to make the audience follow it.** *"Wait, wait — **can you, before you move on**…
   because **some people might know exactly what we're talking about and the rest [are] like *wait, what's
   going on here*.**"* And: *"**I just need you to finish that story. The completionist in me.**"*

★★ **He also asks for the mechanism, not the anecdote** — after a long story he counts back: *"you listed
five things but **I couldn't write that fast** — are you creative enough, can you do it, do you want it, is
this important to you… **was there something else in there?**"*

## ★★ Chris's own contributions of substance

- ★★★ **He does the same thing she does, informally**: *"**I do this for my friends too.** You tell me
  their story and **it's dragging** — **they put too much detail where none is required, they skip over all
  the conflict [and] emotional turmoil** — and then I'm like: **okay, so here's your story the way I heard
  it**, and you say it back to them."* ★★ His read on why it works: *"**to be able to see themselves from
  another point of view** — you could describe that as **a form of meditation, but you're doing it with
  another person.**"*
- ★★★ **The framing he opens with**, which is a position: *"I am **deeply fascinated by story**… **we all
  should be very good at it. I think we WERE when we were kids** — and **somewhere along the way we've lost
  our ability to tell stories, and we get really self-conscious.**"* ✅ Consistent with **build a world, not
  a narrative** and the **character/want/obstacle** formula.
- ★★ **He extracts the pitch lesson in his own words** and gets it confirmed: *"in a new business pitch
  there are **finite constraints**… **so it isn't necessarily about coming up with the best creative** —
  **it's showing them that you want the business, that it's important to you, and that the creative team are
  willing to move heaven and earth for you.** **Here's just an example of what it would be like to work with
  us** — knowing very well that if you award us the account, we've got to do the real work."*
  ✅ **Directly relevant to his own pitch doctrine** ([[2025-03-13-yt-sLe5iNSyejk]]).
- ★★ **The loss-leader logic, named by him**: *"this is like **a loss leader**… **the jewel in the crown**:
  if you bring the big brand to your agency, then the other brands **chase after you at that point.**"*
- ★ **A small biographical note, repeated**: *"I for **a very brief period of my life** worked in
  advertising"* — ✅ consistent with the Cole & Weber period in `persona/biography.md`.
- ★ **Self-deprecation in passing**: *"I can see your brain is moving really fast too — and **as a
  not-fast-moving brain**, I just need you to finish that story."*

## ⚠️ Andrea Sampson's material — HERS, fenced, not persona data

Recorded so the ideas are attributable if they surface elsewhere. **None of it may enter `persona/`.**

- **What an advertising strategist actually does**: *"we're **the voice of the consumer inside the
  agency**"* — primary and secondary research synthesised down to *"two, maybe three insights, and an
  insight would be **nothing more than a sentence**"*, which becomes the creative brief. ★ Her aside:
  *"**insights are often boring — it's creative that actually brings them to life.** I always did the most
  boring part of the job, but without it you would never have creative. **We were the unsung heroes.**"*
- **The E*Trade Canada pitch story** (~2001), including the segmentation (*"get-rich-quicks"* vs *"aggressive
  affluent"*), the insight (*"they secretly desired to be like the get-rich-quick guy but weren't willing to
  take the risk"* → *"beat your broker"*), and the **$1.79 squeegee** punchline — a finished spot the agency
  president assumed had cost six figures.
- **Idea-centric storytelling**: *"any good TED Talk is **an idea that's wrapped in story**"* — listen for
  the *mechanism* inside the idea, then find a personal story that works as a metaphor for it, using details
  that are *"transcendent across time and space"*.
- **The tell-it-back technique**: after an hour or so, *"**I tell it back to them as if I am them**… **for
  the first time somebody understood them**, and **they've never heard their own story told back to
  them.**"*
- **Her own biography and disclosures** — 25 years in advertising, the decision at 50 (*"**it never felt
  like a crisis, it felt like an awakening**"*), the *"vibrant"* year, the hair-colour confrontation with an
  HR director, dropping out in grade 10, and **a neurodiversity disclosure**. ⚠️ **Hers, on her own terms.
  Recorded minimally, not to be elaborated or repurposed.**
- **Her clients' stories** — the geneticist, the São Paulo entrepreneur, the South Africa account.
  ⚠️⚠️ **These are third parties' personal tragedies, related by her. Recorded only by category, never
  retold.**
- ★ **Her line worth attributing to her**: *"**we teach what we most need to learn.**"* And her positioning
  answer: *"**it's actually your life and the context of your life that makes you different**… **it doesn't
  matter that a million other people are saying the same thing — the way you say it, because of who you are,
  means somebody else understands it in a way they never could have.**"*
