# What Successful Personal Brands Do Differently

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do live, day three of a multi-day workshop**, working a room directly. Participants
> answer, are named in passing, and are **context only**.
> ⚠️ **Two handling notes.** (1) **This is the abrasive register in operation** — he presses one participant
> hard to name something she is ashamed of, and tells the room *"I'm throwing up in my mouth right now"*
> about a stock interview answer. Recorded as it happened. (2) **Participants disclose personal material**
> — one describes a family breakdown and a religious experience. **Summarised minimally and respectfully;
> context only, never elaborated.**
>
> ★★★ **A landmark restatement of the personal-branding thesis** — the corpus already holds it (branding
> F52) — **but this telling adds four things it did not have**: ★★★ **"you to the power of you"**,
> ★★★ **the authenticity gap as a measurable thing**, ★★★ **the feedback-as-instructions definition with a
> reply template**, and ★★★ **Pixar's rule #1**.

## ★★★ The thesis, restated — and the church metaphor extended

*"A lot of the way people talk about personal branding just doesn't fit with the way I see the world…
they've all been going to **the same church of personal branding**, preaching from the Vatican — how to
market, how to do sales, **how to build a funnel**, how to get more clients; or **your colours and your
logotype and showing up consistently.** ★★★ **I don't believe in what they're doing.** … **I'm against
everything they talk about**, so I may be exiled from the church."* ★ The room supplies the escalation —
denomination → schism → sect → *"if they really don't like you, you're just straight up **cult**"* — which
he turns on himself: *"**some people do accuse us of being a cult. Yeah, we might be.**"*

> ★★★ **The core claim, unchanged and stated cleanly**: *"personal branding **isn't what you do**… **because
> you are more than what you do.** One day you were a student — does that mean you're a student? One day
> you're a graphic designer, another day an art director. **What you do changes over time, as it should**…
> **so the only thing that's left: it's not what you do, it's WHO YOU ARE.**"*

★★ **And why he thinks it matters**, in psychological terms: *"in psychology **the biggest breakthrough is
this thing called the self-concept**… **who am I and what makes me me.** And **you might not ever get to the
answer before you pass away** — you might be on your deathbed asking **what was my life about? Who am I
really?**"* ★ With an aside that is characteristic: *"**I wonder if humans are unique in the animal kingdom
in that we're the only creatures with an imagination who start to ask who we are.** I wonder if the tiger is
asking: *because I roar, am I a tiger?*"*

## ★★★ The infinite brand, and the authenticity gap

★★ **The definition he builds from**, credited to **Marty Neumeier** (*The Brand Gap*) — *"he literally
wrote the book on brand… **he's the godfather of brand**"*: *"**a brand isn't a logo** — that's part of it;
**it's not the product or the packaging**, although that's part of it; **it's not the ads or the PR.**
★★★ **It's a person's gut feeling** about a product, a service, or an organisation."* → *"**when enough
people have a similar gut feeling, then you have a brand.**"* ★ On the threshold: *"what that number is is
**kind of arbitrary, a little subjective** — **if 70% of you thought I was a certain way, that is my brand.
It could be good, it could be bad, and I'm okay with it either way.**"*

★★★ **Then the personal application, which is the new part.** He walks the room through who holds a gut
feeling about them — mother, father, sibling, grandparent, teacher, boss, employee, client — and asks:
*"**are they consistent? Are they all over the place?**"*

> ★★★ *"**You actually don't have one brand — you have an INFINITE brand.** It's like **the multiverse**:
> **there's an infinite number of versions of you depending on who shows up where.** Your pastor has a
> different version of you… **and if you're playing video games online you might have a whole different
> personality.**"*

★★ **His own example**: *"**my mom always looks at me like I'm a kid** no matter what — **I'm 52 years
old**, I'm still her little kid. **She even calls me my nickname as a kid: *small* or *tiny*.**"*
> ⚠️ **AGE DISCREPANCY, unresolved.** He says **52** here (2025-03-23) and **53** two days later
> ([[2025-03-25-yt-lXTf0ypur0Q]]). **Both are his, two days apart, and a 1972 birth year makes 53 the
> likelier figure — but neither is hardened.** Say *"early fifties."*

★★★ **The diagnosis, and then the measurement.** *"There's **this real you**, and there's **this person you
pretend to be to different people — to be loved, to be accepted, to be respected, to be liked.**"* His
picture of the real one: *"**the you that shows up when no one's watching** — you're in your underwear,
you're eating cold three-day-old pizza, **you haven't washed your hair and you're kind of ugly in that
moment** — physically, not inside. **That's the real you.**"*

> ★★★ **THE MEASURE — new to the corpus**: *"**the gap between these two points determines how real, how
> authentic you are.** Because **we're living this Photoshop life** — *I'm on vacation, happy marriage,
> perfect kids* — **that ain't real.** … **What we want to do is close this gap as small as possible. And
> how small is up to each one of us** — our own level of comfort, **the inner work we've done.**"*
> ★★ With the honest limit: *"**I'm not saying go out and be your unfiltered self all the time** — I'm not
> even suggesting it's possible for that person to show up all the time.**"*

★★★ **The contradiction he names in one phrase**: most people post to *"build a genuine, meaningful
connection with a bunch of strangers — **it's how we build trust at scale**"* — but what they actually put
out is ★★★ *"**honest. Inauthentic. Not genuine.**"*

> ★★★ **THE NEW FORMULATION**: *"to be different you just need to be you; **to stand out** you just need to
> be you; **to be memorable** you just need to be you. So I have this idea — ★★★ **you to the power of
> you.** **The more you are, the more powerful you become — on an exponential scale.** **The problem is most
> of us are pretending to be someone else.**"* ★ He credits the seed of it to someone in the room (*"I think
> Jason had said this"*).

## ★★★ Praise, criticism, and a reply template

✅ **The praise-is-a-trap doctrine already in the corpus, but with two additions.**

★★★ **The definition, which is the addition**: *"**feedback is a response to something I've put out and
you're opining about it** — that's just an opinion. **But feedback, or criticism, especially if it's well
articulated, [is] DETAILED INSTRUCTIONS ON HOW TO SUCCEED.**"*

★★★ **The reframe stated flatly**: *"**the reason why I am where I am is because I love criticism. I
actually don't like praise so much**… *you're amazing, your work is supreme* — **where do you go from that?
How do you grow?**"* And the mechanism: *"**it's like a really weird drug** — the first high is amazing, the
second is not as good, the third is less and less — **and now you're addicted and you need it so bad.**"*
★★ From coaching: *"I'll go through critical feedback [and] they're like **now tell me what I did good** —
**I don't think you heard the 17 things I said on how to improve.** ★★★ **Why would you ask for a compliment
right now? Is it meaningful to you? Does it help you right now?**"*

★★★ **The worked example, on himself.** He distinguishes noise (*"don't trust anybody who wears a baseball
cap — **sure. I don't know what to do with that. That's not instructions on how to succeed**"*) from usable
criticism: *"**I don't really like your tone, Chris. You're a pompous a-hole.** — Is that criticism? Is that
feedback? ★★★ **See, I LOVE that** — because **my intention wasn't to show up as a pompous a-hole.** I need
to **check in with myself on my tone** sometimes. **I need to be a little softer from time to time. I don't
want to be that person.**"*

> ★★★ **THE REPLY TEMPLATE — new, and portable**: *"even if it's the meanest feedback, **I just type in:
> *thank you for the feedback.*** **I'm not saying it's good, I'm not saying it's bad — but thank you for
> giving me a point of information. I will do with it what I need.**"*

★★★ **And the 99-to-1 problem, with a diagnosis**: *"what happens when you read 99 pieces of positive
feedback and you get **one a-hole dropping a bomb** on your face? **It sticks out.** Why? ★★★ **Because
that's the one connection you didn't make** — and that was what you were trying to do."* Then: *"**we're so
unsure of ourselves, we don't know who we are**, that one comment can leave you asking **am I really that
person? Am I insensitive?** ★★★ **And that's why we tell our story — so we know: no, that is NOT consistent
with who I know I am.**"* ★★ With a live example of the boundary: *"somebody will say **Chris, this is not
in alignment with your mission** — bro, **I wrote my mission. I know what my mission is. Who are you to tell
me that?**"*

✅ **Also here**: *"**every failure is an opportunity in disguise** — it's a lesson, and **it's the tuition
you pay for success.**"*

## ★★★ Why we tell stories — and the three stories again

★★★ **Credited to Vinh Giang** (*"a buddy of mine… an Aussie, he does a lot of communications and
storytelling"*), and he treats it as better than the room's answer: *"**we tell stories to remind ourselves
of who we are — so when we're lost, we remember.**"* ★★ The room's answer was *connection* and *to feel
heard*, and he corrects the ordering rather than dismissing it: *"**the connection comes first, before being
heard.**"* ★★ And his own version: *"when we tell our story, **other people get to identify with you**…
★★★ **the story is a shorthand for us to build instant connection.**"* ✅ Illustrated with the same
South-Bronx keynote speaker as [[2025-03-04-yt-2dY6MsSedKg]], and the same term: **cultural currency**.

✅ **The three stories, second attestation in 19 days** — now with a **Spider-Man** worked example:
1. **Origin story** — parents, birthplace, birth order, siblings, their education. *"If I say **both my
   parents are professors who taught philosophy**, you know something. If I say **my parents escaped from
   Vietnam and started over in the United States** — **that's a different story.**"*
2. **Inciting incident** — *"the moment your world changes forever. **We'll call it the spider bite.**"*
3. **Defining moment** — *"now you know who you're going to be for the rest of your life… **for Spider-Man
   it's when Uncle Ben dies.**"* ★★ With an age note: *"some of you have hit that; younger people probably
   not. **If you're above 30 or 40 you probably have that already.**"*

★★★ **The live edit of a participant's origin story is the most useful part** — he takes *"I grew up on a
small farm in Eastern Washington, very cold, very snowy"* and pushes: *"**how many stoplights were there in
your small town?**"* — zero — *"**don't you think that would be interesting to mention?** **I grew up in a
town with zero stoplights. My high school was 50 kids.** Now we know how small it is."* → ★★★ **the rule**:
*"**specific details communicate much faster.**"* And on the cold: *"**give us a really weird fact** — *so
cold that you do not want to put your tongue on anything metal outside.*"*

★★ **He also corrects story SHAPE, not just content** — told that a participant's inciting incident and
defining moment were *"within months"* of each other: *"**usually these things are pretty far apart** —
**we want you to be lost in the desert for a while**, we want you to **suffer through trials and
tribulations**, and then finally the answer is revealed. [Otherwise] **it's not a very interesting
story.**"* ⚠️ **Note what this is**: a craft judgement about narrative, applied to someone's real life,
delivered bluntly. **Recorded as said.**

## ★★★ Pixar's rule #1 — new to the corpus

> ★★★ *"**Pixar — the most successful film studio of all time** — they know a thing or two about making
> stories. **Rule number one: we admire characters more for their struggles than for their success.**
> **If you need to write that down** — we admire characters more for their struggles than for their
> success."*

★★★ **Worked through *Toy Story***: Woody is *"**selfish, jealous** — he was number one on Andy's playlist,
then Buzz Lightyear comes in… **and so he tries to sabotage Buzz.** ★★★ **He's a flawed character, and we
love him MORE because he reminds us of ourselves** — because **we have felt petty, we have felt jealous.**"*
And the inverse: *"if we're all *yes, everything's great, I know how to accept new friends, it's great to be
number two* — **we're like: who's that? Why are you so perfect? …And we feel too distant.**"*

★★★ **Then he turns it on the room's feeds**: *"when you post on social, **what trials and tribulations are
you talking about? Vacation. Good stuff. Your Gucci bag. Your fancy watch. No struggles.** ★★★ **Why would I
care about you? It looks like you're just flexing.**"*

★★★ **And the systemic version, which is the best phrase in the source**: *"we emulate those we think are
successful — so there's this **weird echo chamber of fakeness**. They put out something fake, and it's a
★★★ **nuclear arms race on fakery**: *I'll put out something fake*, and somebody says *no, I can out-fake
you*. **So we spiral out of control — we buy clothes we can't afford, drive cars we shouldn't be driving,
stretching our budgets to the maximum — instead of doing the deep personal work.**"* ★★ Plus the second
charge: *"**the other sin is that almost all of your content is advertising.** You're marketing, you're
selling your services all the time — **it gets really boring.**"*

## ★★ The name, as the carrier of a brand

*"**What is the first thing anybody learns about a brand? The name.** … ★★★ **A name is the most portable,
efficient delivery vehicle for a brand** — **all the goodness, the feelings, the sentiment, the gut**, you
have to have a name [for]. **That's why people trademark their brand name — because they work so hard**
[for it]."* ★ With the historical sketch he begins: before brands, a local grocer could vouch personally;
as towns grew, that stopped scaling. ⚠️ **The transcript cuts off mid-example.**
