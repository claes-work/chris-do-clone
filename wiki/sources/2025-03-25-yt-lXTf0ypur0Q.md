# 40 Brutal Business Truths I Wish I Knew in My 20s

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo, scripted, 40 items in 22 minutes.** No guests. Fully persona-eligible.
>
> ⚠️ **Format caution: this is a COMPENDIUM.** Most of the 40 are compressed restatements of doctrine the
> corpus already holds in fuller form. **The value here is (a) the ~12 items that are genuinely new or newly
> sharp, and (b) the compressed phrasings themselves**, which are the most quotable versions of several
> positions. **Do not let the compendium overwrite the fuller tellings** — it supplements them.

**Framing claims** `[self-reported]`: *"I've been in business for **over 30 years** and **I've built two
eight-figure businesses**."* ⚠️ **A new self-report** — the corpus has Blind's *"$80M lifetime billings"*
and The Futur separately; **this is the first time he characterises both as eight-figure businesses.**
Recorded as stated, not reconciled.

## ★★★ Genuinely new, or newly sharp

- ★★★ **Practice intentional incompetence.** *"**Don't get good at things that you don't want to get good
  at doing.** In my own life **I try not to get good at production work, or bidding, or accounting** —
  because that's not what I want to get good at."* ✅ The active form of the delegation doctrine: not merely
  *delegate*, but **deliberately refuse to acquire the skill.**
- ★★★ **Don't get stuck in the tyranny of the how.** *"Don't ask **how can we afford this, how can I get
  someone to do this work, how will we figure out the technical challenge** — because **when you do that you
  eliminate ideas prematurely.**"* ★★ With the teaching evidence: *"my former design students would
  **generate ideas that were within the boundaries of what they knew how to do**, therefore limiting the
  potential growth."*
- ★★★ **Be flexible with the plan, but not the goal.** *"Be **super rigid** about where you see yourself in
  five or ten years, but **be willing to change the course** — **the worst thing you can do is be wed to
  your plan when the evidence is telling you to change.**"*
- ★★★ **Don't manage time — manage outcomes.** *"When you manage time, all you're doing is **watching what
  people do, not the results you want** — and **time is not a good measurement of productivity, efficiency,
  creativity or innovation.** … **If you promote people for working longer, guess what: they'll just work
  longer.**"*
- ★★★ **Sales is change management.** *"What people pay money for is **to go from the current state to a
  desired future state.** So in sales you need to understand **where they are and where they want to go** —
  **and if you have a plan on how to do that, that's what you're selling.**"* ✅ Sharpens the
  *desired-future-state* material already in the corpus into a one-line definition of the job.
- ★★★ **Sell before you build.** *"Build the **MVP — just a sales page** — to see if people are interested…
  **and if you have enough interest, that gives you excitement, momentum, and most importantly capital** to
  go and produce the thing."* The failure it prevents: *"entrepreneurs **refine and produce something so
  it's a beautiful shiny diamond**, put it on the market, and **no one wants to buy it.**"*
- ★★★ **Sell how you buy.** *"I see people **emulating things that are super aggressive and predatory** —
  why would you do that? **Just look at how YOU personally make decisions, who you trust, and where you
  spend money. Reverse-engineer that and do that with other people.** **It's consistent with your values,
  and I promise you it's way more effective.**"*
- ★★★ **The three-word findability test — concrete and new.** *"**Try to Google yourself without using your
  company name or your name** and see how difficult it is to find you. Chances are **you don't even rank in
  the top 10.** Now: **find a three-word combination that describes generally what you do** — and **when the
  top-10 results are all the people you want to compete against, you know you found the right three
  words.** Then **use those three words to seed most of the content you post**… in **two to three months
  you'll rank in the top 10 organically.**"* With the framing: *"**you don't have to compete against
  everybody — just the ones that are going to take money off your table.**"*
- ★★★ **The client is more afraid of you than you are of them.** *"When they reached out, **you are
  theoretically the expert at a very specific niche — they run a broad business.** So on the phone
  **they're not going to disclose information — not because they're trying to manipulate you, [but] because
  they're afraid of sounding stupid.** ★★★ **What you need to do is give them permission to sound stupid** —
  and **speak the client's language, not industry jargon.** **The last thing you want is to impress them
  with your knowledge, because that's only going to make them feel dumber** and less likely to disclose
  valuable information."*
- ★★★ **Sometimes the client is right.** *"**The ego of the creative says we must always be right and all
  client suggestions are dumb. That's not the case at all.** … ★★★ **The clients will forget more about
  their business than you hope to learn about it** — and **it's arrogant for us to assume we know better
  than the client how to run a bakery, a print shop, a technical firm.**"*
- ★★★ **Say the price FIRST when you know what you're doing — LAST when you don't.** A real refinement of
  the anchoring doctrine, with the reason: *"say the price first when you know what you're doing — **that
  anchors the client at a higher price so they don't have the opportunity to lowball you.** ★★★ **But the
  caveat: when you DON'T know what you're doing, you're going to shoot yourself in the foot by saying a
  price you think is really high when in truth the price is much, much higher.**"* The worked contrast: a
  logo designer of ten years quoting their own known range, versus an unfamiliar sponsorship offer where
  *"you could say **how about $3,000** — when in fact **they have [tens of thousands] to pay people just
  like you.**"*
- ★★★ **Don't let the fear of firing someone be the reason you're not hiring** — and he says it is his own
  history: *"**I did this myself for years. I toiled, doing all the work myself, staying up late, feeling
  burnt out** — because **I was really afraid of looking at another human being and saying: I control your
  livelihood.** Of course that's not true — **but that's the reason why I didn't hire people.**"*
- ★★★ **Good is the price of entry.** *"**Good is expected.** Instead of being good, **focus on being
  different — because different is better than better.**"*
- ★★★ **Create how you consume.** *"You enjoy a certain type of content, you like it, share it, save it,
  bookmark it — **and then you turn around and create this super annoying, super salesy direct-marketing
  content. What gives?** **Study the things you like and try to do more of that. Put more good into the
  world instead of creating more noise.**"*
- ★★★ **You want better clients? Charge more** — with a reason that isn't about status: *"people who pay
  more value things more — **and the thing they value most is their own time.** So **they're not going to
  sit there and micromanage you.**"* ★★ And the correction to the usual objection: *"the common mistake is
  thinking **that by charging more you have to create a totally different product. It's literally the same
  thing** — you're just doing it for a higher-end clientele."*
- ★★★ **Being needy is repulsive.** *"Wanting it too much **repels people** — they're thinking **maybe this
  person has no business**, or **maybe I should be suspicious as to why they're willing to give so much of
  themselves.** **Map this to personal relationships and you know what I'm talking about** — that needy ex
  who's always hanging on you, who needs to be complimented, who needs to be affirmed."*
- ★★★ **Sunk-cost bias in the pitch.** *"You'll have a meeting, then a call, then **write up a beautiful
  proposal**, maybe **do some spec work** — and then the client says **we have half the budget**… **the only
  way I could recover my cost here is to do this work for less money.** **It's the money pit.**"*
- ★★★ **Price the client, not the job.** *"Different clients have different expectations and different value
  systems, and **price, or fairness, or beauty, is very subjective.**"*
- ★★ **Culture equals brand**, credited to **Tony Hsieh** (*Delivering Happiness*): *"forget about branding,
  **get the culture right — because when the culture is not aligned with the brand, who cares what you
  say?**"* The illustration: meet a few people from a company at a bar and *"they're total toxic bro
  energy — you're going to say **that company is a bunch of toxic bros.**"*
- ★★ **Get paid up front.** *"**Do not finance your client's projects. That is not your business.**"*
  Minimum **50%** — and the confession: *"**don't do what I've done in the past** — don't start the project
  so eager to do the work and then find out later they changed their mind… **you have no one to blame but
  yourself.** ★★ **And people change their mind all the time. You know how I know? Because you do it
  too.**"* ✅ Consistent with *permission to change your mind* (2025-02-23).
- ★★ **Build in public**, via the Hollywood model — teaser → trailer — *"because **what's really scary is
  launching something and not knowing if anyone's going to show up.**"*

## ★★ Compressed restatements of existing doctrine (kept for phrasing)

- ★★★ *"**Work hard at your job and you can make a living. Work hard on yourself and you can make a
  fortune.**"*
- ★★★ **Passionately detached** — *"be passionate about the things you do, **and the minute you're done, be
  detached from the outcomes.** **Attachment and expectations are a source of a lot of disappointment.**"*
- ★★ **Your only competition is the old you**; *"comparison is the thief of joy."*
- ★★ **Act as if**, with *"**words shape world**"* ✅ (a recurring formulation).
- ★★ **Don't be the bottleneck** — *"all the roads will lead to you… **you eliminate everyone's ability to
  think for themselves.**"*
- ★★ **Keep the main thing the main thing** — *"shiny object syndrome, squirrel."*
- ★★ **Never justify**, with the scripts: *"why is the price this much? — **because it is.** Why does it
  take this long? — **because it takes that long to do good work.** Why should I hire you? — **I don't know.
  Why do you hire anyone?**"* ✅ The compressed version of `pricing` §52.
- ★★ **Anchoring** as a named cognitive bias — *"**even when we know it's working, we can't help but believe
  it to be true.**"*
- ★★ **Don't make people think**, credited to **Donald Miller**: *"**if you confuse, you lose**"* —
  ✅ and *"confusion [and] complexity is the enemy of understanding [and] retention"*, **verbatim the same
  line as twelve days earlier** ([[2025-03-13-yt-sLe5iNSyejk]]). **Settled phrasing.**
- ★★ **Know, like and trust** — *"**the first and most important part of this equation is GET KNOWN. That's
  the business that you're in.**"*
- ★★ **Quantify your goals** — *"unclear goals, unclear results"*, framed as **success metrics** and as the
  raw material for case studies.
- ★★ **Start at the end**, credited to **Brian Tracy** — imagine the completed call, date, or project, *"and
  then **you build the road towards that ending.**"* ✅ Already in the corpus; the public-speaking
  application is the clearest version.
- ★★ **A-players hire A-players**, credited here to **Guy Kawasaki** — *"**B players hire C or D players
  because they don't want to be outshined.**"* ⚠️ The corpus elsewhere attributes this to Silicon Valley
  generally; **record the Kawasaki credit as his, without asserting origin.**
- ★★ **A good entrepreneur is a good teacher** — *"they understand their own process, understand what
  excellence looks like, **and can break it down into bite-sized pieces**"* ✅ the *entrepreneur = educator*
  position.
- ★★ **Do as you're instructed** — *"**don't try to modify or change what your coach, your therapist, your
  teacher says**… amateurs take the instruction in and go *yeah, that kind of works for you, but I'm going
  to change everything* — **and then you didn't learn the lesson, and what you did was a piece of crap
  anyway.**"* ✅ The compressed mentorship doctrine (2025-02-23, 2025-03-06).
- ★★ **Successful entrepreneurs spend money to save time; struggling entrepreneurs spend time to save
  money**, credited to **Allan Dib** — *"you may be the person who **drives across town to wait in a long
  line to save $4.**"* ✅ Pairs with *time is precious, non-renewable and perishable*.
- ★★ **Two types of people: those who run from fear and those who run towards it** — *"**fear is usually an
  indicator of something that you really need to do.**"*

## ★★★ Two more Kier McLaren maxims

- ★★★ **"Say what you think."** *"**Biggest lesson I learned from my business mentor.** We're stressed
  thinking about how the client will respond, or how our employees will feel… **the best way to release the
  stress is just to say what you think.**"* ★★ **With the half he flags as unfinished**: *"the second part
  is **to learn how to say it in an objective, non-violent way** — **but that's a story for another
  day.**"*
- ★★★ **"Don't be the world's best kept secret."** *"Many years ago my business coach told me: **you're so
  busy doing the great work that no one knows about — how is anybody ever going to find you?** And **my
  introverted self was freaking out**: what do you mean? He goes: **do public speaking, go talk to clients,
  go to dinners — because your network is your net worth. You've got to get out there, kid.**"* ★★★ *"I took
  his advice, and years later **I can't think of a better piece of advice.**"*
  ✅ **The origin of the public-speaking push already recorded in `persona/biography.md`.**
