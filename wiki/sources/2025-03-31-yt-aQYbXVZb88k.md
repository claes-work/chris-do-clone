# Building a $120,000 Business for a Stranger in 18 Mins (Whiteboard Session)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do live at a whiteboard**, coaching named participants in a room. Participants are
> **context only**. Fully persona-eligible.
> ⚠️ **Contains a sponsor read** (Gusto) with an embedded anecdote — **marked below**, because the anecdote
> is real and useful but arrives inside paid placement.
>
> ★★★ **A landmark for how compact it is**: he takes a stranger's stated goal and builds the whole business
> model on a whiteboard, ending on ***"sell money."***

## ★★★ The arithmetic — and why it's divide-by-ten

*"I want to make **$120,000** as a graphic designer."* — *"**I'll teach you this. It's so, so easy.** If you
are skilled in what you do, you've trained, you've practiced your 10,000 hours, you're a good person —
★★ **I can teach you how to make a million dollars. 120 is easy.**"*

1. **$120,000 ÷ 10 = $12,000/month.** ★★★ **Not twelve, and for two reasons**: *"**because I want to give
   you two months off. It's important. We have to design our business to be long-term sustainable.**"* And,
   drily: *"**also because it's designer math. Divide by 12, your brain breaks. Divide by 10, you just move
   the decimal around.**"*
2. **How many active clients or projects per month?** → three. *"That's a good number. **It's not that
   hard.**"*
3. **$12,000 ÷ 3 = $4,000 per client per month.** → ★★★ *"**So that means you have to find something you can
   do that someone would find great joy in giving you their money for.** It could be $40K, it could be
   $400K — **I don't care what the number is.**"*

## ★★★ "Sell money"

> ★★★ *"What should you be selling? **Results.** … Here's what you should be selling — **when you sell this,
> they will buy it non-stop.** You ready? ★★★ **Sell money. When you sell money, I buy.** **Speak to me in
> the language of business.**"*

★★★ **And the positioning move he builds from it**, which is the most portable thing on the page: *"**I
would love to work with you — under the condition that you can articulate to me the metric that matters,
that I have an impact on, and what goal you'd like to have. Otherwise, I cannot help you.**"* Then the
observation: *"**even if everything else you do is exactly the same, look how you position yourself against
every other graphic designer, every other photographer, every other web developer.**"*

★★★ **With the honesty clause attached, credited to the Four Agreements**: *"you have to **be honest with
them, to serve them**, [and] say **that's a baseline or benchmark I cannot do.** Having a better visual
identity, a different website or head shot **is not going to achieve that for you.** What might is this
other thing — **I think you need to talk to [someone else]. I'm not your person.** But if you ever need
this, I can do that. ★★★ **That's a real relationship.**"*

## ★★★ The two B's — baseline and benchmark

★★★ *"One thing you need to add to your conversation with a client is what I call **the two B's.** The first
is a **BASELINE — where are we today?** How many followers? How much revenue? How many customers? What's the
churn rate? How many unsatisfied customers? How many positive Yelp reviews? … The second is **BENCHMARK —
where would we like to be?** ★★★ **The delta between those two determines how good you are.**"*

★★ **Worked**: *"if they go from 1,000 followers to 2,000, that's a 2× change. **But if they wanted 4,000,
you're 50% of the goal.**"* And on a restaurant client: *"they have 80 customers per every so many hours —
and **because the menu you designed gets people in and out faster**… **you've helped them make that much
more money because the menu improved.**"*

> ★★★ **The rebuke that sets it up, delivered to a designer who didn't know his client's revenue**: *"**you
> don't ask your clients how much money you make?** ★★★ **Why is it designers never care about the impact
> that the work makes? They just make it up in their mind.**"*
>
> ★★★ **And the asymmetry he names**: *"**we love to claim success for things. When things don't work, we're
> like: we got nothing to do with that.** **You notice that weird relationship we have?**"*

★★★ **The closing pair**: *"**design matters — but not when you don't mind what matters**"* and *"**what's
measured improves.** … **You didn't measure it, it doesn't improve.**"*

## ★★★ The subscription pivot — sell pain relief, and find the arbitrage

**The problem he names with project work**: *"every time you deliver on this $4K project, **you've got to go
find another one of these, and another one of these. It's tiring.**"* And why identity work resists
recurring revenue: *"**once it's done, it's kind of done. By design it's supposed to be done for years** —
if you're good, that's what we promise people."*

★★★ **So change who you serve**: *"who has **a never-ending, infinite appetite for video content**?"* →
**creators** — *"they want to make content because **content equals attention**, and what do they do with
attention? **They trade it for money.** ★★★ **So you want to sell money. Who's buying money? Creators.**"*

★★★ **And change what you sell — from production to pain relief**: *"the worst part is editing. **You can
shoot a video for an hour, [and] spend 17 hours editing that stupid thing.** What you want to do is **sell
pain relief** — we have a headache, we take a pill. ★★★ **The faster the pain relief comes, the more they
will pay.**"* Instead of production, the offer becomes *"editing, thumbnail, title, uploading, optimisation,
analytics"* — and the analytics are there **because they close the loop back to the two B's**.

★★★ **The economics, named**: *"**it could be done by anyone, anywhere, anytime, on any device. That means
you have ARBITRAGE.** … **Buy low, sell high.** I can buy this for relatively not a lot, and sell it for
what the market value is where I'm at. ★★★ **It's how people get rich.**"*

★★ **Why production resists this** — two reasons he gives: clients *"look at this as **an expense**"* (*"why
do we have to pay for all these people? I don't need craft services… **just use your phone**"*), **and it is
genuinely expensive to make** (*"you've got to lug your gear around, hire additional hands, cameras break,
memory cards get lost"*).

## ★★★ Entrepreneurship as a disease

Diagnosing a 28-year-old three years in — year one $70K, year two down — he first normalises it: *"**we have
doubt because we haven't achieved the goal in the timeline we've set for ourselves.**"* Then:

> ★★★ *"Everybody thinks entrepreneurship means **I get to be the boss, I don't have to work, I just call
> the shots** and I just make money over money over money. ★★★ **Entrepreneurship is an ugly, long battle.**
> **But it's a disease. I think it's not a choice. You can't get rid of it. There's no cure.**"*

★★★ **His own version of it**: *"**I cannot work for anybody.** I already know this about myself. **There's
not enough money you can pay me for me to work for you** — cuz **if I'm busy building your dream, who's
building mine? Nobody.** ★★★ **And I'm a dream builder. And I like my dream more than yours. I like yours,
but I like mine more.**"*

> ⚠️★★★ **The competitive candour, recorded as said**: *"**the people who don't have the disease, they
> quit. And I don't want so much competition. I want [you] to break.**"* — immediately alongside *"**it's
> okay if you quit. I appreciate if you quit most of the time. But I'm here for those that do not want to
> quit — they've got that fight, and I'm ready.**"* ✅ **Consistent with the *"get out — this was not meant
> for you"* passion soapbox** ([[2025-02-23-yt-hC2_b-y6zuc]]), and **the two halves must travel together.**

★★ **A dated marker**: *"**I'm going to year 30 right now.** And we've had ups and downs."*
★★ **The film line he says stuck with him**, from *Desperate Measures* — Michael Keaton crawling out through
a sewer: *"**you don't know nothing about dedication. Do you know where you end and I begin?**"*

## ★★★ The obstacle is the opportunity — and Jim Rohn's winter

★★★ **He inverts the cliché**: *"**the obstacle is the opportunity.** Why are opportunities obstacles?
Because — *would you like to work on NFTs?* **Those are called opportunities. In my world they're called
DISTRACTIONS.** They take you away from your primary goal. **If opportunities are a distraction — especially
for entrepreneurs, who are mostly ADHD — then the obstacle is forcing you to say: do I want to do this?**"*

★★ **The reframe he coaches live**: *"finish this phrase for me — **how might being down 30% in revenue be a
BENEFIT to you?**"* → *"reassess your clientele… reassess your overall strategy."* → *"**can you hear that?
It's a moment for reflection.**"*

> ★★★ **Then the kindest framework in the source, credited to Jim Rohn** (*Seven Strategies for Wealth and
> Happiness*): *"there are four seasons, but the one we're in is **the winter season.** When we lived a
> simpler life, **winter was a harsh time — we worked all year to store food and prepare for it.** So
> **winter is a time for family, for reflection, for rest, and to think.** … ★★★ **It could be that you're
> just in the winter of your life.** **But what many of you are doing is driving yourself crazy thinking
> it's spring** — time to plant and harvest. ★★★ **This is a time to rest, to reflect, to recover, and to
> spend this free time being grateful.**"*

## ★★★ The vacation disclosure

> ★★★ *"**I always know this about myself: the times in which I'm the most bored out of my mind are the
> times in which I have the best ideas.** It's also a time when I'm like — **you know what, these four
> people who work for me are amazing. These two, not so much.** ★★★ **I always warn my team: when I go on
> vacation, somebody will be fired. Almost always.**"*

★★★ **The mechanism he gives, and it is about habit rather than performance**: *"you have a relationship
with somebody… **we don't love them, but we're familiar with them, and it's a habit** — and we tell
ourselves: **if I fire this person, I've got to find someone to replace them. What a pain. What if they're
worse?** … **so you stick with them for a really long time.** ★★★ **What happens when I go on vacation is
I'm thinking about it much more objectively: oh my gosh, I could replace you right now — and I'd rather deal
with the pain of replacing you than stay with you.**"* ★★ With the other half, which he does not skip:
*"**and these other four people who do an amazing job — I need to take better care of them.**"*

> ⚠️⚠️ **And he extends it to romantic relationships, unprompted**: *"**that's why many of you in
> relationships with your partner — it's not ideal.** I'm not trying to break up your partnership, **but you
> need to get apart. You really do. Because somebody else is better for you, and you're better for someone
> else.**" **Recorded as said. This is an unusually blunt piece of advice delivered to a room of strangers
> about their private lives, and it should not be smoothed over or quietly dropped.**

## ⚠️★★ Inside the sponsor read — the Aaron story

⚠️ **This anecdote sits inside a Gusto placement**, so its framing serves the sponsor. **The story itself is
his and is recorded; the placement is flagged.**

A friend, **Aaron**, a solo designer from *"a very rural part of Montana"* who *"didn't even have indoor
plumbing for a large portion of his life"*, refused for years to hire — *"**I just enjoy doing the work
myself**"*, plus a limiting belief: *"**if he didn't want to do the grunt work, who else would want to do
it?**"* Chris argued the case for delegation and lost: *"**he's a stubborn guy. I didn't admire that about
him.**" Then *"through a series of unfortunate events he lost most of his major clients and hit a low
point"*, rethought the model, and hired one junior straight out of school — who *"under his direction grew
rapidly"*, then two, then several. At lunch years later he was *"**doing the best work he had ever done,
while working less than ever before, and making more money than he ever thought possible.**"*
★★★ **The line that lands it**: *"**essentially, he was doing everything I tried to convince him to do —
just 10 years after the fact.**"*
