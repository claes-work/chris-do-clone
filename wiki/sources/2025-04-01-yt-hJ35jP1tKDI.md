## ★★★ 1. Communication — and the non-violent thread completed

★★★ **The diagnosis, and it defines the word he has been circling for weeks**: *"most of us are not speaking
with great intention. We're using **abstract, unclear language**, and we're speaking often times **in violent
ways.** ★★★ **I don't mean *I hate you, I want to kill you* — but violent in that we're PREJUDGING, we're
PRE-DIAGNOSING, and we're making a lot of assumptions.**"*

★★★ **The prescription**: *"learn to **speak clearly, enunciate your words, speak in a calm, slow and
deliberate way** so that when you say something, **people lean in instead of roll their eyes.** … Master
**objective, non-violent expression and communication**: ★★★ **strip out all the opinions and try to talk
about things for the way they are, not the way that you might see them.** ★★★ **Do this especially when
you're under pressure — that's when it's most difficult to do.**"* Plus: *"**it's not just what you say,
it's how you say it. The tone, the intentionality behind the words, actually carry more meaning than the
words themselves.**"*

> ✅✅ **This is the fourth consecutive appearance of the non-violent thread and the fullest definition of
> it.** 2025-03-25 gave Kier's *"say what you think"* and flagged the missing half; 2025-03-29 supplied
> *"use less violent language"*; **this defines what *violent* means** — prejudging, pre-diagnosing,
> assuming — **and adds the under-pressure test.** **Promote the whole thread as one entry.**

## ★★★ 2. Listening — and the body cannot lie

*"People do this the wrong way by **listening to respond**… instead of **listening to understand.** It takes
a lot of energy to listen to **what the words mean, the way it's being delivered, and the body language** —
**there are three tiers of listening.**"* Why it pays: *"when you feel like someone's really listening to
you, **it starts to develop feelings of trust and likability**"*, and *"**often people don't say what they
really mean** — so it's your job to listen to **surface the deeper motivations** or **resolve conflicting
things.**"*

> ★★★ **New, and concrete**: *"**the body lives in reality, where the mind lives in fantasy** or imagination.
> ★★★ **The body cannot lie.** So somebody will **say yes — while shaking their head.** … **The body is
> saying: I don't want to do this, I feel uncomfortable, something about this doesn't sit right** — but
> because they feel they need to be polite, they say yes."*
>
> ★★★ **The intervention**: *"pause just for a minute and say — ***I sense just a little hesitation. Can we
> talk about why you might not want to move forward?*** — **and then they'll tell you something super
> valuable**, and you can address their concerns right then and there."*

★★★ **And the definition of love he attaches to it**, credited to *"the doctor who broke this down"*
(unnamed): *"**love is basically: understand me and appreciate me.** ★★★ **So if you can have half that
equation — just to understand someone — they're going to feel the sense of being loved.**"*

## ★★★ 3. Empathy — defined against the common error

★★★ *"Often people mistake empathy as: **when somebody's going through an emotional moment, you make it about
YOUR emotional moment** — you're reliving your pain, your trauma, **and you direct the attention away from
the other person.** **A lot of people do this. They think that's the expression of empathy.**"*

> ★★★ *"**True empathy is to understand what that other person needs in that moment in time.** And
> **sometimes they need you to be stoic** — they need you to be resolute — **or they might need a shoulder
> to cry on, and not for you to go and have your own triggered moment.**"* → *"**empathy turns emotional
> intelligence into influence.**"*

★★★ **The exchange that grounds it, with a colleague**: *"**Chris, you have this ability during a sales
process to speak from your heart. You make people feel like you really care. What's your secret, bro?**"* —
★★★ *"**Mo, you're not going to like this answer. It's because I actually really care.**"* And the practice:
*"when somebody's showing me they're having a bad day, or they're anxious about this offer, or they can't
afford this — **I want to pause. I need to put my own needs — to make money, or to be liked, or to be
popular, or to be right — aside**, and say: **what's this person going through right now?**"*

★★ **How it shows on stage**: *"I don't just fire back an answer — **I ask: what would be helpful for you
right now? What are you going through? What are the options you're considering?**"* → *"**the person just
wants to be seen and heard.**"*

## ★★★ 4. Asking great questions — with the two failure modes named

*"**Questions are greater than answers.**"* ✅ Existing doctrine — but **the diagnostic is new**:

- ★★★ **Leading / binary questions**: *"the answer seems to be yes or no, or it seems **motivated by some
  agenda you have** — *would you like pasta or pizza?* **Well, you're not giving me a lot of options — and
  maybe I don't want any carbs.**"*
- ★★★ **Abstract questions**: *"**how are you feeling today?** — **well, I don't know how I'm feeling, and I
  don't know where this is going.**"*
- ★★★ **The target**: *"**the magic is somewhere in the middle.** It's not an exact science, but you're
  trying to ask a question that **gives them direction, versus just making it *anything is possible*.**"*
  His example: ★★★ *"**what's motivating our conversation today?**"*

## ★★ 5–7. Influence, coaching, decisiveness

- ★★ **Influence** — *"**your ability to enroll people into your ideas.** You have a vision, but **if you
  don't have the ability to influence people, no one's going to help you build it.**"* ★★ **Including
  domestically**: *"…or **to get permission from your life partner, your spouse**, to say **yes, I believe
  in this, I support you, let's go all in.**"* → *"**influence equals trust, clarity and consistency.**"*
  Credited: **Robert Cialdini**, *Influence*.
- ★★★ **Coaching** — *"**your ability to spot talent**… and then **to draw out people's potential.** It's
  really important that you **create safe space for them to grow, and guide without controlling them.**
  ★★★ **Coaches help other people self-discover solutions** — your ability to ask them questions **so that
  they solve it for themselves.**"* Credited: **Michael Bungay Stanier**, *The Coaching Habit*.
  > ⚠️ **Note the tension with his own teaching persona**: *"create safe space"* here, against *"I like to
  > create **unsafe** space"* (settled doctrine from Jan–Feb 2025). **Different objects — a manager growing
  > a report vs. a teacher creating friction in a workshop — but record both, do not merge.**
- ★★ **Decisiveness** — *"looking at all the possible options **looking for guarantees** [is] **paralysis by
  analysis**… **there's no such thing as a perfect plan. Action creates momentum** — you activate **big
  Mo** — **and inaction kills it.**"* ✅ Same *Big Mo* term as 2025-03-06. Credited: **Bronnie Ware**,
  *The Top Five Regrets of the Dying* — *"**all of these have the same theme: not taking action on
  aspirations.**"*

## ★★ 8–9. Grit, and breaking the rules

- ★★ **Grit and discipline**, via a **Mike Tyson** paraphrase: *"**discipline is doing things that you hate,
  but doing it like you love it.**"* With the image: *"**the level underneath the water of the iceberg,
  where no one sees the invisible work that no one cares about.**"* → *"**consistency over time beats raw
  talent.**"*
- ★★★ **Break the rules — and the classroom story that proves it.** *"We're all educated in a system that
  teaches us to **follow the rules, paint within the lines, raise your hand before we speak** — and whoever
  has the best answer wins — **and to memorise things versus to learn and apply.**"* Then: *"I used to teach
  a design class and my students would fall into the same trap. I'd show them: **but you could do this** —
  and their minds would be blown. And they'd say: ***but I thought we could not do that.*** ★★★ **I said:
  WHO TOLD YOU you can't do that?** **And they would pause and look around** — *who's the they?* — ★★★ **and
  they could never name any specific person, or even a group.**"*
  > ★★★ **The conclusion**: *"**we inherit these rules that we think define reality.**"* (With a *Matrix*
  > reference — ⚠️ he attributes the line to the wrong character; see caption notes.)
  > ✅ **And here he CREDITS a line he used uncredited a week earlier**: *"a great quote from **Sally
  > Hogshead**: **different is better than better.**"* — cf. [[2025-03-25-yt-lXTf0ypur0Q]], where it appears
  > with no attribution. **Record the credit; it belongs to her.**

## ★★★ 10. Intrinsic over extrinsic — and a biographical passage

*"We're living for **likes, shares and followers** — this is a prime example of extrinsic motivation. **You're
showing up online to make other people happy, when inside you could be the most miserable, depressed person
in the world.**"* → *"**what are you going to do with your life to put a dent in the universe?** … **This
internal purpose will sustain you for those dark moments** — **long after all the extrinsic motivation
fades.** ★★★ **True wealth is built by those driven by meaning, not just the pursuit of money.**"*

> ★★★ **The biographical close, and it is the best part of the source**: *"when I was growing up I would ask
> my friends **what do you do?** — and they would pretty much without fail say ***I'm going to be rich.***
> **The ironic thing is they never actually achieved any of that**, because they weren't driven by some
> deeper passion. ★★★ **And here I am — some kind of weird art kid** who's like *I want to make designs, I
> like these logos I see on shirts and patches, I love graphics on skateboards.* ★★★ **And so I was
> thinking: I'm going to be a total loser in my life. I'm barely going to have enough money to pay rent.**
> **When it turns out I've actually made a great living — because I've been able to pursue something that's
> just burning inside my soul.**"*
