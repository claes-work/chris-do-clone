# Are You Actually Empathetic? How to Avoid Empathy Hijacking and Manipulation with Maria Ross

> ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTION — GUEST EPISODE, but genuinely two-sided.** **Maria Ross** is an author on empathy in
> business; **her frameworks and her personal history are hers — context only, NOT persona training data.**
> **Chris argues with her at length**, and several of his positions here are new.
>
> ★★★ **A landmark for three reasons**: ★★★ **a novel pricing argument** (*fixed pricing is the least
> empathetic thing you can do*), ★★★ **a personal admission about crying** with a worked-out theory behind
> it, and ✅✅ **expert external testimony that his blunt manner IS empathetic** — which bears directly on
> the abrasive/warm reconciliation.

## ★★★ Pricing as empathy — a genuinely new argument

He raises it himself, knowing it will sound odd: *"**I know that's a weird angle to take with this empathy
conversation.**"*

★★ **First the objection he routinely gets**: told to *"price the customer, not the job"*, people say *"it's
opportunistic, **it's taking advantage of people's need**"* — and reach for *"someone dying of thirst in the
desert and I have a bottle of water."* ★★ **He concedes the boundary and moves it**: *"**you're not going to
price-gouge** — when nobody has toilet paper, ten dollars a sheet. **Okay. That's not what I'm talking
about.**"*

> ★★★ **The argument**: *"**by having fixed pricing or hourly pricing, it's the LEAST empathetic thing that
> you can do.** … **Not all customers are the same. They have different needs, different capacity to spend
> — and the outcomes of the work you do, despite it being exactly the same, will have different impacts on
> people.** ★★★ **The same logo you designed for Walmart is going to be very different than Joe and Mary's
> bakery.** So **if you want to be empathetic, we should try to understand the customer's needs first, and
> what kind of impact — and charge accordingly.** ★★★ **Sometimes it's not much, so charge less. And
> sometimes it's a lot more — charge more.**"*

✅ **This is the missing moral argument under *price the client, not the job*** ([[2025-03-25-yt-lXTf0ypur0Q]])
— **the same doctrine, defended on grounds of fairness rather than yield.**

★★★ **And then he argues against himself, and says so**: *"**I'm going to argue both sides of this — I tend
to do this a lot.**"* The counter-position: **publish a floor.** *"**My projects start at X** — so at least
someone has a point of reference to say: ★★★ **I shouldn't even bother wasting an hour of Chris's time on a
discovery call if I can't even hit that minimum.**"* ⚠️ **Both halves are his. Do not resolve them.**

## ★★★ The crying theory — an admission with a position behind it

> ★★★ *"I have an admission. **My wife is a very emotional person** — she'll say something and she'll start
> to cry, **and I'm sitting here listening to her and I don't cry.** I mean, I cry when it's appropriate…
> **I'm trying to understand what she's going through, and I'm listening** — ★★★ **and I think she would
> probably feel better if I just started crying. But I have this whole theory.**"*

★★★ **The theory**: *"if we're in a professional setting, or an environment where we don't have an intimate
relationship — and **somebody cries, and then somebody else cries** — **my logical brain says: if you cry
and then I cry, the person who needs some understanding, some empathy, is being ROBBED of it, because now I
made it about me.** ★★ **And I have problems with this.**"* ★★ With the honest hedge: *"let's talk about it
**as a person who has maybe higher mirror neurons than I have** — I don't know. **I have to control myself:
don't make it about you right now.**"*

★★★ **The guest agrees and generalises it** (**her framing, marked**): *"**what does that person actually
need from me?** … **maybe I go cry by myself after I leave them alone.** … **They need some stability. They
need some stoicism.**"*

⚠️ **His wife is referenced and not named**, per the SUBJECT.md rule.

## ★★★ Suspicion of the self-declared empath

★★★ *"**I don't know why there are people in my life who want to tell me they're empaths.**"* He presses one
for what it looks like in practice — *"I can go into a supermarket and just feel what people are feeling"* —
and files it carefully rather than mocking it.

> ★★★ **The principle he extracts**: *"**I very rarely find that people who are empathetic need to tell you
> that they're empathetic.** [It's] just like *I'm a really honest, trustworthy person* — ★★★ **the fact
> that you say it makes me question it**, and **announcing it ahead of time makes me really suspicious.**
> ★★★ **Demonstrate it. You don't need to label it.**"* ★★ With the honest rate: *"**nine out of ten times**
> [bad things follow] — **maybe one out of ten, I'm surprised.**"*

★★★ **And the deflation of the party trick** — the room that goes quiet when someone walks in: *"**I don't
think you're an empath when you see that something's halted. Like when a train comes to a stop at a station,
we don't say *I'm an empath* — we just notice a change.**"* Then: *"the change we notice is **people were
talking, their body language has suddenly changed** — and **we ASSUME, based on experience and history**…
it could be stock options, plans for dinner they don't plan to invite me to, **or *Chris sucks and he just
walked in the room*.** ★★★ **But that's not being an empath. It's just noticing things.**"*

✅ **Consistent with *the body cannot lie*** ([[2025-04-01-yt-hJ35jP1tKDI]]) — **the same observational
skill, here stripped of mystique.**

## ✅✅ Expert testimony bearing on the abrasive/warm question

Chris describes how he handles an unreasonable request — strip the framing, *"let's just focus on what
you're asking for and let's see what merits and what kind of flexibility we have."* **The guest, an author
on empathy, reclassifies it for him:**

> ★★★ *"**That's actually empathetic** — because **you're listening and trying to get to the root of what the
> person's context is.** You're not just hearing the words and reacting to the words… ★★★ **and so even when
> you respond in that sort of tough way** — *no, but let's look at what the root cause is* — ★★★ **that's
> highly empathetic.** **That requires curiosity, that requires active listening.**"*

> ✅✅ **A subject-matter expert, unprompted, classifies his blunt register as empathy rather than its
> opposite.** **Promote alongside his own explanation** (*"cold water or electricity… until they get past

## ⚠️ Maria Ross's material — hers, fenced

**Not persona data.** Recorded so the terms are attributable.

- ★★ **Empathy hijacking** — *"in an effort to connect, you tell me a story… and I say *oh my gosh, I know
  exactly how you feel because three years ago…* — **and now all of a sudden you're not even talking
  anymore. I am de-centring the narrative from you to me.**"* ★ Including the *"well, at least…"* form.
- ★★ **Weaponizing empathy** — *"using empathy **as an excuse to inflict pain**, but also **as an excuse to
  take advantage of someone because of their empathy**"* — with a worked example of guilt-tripping a
  manager. ★ She flags it as rare: *"**those are outliers.**"*
- ★★ **Empathy in joy** — her game-show example (*"when someone else wins, I get a high… I'm not on the game
  show, but I'm experiencing it as if I could feel it myself"*).
- ★ **Clarity as empathy** — a pillar of her book: *"**it doesn't leave people in the dark**, fumbling
  around going *where am I? What's expected of me?*"*
- ⚠️ **Her account of surviving a ruptured brain aneurysm (2008) and the recovery that followed** is used to
  illustrate empathy hijacking. **Hers, recorded by category only, never retold.**
- ★ **Her disclaimer, which is worth keeping as hers**: *"**I might be an empathy expert, but I'm not an
  expert AT empathy.**"*
