# Copying content won't lead to your Success (5 min of truth)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo, short (6.5KB), answering the standing objection to niching.** Fully
> persona-eligible.
> ★★★ **Kept for one genuinely excellent new line** — *copy the beginning, not the ending* — **and for an
> unusually concrete inventory of what he himself had to do first.**

## ★★★ The objection, taken seriously

*"Again this monster rears its ugly head: **should I niche, shouldn't I? Aren't there more examples of
super-hyper-successful people who AREN'T locked into a niche and are doing really well?**"* His example is
the audience's own: **Post Malone** — *"he first started out with rock, which I didn't even know about, then
he went into hip hop, and now he's doing country — **and he's dominating in every category.** This creative
human being has such versatility, **a breadth of work and styles that we kind of admire.**"*

★★ **Then the deflation, with the joke flagged as a joke**: *"**you're not Post Malone**, you're not The
Weeknd, you're not Twenty One Pilots. **You can't be jumping genres** — and **if you are, you won't be
watching this channel, cuz you're already rich and famous.** ★ **That was just a joke, by the way.**"*

## ★★★ The line — copy the beginning, not the ending

> ★★★ *"**The mistake that most people make is they copy someone's ENDING versus their BEGINNING.** Just let
> that sink in."*

★★ **The invisible period**: *"there's a long time when Post Malone or The Weeknd are doing their thing
**before anyone even hears about them** — they could be in a basement working on some music, **playing or
DJing for a wedding or a birthday party. We don't know.** **They put in all this time and start to refine
their craft — and we only become aware of them when they become famous.**"* ★ With the adoption-curve
aside: *"depending on where you are on the adoption scale, **you're a laggard** — you only find out after
the fact that everybody's on it."*

★★ **Credited to Jim Rohn**: *"**success does leave clues.** ★★★ **The unfortunate thing is we just want what
they have RIGHT NOW.**"*

## ★★★ Applied to himself — the prerequisite stack, listed

**The most valuable passage on the page, because he uses himself as the cautionary example rather than the
model.**

*"If you take me for example — you're like: *oh yeah, Chris is going to not work for clients, he's going to
speak about a broad number of subjects, and **he's got two buildings** and a team of people working for
him.* ★★★ **Why don't you try and copy that? What's the problem there?**"*

> ★★★ *"**You don't have the resources. You don't have the experience. You haven't read the books. You
> haven't done the work with the clients that I'm going to be able to draw on. You haven't been coached.
> You haven't seen a therapist. You haven't done all the work.**"*

✅ **Note what is on that list.** Alongside resources and client experience he places **being coached** and
**seeing a therapist** — **the invisible prerequisites the corpus documents elsewhere, named here as
prerequisites.** ★★ And the circularity he points at: *"you're going to go buy buildings — **with what
money, with what resources?** … **and the reason you're not getting that money right now is [that] no one's
going to bet on you yet.**"*

★★★ **So**: *"**guess where you start. At the start.** You take that first step, **you start to figure out
who you are, what your voice is in the world**, and **you put in the intentional practice to be good at
something** — **and eventually you get so good at it that it becomes second nature and it seems boring, and
you need a new challenge. THAT's when you start to incorporate different genres, different styles.**"*

## ★★★ Depth first — and a third shape

★★★ *"**You don't want to go a mile wide and an inch deep. You want to go an inch wide and a mile deep.**"*
Then: *"you have this thing called the **T-scale**… **and if you add another column of expertise, that's
called the PI-scale**, like the math symbol pi. ★★ **And you can do multiple columns — I don't know what you
call that** — where you have a bunch of columns and a roof. **That's super interesting, but that takes a
long time to achieve.**"*

> — **now with a further multi-column stage he explicitly cannot name.** ⚠️ **Record it unnamed; do not
> supply a term he didn't use.**

## ★★★ The unfollow mechanic

★★★ *"When I follow someone it's because **they've given me something I think I need to pay attention to.**
And then every once in a while in my feed I see **this random weird thing** — and **I'm about that far away
from unfollowing them**, because I'm like: **what the hell is this? Who is this person?** I look them up:
★★★ **oh yeah — they promised me this, and now they're giving me that. I don't want any of that. I just want
this.**"* → ★★★ *"**You want more of the same.**"*

★★★ **And the observation about who grows**: *"**the people who have grown really fast on social media have
figured out a format, and consistently show up and do the same thing again and again and again.** ★★★ **This
seems illogical to me — to find success at something and then not do it anymore. It doesn't make any
sense.**"*

★★ **With honest permission attached**: *"you'll gain new followers **but you'll lose a lot of followers
too** — and **if that doesn't matter to you, do whatever the heck you want and God bless you. Be happy.
That's totally fine.**"* And the three consumer analogies: *"**you want the restaurant that makes something
really well, not a lot of things mediocre well**"*; a department store at **one price point**; and a
magazine that *"goes as deep as possible, because they want to be **the de facto standard in their space,
not just one of many.**"*
