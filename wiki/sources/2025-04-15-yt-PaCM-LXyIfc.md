## The ten, with what he takes from each

### 1. ★★★ Jim Rohn — *Seven Strategies for Wealth and Happiness*

★★★ **The reason it's first is an argument about sources**: *"when you follow people that are influencing
the influencers, **all the names lead back to one person**"* — Darren Hardy (*The Compound Effect*), Brian
Tracy (*The Power of Self-Confidence*), Tony Robbins (*Awaken the Giant Within*) — *"**they all thank this
one gentleman: Jim Rohn.** ★★ **He's the OG — the original gangster of self-help.**"*

**The three he quotes**:
- ★★★ *"**Success is not to be pursued. It is to be attracted by the person you become.**"* His gloss:
  *"**it's important to have goals, but it's more important [who] you become in the pursuit of those
  goals**… **the real gem is not the goal itself, but how you transform in the pursuit of it.**"*
- ★★★ *"**Work harder on yourself than you do on your job.**"* → *"most of us spend almost all of our
  available resources working for someone else… **until we're middle-aged and we realise: this is not my
  beautiful life.** **Do a great job — but keep something in the reserve tank for yourself**, because **if
  you're not building your dreams, you're busy building someone else's.**"*
- ★★★ *"**You are the average of the five people you spend the most time with.**"*

> ⚠️★★★ **And an attribution jab, recorded with care.** *"You've heard people like **Gary Vee** say something
> like ***get rid of one loser friend.*** ★★★ **I wonder where he got that idea from.**"* → *"**when you go
> back to the source code, like Jim Rohn, you're understanding the idea as it was first articulated**…
> ★★★ **I like going back to the source.**"*
> ⚠️ **A pointed remark about a named living person, and this roster contains a separate Gary Vaynerchuk
> clone. Recorded as Chris's dated aside; NOT usable for that clone.** ★ Note he immediately concedes the
> regress: *"maybe you have to go back to biblical times to find where [Rohn] may have drawn inspiration
> from."*

### 2. ★★★ Michael Bungay Stanier — *The Coaching Habit*

*"…with whom I've had the pleasure of talking to."* ★ On the title: *"**it's a strange title**… **maybe it's
mistitled** — but it didn't matter, [it's] sold over a million copies. **Can a million people be wrong?**"*

★★★ **The premise he takes**: *"when we are quick to give advice… in our mind we think we're being sharp,
efficient and valuable. **But what we don't realise is we create a system where people become dependent on
us for answers. It robs them of the ability to think for themselves, to problem-solve, and to have agency
over their lives.**"* → *"**be slower to give advice, be curious for a little bit longer, and ask a few more
questions.**"*

> ★★★ **The hot take, which is the genuinely new bit**: *"**I believe The Coaching Habit is one of the best
> SALES books that you can buy** — because if you apply the framework with your prospects, **you'll find
> there's a lot less friction. You're helping them articulate what they want, get clarity on their goals,
> and outline what obstacle stands in their way.** ★★★ **What could be better for a sales conversation than
> learning those things?**"*

### 3. ★★★ Blair Enns — *The Win Without Pitching Manifesto*

> ★★★ *"**Probably the book that I refer to the most — hands down, bar none.**"*

*"Written in manifesto style — **12 proclamations** — but the summary is that **we're so busy pitching ideas
that we never really learn how to actually win clients.**"* → *"we were taught **we must pitch, we must
perform, we must present** — **so this is a giant slap to all that: stop it. Ask questions, dive deeper —
and this is how you derail the pitch process itself.**"*

★★★ **The worked anchoring example, with the silence instruction**: *"when the conversation inevitably leads
to *how much do you charge*, you should say: ★★★ ***for clients like you, projects like this cost between
$20 and $50,000.*** **You've bracketed and anchored quite high** — the middle of which is about $35K.
★★★ **And the next thing you need to do is be totally silent. You say that big number and you just be quiet.
Count to three if you need to, as Blair suggests — and then see what the client says.**"* Then the
walk-through: *"*50 is a lot*"* → **nod** → *"*can we do something for less?*"* → ★★★ *"**How much less?**"*
→ *"*what about 42?*"* → *"**let me see what we can do.**"* → *"**and this in all likelihood is way more than
you would have asked for if you just told them a price.**"*
> ⚠️ **The captions render the bracket as both *"20 to $55,000"* and *"20 to 50,000"*. His own arithmetic
> (*"the middle of which is about 35K"*) fixes it at **$20–50K**; the other figure is a transcription
> artefact and is not asserted.**

### 4. ★★★ Tony Hsieh — *Delivering Happiness*

> ★★★ *"**[It] profoundly shaped the culture of my company.**"*

**What he took**: *"how to build a healthy company culture built around **creativity, weirdness, and
empowering people to make decisions in the best interests of the company and the customer** — **and most of
them cost little to no money.**"*

★★ **The Zappos story he retells**: a customer whose wedding shoes hadn't arrived; a rep **empowered to act
without escalating** asked when and where the wedding was, *"got the shoes, hopped on a plane, and delivered
it to the groom at the wedding ceremony"*. *"**The time and energy far exceeded [the shoes] by orders of
magnitude — but they created a customer for life.**"*

> ★★★ **And what he actually did with it, including the cost**: *"**we literally copied the core values from
> *Delivering Happiness*, modified it a little bit, and started to live by them — to HIRE and to FIRE our
> team based on this.** ★★ **It took us probably a year and a half before we were able to change our culture
> around.** ★★★ **What happens is when you start to live by your values, people start to quit — and then you
> have to ask a couple of people to leave. But what you're left with is an amazing team that's aligned
> around your values, so you don't have any more of the drama.**"*
> ✅ **This is the operational history behind *culture equals brand*** ([[2025-03-25-yt-lXTf0ypur0Q]]).

### 5. ★★★ Scott McCloud — *Understanding Comics*

*"The next book is going to throw everyone for a WTF moment."* Read **in college**. *"An art form mostly
considered [for] children and adolescents **is an extremely powerful hybrid medium** — **when you mix images
with words put in a sequence in a very specific way.**"*

★★★ **What he took, and still uses**: **closure** — *"in one frame someone says *don't drink and drive*, and
the next frame is a tombstone. **What happens in there?**… **between these two frames you're telling
yourself a lot of story** — **it's super efficient and powerful**, [and it] **allows each of us to figure
out the narrative between point A and point B.** ★★★ **It's something I use a lot in storytelling.**"*

### 6. ★★ Jeremy Donovan — *How to Deliver a TED Talk*

★★ **The occasion**: asked to speak at *"a TED-talk[-style event] for the motion design industry"*, run by a
woman whose *"bar was very high — **it needed to be short, concise, and you need to MEMORISE it.** Something
I'm not used to doing."* ★★★ **The dating detail**: *"this was **still very early on in my public speaking
career**, where **I got the butterflies like you could not believe.**"*

> ★★★ **And a warning he attaches to his own recommendation**: *"**I want to warn you about this book.** When
> you study and break down the very best talks ever given on a platform, **it can create a lot of impostor
> syndrome, as you can imagine.**"*

★★ **What he took**: **a catchphrase** — *"start with why", "leaders eat last"*, or TED's own *"ideas worth
spreading"* — *"**something memorable, short and hooky, so that after you're done talking people can walk
away with something. It gives you that definitive note.**"* ★★★ **The failure it prevents**: *"many of us
will give a talk and have ★★★ **the leaky-tire syndrome** — **the air is just escaping and we run out of
energy.**"* → *"**if you want a better talk, start at the end and work your way backwards.**"* ✅ Consistent
with the **Brian Tracy** *start-at-the-end* material.

### 7. ★★ Robert Cialdini — *Influence*

**What he takes**: **reciprocity** — *"when somebody does something for you, **you feel compelled to return
the favour**… **most people don't feel good when they're just takers.**"*

★★ **The study he retells** — the **Hare Krishna** gifting books before asking for donations, *"and if they
followed the people who didn't want to carry a book, they'd **go in the trash and pick it out and give it to
the next person**"*, before switching to flowers because *"books are heavy [and] expensive."* ★★ **The
modern coda**: *"**because this is overused, we've become numb to this** — we'll either say no outright, or
**we take something and don't give them anything, because we understand the game.**"*

### 8. ★★★ Marty Neumeier — *Zag*

*"Probably not even Marty's best-known book, **but I think it's one of his best.**"*

> ★★★ *"**Branding is really about not being different, but being RADICALLY different**… ★★★ **Branding is
> the act of intentional differentiation.**"*

✅ *"**Different is better than better** — ★★★ **that's a Sally Hogshead quote**"* — **second consecutive
crediting** ([[2025-04-01-yt-hJ35jP1tKDI]]), after an uncredited use on 2025-03-25. **The credit is now
settled behaviour.**

★★★ **The framework he names**: *"he shares this framework called ★★★ **your ONLYNESS statement** — **the
thing that only you can do**, your brand's unique value proposition into the world."*

### 9. ★★★ Jason Fried & David Heinemeier Hansson — *Rework*

★★★ **The idea he takes is *think like a chef*, and he argues it against a friend.** The friend — *"an
amazing designer-illustrator"* — refused to teach: *"**Chris, I don't want to create a bunch of clones of
what I'm doing. I don't want to compete against myself with poor copycats.**"* ★ *"I understand his
sentiment."*

> ★★★ **The rebuttal, by question**: *"**who are the most famous, celebrated and successful chefs that you
> know?**"* — Emeril Lagasse, Gordon Ramsay, Bobby Flay — ★★★ *"**why is it that they're the most celebrated?
> Because they've done a really good job of sharing their secret sauce.**"*

★★★ **The mechanism**: *"**create recipes for what you do. Step-by-step instruction. Compile it in a book.
Sell the book.** Take it further — **create a show, a YouTube channel, and teach people how to make those
dishes.** ★★★ **Both will create tremendous demand for you to cook for people** — because **as much as we
love watching people do things, very few people actually take action to apply it themselves. We watch
something done by a master, [and] we want the master to do it for us.**"*

★★★ **The warning**: *"**if you want to get busy being the world's best kept secret and hoarding all the
resources yourself — do so at your own peril.** **I would rather share all my recipes.**"*
✅ **Closes a loop with Kier McLaren's *"don't be the world's best kept secret"*** ([[2025-03-25-yt-lXTf0ypur0Q]]).

### 10. ★★★ Austin Kleon — *Steal Like an Artist*

★★ *"Picasso once said **good artists borrow, great artists steal** — ★★ **and he stole that from someone
else.** So there you go."* ⚠️ **He flags the attribution problem in the same breath he uses the quote.**

★★★ **The argument**: *"**all of us are stealing subconsciously**, and not in ways most of us would deem
unethical. If you went to school, if you looked at a book, if you watched any work from any other artist —
**why are you looking at that work? For quote-unquote inspiration.** … **The way we're taught to draw is to
literally copy the masters** — **to copy as faithfully as we can, not to emulate**, [to see] **how that
artist looked at the world and shaped it.** ★★★ **Our proximity to that great piece of art determines our
future potential.**"*

★★★ **And why copying isn't the end state**: *"**what we can't help doing is copying and then putting our own
spin on it, because that's just the nature of how we are**… ★★★ **in copying, we learn how to grow.**"* ✅ Via
**Kirby Ferguson** again: *"**we copy first, we transform the work, we combine [it] with other things, and
then create our own take.**"*

> ★★★ **The sharpest line in the section**: *"**so many of us are afraid to copy work — to our own
> detriment.** We're not busy studying and copying faithfully what came before, **so what we wind up doing
> is a really bad imitation. ★★★ And to me that's the biggest insult.**"*

★ **An aside he explicitly declines to develop**: *"a lot of us are critical of **AI generative imagery**
because **it is built on models of looking at art that it did not own or have rights to.** ★ **That's a
topic for another day.**"* ⚠️ **Recorded as flagged-and-deferred by him; not a stated position.**

## ★★★ The closing caveat — the best part of the source

> ★★★ *"**Reading can only get you so far. Reading is not going to make you rich, successful, happy, more
> self-confident, more creative.** **It's actually when you APPLY these ideas into your work, into your
> life, that you're going to move forward.** ★★★ **Personal development doesn't work if you actually don't
> do any of the development.**"*
