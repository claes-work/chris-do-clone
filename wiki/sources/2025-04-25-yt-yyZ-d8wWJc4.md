# The Real Job of a Sales Call (It's Not What You Think)

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do teaching a live sales workshop in Dubai**, working the room by name. Participants
> are **context only**. Fully persona-eligible. ★ **Part 1 of 3**; the rest is behind YouTube membership,
> **noted as a gate.**
>
> ✅ **The deferral that produced this page was justified.** It was held back from batch 166 specifically to
> be read **against** the *Socratic Selling* page three days earlier — and it **does** overlap
> (*"if you say it you're selling, if they say it you're closing"*; current-state → desired-future-state →
> obstacles). **But roughly two thirds of it is new**, including **an exercise he invented two days before
> the workshop.**

## ★★★ The thesis — the job is not to sell

> ★★★ *"**Our job when we're selling is naturally NOT to sell.** What we want to do in the buy–sell cycle is
> **just to help the other person make a decision — cuz they're carrying around a lot of weight.** ★★ **And
> if we just use that as our north star, I think it's going to work really well.**"*

★★ **The self-audit he opens with**: *"think back to your last sales conversation. **Were you serving or
were you taking?** … **Were you telling or were you asking? Were you speaking a lot — long-winded — or were
you listening? Did you understand their pain? Did you empathise?**"*

★★ **And the polite struggle he names**: *"when we get on a sales call, **they want to tell you what to do
and you want to tell them what to do.** ★★ **So there's a polite battle here.** And if we can set the right
tone and ask the right things, **then they will give us the control happily.**"*

## ★★★ The ant exercise — invented two days earlier

★ **He says so himself**: *"**I've never done this before, but I thought about it in the shower two days
ago. So we're going to try it.**"*

**The setup**: walk around, observe the room, then *"**draw yourself sitting at the table again — but this
time from the point of view of an ant.**"*

> ★★★ **The point**: *"**the point of it is not the [drawing].** The point is **you can never understand what
> the ant sees or knows, because you've never BEEN the ant.** ★★★ **And when I ask you to draw it, most
> people draw it from their own point of view.** **It doesn't take into consideration the other person's
> point of view.**"*

★★ **Credited to Phil M. Jones** (*Exactly What to Say*): *"**people do things for their own reasons.**"*

★★★ **And the resolution, which is the whole method in one image**: *"**when we think we know the other
person's perspective — just remember: you've never been the ant. You do not know their perspective.** And
the best way you can find out is to say: ★★★ ***Mr or Miss Ant, what do you see? What's it like on the
ground for you?*** **And you just ask questions. And then the ant will tell you.**"* → the three he returns
to: *"**what was the problem in the past? What is your desired future state? What are the obstacles getting
in your way?**"*

## ★★★ The four rules

1. ★★★ **"Whoever asks more questions — or talks less — is in control."** *"**We think pitching and selling
   is talking. But when you talk, you're not in control.**"* Via the interrogation cliché: *"**I'll be the
   one asking questions.** Who's in control? **The person asking the question.**"*
   > ★★★ **A concrete instrumentation, new to the corpus**: *"if you have your last sales conversation
   > recorded, **ask AI to count how long you were talking relative to the other person talking.**"*
2. ★★★ **"Whoever wants it more has less leverage."** *"**The client has a problem, they want a solution.
   You have a problem — I have no money — so you want money.** ★★★ **Who wants it more?** **Now if you go
   into the relationship wanting it so bad, you have no power.**"* → ★★★ **the structural fix**: *"if you
   learn how to do sales and marketing, **you should have more opportunities to make money than the client
   has solutions to buy**… ★★★ **your job is to reduce down your need to make money versus the demand for
   your services.**"* With the luxury analogy: *"Ferrari, Lamborghini, Rolls-Royce — **they have more
   customers and not enough product. They're on the right side of this equation.**"*
   ✅ **This is the mechanism under *being needy is repulsive*** ([[2025-03-25-yt-lXTf0ypur0Q]]) — **the same
   claim, expressed as leverage rather than as impression.**
3. ★★★ **"Simple, straightforward answers are a sign of confidence and truth."** *"**When you give a super
   complicated answer to a simple question, it starts to make me feel like I don't believe you.**"* Via
   reality TV: *"when somebody's lying and they're asked a simple question — *well, you know, I was walking
   home and then his dog and then Mary said this and then Bobby* — ★★ **you're lying, bro. Because the truth
   is very simple.**"* → *"in the role-play, **if you're talking too much: first of all you're not in
   control, and you might be lying.**"*
4. ✅ **"If you say it, you're selling. If they say it, you're closing."** — with ★★★ *"**when you're
   pitching, the most beautiful pitch is when they do 90% of the talking.**"*

## ★★★ Pricing is positioning — and the $10,000-a-day story

★★★ *"**Pricing is positioning.** When you see similar objects — water, wine — **if you see things priced
differently, we just assume the more expensive thing is better**, that more people want it, it's more
premium. Hotels, cars, jewellery."* → ★★★ **the instruction**: *"**tell them what your prices [are].** And
if you're working on project fees, **say *projects start at this amount*, so that there's room to go up.**"*
✅ **This is the "publish a floor" position he argued against himself three weeks earlier**
([[2025-04-02-yt-2FI0Dk44-vo]]) — **here stated straight, with no counter attached. Both remain his.**

> ★★★ **THE STORY, new to the corpus.** *"When I started to sell strategy, **I had in my mind it was going
> to be $10,000 a day.** ★★★ **I got it because somebody else told me it could be done. I'm like: if it can
> be done, I can do it.**"* The script he used: *"**before we get too deep, I just want to let you know:
> whatever you ask me to do — it doesn't matter, the logo, look at your copy, send you some colours — it
> doesn't matter how much work it is, it will be $10,000.**"* Response: *"that's a lot of money."* — *"I
> know it's a lot of money. ★★★ **So I got rid of them as fast as possible.**"*
>
> ★★★ **The turn**: *"eventually one person says **I'm intrigued, let's keep talking** — and that person
> agreed. **Then from that point forward, my rate was 10,000 bucks.** ★★★ **I just need ONE person to say
> yes so I can say it's happened.**"* → ★★★ *"**You get a lot of nos — but I'm setting the benchmark, and
> I'm getting rid of them as fast as possible.**"*

★★★ **And the awkward transition, told against his own vendors**: *"**I run into this problem all the time.
I hire creative people. I like the portfolio. I'm ready to give them money.** We have a conversation, I ask
about their process — ★★★ **and they can't tell me how much it's going to be. I'm not going to do their job
for them. So I wait, and I wait — and then it comes in an email. They never say it.**"*

## ★★★ The most generous interpretation

Running a listening exercise on a hostile-sounding client statement (*"we had a designer before, but they
were too expensive… every time we needed a small revision, they charged us extra"*), he demonstrates the
reading he wants — *"**it sounds like you don't mind paying more if the work is really good. You just don't
want any surprises. Is that a summary of what was said?**"* — and then, asked how he compressed two
paragraphs into six words, gives the method:

> ★★★ *"**I strip away the emotion. I lean into the logic.** And I say: ★★★ ***if this were my best friend
> who said this to me, someone I deeply care about — what is the most generous interpretation of what
> they're saying?*** — **versus *this is my worst enemy*, someone who's going to torture me for the rest of
> my life.** **We bring that negative emotional baggage and it changes our state.**"*

★★★ **The consequence**: *"when we're angry, **everything that is innocent seems like it's affecting us.**"*
→ ★★★ *"**Not everybody's trying to insult you. Not everyone's trying to take advantage of you. Not everyone's
trying to give you money. Be objective. That's the name of the game.**"*

✅✅ **This is the operational form of the non-violent-communication thread** — *"strip out all the opinions
and talk about things for the way they are, not the way that you might see them"*
([[2025-04-01-yt-hJ35jP1tKDI]]). ★ **And he uses the word in the room**: correcting a participant's
interpretation, *"**you bring too much violence.**"*

## ★★★ Two scripts from the live coaching

★★★ **The reflection, and its hard limit**: a participant offers *"sounds like you're under a lot of
pressure"* — he accepts, extends it to *"**and you're behind schedule**"*, and then stops: ★★★ *"**That's
it. Let's not add anything else. Everything else is PROJECTION.**"* ★★ **Why the order matters**: *"when you
say what it is that I've been trying to say, I'm like **exactly** — **you can now pivot to a good question.
Because if you mess up the first part, [then on] the next question I'm like: we're not seeing eye to
eye.**"*

★★★ **The neutral question, which he names as such**: *"for me it's — *sounds like you're behind schedule
and you're in a jam* — and I would just ask: ★★★ ***what needs to happen to get you out of this jam?*** —
**the most objective and neutral question I can ask.**"* Because *"**I don't want to lead the solution. I
want to invite them to tell me what the solution is** — because remember how **you couldn't see from the
point of view of the ant.**"* ★★★ **And what comes back**: *"**all the information is coming to you** —
*I have $100,000, I need it done in a week, we can do it in three phases, I need to pick a vendor today and
they have to be government-certified* — ★★★ **versus *would you like apples or oranges?* That's the kind of
question you guys are asking.**"*

★★ **His live corrections are consistent with the doctrine**: *"**do you have a project plan?** — that's a
yes-or-no question"*; and to someone who jumped to offering daily updates and a concession: *"**the problem
is you're leading too much**… **nobody's talking about that just yet. We want to go step by step. Help them
to make a decision.**"*

★ **A small AI-in-workflow datum**: *"**ChatGPT wrote this for me** and I said: **each time, make it a
little bit harder.**"*
