# How to Sell Without Selling (Secret Framework) with Victoria Dior Wang (2025-04-30)

> **Attribution:** ✅ **Chris answers throughout.** He states the format explicitly — *"I have asked
> Victoria to come at me with a bunch of questions, especially to highlight some of the belief systems
> that might persist in that part of the world."* The interviewer's own observations are `[HOST]` and
> are not persona data.

## Summary

★★★★ **Two strong items:** **brand as the accelerant**, argued with a costed example, and a pointed
cultural argument about **why Asian brands don't travel** — which is the sharpest form in the corpus
of the counter-stereotype thread. Plus a concrete account of **what credible outreach looks like**
from the receiving end.

## Key claims (2025-04-30)

### ★★★★★ Brand is the accelerant — and not all brands leverage

> ★★★★★ *"**Brand is THE ACCELERANT to almost everything that you do.** And that's not to say that all
> brands are created equally. **You and I both have brands, and everyone listening has a brand — BUT
> NOT ALL OF US ARE ABLE TO LEVERAGE that brand to create opportunities for us to make things move
> faster.**"*

★★★★ **The costed example**, which is what makes it more than a slogan: a friend running a
1,000-person event in Las Vegas was at **89% of tickets sold** and expected a sell-out within a week
or two — *"**he said LAST YEAR IT WOULD HAVE TAKEN ME ANOTHER TWO MONTHS OF SELLING to close and
finish this thing.**"* The mechanism was **one multi-hour webinar with guests**, doors opened, then
follow-up and retargeting of attendees. *"And he goes: **because EVERY YEAR HIS BRAND BECOMES MORE
POWERFUL AND HE CAN LEVERAGE IT MORE.**"*

★★★★ **The instruction:** *"if you want more people to volunteer to join **WITHOUT HAVING TO TWIST
THEIR ARM — start to think about HOW YOU SHOW UP IN THE WORLD, whether you give people an opportunity
to CONNECT WITH YOU BEYOND THE BUSINESS ASPECT.** And you'll start to see that **this is the accelerant
to everything that you want.**"*

### ★★★★★ Why Asian brands don't travel — the sharpest form of the counter-stereotype argument

> ★★★★★ *"This is something I want to talk to the potential local listeners about: **why are we — as
> Singaporeans, Asian-Americans, expats, whatever — SO READY TO GIVE EXTRA MONEY TO WESTERN OR EUROPEAN
> BRANDS OVER THE LOCAL ALTERNATIVE?** **It bothers me a little bit that there aren't as many — NOT
> EVEN CLOSE, BY A FACTOR OF 100 — of Asian brands that have translated into markets outside their
> local market.**"*
>
> ★★★★★ *"**We become TOO COMPLIANT, TOO BLEND-IN, too much desire to FIT IN — that we just DON'T
> STAND OUT. And we become frustrated, and we feel like we don't have a voice.**"*

✅ **The commercial form of a thread the corpus records personally.** Elsewhere *"embrace your inner
weird"* and the counter-stereotype mission are about individual identity; **here the same argument is
made about brands and markets**, with a claim attached (the factor-of-100 gap) that is his own
estimate.

⚠️ **The setup is the host's, not his:** she observes that *"we're so used to working hard — we feel
that working hard is what gives us the value… and having leverage seems to be something that is not
top of mind."* **Hers.** Chris builds on it rather than originating it.

### ★★★★ What credible outreach looks like — from the receiving end

> ★★★★ *"**People do reach out to me all the time: come to our city, come to our country, let's do
> something together. And MOST OF THEM ARE NOT SUPER CREDIBLE. Most of them are NOT BUILT TO
> ACCOMMODATE ME** and to be able to do whatever it is I need done."*

★★★★ **What was different:** *"**you put together A PLAN. THERE'S A SPREADSHEET. EVERYTHING WAS ABOVE
BOARD.** And you said: we're going to do this super professionally — **and to your credit, you did
exactly that.** You met me at the airport with flowers and a private car… **from beginning to end it
was first class all the way.**"*

✅ **A usable standard**, stated by the person being pitched: a plan, a spreadsheet, transparency, and
delivery against what was promised.

★★★ **On the place:** *"highest earning per capita, often number one, two or three in the world in
terms of education. **I'm passionate about education, and it's awesome for me to go to a place where
PEOPLE LIKE TO LEARN and to do self-development.**"*

### ★★★★ The cold-open line

> ★★★★ *"**Creative people are just funny like this: SOMETHING GREAT HAPPENS AND WE DON'T WANT TO DO IT
> AGAIN. SOMETHING HORRIBLE HAPPENS AND WE REFUSE TO CHANGE.** So — **are we destined to suffer all of
> our lives?**"*

## ⚠️ Host material — context only

She frames money as *"**a thank-you note** — the more thank-you notes you get, the more you know the
clients appreciate what you do"*, and warns that *"when someone feels desperate and has a fixated
agenda and is chasing after the money, **they end up pushing away the sales** even if the client is
right for them."* ⚠️ **Hers.** ✅ Worth noting only as a resemblance: **Blair Enns's "certificate of
appreciation"** at [[2017-05-03-yt-KtL02T5MonA|2017-05-03]] is the same image. **No connection is
established** — record it, assert nothing.
