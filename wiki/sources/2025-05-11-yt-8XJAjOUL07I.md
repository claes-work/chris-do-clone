# Don't Talk About Price Until You Do This

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do teaching the Dubai sales workshop**, same series as [[2025-04-25-yt-yyZ-d8wWJc4]].
> Participants are **context only**. Fully persona-eligible. ★ **A "glimpse" of a longer workshop; the full
> version is membership-gated — noted.**
>
> ★★★ **A landmark**, and it earns it twice: **the five-part sales framework, named as a sequence**, and
> **a complete value-price derivation performed live, from "I can't generate leads" to a number.**

## ★★★ The framework — now, change, obstacle, future, impact

★★★ **The premise**: *"**everybody has a present, a past, and a future.** When they come to you, **they're
in their now state.**"*

1. ★★★ **The NOW story** — *"**where are you in your business right now?** Just give me a high-level summary
   of **who you serve, what problems you solve.**"* Its job is context.
2. ★★★ **The CHANGE story** — *"**something has interrupted you to create a problem.**"* The test he uses is
   domestic and instantly clear: *"**if nothing changes, everything's cool. If you're happy in your marriage,
   no problem. If you're having some friction, some communication problems — something interrupted here.**"*
   → *"**what we want to do is find out how they're feeling this pain.** ★★★ **And the more pain they feel,
   the more urgent they seek a solution.**"* ✅ *"**Before we can go to the future, we must revisit the
   past**"* — same rule as [[2025-04-22-yt-NQT1lgXPPHs]].
3. ★★★ **The OBSTACLE** — *"**something is standing in our way, because I want something and I haven't been
   able to figure it out.**"* Questions: *"**what's standing in your way? Why haven't you been able to
   achieve this before? What have you tried in the past?**"*
4. ★★★ **The DESIRED FUTURE STATE** — *"the goal, the result, whatever it is."*
5. ★★★ **The IMPACT** — *"**if at all possible, we want to quantify, to measure the result.** ★★★ **The more
   measurable a result is, the easier it is for us to make it feel less abstract — and then we can assign a
   value to it.**"*

> ★★★ **The abstraction argument, which is a management insight as much as a sales one.** *"If my goal was
> **I want my designers to be better** — is that abstract or quantifiable? **It's super abstract.** ★★★ **And
> what happens then is: if you tell your designer *I just need you to be better* — they're going to feel
> really bad, they're going to be afraid they're going to lose their job, and YOU'RE going to feel
> frustrated that they never achieved the goal — because the goal is super abstract.**"*
>
> ★★★ **The question that hands the measuring back to them**: *"**this result that you want — how will we
> know when we get there? How do we measure it? Help me out.**"* → *"customer satisfaction is up, turnover
> rate is down, more visitors to the website — whatever it is. ★★ **Help them measure it.**"*

★★ **And the five whys**, loosely: *"the five W's is **whatever you want, I just keep asking you why**.
Sometimes three times, sometimes four — **but five is more than enough.**"*

## ★★★ The withdrawal move — and why it can't be reversed

> ★★★ *"**If there's no pain, we find a different problem.** Or we say: ***I don't think there's anything I
> can do for you. It seems like everything is okay. Seems like if you do nothing, you'll be all right. You
> don't need a new website. You don't need a new identity design.***"*

★★★ **What usually happens**: *"and the funny thing is, **when you say that, most of the time they say: *no,
I really want the website. Identity is really important.*** So then **they'll tell you: I don't care what
you say, it's still urgent, I still feel that pain** — even if it's really small."*

> ★★★ **And the asymmetry, which is the actual teaching**: *"**but if you try to convince them that it's a
> really big problem FROM a no-problem, they're like: *no, you're trying to push a sale on me.*"***

✅ **This is Blair Enns's *retreat and follow* in operation** ([[2025-02-20-yt-e2zox3-QL5Q]]) — **stated here
as a one-way door: you can withdraw and be pulled back, but you cannot manufacture urgency that isn't
there.**

## ★★★ The "if" ladder

Coaching a video-editing agency owner whose stated problem is *"I can't generate more leads"*, he tests a
narrowing with a hypothetical: *"**if you were able just to focus purely on [your best segment] and not see
a loss in revenue, would you consider just focusing on them?**"* — and then explains what he just did:

> ★★★ *"**See how easy that was? His fear was losing revenue. So if you just remove it.** ★★★ ***If* is a
> safe space to play with our imagination — low commitment.** And the weird thing is, **momentum is a good
> thing. So I take a little half baby step in the *if* — but then I realise it's not that scary, so I can
> take another half step.** ★★★ **Before you know it, they've agreed.**"*

✅ **This extends the conditional close** ([[2025-04-22-yt-NQT1lgXPPHs]]) **from a single move into a
ladder** — the same *if*, applied repeatedly, as a commitment escalator.

## ★★★ The reframe — the stated problem was the wrong problem

★★ **He compresses the brief ruthlessly**: *"**he's like: I want more money.** I want to make more money. And
he wants more money. **It's easy. It's just more money.**"* ★ And on the notes: *"**you notice how there's a
lot of empty spaces — because it's not that complicated.**"*

★★★ **Retention over acquisition**: *"**if you could have recurring revenue, do you feel like you would need
to generate as many leads?** If the clients consistently came back, would that generate the money you
want?"* — because ★★★ *"**it's much harder to get a new client than it is to keep a client.**"*

★★★ **Then the churn diagnosis**: *"**I suspect the creators who disappear — something is not working in
your system**, because I think they would want more."* Told that some simply give up posting: ★★★ *"**then
the insight is you have to find different creators — ones who are already committed, already successful —
whereas paying you a couple thousand bucks a month is a bargain. There are lots of those.**"* ★★ And where
to find them, said to a room full of them: *"**it's the [content] summit. Everybody here is a content
creator.** ★ **If I throw this marker, I bet you'll hit a content creator.**"* → *"**be introverted, right?
That's a new problem.**"*

★★★ **The verdict, which reverses the brief**: *"**the real insight is: focus on your [best-paying segment],
because they pay you more. And now we just need to find more of them that want more of this at a higher
level.**"*

## ★★★ The value arithmetic, done live

> *"**If I were to present to you a plan that helped you get more [of those] clients — what would that be
> worth to you?**"* — *"5k."* — ★★★ *"**That sounds a little off.**"*

**Then he does the maths with him rather than at him**: current ~10k/month → doubled → +10k/month new →
*"70% of which"* → **7k profit a month** → ×12 → ★★★ **$84,000 a year.**

> ★★★ *"**So you're going to make $84,000 more a year, and have a system that does this — and you just want
> to spend how much? Is 10% good enough?**"* — *"I don't know… would you be okay with 10%?"* — *"**So that
> would be $8,400.**"* — *"Yes."*

★★★ **And then he goes UNDER his own anchor**: *"**I can tell you what — I could probably come up with a plan
for you for less than that. Would you be interested in that?**"* — *"Sure."* — *"**Maybe 7,500.**"*

> ⚠️ **Note the shape of the move.** The number rose from **5k → 8.4k** by arithmetic the client did himself,
> then Chris **voluntarily settled below it**. ✅ **The reciprocity is left implicit and is not claimed** —
> but the sequence is recorded exactly.

★ **A self-referential aside during the maths**, recorded because it is his own and characteristic of the
room's mood: *"**the Asian in the room's like: 84, bro.**"*

## ★★★ Pre-empting the objection — "you're going to come back and say *what a jip*"

★★★ *"**You're going to do this and you have the exact same problem — you're going to come back and say:
*what a jip. This never works in real life.***"* Then the answer:

> ★★★ *"**At any point here there could have been friction — and there wasn't**, because **it's a
> conversational thing that we escalated, or scaffolded, towards the things we needed to know.** ★★★ **I
> didn't just go into: *what can you spend, bro? What's your budget? How much money do you make?* — and
> that's how you guys ask that question. THAT's the problem.** ★★★ **You haven't built trust. You haven't
> demonstrated that you understand how businesses work. So why would I give you any of that
> information?**"*

★★★ **And the diagnosis of the failure mode**: *"**most of you FAKE this conversation only to find out this
number — and then you go for the throat. And they feel it too.**"* ★★ Plus the counterpart: *"**a smart
business person knows that talking to another smart business person, being clear and transparent, is only
going to help him — and if he doesn't do that, it's going to hurt him.**"*

## ★★ The close — a fair expectation

> ★★★ *"Today I feel like **we're playing basketball. I taught you how to dribble, how to pass, and how to
> shoot.** ★★★ **That doesn't mean you're going to be an NBA player — but it does mean you know the
> foundational things.** And **with enough practice, with confidence, you'll start to get better.**"*
> ✅ **Notable for what it refuses to promise.**
