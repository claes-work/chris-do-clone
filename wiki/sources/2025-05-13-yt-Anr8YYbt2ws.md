# If You're Introvert But Done Hiding, Please Watch This!

> **ATTRIBUTION — Chris Do solo, scripted, eight strategies.** No guests. Fully persona-eligible.
> ★★★ **A top-tier biography landmark.** The corpus has recorded his introversion in fragments for a year
> — *"I would just hide in my room"*, *"my introverted self was freaking out"*, *"being an introvert made
> [sales] even worse"*. **This is the whole account, deliberately told**, and it contains **a fourth
> corroboration of the 2014 hinge**, **the fullest version of the Kier McLaren public-speaking origin**, and
> **the most detailed anxiety narrative in the corpus.**

## ★★★ The self-description

> ★★★ *"**As a LOUD INTROVERT who's built an eight-figure business, spoken around the world, and led
> thousands of creatives** — these are the eight strategies that helped me **stop hiding, start leading, and
> succeed without pretending to be someone I'm not.**"*

★★★ **And the framing he offers the audience**: *"**you're not broken. You've just been trying to win a game
that wasn't designed for you.**"*

## 1. ★★★ Listening is not passive — and the cobra

*"**Listening isn't a passive thing if it's done correctly.** It's a way for you to **process, absorb
information, and think about something thoughtful to say.**"* → *"**you don't need to be the loudest… you
need to be the most attuned, the most aware, the most observant.**"*

★★★ **The story, and the self-placement is the striking part**: *"I was recently at a meeting with some very
powerful, successful people. ★★★ **In fact, I think I'm the least successful, the least accomplished, and
the poorest person at the table.** And what I saw was **everyone trying to outdo each other talking, and no
one's listening** — everyone is cutting the other person off. ★★★ **The introverted me starts to recoil and
think: I need to escape this conversation.**"*

> ★★★ **Then the turn**: *"**but like the cobra kind of coiled in the basket**, there was an opportunity to
> contribute something meaningful — ★★★ **and then I struck.** I said what I needed to say, and **I can tell
> from the room everybody kind of just was thrown back a little bit** — like ★★★ **there's been this sleeper
> here, just quietly, patiently waiting to contribute.**"*

★★★ **The instruction**: *"**let the others do that. Stay in your pocket. Stay where you're strong.**"*
★★ And what it does to the room: *"when everybody's finished talking… **and you're able to add that one
clarifying point** — **they all sit back and say: whoa, that's the deep thinker in the room.**"*

## 2. ★★★ Quiet in person, loud online — and the strategy that replaced social skills

*"**The internet is [the] introvert's stage to play. It's our playground.** It lets you **control the
spotlight, rehearse your message, and scale your voice** way beyond your in-person circle **without draining
yourself socially.**"* ★★ **Stated plainly**: *"**as an introvert, I am just socially drained by being around
people, and I dread social gatherings.**"*

> ★★★ **The admission that reframes his whole career**: *"**I often tell people that I don't have social
> skills, that I'm super socially awkward.** ★★★ **And my strategy to survive — and to play with people on a
> much bigger scale — was to be able to CREATE CONTENT SO GOOD THAT I DIDN'T NEED TO DEVELOP SOCIAL
> SKILLS.**"*

★★★ **The mechanism he names**: *"**you speak once, but a thousand people can be listening**"* — *"so when I
enter a room in real life, ★★★ **people already have a sense of who I am, what my ideas are about, and
they're happy to come up to me** — **and I'm happy to talk to them at that point, because now they don't
feel like such scary strangers.**"*

> ✅✅ **FOURTH CORROBORATION OF THE 2014 HINGE**: *"**I didn't start making content until I was 42 years
> old.**"* Born 1972 → **~2014.** Joining the Draplin moment, *"why did it take me until 42"*, and the end
> of the McLaren coaching relationship. **Four independent framings, one date.**

★★ **And the honest arc**: *"you've seen me through the struggles of **finding my voice, showing up online,
and slowly becoming comfortable in my own skin on camera.**"* ★★ Platform advice follows temperament: *"**if
you love to write, write.** If you have a great speaking voice, use that on a podcast."*

## 3. ★★★ Reps, not fearlessness — and the fullest Kier McLaren origin

> ★★★ *"**Confidence isn't a personality trait. It's a byproduct of you putting in the necessary reps.**
> ★★★ **You don't become fearless. It just becomes familiar.**"*

★★★ **The origin, with detail the corpus did not have.** After observing him for years, his coach asked:
*"★★★ **Why are you so busy being the world's best-kept secret?**"* — *"and it kind of caught me off guard."*
The elaboration: *"**I think you're brilliant. I think you have a lot to offer the world — but you're so
reluctant to go out there, even to meet your clients who are here at the company.**"* → *"so I asked him:
**what am I supposed to do?** He goes: ★★★ **volunteer to speak.**"*

★★★ **The line he credits to Kier**: *"**there are two types of people in this world: people who run TOWARDS
change and people who run AWAY from change.**"* → *"**and in my own life, every time I stepped into change
to grow, opportunities met me there.**"*
> ⚠️ **A variant worth recording**: on [[2025-03-25-yt-lXTf0ypur0Q]] he gives the same structure as *"those
> who run away from FEAR and those who run towards it"*, uncredited. **Here it is *change*, and credited to
> McLaren. Record both; do not merge.**

★★★ **The failure, stated plainly**: *"although I was **sweating bullets** during my first talk, and ★★★ **I
bombed horribly the first couple of talks — I didn't give up.** Each time I'd go back, **look at what I did
wrong, what I did well**, and just try to improve."* ★★★ **And the present tense**: *"the idea of being in
front of a lot of people… **is, and STILL IS, scary to me.**"*

★★★ **The technical fix, also from Kier** — and this is the fuller version of a story the corpus has only in
compressed form: *"he goes: **you can present to lots of people if there's an image** — right, like in front
of clients? I'm like: **yeah, I do that all day long.** So he says: ★★★ **turn your whole presentation into
a series of slides that you could just talk about.**"* → *"**it seems so obvious** — but **I could present a
series of images and speak freely, without trying to memorise any specific key points or data.** Over time
I'd remember those points anyway."*
✅ **Compare [[2025-02-26-yt-HtkujhkfHOM]]**: *"let's find something you like — you like making images, don't
you? …make a bunch of images and make up a story between them."* **Same intervention, told twice; this is
the operational version.**

## 4. ★★★ Be the tree — the Design Thinkers story

**The most detailed anxiety narrative in the corpus, and he tells it as a sequence of stalls.** Speaking at
Design Thinkers in Toronto, dreading the speakers' party:

*"First, **I just get dressed, cuz I know I can't go anywhere if I don't get dressed.** Then I'm standing in
my hotel room thinking: **I'm dressed. What should I do now?** So I go down to the lobby. **I just stay in
the lobby for a while**… ★★★ **just lying to myself: *oh, you could be fashionably late.* But what I was
really doing was procrastinating.** And **instead of taking an Uber, I WALK there — just to prolong this
whole process, as if it's like my death march.**"*

★★ **Arrival**: the host is *"the first and only familiar face"*; the friend who promised to come **isn't
there** — ★ *"**and that rat, he was not there**"*. *"**I'm looking around the bar. It seems like everybody
knows everybody**… I'm just feeling like **I'm going to jump out of my skin and run for the door.**"*

★★★ **The self-negotiation, and the commitment**: *"I have a conversation with myself: **what's going to
happen if you stand here so awkwardly? What would happen to you?** ★★★ **So I made the commitment that no
matter what, I was not going to leave this bar until they kicked us out.**"*

★★★ **The tactic, which is absurd and specific and therefore useful**: *"I got a cup of water. I stood in
the middle and ★★★ **strategically placed myself between the bathroom and the bar — the high-traffic
zones** — **in case someone separated from their pack and would walk up and talk to me.** ★ **Fate was not
that kind.** I just stood there and ★★★ **imagined myself to be a tree, with my feet as roots planted into
the ground, immovable.**"* ★ *"As awkward as that sounds, **it was even weirder to experience through my
lens.**"*

★★ **The payoff, and the punchline**: *"someone says *hey, Chris* — **thank God, thank God somebody finally
said something**"*, he's invited to a table, and *"**as soon as I'm in a groove with a conversation**, Mr
[friend] **casually saunters into the room** like *yeah, the party's about to start.* ★ **You rat. You dirty
rat. You came after I already met some people.**"*

> ★★★ **The rule**: *"**find a mental trick** — and the trick I always use is **take very small steps**, [so
> that] **it'll seem silly that you don't take the next evolutionary step.** And **do everything in your
> power to not run away.** ★★★ **Be the tree.**"*

## 5–6. ★★★ A different operating system, and rehearsing a new self

> ★★★ *"**Introversion isn't a limitation. It's not something to be ashamed of. It's just a DIFFERENT
> OPERATING SYSTEM.** ★★★ **Stop looking at introversion as a bug** — it's not something to be fixed or
> solved. **It runs better in long games, not quick wins.**"*

✅ *"**Words shape worlds.** The words you choose to describe events, **and how you record things in your own
self-story, determine largely how successful and how happy you're going to be.**"*

★★★ **And the account of how he changed**: *"people meet me in person and I tell them I'm **a painfully,
awkwardly shy introvert** — [and they say] **there's no way you can be that person.** And it's just because
**I've put in the work.** ★★★ **I've rehearsed a new, more improved version of me. And eventually I filled
those gaps. I filled into this new skin.**"* → ★★★ *"**that's the most beautiful thing about human nature —
we can evolve, and we can become whoever we set out to be.**"*

## 7. ★★★ Be interested, not interesting

★★ **The story he relays** (⚠️ *"I forget who tells this story"* — **the source is not asserted**): a
well-known speaker arrives a day early, meets an older man at the hotel bar with no connection to the
conference, and **spends the whole night asking him questions**. On leaving, the older man says: ★★★ *"**young
man, you're the most interesting person I've met in a really long time.**"* — *"**and he was thinking: that's
funny, cuz I barely talked about myself.**"*

> ★★★ *"**If you want to be the most interesting person — be interested.**"*

★★★ **Why it suits an introvert specifically**: *"**you don't have to know what to say. You don't have to
talk about yourself. You don't have to put yourself in a spotlight.**"* ★★★ **And the claim underneath it**:
*"**I don't care how popular, successful or famous you are — you never tire of people who are interested in
you in a genuine way.** **That's what people want. They want to be seen, heard, understood and
appreciated.**"*

## 8. ★★★ Introversion is a sales advantage — objections as requests

★★★ *"**Extroverts want to do all the talking**, so when they hear an objection **they don't even want to
know what is underneath it — they're going to be so quick to respond.** ★★★ **This is where your
introversion is a huge advantage: before we speak, we want to have an understanding of what is being asked
of us.**"*

> ★★★ **The reframe**: *"when they say ***this is expensive***, **we don't hear it as *I can't afford you,
> you're not valuable enough.*** **What we say is: ★★★ *what is expensive about this? What do you find to be
> valuable?*** — and we are engaged in conversation and listening."* → *"**often we can resolve objections
> just because we help the person talk and think through the problem — and often it's not what we think it
> is.**"*

★★★ **The worked case**: *"*this is a lot more than I thought I was going to pay.*"* — **knee-jerk**:
*"well, let's reduce the price then; how much do we need to discount?"* — **introvert**: ★★★ *"**what about
this is too much? What were you expecting to pay? And what needs to happen for you to feel like it's worth
whatever you're going to invest?**"* — *"**and then we just let them talk.**"*

★★★ **And what actually builds the trust**: *"**your ability to ask smart questions, to process quietly
without interrupting, giving them space to speak, then asking deep follow-up questions — so they feel: my
God, everything I say is important.**"* ✅ **Credited to Kevin Daley's *Socratic Selling* as *full value
listening*** — **second consecutive crediting** ([[2025-04-22-yt-NQT1lgXPPHs]]).

## ★★ The close

*"**You can have your cake and eat it.** You could be **the deep thinker**, **introspective**, **highly
observant and in tune to the needs of others** — and **if you're able to leverage media to play in the
social space**, you can take **these big beautiful ideas that only you are able to see** and amplify them.
★★★ **So that when you walk into a room of strangers, everybody turns to notice you — because they've heard
your voice, they've read your thoughts, and you've moved them in some way.**"*
