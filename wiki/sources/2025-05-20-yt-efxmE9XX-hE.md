# Your Personal Brand Will NEVER Grow If You Keep Doing This (2025-05-20)

> **Attribution:** solo teaching video. Every statement below is
> [[../../persona/biography|Chris Do]] speaking in his own voice unless marked
> otherwise. Third parties (Uma, Molly, a former employee) are quoted *by* Chris and are
> flagged as such — their words are not persona training data.

## Summary

A structured solo lecture built on one named framework: **the authenticity gap**. Chris
defines it, lists five diagnostic signs, walks the consequences, then gives a concrete
four-column exercise for closing it. The back third pivots into an unusually direct
attack on the personal-branding industry ("what they're really talking about is how to
build sales funnels"), landing on the fullest version of his **water/brand argument**
recorded in the corpus so far — Fiji's provenance line, pull-the-label-off test, and the
turn that makes it personal ("if the majority of who we are is water, what is the
package?").

## Key claims (2025-05-20)

### The authenticity gap (named framework)

- **Definition:** *"the invisible distance between who you really are and the person
  that you show up to the world to be accepted."*
- It is **not** lying: *"You might not be faking anything, but you're definitely
  performing to a degree"* — adjusting how you speak, dress, express yourself *"to fit
  in to what people already think and want from you."*
> ✅ **Intellectual source, found later:** in a clip replayed on
> [[2025-05-29-yt-4ZS8dMwPObk|2025-05-29]], Chris grounds this framework in **Jung** —
> *"in Jungian ideology, the ego regulates between **the shadow self**… and **the persona,
> who you show up in the world to be accepted. Most of us live in the persona, and we
> don't even know it.**"* The authenticity gap is his applied restatement of Jung's
> persona/shadow, and the *"normal means average"* argument appears verbatim there too.

- **Root cause:** *"a deep-rooted fear of not being loved, not being accepted."* He
  explicitly declines to moralize about it: *"these are survival mechanisms… we might
  even be doing this subconsciously."*
- **The danger:** *"If you pretend long enough to be someone else, you forget who the
  real you is."*

**Five signs you're in the gap:**

1. You feel **drained** after making content — dreading it, depleted afterwards.
2. You **procrastinate posting** because it doesn't feel like you: *"it takes a lot of
   effort to pretend to be someone else."*
3. You **second-guess** your tone, message, vibe — *"are they going to think I'm a
   fraud?"*
4. You attract **wrong-fit** clients and followers: *"after a while you're like, this
   ain't my tribe."*
5. Your **growth is inconsistent** — *"you're following all the trends, you're doing as
   prescribed, but it's just up and down, the peaks and the valleys."*

**Consequences:** burnout, lost motivation, disconnection from your own message, and —
the one he flags hardest — *"you're going to lose the joy of creating."* Plus the
brittleness: *"anytime somebody asks you a question that pushes a little bit beyond the
surface, you're going to fall apart like a house of cards… their brain is breaking. Do
they continue to lie or do they finally come clean? And most often they choose to
continue the lie."*

### The dating story (told as the mechanism, not a moral)

A young man who worked for Chris was living at home and dreaded the question *"where do
you live?"* on dates. He'd have himself dropped off at a nice house, call his parents
*"my roommates,"* and run inside. Chris's read is about **presence**, not honesty as
virtue: *"the whole time he's not present, he's not attentive and attuned to what's
going on, because he's afraid of being discovered."*

Chris's own scripted answer, given to him: *"I still live at home because right now I'm
still trying to figure out my life and I'm using every dollar I make to invest in my
career as a cinematographer and as an editor."* With the filter attached: *"if she ain't
into you, then she ain't into you… I might not have what you may want right now, but I
got that one thing — it's called ambition, drive, and a lot of self-discipline. And if
she's not impressed by that, she's not worth your time."*

The bridge to the topic: *"you pretend to be someone, but eventually these people will
meet you at a talk, at a workshop… and you don't have your makeup on, you don't have
your game voice on."*

### The Molly story — and why he refused the compliment

At a marketing mastermind, a woman named **Molly** (who had met him at Atomicon) raised
her hand: *"You know what's refreshing about him? He's exactly who he is online."*
Chris's answer is the point of the story:

> *"That's very kind of you to say, but I find it sad that you have to say things like
> this, because I wonder — why is this the exception? **Why are we applauding people for
> being who they're supposed to be?**"*

### Normal means average

- *"I kind of think about this word normal a lot. What does normal mean? **Normal means
  average.** It's in the middle. It's the statistical average of things."*
- *"I don't know who's going around telling each other that I want to be average. I find
  that to be super insulting. If you were to look at my content and say, 'Chris, I love
  your content because it's average,' I would be horribly offended."*
- The diagnosis: *"we're working really hard to **hide in plain sight**, to blend in.
  That's what we're doing with all of our content."*

### The exit: differentiation, then the four columns

- The definition, restated: **"Branding is intentional differentiation."**
- *"Because when you look at all of who you are, it's a very rare combination that very
  few people are going to be able to claim. This is your superpower."*
- The solution is deliberately anticlimactic: *"You see the solution at least twice a day
  — once in the morning when you're brushing your teeth and once at night when you're
  washing your face."*

**The four-column exercise** — this is **ikigai**, which he does not name in this source
but *does* name explicitly in [[2025-05-29-yt-4ZS8dMwPObk|2025-05-29]] nine days later,
teaching the same four circles with their four intersections (mission / passion /
profession / vocation). See [[../topics/mindset/mindset|mindset]]:

| # | Column | Prompt |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What are you good at?** | trained to do, apprenticed at, *"where you put your 10,000 hours in"* |
| 2 | **What do you love?** | passions, hobbies, *"what idiosyncratic things do you explore when no one's watching?"*, *"if you had all the money in the world, what would you be doing in your free time?"* |
| 3 | **What does the world value?** | *"what is something you're going to be paid for that is in demand at this moment"* |
| 4 | **What does the world need beyond money?** | *"does it need a solution to a much bigger problem?"* |

*"Column one and two is looking inward. Column three and four is looking outward."* Then:
underline anything *"that gives you a little joy, that sparks a little excitement,"* and
*"find the intersection of all those words and design the perfect version of yourself —
the one that plays to as many of your unique traits as possible."*

The stated precondition: **generate without judgment.** He attributes the supporting line
— *"the highest form of intelligence is observation without judgment"* — to a name the
captions render as "Christian Birdie."

> ⚠️ **Caption garble / attribution uncertain:** the quoted line is most commonly
> attributed to **Jiddu Krishnamurti**, which "Christian Birdie" plausibly garbles. Not
> asserted here; recorded as spoken, flagged as unresolved.

### Uma, and the gifts you have vs. the gifts you want

Chris walks the exercise with a friend, **Uma**, who coaches women through corporate
transitions and *"makes it safe for women to show up as themselves."* The unused
ingredient was childhood piano. Asked what made her good, she said (quoted by Chris):
*"I know how to draw out emotions by playing the keys. **Some people play the song, I
play the emotions.**"*

His own limit, offered as the counterweight — and the sharpest line of the section:

> *"If I could sing, I would sing on stage. If I could dance, I would dance my butt off
> on stage while I'm giving my presentation. But what I have to do is **lean in on the
> gifts that I have, not the gifts that I want.** And that's a big difference."*

### The attack on the personal-branding industry

- *"Most of them use the word personal brand because it's on trend, it's popular, it's in
  demand. But what they're really talking about is **how to build sales funnels or
  marketing funnels.**"*
- He concedes the use before rejecting the trade: *"there are reasons why you need to do
  this — performance marketing helps to bring in money, it helps to support your business
  and cash flow. **But the more performance marketing you do, it comes at the cost of
  building long-term brand value.**"*

### Water — the fullest version of the brand-value argument

- Brand value is measurable in practice: *"when you take the logo or the mark of a brand
  that's very valuable and you apply it to something else and it increases the value of
  that thing, you now can recognize how much brand is worth."*
- Water is the proof: *"one of the most ubiquitous available resources on the planet…
  literally comes from the sky… yet there are companies out there who sell you this free
  resource for a little bit to a lot of money. And most of it is built on brand."*
- **Provenance:** *"Fiji claims untouched by man, filtered through a thousand years of
  lava rock. That's its story, that's its provenance. Those brands command premium
  price."*
- **Two destruction tests:** *"all you have to do to prove this is to pull the label off
  the bottle"* — and *"if you take that water and pour it into a glass, you've completely
  devalued that water at this point, because you've removed any story or anything that
  makes it different."*
- **The turn** (this is what makes the analogy his rather than borrowed): *"the majority
  of our body is made up of water. So if the majority of who we are is water — **what is
  the package? What is the story? What is the brand?** Are you unintentionally walking
  around without a label, without a story, to devalue who you are?"*

### The closing position

> *"Instead of looking at this as a marketing platform to go get more customers to fill
> your pipeline… learn about who you are, connect with it authentically, and express that
> in the purest form to the world. Because in the long run that's what's going to make you
> different… exponentially more valuable than what you can make by trying to sell your
> services through the label of brand."*
