# Your Dream Doesn't Need Money, It Needs This — w/ Simon Squibb (2025-05-22)

## Summary

Chris interviews Simon Squibb — the "what's your dream?" street-interview creator — about
how a homeless 15-year-old became someone who sold an agency to PwC and now gives money
to strangers on camera. Chris runs the episode as a **structured challenge** rather than a
friendly profile: he keeps naming the objection the audience will make and forcing Simon
to answer it. The most valuable material for the clone is therefore not the guest's story
but **Chris's interviewing method stated out loud**, several of his own positions
(passionate detachment, identity-is-not-the-company, gift-with-expectation-is-
manipulation, the social-capital counter-theory), and an unusually candid personal
admission near the end about his marriage and the pace of his own life.

## How the episode came about (2025-05-22)

**[CHRIS]** — biography, dated. At the **1 Billion Followers Summit in Dubai**, Chris saw
a crowd around a man he couldn't identify. On a later day, riding down the escalator, the
same man walked out opposite him: *"He goes, 'Chris.'"* They talked; Simon said *"maybe
this was meant to happen,"* gave him the book **What's Your Dream?**, and they agreed to
make content together. Chris was in **Singapore** the day after the summit's award night,
where he saw clips of Simon winning the **$1,000,000 prize** in tears. The episode was
recorded in Los Angeles at the house Simon's ten-person team was staying in.

> This places Chris in **Dubai** and then **Singapore** in early 2025, adjacent to the
> Dubai pricing workshop recorded on [[2025-05-11-yt-8XJAjOUL07I|2025-05-11]].

## Chris's own material (the persona-relevant half)

### His interview method, stated explicitly

- **[CHRIS]** *"I like to get into the early parts of people's lives because **they leave
  clues as to who we're going to become.**"*
- **[CHRIS]** On why he keeps interrupting: *"The reason why I'm ranging left and right is
  because **the plot thickens every time you tell me a story.** Because then it's very
  clear who you become, why you choose to do what it is that you do today."*
- **[CHRIS]** On the adversarial posture: *"What I'm trying to do is **push against this,
  because that's what's going to happen in the comments and then it'll be too late.** I
  want to surface this so that they can either get on one side of the fence or the other
  — and that's up to them."* And: *"Everything you say, I'm like, **what is our audience
  going to say?**"*
- **[CHRIS]** He also declares the thread before pulling it — *"I told you I was going to
  thread it"* — after connecting Simon's own moment of being given a chance to his giving
  money to strangers now.
- **[CHRIS]** On restraint: *"I think I can turn this into a 17-hour podcast and I'm
  trying not to, because you keep opening more doors. It's like this infinite web in
  **Loki**, the sacred timeline — it's got too many branches and we need to prune some
  down."*
- Simon's unprompted outside verdict on the method: *"what I really like about this type
  of interview is that you're thinking what the audience are going to ask, and I want
  those questions answered."*

### Why people cry, and why the underdog story always works

**[CHRIS]** — one of the strongest passages in the episode:

- *"I have a whole theory about why people cry… when you break down love, [it's]
  **understand me and appreciate me** — and you felt it all in that moment and it just
  came out of your eyes."*
- *"My theory about why people cry is because **there's some hurt in there somewhere.** If
  you feel self-love and love all the time, maybe when you feel it, it's like okay, that's
  just normal life. But **something broken gets appreciated and healed a little bit.**"*
- **[CHRIS]** *"**You will never go broke telling a story of an outsider being overlooked,
  the underdog.** Because even if you have all the privilege in your life, we always feel
  like internally there's a child who needs help — **and that's us, by the way** — we feel
  like the odds are stacked against us, no matter how much privilege you have. So when you
  tell that story, the child who's hurting, who's broken, who wants a warm embrace, they
  connect to that moment, and then the tears start to flow **because they feel hurt in
  that moment and feel seen.**"*

### The film-director lens (still active thirty years on)

**[CHRIS]** *"I used to make commercials and music videos for a living… Here's what
happens in the cinematic version I direct of your life. The camera pushes in — it's a
**very long lens, shallow depth of field** — and your eyes are starting to well up. And in
that moment that you realize you've won, **we flash back, cut all these dramatic moments
in your life where everybody tells you you don't deserve to be here**, and that all comes
crashing into the singular moment, and then your eye twitches a little…"* — then, when
Simon corrects the timeframe (five years, not thirty): *"Okay, let me re-edit the thing."*

### Why the audience attacks people who do good — his counter-theory

Simon's explanation was **tribalism** (5,000-person tribe DNA, one hater feels existential).
**[CHRIS] disagrees on camera** — *"I don't know if I agree with that"* — and offers this
instead:

> *"Let me be careful how I say this — or be intentional. **They see a straight white man
> do really well in his life and they're already going to hate you for that.** And then
> you're trying to do something good in the world. And **we're very suspicious of people
> trying to do good in the world, because we have long examples of people pretending to be
> somebody that they're not only to steal from other people.** So our guard's up —
> especially in this hyper-media world — **everyone starts with the knives out first
> before they open their hand** and say welcome to my home. So over time **it's upon you
> and not upon them to show up consistently to reveal your character**… because there are
> too many charlatans out there doing things for nefarious ends."*

**[CHRIS]** — the asymmetry he objects to, stated as his own position: *"I do not
understand why we think if you do good in the world, you must do it for the goodness of
doing it, versus being able to run a business. **Whereas if you do anything else for
profit, no one even bats an eyelash at how much money you make.**"*

### Privilege — named, then set aside, with himself in the frame

**[CHRIS]** *"Number one — I just want to say this, but I don't want to dwell on it.
**You're a white guy knocking on doors, doors open.** You're a guy asking for work who
doesn't have equipment and doesn't have skills, and they say yes. I just want to put that
out there because people are going to say that. **But here's where it's not easy: a lot of
us wouldn't knock on those doors. I put myself in that category.**"*

### Hunger, not an "entrepreneurial muscle"

**[CHRIS]** — reframing Simon's term: *"What you call the entrepreneurial muscle — this
need — I think there's another word for it. **It's just called you're hungry. Literally
hungry**, right? Because you don't have a choice… **So many of us live in a place where we
don't have that kind of backed-into-the-corner feeling. So we just wait and wait and wait.
And some of us just wait too long, until it's too late for us to do something about it.**"*

And the respect, precisely qualified: *"I respect anybody who has the guts to go up to the
door, talk to a bunch of strangers, and give a pretty okay pitch, not knowing if they can
even do it — and then, **being lifted by the confidence of your first sale, have the guts
to say 'let me do that 99 more times'** and not just quit with the first one."*

### Passionate detachment — restated, and challenged

**[CHRIS]** *"I have this kind of personal philosophy about practicing some kind of form
of **passionate detachment**. I want to be passionate when I'm doing things, but when I'm
no longer doing it, **I'm totally detached from it — so I can see things objectively.**"*

✅ **Second attestation** of a named philosophy first recorded on
[[2019-09-25-yt-DGGUB_XlH8k|2019-09-25]] — nearly six years apart, unchanged.

> ⚠️ **Challenged, and not conceded.** Simon pushes back directly: *"I think that's
> oversold to people… it's a bit like saying don't be too much in love because when you
> break up you might cry. There's a part of me like — I'm just going to do the all-in love
> thing… I do fall in love with what I'm working on and I think that allows me to really
> be all in. That's why I've been married for 23 years."* Chris does not concede; he
> presses the inconsistency instead (*"you were madly in love and now as soon as that love
> is over, you're ready to…"*). **The tension is left open in the source and should be
> recorded as an open tension, not resolved in either direction.**

### Identity is not the company

- Simon: losing Fluid felt like *"losing my family again."*
- **[CHRIS]**, asked whether losing "The Futur" would feel like losing part of himself:
  a flat **"No."**
- **[CHRIS]** — the argument, via **Vanilla Sky**: *"If you lost a finger tomorrow, are you
  like **98% less** Simon Squibb? No, of course not. And a finger is much more serious than
  a company. But you're still you… **as long as you're conscious and you have a soul and a
  spirit and you're still able to do what it is that you want to do, you're still you.**"*

### Giving, gifts, and manipulation

- **[CHRIS]** *"A gift is a gift. You give or you don't give, **but you can't give and
  expect people to do things. There's a word for that. It's called manipulation.** …If you
  get good grades, then I'll give you something. If you basically do everything I tell you
  to do, then I will reward you — with love, with appreciation, with acknowledgement, or
  with a roof over your head."*
- **[CHRIS]** cites a science podcast on money and happiness: spending received money **on
  yourself** produces diminishing happiness; being **charged with giving it away** raises
  happiness — *"so if each person who receives the money continues to give it to people,
  the levels of happiness go up."*
- **[CHRIS]** on the **Jason Silva** TED talk — with an on-camera self-correction worth
  noting for fidelity (*"I said the wrong person's name. There's a gentleman, his name is
  Jason Silva"* — he had said "Eric Silva," then stopped the interview to fix it):
  *"In the 21st century the new billionaires aren't the ones who have a billion dollars in
  the bank account. **It's the ones who can make a positive impact on people's lives.**"*
- **[CHRIS]** *"**Do good now. Don't wait until you're 98 to give away your money.** That's
  how most rich people do it — they find God, then they find religion, and then they want
  to give it away. But you can do a lot of good right now in the moment and still build a
  really good business and a life for yourself."*
- **[CHRIS]** *"**I don't have a lot of love for people who use money to make money**,
  because they don't really generate anything in the world. I also don't have a lot of love
  for people who move money around as a means to make money, because they've yet to do
  anything."*

### The social-capital pushback (his substantive counter-argument)

Simon's model: pre-modern tribes helped each other for nothing; money made everything
transactional. **[CHRIS] accepts the diagnosis but rejects the mechanism:**

> *"Here's the pushback. I think **there's social capital**, and it's how through thousands
> of years we can form societies and villages — because **there's a bargain that's made.
> It's not transactional in cash and in money**, but it's like, hey, I have to go and hunt
> today. Can you look after the kids, and when I come back we will share the bounty of what
> it is I do. **There's still a trade there. That's a community trade.**"*

And the correction mechanism: *"somebody's tent falls down and you raise your hand, you
help — and then one day you don't have clean water and no one comes to your aid.
Eventually you're like, this is not my community, I have to leave. **And that person's a
taker.** So eventually it needs to correct itself."* He credits the reciprocity literature
(captions garble the name; see below): *"**it doesn't have to be quid pro quo** — this
exact amount for that exact amount. **But it's through our need to support each other that
we can grow strong.** If an invading tribe came and they all split and left, that's not
much of a tribe either."*

### The C-vs-E framework (invented live)

**[CHRIS]**, building a distinction on the spot to test Simon's giving model: *"We're
going to call one a **C** and one an **E**."* — **C = charity**, **E = entrepreneur.**

- *"When you meet a C, it's like: **I don't know what I'm going to do, just give me
  money.** And more likely than not, they're going to just blow the money — not any
  different than an addict — because they're going to support the same habits that got them
  in this position in the first place."*
- *"You want to give to **entrepreneurs who just need someone to believe in them and a
  little bit of startup capital** so they can get on with their lives and break the system
  in a very good way."*
- The challenge that follows: *"If you're against the charities, **why don't we find more
  E's** — but there has to be a process other than you just walking up to people and saying
  what's your dream. **Because when you find the E, they multiply what you do.**"*
- He also turns it inward on Simon's own team: *"it sounds like you found two E's, not two
  C's."*

### On money questions, and one refusal

**[CHRIS]** refuses to ask the obvious question, and says why: Simon expects *"how do you
justify spending $50,000?"* — Chris: *"**I would never ask that.**"* → *"**Small-minded
question.** Because **it doesn't matter how much you spend. It matters how much money you
make or take.** If you bring in $5 million, 50k is a dumb small amount."*
(Simon, on the refusal: *"If you were good at podcast, you would ask it."*)

He also does the arithmetic on air to back-solve Simon's undisclosed exit — *£20,000/month
× 50 years ≈ £12 million* — and rejects the answer as too low, then drops it: *"I actually
think this whole 'how much' fascinates people, but I don't think it matters."*
**[CHRIS]** on why net worth understates it: *"**brand has value.**"*

### Personal — the most candid passage

**[CHRIS]**, on watching Simon's ten-person team living and working together in one house:

> *"I want to say this as a fan of yours, as a person who's gotten to see behind the
> curtain — seeing this team, **this is a dream for me.** Currently, **full disclosure, my
> wife and I, we do not share this dream. So it creates a lot of friction for us**, because
> she's like: 'Enough. Enough. **We're supposed to be winding down into our chill life and
> you're winding up into the crazy frantic life.**' And she just looks at my calendar and
> gets stressed out just by looking at the calendar. I'm like — **imagine living it.** You
> just look at it and that's too much for you."*

**[CHRIS]** also states, plainly: *"**Almost 30 years**"* married (asked directly).
✅ Consistent with the ~21-year figure given in 2016–17 — both place the marriage at
**~1995**. Wife referred to by role only, per the standing privacy rule.

He declines to discuss the marriage further on the spot (*"that's another 4-hour
episode"*) and instead issues a call to action asking the audience to request it: work-life
integration, raising a child, travelling together.

- **[CHRIS]** on extended adolescence: *"if we look back a couple hundred years ago,
  leaving home as a 13- or 12-year-old boy is pretty normal. **It's only in modern times
  that we hold on to our children much longer. I don't think it's healthy.**"*
- **[CHRIS]** — a face-reading claim, offered as theory: *"I have this theory about reading
  people's faces. **People who have ill intentions, it's on their face. People who worry a
  lot, it's on their face. And people who are joyful inside and out, it's on their face.**"*
- **[CHRIS]** on property: *"**I'm very anti-property.**"* (qualified immediately — a

- **[CHRIS]** restates The Futur's mission: *"our mission is to **teach a billion people
  how to make a living doing what they love**. And we believe that there must be a better
  alternative to the system that exists today."*
- **[CHRIS]** — the metaphor he offers Simon: *"you're like the **British Johnny

  day there's going to be an orchard of entrepreneurs."* (Simon declines the credit —
  *"people watching the content are planting their own seeds"* — and Chris needles the
  modesty: *"It's very British to not take any credit."*)

## Simon Squibb's account (guest material — context only, NOT persona data)

Recorded so the interview is intelligible, and because Chris's questions are shaped by it.
None of this is subject-attributed.

- Father died and mother put him out at **15**; **8 weeks on the street** in England;
  couldn't get benefits, a job, or even beg successfully. Started a **gardening business**
  by knocking on doors, borrowing the homeowners' unused equipment, and taking deposits to
  buy supplies — 12 customers.
- Moved to **Hong Kong at 23** (1997) with ~**£80,000**, slept on a friend's sofa to avoid
  rent. Built **Fluid**, a Hong Kong creative agency (~200 people incl. contract),
  co-founded with **Helen Griffith**, now his wife; **sold it to PwC** at 40 after
  installing a management team (*"if you end up being the only person that can run it, it's
  very hard to sell it"*). Amount under NDA.
- Post-exit: *"I fell into neither want nor need, which is even more dangerous… basically
  you're lost."* Two years off, some depression, then: *"**create a purpose that creates the
  need.**"*
- Won **$1,000,000** at the 1 Billion Followers Summit; says the tears were about
  validation, not money.
- The **business of giving** model as he lays it out: (1) *"act like you've got no money"*
  — *"money makes you weak"*; (2) a **core belief** (his: the education system is broken)
  — *"don't manage a team, **manage a purpose**; hire people on that purpose and they manage
  themselves"*; (3) an **income model tied to the giving**. Stages: AdSense → brand deals
  (~12, ~$150k/deal) → own products (Help Bank, Dream Brew, a clothing line).
- Figures he gave on camera: **£43k** YouTube last 28 days (mostly one video), **$4,897**
  TikTok, **$50,000** to bring 10 of 29 staff to LA for two weeks; started with a **$100
  microphone** his wife gave him and **100 episodes** of the *Good Luck Club* podcast before
  GoDaddy sponsored him at ~15,000 followers; gave his first hire **50% of YouTube revenue**
  instead of salary.
- On judging recipients: *"**I really try to remove judgment.** It's not about whether
  they're a good person or not. It's really about hope."* And: *"The real value isn't the
  money I give people. **It's the exposure** — the chance I give their dream to live in the
  world."*
