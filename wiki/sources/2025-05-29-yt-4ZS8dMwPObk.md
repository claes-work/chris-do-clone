# Content Strategy for Business Owners (That Actually Works) — w/ Brendan Kane (2025-05-29)

> **Attribution:** two-hander with **Brendan Kane** (Hookpoint; author of *One Million
> Followers*, *Hook Point*, *The Guide to Going Viral*). Brendan supplies the framework —

## Summary

★★★ **LANDMARK for the persona, despite being a guest episode.** Kane runs a
format-analysis clinic and critiques six of Chris's own videos side by side. That format
forces Chris into unusual territory: **explaining his own content, his own limits, and
his own psychology out loud.** The result is the single densest source of Chris
self-analysis in the recent corpus — including **the Spock story**, the **operational
mechanism of his introversion strategy**, a **dated reversal of his own position** on
faceless content, and his own account of **why the 50% deal in his most viral video is
set at 50%**.

## ★★★★ The Spock story — self-concept, with an outside verdict attached

Told unprompted at the very end, after Kane explains that his own dominant mode is
"facts and logic":

> **[CHRIS]** *"To share a little personal note with you — I was talking to **my
> therapist** many years ago and she goes, 'Are you a fan of Star Trek?' And I'm like,
> 'Yes.' She goes, 'Which character do you most identify with?' I'm like, **'I aspired to
> be Spock.'** And she goes, **'I've never seen or heard anybody describe it like that.
> You don't see yourself as Spock — but you aspire to be like him.'** And **what I
> described as my hero, she thought was such a tragic character. A person struggling to
> find their humanity.** Where I was like — **Spock always makes the right decision no
> matter what.** And the line, **'the needs of the many far outweigh the needs of the
> few.'** I aspire to be like that."*

Kane's closing read: *"maybe yours is already Spock."*

> ✅ **This lands directly on the Daywalker / "emotion through the cognitive lens"
> self-concept.** It is the same trait — and this time it comes with **a professional
> outsider's reframe of it as tragic rather than aspirational**, which the corpus did not
> previously have. It also supplies a rare dated reference to therapy (*"many years
> ago"*).

## ★★★ Introversion — the mechanism, finally stated

The best operational account in the corpus, extending the
[[2025-05-13-yt-Anr8YYbt2ws|2025-05-13]] landmark from *what he is* to *how he works
around it*:

> **[CHRIS]** *"**I don't like going into rooms of strangers.** I don't think anybody
> does, but **extroverts can just swim in there** and just like, 'Yeah, hey you' — and
> then they're all best friends by the time the party's over. For me, **there's a whole
> strategy about how to do that.** But I found that **through creating content, you get
> invited to speak — and when you speak, there's this introduction. So they know you
> before you know them. So technically, you never really walk into a room full of
> strangers.** You just have to **wait long enough and deal with your discomfort** so that
> somebody who's seen you, who wants to talk to you, will come up to you. And **I try to
> make myself as approachable as possible in all formats** so that there's not this weird
> 'he doesn't want to be talked to.'"*

And the causal claim that follows it — the personal brand as an introvert's inbound
machine:

> **[CHRIS]** *"That's also just **the underlying reason why we build personal brands.**
> I've built businesses where you actually have to go out and seek out the business,
> versus the business coming to you. And **that's why I invested in a personal brand —
> because I just know my personality and I don't want to be that person banging on
> doors**, and because it takes so much time and energy to do it the other way."*

He also **declines a format on personality grounds**, which is unusual and specific:
asked about "man on the street," **[CHRIS]** *"I tested it and I didn't like it… **It's
because my personality. I'm not extroverted.** Just going up to a bunch of random
people… **I imagine that to be just an issue of tonnage** — you've got to go through a
bunch of people, and then that's the one."* (Kane confirms the economics: Simon Squibb
*"goes through like 60 people and gets two or three clips out of it."*)

## ★★★ A dated reversal — AI makes the human non-optional

Asked whether you can go viral without showing your face, Chris **flags the change in his
own position before giving it**:

> **[CHRIS]** *"**I would have answered this question differently two years ago**, but I'm
> going to answer it this way. With the onslaught of **AI-generated video, voice, and
> scripts**, it's now **more important than ever to put YOU in it so that people know a
> human is involved. Every time I realize it's AI content, I don't want to consume it at
> all.** That doesn't mean you have to put your face in it — but your voice. **If you have
> a really good voice and maybe not such a good face, use your voice. Use what you've got,
> things that make you uniquely you.**"*

> **Synthesis flag:** this is a **new face of the AI position** and — more importantly —
> **the first time in the corpus he explicitly dates a reversal of his own view.** It must
> be recorded with its "two years ago" marker intact.

## ★★★ Explaining his own most-viral video (the 50% deal)

Kane replays the 40M-view short *"When clients don't want to pay what you're worth."*
Chris then explains the strategy behind numbers he has never justified on camera before:

- **Why 50%:** *"**Because I'm going to take the risk. If you take on the risk, you need to
  make more money than that.**"* — anchored on *"in business, **all profit comes from
  risk. Peter Drucker.** So whoever is willing to carry the risk makes more money."*
- **On the objection:** *"Some people are also saying, '**Chris, 50% is lunacy. It's
  outrageous.**' — **It kind of is, and they knew that**, fully expecting them to
  negotiate with me. **But I'm not going to negotiate myself to like 10% right away. Then
  they will say 3%.** This is where we're going to get stuck. I don't want to work for 3%
  — **I have to send you 600 new customers before I actually see any kind of real
  margins.** Doesn't make sense for me."*
- **The actual purpose of the whole manoeuvre:** *"**the whole point of setting up this
  deal was to let them know that the $1,000 is really cheap.** Just pay the $1,000
  already. **Be a cheap bastard — just pay it.** You don't have to deal with any of this
  stuff."*

## ★★★ His own content, analysed by him

- **The format he actually wins with:** *"The most viral short videos we've made are
  usually in a **workshop format**. There's a whiteboard and there's a **heated topic that
  really riles people up emotionally** — and **you can actually be on either side of the
  argument and still feel like you're still right.**"* The logo-pricing video is *"the most
  high-producing video we've ever made."*
- **The single underlying theme, confirmed:** asked whether it all comes back to
  self-valuation — **[CHRIS]** *"Yes, that's the underlying thing. Because **money, what
  you charge, is an extension of how you see yourself in terms of your value.**"*
- **On Mo, his on-camera foil:** *"I always thought my videos need Mo in it because **Mo
  likes to argue and Mo brings this tension energy**… what our audience feels in him is
  that he represents a certain kind of **scumbaggy kind of client who wants their cake and
  to eat it** — they don't want to pay you. **And I'm asking them to confront that
  issue.**"*
- **The editing rule he gives his team:** *"**We know what we're talking about, but we're
  not cutting the video for us. We have to cut it for someone who's coming in cold.**"* He
  attributes the 50M-vs-9,100 gap between two clips from the *same workshop* to which
  editor cut them.
- **A real and unusual channel fact:** *"we're one of those channels that — I think it's
  kind of strange — **we actually get more views for our long-form content than we do for
  the cut-down of that long-form content**, because people who like the kind of teaching I
  do **are in it for the long haul.**"*
- **Self-aware limitation, volunteered:** *"**I don't speak in sound bites. I'm not
  thinking about the hooks** — which maybe I need to, if I want to grow the audience much
  faster."*
- **He admits he doesn't watch his own work:** *"I actually don't even look at my content
  that much."*

## ★★★ Structure vs. chaos — his sharpest formulation yet

> **[CHRIS]** *"A lot of creatives — and I taught creatives for many years prior to
> getting onto social media — **they swim in chaos** and they like this idea like 'I'll
> just figure it out, I'll invent things.' And **they're delusional, because what they
> think is original — it's just a lack of history, what's come before.** So that
> expression is cliché at this point: don't reinvent the wheel. **Reinvent how it's
> used.** And that becomes much more interesting."*

> **[CHRIS]** *"**Structure and creativity go really well together. Whereas chaos and
> creativity — it's just a mess**, because you don't know what… and it's probably one of
> the reasons why **a lot of super-creative people struggle with creating consistently and
> getting consistent results.**"*

With the cooking frame: *"if you want to do that dish right not one time but a hundred
times — especially when it's critical, if you're trying to impress the in-laws — you want
to be able to make that again and again."*

## ★★★ Originality — Picasso, and the cross-industry rule

> **[CHRIS]** *"I'm going to butcher the quote, but I think it's **Picasso** who said
> something like **the secret to originality is your ability to hide your sources.** So
> **if you're in cinema, be inspired by music, be inspired by plays — not other directors
> directing the same genre of film that you are**, because **you'll just be the poor
> person's version of that.**"*

Applied as a warning: *"if you're in the real estate industry, **don't copy other real
estate professionals**, because you'll just be seen as a copycat."*

## The Dip, and the label inside the jar

- **[CHRIS]**, on Kane's datum that the average channel needs **492 videos** to reach 1M
  subscribers: *"I want to reference **Seth Godin. The Dip.** It's fun in the beginning —
  you get followers and attention and return on investment. Then it starts to get harder
  and it feels like you're putting in a lot of work and not getting results. And then you
  get into the valley, the dip. **This is where most people quit.** … **What if you're at
  video 400 and you're like, I'm not making progress? It could be 92 more videos where you
  actually have that breakthrough.**"*
- **[CHRIS]** *"Have you heard the expression, **it's hard to read the label when you're
  inside the jar?** … Most people are not that objective when they're making stuff because
  they're involved. They have attachment, subjectivity, biases — 'I worked really hard on
  this one.' **That's why people hire coaches and consultants.**"*
- **[CHRIS]** on batch-producing content: *"when you make 10 pies at a time, what people
  don't realize is it's pretty crappy and **you serve the other nine cakes too.** … 'It was
  not good, but now I've got nine more not-good things to deal with — so maybe I'm giving
  up on this person.'"* → *"you want to **innovate and learn as you go, versus moving
  towards efficiency. Efficiency is good once you figure out your formula** — but until
  then, stay away from that."*
- **[CHRIS]**, on deleting weak posts: *"so it's like **you make 10 bad cakes, get rid of
  the 10 so people don't have a bad sample.**"*

## ★★★ A live man-on-the-street demo — the Socratic method compressed

Chris improvises the format on the spot against a deliberately hostile stranger (played
by a team member), and it is his discovery method in miniature:

> *"How much do you charge an hour?" — "$25." — "And what do you do for 25 an hour?" —
> "Graphic design… websites." — **"So why not $50 an hour, or why not $10? How'd you
> arrive at 25?"** — "I think it's probably too much for people to pay for my experience."
> — **"And do you have evidence that led you to this conclusion?"** — "No." — **"So what
> are you basing your beliefs on?"** — "Honestly, I don't know." — **"What if I told you
> there are a lot of people who are just like you who are charging ten times more, and
> happily doing it, with happy customers? How would you respond to that?"** — "I would ask
> how do I do that?" — **"Great. Let's have a conversation."***

## Brendan Kane's framework (guest material — context only, NOT persona data)

- **Formats** = storytelling blueprints reused 5–10+ times by the same creator; ~300 in
  Hookpoint's library. Most predate social media (man-on-the-street → *Tonight Show*,
  1954; two-characters-one-lightbulb → *Austin Powers*, 1997).
- **Five steps:** understand formats → select and analyse one → ideate inside it → produce
  **one video at a time** → analyse with the "mirror" (gold/silver/bronze cross-analysis).
- **Brutal truths** as he frames them: it's storytelling, not hacks; *"your social media
  profile is not your website and your organic content is not your ads"*; shadowbanning is
  *"99.9%"* a myth (he cites managing **$200M** in ad spend); *"anything can go viral with
  the right context"*; ROI *"is the size of your vision."*
- **Performance drivers** used on Chris's clips: **perspective shift**, **work-to-wow
  ratio**, **promise of value**, **effect on viewer**, **tension**.
- His own brand story: three books, a stated dedication about suppressed voices, and a
  self-described **facts-and-logic** dominant communication mode.
