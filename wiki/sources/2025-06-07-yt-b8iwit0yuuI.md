### ★★★★ The Futur's commercial problem, stated plainly

> **[CHRIS]** *"One of the technical challenges I have is: **we have a good-sized, really engaged
> audience that are incapable of buying anything that we do.** They will show up to these things in
> droves, but **a vast majority of them are not the right fit.**"*

This is an unusually direct admission of a structural mismatch between reach and revenue, and it sits

**The pricing response, disclosed:** at *"500 bucks a month, or 200–250 a month, it's not enough"* —
so *"**what we're doing is we're actually planning on raising the price every benchmark we hit**, so
that we get it to the price point where it needs to be."*

**The cadence correction, also disclosed:** *"we went to **once a quarter** and now we're back to
**once a month** — because once a quarter **put a lot of pressure on us closing a lot of people. It
was a do-or-die thing, and we weren't executing properly.**"*

### ★★★ Pitch vs. invitation, and permission as the standard

He draws the distinction out of the guest rather than being handed it:

> **[CHRIS]** *"Is that more than just the language you use, or is the structure of it very different?
> Because instead of saying, '**hey, I've got a program, and this is what it costs**' — that sounds
> like a pitch to me — versus saying, '**I'm inviting a couple of people just like you to explore X, Y
> and Z.**'"*

And his summary of why he likes the sequence: *"it's **very permission-based**, which is what I like.
It's like: **only do this if it gives you joy. Otherwise, I'm good. You're good.**"* ✅ Credited to
**Daniel Priestley** as *"**with or without you energy**"* — *"I'm going to talk to a few people who
are interested in this. If you are, let me know."*

### ★★★ Why you can't jump from content to a call to action

> **[CHRIS]** *"You don't want to rush from content to call to action, because **it's too abrupt of a
> step. It's too much tension there.** And it helps you and the potential customer save a little bit
> of time — **are you right for me? Am I right for you? Those are the two things that people are
> asking themselves.**"*

**On what a lead magnet has to be:** *"something of high value, something that demonstrates your
expertise, your point of view — so that **it's a sample of what you're going to get if you work with
each other.**"*

## Matthew Lakajev's system (guest — context only, NOT persona data)

Summarised so Chris's questions are intelligible. **None of this is Chris's method.**

- **Thesis**: *"you don't need more than 500 followers on LinkedIn to actually make sales"*; *"**I
  don't believe that content is a requirement for selling** — content is **an accelerant** to start
  more high-quality conversations."* And: *"there's an overemphasis put on brand, especially for
  people starting out."*
- **Three set-up steps**: (1) build connections via **Sales Navigator look-alikes** of your best past
  clients, referencing a shared connection rather than going cold (*"if you don't use [the weekly
  requests], you lose them"*); (2) **audit the profile** so it names one avatar and is legible on
  mobile; (3) put a **lead magnet in the featured section** so you can tell which posts actually
  convert, not which get impressions.
- **Email**: keep the list **narrow** (*"if I call my newsletter 'financial tips', it doesn't resonate
  with that ideal client"*), a **14-day value sequence**, then a soft ask. Cites **Daniel Priestley**
  on micro-niches.
- **Invitation over pitch**, **AIDA**, an **offer doc** in Notion/Google rather than a PDF (*"it feels
  more intimate"*), and suggesting times rather than sending a calendar link (*"the rule of reciprocity
  kicks in"*).
- **The call**: pre-frame with permission and a stated time limit; 20–30 minutes of questions to
  *"stretch the gap"*; ask three times whether anything is missing before price; get **emotional
  commitment before sending the payment link**.
- On pricing psychology: *"people tend to pay about **three times as much for something that's a
  perceived one-off** compared to a retainer."* And *"**when people don't pay, they don't pay
  attention**"* — said of **his own** $45k/month mastermind membership and his two-tier program, **not
  of Chris's**.
