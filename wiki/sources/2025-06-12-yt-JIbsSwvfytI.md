# How to Talk MONEY Without Killing the SALE (2025-06-12)

> **Attribution:** ✅ **A solo monologue — Chris only, start to finish.** No guests. Everything is his
> except two credited lines.

## Summary

★★★★★ **A landmark for `topics/pricing`.** The best explanation in the corpus of **why clients ghost**
— argued from the buyer's side and then **demonstrated against himself, in the present tense** — plus
**the budget-upfront script**, and a genuinely surprising qualification: **he tells most viewers NOT to
use value-based pricing.**

## Key claims (2025-06-12)

### ★★★★ How long a close should take — it scales with the size of the problem

*"The answer is **it depends**… **depends on the size of the problem you're trying to solve.** If you're
going in for **a toothache**, it should be a fairly straightforward procedure. But if you have **an
internal organ issue, possibly cancer — WE SHOULD NOT RUSH INTO ANY PRESCRIPTIVE ACTION AT ALL.**"* →
so six- and seven-figure engagements take longer, *"because **people need to do the DUE DILIGENCE**:
is this going to break? **Are you who you say you are?**"*

★★★★★ **But fit is a first-call question regardless:** *"ideally, you're able to **assess the client's
needs and determine a fit ON THE VERY FIRST CALL. And part of the fit check is THE BUDGET PART.**"*

### ★★★★★ Why creatives avoid the money conversation — the mechanism

> ★★★★★ *"A lot of creatives struggle with this because **they're uncomfortable talking about money.
> THEY HAVE CHALLENGES ABOUT SELF-WORTH**, and they want to delay the money conversation as long as
> possible — because they feel that **if they say the number and the client says no, IT'S GOING TO BE
> NOT JUST A REJECTION OF THE PROPOSAL, BUT A PERSONAL REJECTION — and you're going to FEEL LIKE A
> FAILURE.**"*

★★★★ **So what happens instead:** *"**you'll do small talk. You'll try to be friends with the person.**
And then you'll get to the end of the allotted time and **you'll STILL HAVE NOT TALKED ABOUT BUDGET.**
So then you'll hurriedly say: '**why don't I send you a proposal?**'… and **you'll work hours, sometimes
DAYS, on the proposal. You'll send it. AND YOU'LL NEVER HEAR FROM THEM AGAIN.**"*

### ★★★★★ Ghosting, explained from the buyer's side

> ★★★★★ *"**Put yourself in the role of the person who's THE BUYER, not the seller.**… you're sitting
> there thinking about money: **can I afford this? Is this more than what I need? Are you the right
> person for me?** And **they're uncomfortable that it's taking so long for YOU to talk about money.**
> And eventually **THEY HAVE A NUMBER. Even though they don't say it, even though they might not admit
> it, THEY HAVE A NUMBER IN MIND. And if you go beyond that number, THEY'RE SPOOKED** — 'maybe I
> budgeted too low, maybe I was overthinking this.'"*
>
> ★★★★★ *"So when they receive that number in an email, as a PDF, **they're now stuck with the
> dilemma: WHAT DO I DO NOW? I've already wasted all this time talking to Bob or Mary. Do I really want
> to call them or send them a whole letter? And actually, I've got A THOUSAND OTHER THINGS I need to do
> today. I just need to think about this. IT'S CAUSING ME TOO MUCH STRESS TO MAKE A DECISION. So I just
> wait.**"* → *"and as the hours turned into days and days turned into weeks, **sometimes they've
> already moved on.**"*

### ★★★★★ And he demonstrates it against himself — in the present tense

> ★★★★★ *"I'll give you a perfect example. **Right now, quite literally, we're having our pool worked
> on** because there's some water leakage problems. And when they open up one problem, they find a new
> problem. And then I got another proposal sent over and **it's not a little bit of money. It's like
> \$7,500. I DID NOT WAKE UP THIS MORNING THINKING I'M GOING TO PAY ANOTHER \$7,500** on top of the
> nine I've already spent. **And so I'M DELAYING AND DELAYING — and the contractor is wondering when
> are you going to make a decision, so they're not sending their people out. YOU SEE THE PROBLEM? WE'RE
> GUILTY OF IT ON BOTH SIDES.**"*

✅ **This is what makes the section persuasive.** He does not describe buyer behaviour from above; he
catches himself doing it **while it is happening**, with the number attached.

★★★★ **The asymmetry of control:** *"**you can't control what it's like to be a client — but you CAN
control what it's like to be a SELLER of services.** So **you owe it to yourself to be super clear
upfront about WHAT it is that you do, HOW LONG it's going to take, and especially HOW MUCH IT'S GOING
TO COST.**"*

### ⚠️★★★★★ He de-recommends value-based pricing — to most of his own audience

> ⚠️★★★★★ *"There's a lot that I personally talk about in terms of **VALUE-BASED PRICING. THAT'S AN
> ADVANCED PRICING STRATEGY. I DON'T RECOMMEND IT FOR EVERYONE** — especially if you're probably
> watching this video, **IT'S MORE THAN WHAT YOU NEED.** Just sit down and think: **what should I charge
> for work like this?** And **build a little bit of flexibility into your language.**"*

✅✅ **Notable, and it should not be smoothed over.** Value-based pricing is one of the frameworks this
corpus most associates with him. Here he classes it as **advanced, optional, and probably wrong for
the viewer** — consistent with the *"value-based pricing is optional"* note already recorded, and the
sharpest statement of it.

★★★★ **The flexible-language script he recommends instead:** *"'**this is what it should START at.** And
if there's nothing crazy, I can deliver the project for that price. **But if you want to add other
kinds of things, we can sit down and do a more customised, bespoke solution — BUT IT WILL COST
MORE.**'"*

★★★★★ **The magnitude check, and why it matters even at the top end:** *"even if it's a high six-figure
or seven-figure job, **you don't want to go through weeks of procurement and due diligence ONLY TO FIND
OUT THEY WERE THINKING 50,000 AND YOU WERE SITTING THERE COMING IN AT 500 GRAND.**"*

### ★★★★★ THE POWER TIP — the budget-upfront script

> ★★★★★ *"'**Out of respect for your time and mine, I'd like to discuss the budget upfront — so that if
> it's not a good fit, we don't have to waste time knowing that it's not going to work out anyways.**'
> And then you just tell them. '**Now, before we get into it — I'M NOT EVEN COMMITTING TO THIS — but I
> just want to let you know THE BALLPARK PRICE is going to be [X].**' **And so you need to then BE
> STILL and see how they respond. 'How does that sound to you?'**"*

✅ **Note the two de-risking devices**: the reason given is *their* time as well as his, and *"I'm not
even committing to this"* lets the number be said without being a quote. ★★★★ **Then the same
then-shut-up discipline**: *"you need to **BE STILL** and see how they respond."*

★★★★ **And what the answer buys you:** *"'that sounds perfect', 'we're in the ballpark', 'you're a
little bit high', 'it's actually a lot less than I thought' — **you're getting VALUABLE INFORMATION at
this point.**"*

### ★★★★★ The graceful exit — and it names temperament as a legitimate reason

> ★★★★★ *"'**I cannot in good faith move forward knowing that we're not going to be compatible with
> each other**' — for a number of reasons. **It could be budget, scheduling, expertise, or JUST A GOOD
> CHEMISTRY FIT.** … '**you want to move really fast, I don't move fast**' — or **I move really fast and
> I can see that you need days to make decisions, and it just doesn't work for me.**"* → *"'**the best
> thing for us to do right now is for me to CONNECT YOU WITH SOMEONE WHO'S MORE COMPATIBLE with you,
> with your budget, your timeline, and YOUR TEMPERAMENT**' — and shake hands, and hopefully sometime in
> the future we can work together."*

### ★★★★ Trust the body before the words — credited

★★★★ *"**I heard this clip from VANESSA VAN EDWARDS when she was talking to STEVEN BARTLETT**, and she
goes: **'The body lives in reality. It's only the mind that lives in imagination. So when we say
something that's not true, our body will communicate that.'**"* → his application: *"you say 'would you
like to move forward?' and they say yes. **So the body says no, the mouth says yes.** That's telling you
**something is not connecting. TRUST THE BODY BEFORE YOU LISTEN TO THE WORDS.**"*

⚠️ **The formulation is Van Edwards's, quoted from another show.** Chris's contribution is the
application to the close.
