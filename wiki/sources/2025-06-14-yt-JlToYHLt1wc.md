# He Designed Beats, Apple Products… Then Changed the Game — w/ Robert Brunner (2025-06-14)

> **⚠️ ATTRIBUTION — GUEST-DOMINATED.** The guest is **Robert Brunner**: founder of **Ammunition**,
> former **director of industrial design at Apple**, former **Pentagram** partner (San Francisco),
> designer of **Beats by Dre**. **The large majority of this episode is his**, and his material is
> summarised below **as context only**. Chris's own contributions are marked **[CHRIS]** and are the
> persona data.

## Summary

An interview about craft, clients, and when to stop designing. Chris is in interviewer mode
throughout, so the harvest is smaller than a solo episode — but it is unusually **craft-specific**:
his **print-it-out test**, a **buzzword red flag** and **sales rules** from the Blind years, an
**Art Center** biographical detail, and a genuinely well-posed tension he raises himself — **the
"good enough revolution" versus the case for greatness** — followed by his own statement of **the
perfection trap**, including a waste argument the corpus has not had before.

## Chris's own material

### ★★★ The perfection trap — and the waste argument

> **[CHRIS]** *"It's such a tricky position for creative people to be in, whatever your discipline is,
> because **there's this thing where we want to make something perfect. Maybe that's not possible, but
> we try to — and then we never release anything.** So we don't have the opportunity to learn from that
> first iteration and improve on it. **Yet we also don't want to release something prematurely,
> because it can create a lot of waste in the world.** Wasted paper, wasted materials, and it winds up
> in the landfill. **That feels really irresponsible.** And so there's just **this very narrow window
> that you've got to hit — and it's not easy to do, otherwise everyone would be great.**"*

Both horns are his, and the **environmental** framing of premature release is new to the corpus.

**With the confession attached:** *"I remember when I was a lot younger and I was working for
somebody. I was working on something like — **I don't know what the finish line looks like, so I have
to show someone.** And they were like, 'yeah, you did it,' or '**you actually overworked it.**' And
that's another problem too — **now it feels very contrived**, or just unnecessary."*

### ★★★★ The question he teed up: the good-enough revolution vs. greatness

> **[CHRIS]** *"Years ago I read this article in **Wired** magazine, and **it affected me in a lot of
> different ways.** It was titled something like **the good enough revolution** — there was a time and
> age when we wanted the very best of everything. **But if it's good enough and it's cheaper and easier
> to use and has higher utility, it's good enough.** The example they talked about was **drones
> replacing fighter jets**: if a drone goes down, no one dies; and you could have one in the air 24/7,
> deployable at any point. **But that seems to be going right up against this idea of making great
> things**, and why great design can get people to love your company or tell a story. **Where do you
> net out on that?**"*

He does not resolve it himself here — he poses it as a live tension. **Record it as a question he
holds, not a position he takes.**

### ★★★ Craft: the print-it-out test

> **[CHRIS]** *"Even in simple print terms, someone will do a beautiful layout and **the first thing
> I'll say is: have you printed that out yet? Can you even read that?** Because on screen you can zoom
> in and zoom in and it's not a problem. And when they print it out it's like — **yeah, I might be
> getting old, but I don't think a normal person can read this, given that it's four-point type.**"*

The general principle he states: *"**all the designers I really respect and look up to make a strong
point about getting your hands in it, prototyping and testing** — because one thing we don't get on
the screen is **a sense of scale.**"* And: *"when you see how fabric moves, or the way it feels
against your skin, **there's something to be said about just being able to hold something — the
physicality part of it.**"*

### ★★★ From the Blind years — two qualification heuristics

- ★★★ **Rules for salespeople**, stated as a lesson learned: *"when we were still in the service
  space, **we would have to make rules for our salespeople** — because otherwise **every job looked
  like a great opportunity**, and we were overwhelmed with the kinds of things that were opportunities
  that **turned out to be totally lame.**"*
- ★★★ **The buzzword red flag**: *"there was a time in advertising when there were so many young
  people as creative directors and art directors on the agency side — and we're on the production side
  — where **if they used too many buzzwords, we're out.** It was just dropping jargon and buzzwords and
  sounding too self-important, and none of them knew anything about what they were doing. So we're
  like: **yeah, this is going to be a horrible process. I'm not really that interested in working with
  them.**"*

### ★★★ Story as the accelerant — credited

> **[CHRIS]** *"I've heard **Michael Margolis** say this in our conversation — he wrote the book
> ***Story 10x*** — he said something like **a product or service without a story is a commodity.**"*

Applied to Beats: *"Beats was a product that needed a good story, and **Dre was that person** — he had
such a personal brand that he could attach it to something like that."* ✅ Brunner confirms it
independently: *"That's 100% true… **the story attached to the product was the accelerant.** It pushed
it into the cultural conversation right away."*

### ⚠️ A rumour Chris raised that the primary source did NOT corroborate

> **[CHRIS]** *"I've heard the long untold story about how **it was Will.i.am's idea** and he shared it
> with somebody, and it became this thing — I think he shared it with Dr. Dre. Do you know about the
> backstory and the origin of all this stuff?"*

**Brunner, who was there "not day zero, but day one," says he has not heard that version** and gives
Jimmy Iovine's account instead (Adidas approached Dre about sneakers; Iovine, fresh from the U2 iPod
with Steve Jobs, said *"fuck sneakers — let's do speakers"*). He allows only: *"I don't know if
Will.i.am had that idea at the same time or talked to him about it… **those things, as time goes on,
kind of develop a life of their own.** I believe the inception story really started with Jimmy and
Dre."*

> ⚠️ **Record as an uncorroborated rumour Chris repeated, explicitly not confirmed by the person best
> placed to know.** Do not let it enter the wiki as fact.

### ★★ Biography and asides

- ★★ **Art Center**: *"I went to Art Center and **I found a lot of kinship with the people in the
  industrial design program. They worked the hardest and had incredible craftsmanship, like drafting
  skills** — and I could see that."*
- ★★ **He inoculates against his own bias before asking about craft decline**: *"a fairly typical
  thing to do is to always **criticise the generation coming after you** as not as capable and not as
  good. And I thought — **this must be going on forever, because it's always 'it was better in my
  day.'**"*
- ★★ **On youthful certainty**: *"maybe we've all been there as young people and thought **I'm going to
  change the world, I'm going to break everything.** And then you get a little bit on the other side of
  experience and age, and **you start to realise how foolish that all sounds.**"*
- ★★ **He raises the Steve Jobs question directly, and flags his own hesitation**: *"**I'm kind of
  reluctant to bring this up**, but… Steve Jobs is not known as a nice guy. He wanted what he wanted,
  and he was a bit of a tyrant, I think, if the stories are true."* (Brunner: *"first of all, I didn't
  work with Steve… one of my running jokes is I was between jobs when I was there."*)
- ★★ **Jason Fried, on the design principle he respects**: *"whether you love him or not, the thing you
  respect is **there's nothing decorative. Everything that you see — if it looks like wood, it's made
  from wood. If it looks like metal, it's made from metal.**" ⚠️ The captions leave the referent of
  *"him"* ambiguous; recorded as heard.
- ★★ **The rapid-fire round he runs** (favourite chair, shelving system, drawing instrument, musical
  device, timepiece, eyeglass company) — and the joke he makes of it: *"**see, it's the industrial
  designer who draws a blank when asked about industrial design.**"*
- ★★ **On AI as a topic**: *"I know we could have gone down the rabbit hole of AI, but that seems to be
  **the topic du jour.**"* — a rare instance of him *declining* the subject.

## Robert Brunner's material (guest — context only, NOT persona data)

- **Identity**: *"I'm a maker"*; raised by a creative-engineer father and an artist/craftsperson
  mother. Worked *with* **Paula Scher** for ten years and names her a major influence.
- **Ammunition's model shift**: the epiphany that *"as designers we really work cheaply"* while the IP
  they create *"goes out in the world and makes hundreds of millions"* — hence **revenue shares and
  equity stakes** (~40 companies in the 2012–2016 hardware boom). The effect he cares about is not the
  upside: *"**we no longer were like the vendor — we became part of their business** and we had skin in
  the game. And so we were respected and listened to."* Roughly **60%+ still traditional fee work.**
- **Qualification**: *"it's almost always about the people… **money and a good idea are not
  extraordinary assets.**"* The pattern he looks for: *"**experience and confidence and intellect
  combined with some humility.**"* Red flags: an *"a-hole factor"* showing up early, and founders who
  discount his experience — *"**they're not rules — there's some physics here.**"*
- **On mediocrity**: *"there's a thing in society… **the idea that not failing is winning. And that's
  what breeds mediocrity.**"* → *"**mediocrity can cost you more than any failure ever could**…
  **greatness doesn't come easy. It isn't packaged or polished or perfect. It asks for sweat and effort
  and sometimes pain. It asks you to stand in the fire, when you could be in the shade of
  mediocrity.** … It's about **breaking free from this trap of good enough.**"*
- **On leaders**: *"the leaders have **a non-negotiable purpose that's above financials.**"* For Dre:
  *"**people are not hearing my music**"; for Iovine: the power of music and connection.
- **Beats marketing**, as told to him: *"**our marketing strategy is: a lot of people owe me a lot of
  favours.**"*
- **On AI**: automation has already erased skills his studio built (contextual imagery), *"and that
  frees you up to do something else"*; but *"I don't believe they'll ever hit that level of
  connectivity and greatness that an individual sweating and grinding to get it just right will."* He
  does accept the role shifting *"more towards producer, editor, director."*
- **On when to stop**: *"if you keep investing in something you start to move away from that purity of
  the idea"* — and *"that comes from **the school of screwing stuff up because you went too far.**"*
  Plus: *"**version 1.2 of something always does exponentially better than version one.**"*
- **On feedback**: *"**my nose gets too close to it**"*; *"**you're only as strong as the backs of the
  people who carry you**"*; hire people better than you; *"you don't have to always take their input
  either."*
