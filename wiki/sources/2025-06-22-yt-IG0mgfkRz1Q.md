## ★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★ The format, stated as a design choice

> ★★★★ *"We had this crazy idea: **what if we had an ongoing series — a limited series — on things that
> we're really interested in: THE MUTUAL OVERLAP, and THE POINTS OF CONFLICT, and the points of
> agreement.**"* → the opening question: *"**when should you reveal your true self — or IS IT EVEN
> POSSIBLE to be 100% truly yourself?**"*

✅ **Building disagreement into the premise** is consistent with the publish-the-pushback practice
attested in the Young Guns episodes — here it is planned rather than tolerated.

### ★★★★★ He tests a claim against his own experience before accepting it

> ★★★★★ *"I saw somewhere somebody talking about this — I think there were therapists — and they were
> talking about how **when we have a negative emotion and we keep it inside, THE NEGATIVE EMOTION
> GROWS**, and the best way to treat that is **to SPEAK that negative emotion.** And **I was trying to
> use MY LOGICAL BRAIN to understand this conversation around emotion**, and I was thinking: **is there
> a time when I felt a negative emotion where, when I kept it in, it grew — and when I said it out
> loud, it diminished?** And I think **it holds true to most things. I COULD NOT FIND A USE CASE OR AN
> EXAMPLE WHERE THAT WASN'T [true].**"*

✅ **A documented method, not just a belief:** he takes a claim about emotion, **runs it against his own
history looking for a counter-example**, and accepts it only when he fails to find one. ✅ Consistent
with *"I aspired to be Spock"* and *"logical access to emotion"* already in
[[../persona/biography|biography]] — **this is that self-description in operation.**

⚠️ **The underlying claim is the therapists' as he relays it**, not his own finding; **what is his is
the test.**
