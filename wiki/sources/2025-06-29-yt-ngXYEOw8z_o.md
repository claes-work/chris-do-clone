# I Grew 5 Million Followers On Social Media DOING THIS ONE THING (2025-06-29)

> **Attribution:** short two-hander with **Mo**. Mo asks the question and gives one outside read of
> Chris near the end (marked). Everything else is Chris.

## Summary

★★★ Eight minutes, unusually high signal. Chris explains **leading with generosity** — including a
complete, worked script for approaching a stranger at a conference — grounds it in **the neglected
child** and **reciprocity as social infrastructure**, then makes two of the strongest self-concept
statements in the corpus: **"underneath a graphic designer is a teacher"** and **"I care about people,
but I don't care about people's opinions of me."** Closes on the paradox principle: *"if you want to
be an impressive person, stop trying to impress people."*

## Key claims (2025-06-29)

### ★★★★ Lead with generosity — and why guards are up in the first place

> *"**We're always walking around wondering: what does somebody want from us?** What do you want from
> me? **Look in your DMs. Look in your inbox.** Look at how people come up to you at a conference or a
> networking event. **Your guard's up, man. Your guard's up all the time** — you're trying to steal my
> time, my attention, my money, my friends, my status. **And until we start to feel comfortable with
> somebody, we're not going to really entertain those ideas.**"*

> *"And I think the thing that is really disarming to people is **to lead with generosity. Not because
> it's a scheme. It's not a Trojan horse — but because it's generally how you operate. It's part of
> your operating system.**"*

### ★★★★ The conference script, worked end to end

His demonstration is worth keeping verbatim because of **where the introduction falls**:

> *"Hey, I see you there. **You look a little lost in the sea of people. Tell me what's going on.**"*
> → *"Well, I don't know anybody here."* → *"**Who would you like to meet?**"* → *"I don't know, maybe
> this kind of person."* → *"**Well, let's go find them together. By the way, my name is Chris.**"*

**The name comes last.** The offer of help precedes the introduction entirely.

Then the follow-through, later in the event: *"Hey Chris, what are you doing here? **Is it your job to
just meet randos in the corner and introduce them to people?**"* — *"Well, no, actually I'm going to
be speaking."* — *"Oh, what are you speaking about?"* → *"**You see, we start to show up for each
other.** 'Is there still room? Can I sit up front and support you in any way?' **And then that's the
beginning of a relationship.**"*

And the scaling claim: *"**Now you can do this at scale using content.** People are spending a lot of
time listening to you kind of as if they feel like you are speaking to them. **When it comes from the
heart, when it comes from a genuine place, I think people can feel that.**"*

### ★★★★ The neglected child — second attestation of the mechanism

> *"Like I told you, **we all want to be seen, to be included.** And there's some part of you in that
> moment — and when I say you, I mean all of you who are listening or watching — **is a child who was
> neglected at some point. No matter how much love you got, it was never enough. And we should not put
> a container on our love**, right? So it could be more. Fine. **So you felt left out. You felt
> overlooked, passed up on.**"*

✅ **This is the same mechanism as the underdog belief** from [[2025-05-22-yt-PjP5wvemDLo|2025-05-22]]
(*"there's a child who needs help — and that's us"*). Second attestation, five weeks later, applied to
**generosity** rather than **storytelling**. **One mechanism, two applications — record as one.**

### ★★★ Reciprocity as social infrastructure

> *"There's something about human nature that **we are going to feel like we need to reciprocate. Our
> social network depends on us doing that.** So when somebody gives to you, eventually you need to do
> something — I make dinner, well I got the dishes; or I bought the groceries; or you drove, let me
> fill up the gas. **It's not a quid-pro-quo mandate, but it's how we continue to build on the
> relationship so it gets stronger versus weaker.**"*

✅ Consistent with **social capital, not pure gift** from 2025-05-22 — the same argument, stated
positively.

### ★★★ The thirds

> *"Let's say we'll divide the population into thirds. **A third already knows what's the game and
> they tune you out immediately. A third are skeptical, but will give you the benefit of the doubt.
> And a third are just totally blind — they have no freaking clue. Take all their money.**"*

*"So let's just talk about the third in the middle. They'll give you the benefit of the doubt, but
they're a little bit skeptical. **They can go down a rabbit hole with you, and they're waiting for you
to show yourself, your true self. And when they see it: I knew it. I knew it.**"*

### ★★★★ "Underneath a graphic designer is a teacher"

> *"**My identity, first, I thought was a graphic designer — but my real identity is a teacher.
> Underneath a graphic designer is a teacher, and I'm here to teach. I'm not really thinking about the
> business.** The irony is: **the more that I don't think about the business, the more business
> opportunities appear.**"*

### ★★★★ The paradox principle

- *"**It's just like when you stop trying to impress people, people might be impressed.**"*
- *"I wrote about something like that recently: **when you stop caring about what people think, people
  will start caring about what you think about.**"*
- ✅ **Being needy is repulsive** — restated, now with its inverse attached: *"**you push people away.**
  Like: can you validate me? Can I have money? Can you introduce me to all these people? It's like —
  **get away from me.** **But if you get into this kind of 'how can I serve you? What can I do to help
  you?'**…"*

### ★★★★ Inner peace as the actual attractor

> *"**I navigate the world in a way that I feel so comfortable in my own skin. I've stopped caring
> about what people think. I care about people, but I don't care about people's opinions of me.** And
> I think that's such an anomaly for people when they run into us, either online or in person —
> **you're drawn to this thing because you're looking for that peace yourself.** And so if somebody
> else is emitting this energy, this frequency, I think on some subconscious level **they feel seen,
> they feel safe, and they want to learn from that.**"*

> *"When we talk about **being attractive**, **I'm not talking about physical beauty** — I'm talking
> about **this inner peace, this joy that you have for life, this optimism that's still rooted in
> reality**… **this powerful contentment that you're okay, and you're very present and grounded in the
> moment.** I think that's why some spiritual leaders, some yogis — **I assume the Dalai Lama is very
> attractive because there's just inner peace emanating from their pores. That's my theory.**"*

He also frames the whole speculation with characteristic self-deprecation: *"**I can only conjecture
as to why people are drawn to watching the content and listening to this bald Asian fool.** I know
what it is — but **you can never know unless you ask all the people: why do you watch this channel?**"*

### ★★ Mo's outside read — and Chris's concession

**[MO, guest]** *"You do a tremendous job in making the person you're speaking to feel that what
you're saying **is not for clout, is not performance — you arrive with truth every time.** Whether
it's sharp razor blade — 'I'm going to give it to you in a way that might hurt' — or 'it sounds like
you're needing this in this moment.' **That gives people a lot of peace, because it's a breath of
fresh air from a lot of people in this space who give me the impression [they] are performing.**"*

**[CHRIS], immediately:** *"**And we all are, to a degree.**"* — a notable refusal to accept the
compliment cleanly. ✅ Consistent with the Molly refusal on
[[2025-05-20-yt-efxmE9XX-hE|2025-05-20]].

**[MO], deflating it:** *"Nah, bro. It's not that. **It's the fact that you made me close a $10,000
deal on that sales call with your questions.** … **Inner peace? Schmitter peace, man. But that ten
thousand — pieces of inner peace right there.**"*

### ★★★★ The closing line

> *"**If you want to be an impressive person, stop trying to impress people. No one who's impressive
> is ever really trying to impress you.** Actually, **most of us will find that to be repulsive.** Like
> — check out my car. Check out my watches. Check out where I live. Check out my spouse. Check out how
> perfect my children are. **Look at my bank account. I'm not really impressed at all.**"*
