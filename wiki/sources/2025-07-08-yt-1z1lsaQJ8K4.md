## Summary

A LinkedIn writing clinic. Alić walks through hook mechanics, "signposting," pinned-comment strategy,
a daily engagement hour, and how to write case studies that don't alienate everyone outside the
niche. Chris interviews, probes for the *why* behind each number, and drives one segment with an
observation of his own about bad questions. The persona harvest is small and is listed in full below.

## Chris's own material (complete)

- **[CHRIS]** Framing the episode around correction rather than tips: *"Help us write better. What do

- ★★★ **[CHRIS]** — the one segment he drives from his own observation: *"This is wonderful. Of course
  we have to ask questions at the end — but **people don't ask really good questions.** So give us

  the strategy, but they're just terrible questions.**"* (The guest then supplies the ladder: an
  open-ended *"what's your biggest failure story?"* is bad because it costs the reader 30 seconds to
  recall and another 60 to write; *"how much did you make last quarter?"* is middling because it's
  easy but pointless; *"**are you above or below your goals this month?**" is good because it's a
  one-word answer that pulls the *why* after it.)

## Jasmin Alić's system (guest — context only, NOT persona data)

- **Hooks are one-liners.** LinkedIn's preview cuts at **three lines including spaces**; a two-line
  hook pushes the second sentence out of the preview entirely. He reports *"a 20% drop in overall
  reach"* when his hook runs to two lines. The test he gives: *"**if your readers could only read the
  first three lines, what would you want them to know?**"* — which is also his argument against
  clickbait: *"they might not even click on see more."*
- **Signposting** — *"as experts, we feel so pushed to share the knowledge that **we forget to tell
  people how we actually know it.**"* One half-sentence of role or experience *"literally says to the
  reader: **you can't find this particular post anywhere else. You can't Google it.**"*
- **Pinned comments as the community backbone** — behind-the-scenes photos, repurposed older posts as
  *"bonus lessons"*, and **all calls to action moved into the comments**: *"it's a very sleazy way to
  do content when you write this beautiful story and then at the very bottom you have this footer that
  says 'if you want me to do the same for you, DM me'… **you don't need to sell in your content to be
  able to sell from your content.**"* His proof: *"**we're booked out six months in advance.**"*
- **A scheduled engagement hour** — posts at 12:30, stays until 1:30, *"religiously"*; he tells CEOs
  who claim no time to put whatever they do have in the work calendar.
- **Kill the niche in the hook** — a case study that opens with the industry alienates everyone
  outside it, so open on the *problem* (*"it's easy to acquire customers, it's harder to keep them"*)
  and only name the niche after the fold: *"**the more niche the example is, the more interesting the
  post is actually going to be**"* — once the reader is already in.
- **Make commenting cheap** — *"if I'm investing 20, 30 seconds reading your post, **don't make me
  invest another 30 or 60 thinking about what I'm going to comment.**"*
