# How to MAKE MONEY Being You! (2025-07-13)

> **Attribution:** two-hander with **Mo**, who brings an audience question and pushes back throughout.
> Mo's framing of the question is marked; all answers are Chris's.

## Summary

★★★ The question: *"how do you detach from outcomes when you actually need the money right now?"* His
answer refuses the comfort and gets progressively harder — a **two-column time audit**, then **talent
vs. skill** and the self-preservation move that hides behind the distinction, then **"artificially
handicapping yourself"** with his own Art Center decision as the counter-example, then the
**double-sided safety net**, and finally a prescription that is close to austere: *"sell all your
junk… live like a monk… eat ramen."* Along the way he corrects Mo's language mid-sentence, which is
the **fifth attestation** of the non-violent-language thread.

## Key claims (2025-07-13)

### ★★★ The two-column audit

*"Take a piece of paper and fold it in half vertically. **Things I do for money. Things I do for
love.**"* The nine-to-five column is untouchable (*"we can't touch that time"*). What the exercise
actually surfaces is the third category: *"**I don't love this, nor am I doing this for money** — try
to get rid of those things, **because you don't need them.**"*

**The basketball argument**, which is the sharp end of it: *"they love basketball so much. They'll do
this until their ACL's torn… and they'll still do it. They'll watch it on TV, check the scores, get
push notifications, follow the trades and the off-season. **Not once, I'm imagining, do they ask
themselves: well, what if I need to make money? You found time, didn't you? You found the interest.
You found the motivation.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**So the hard choice in life is: what am I willing to give up that I love, so I can get what
> I want — which I don't currently love today?**"*

With **Mike Tyson** credited: *"**discipline is doing the things that you hate as if you love them.
That's the difference between you and the person you aspire to become.**"* And the refusal of the
obvious advice: *"**I'm not going to sit here and tell you work harder. You've worked hard enough.**
It's just — **where do you want to apply your skill, time, energy and effort**, especially where it's
not returning anything on you."*

**His own position, stated as sequence not luck:** *"**I love to make content. It's not a burden for
me. I chose this because for a long time I worked really hard on the things that pay, so that I would
have the freedom to do the things that I love.** So today, if I don't love it, I'm not going to be
doing it for very long."*

### ✅ A language correction — fifth attestation of the non-violent thread

Mo summarises back: *"you're wasting a lot of your own time."* Chris stops him:

> *"First I want to clarify something, **because it's such violent language. I'm not so sure you're
> wasting your time. You're just using [it in] an unintentional way.**"*

Then the non-judgemental clause he always attaches: *"if you feel like watching basketball is what it
is, then continue to do that. **Not here to judge you. I have no stock in this** — **if it gives you
joy, just keep doing more of it.**"*

### ★★★★ Talent vs. skill — and the self-preservation move

> *"**Talent is the stuff that you were born with. Skills are stuff you acquire over time and
> practice.**"* → *"**Talent I cannot teach you, but skill I can.**"*

The hard version first: *"**maybe you're just not that good at it. Just because you want to do
something doesn't mean you're good at it.** I'd like to be a world-class dancer in hip-hop. **It's
probably not going to happen.** … **Because you want something doesn't mean you get to have it.**"*

> ★★★★ **The diagnosis that makes this useful:** *"a lot of people mix these two things up. '**I'm not
> good at that, I don't have talent for it**' — but what they really mean is '**I haven't put enough
> time into learning the skill.**' **And what that is, is a self-preservation mechanism. You're
> defending yourself against the idea that it's going to take a lot of work.**"*

**The obligation that follows:** *"what we owe to ourselves is **to put in as much time acquiring the
skills, so that we know if there's a talent gap or not, or if it's a skill gap.**"*

### ★★★ Wrong lane, and the follower mindset — Virgil Abloh

> *"There's a good chance that **what you think you're supposed to be doing is not what you're
> supposed to be doing at all in this life. You haven't harnessed the full you.** Maybe you saw other
> people like you do things like this and you're like, 'that must be for me' — and they're just so
> much better at it. It comes so much easier and faster for them, and you're sitting here grinding and
> struggling. **This ain't your thing.** … There's a lot of us that have **a follower mindset**: we
> see people do something, it feels safe, we should do that same thing. **And it's when you go outside
> of that that you actually have a real breakthrough.**"*

**Virgil Abloh** as the case: trained in architecture, *"had he stayed in architecture, I don't know
if he'd have gone as far as he did in his short life. **But because he used that discipline of
architecture and applied it into a space that hadn't seen that before**, in fashion and design, **he
brought a visual language and a training and a discipline that people in that space did not
understand. And it was this fresh point of view that got him as much hype as he did.**"* ⚠️ He marks
the speculation as speculation: *"I'm not certain, and the world will never know."*

And the cost of the identity attachment: *"**it can be hard to accept, because you've built up your
identity around being that kind of person.**"*

### ★★★ When it *is* time to stop

> *"It's pretty clear **when you start doubting and questioning your motivation. You already know
> you're prolonging the inevitable.**"*

**His own contrast, which is the useful part:** *"I make content all day long. Some of it hits, some
of it doesn't. When it doesn't hit, **I don't sit there [thinking] it's not for me, I should be tap
dancing now. I don't think those thoughts. I just think: it didn't hit. I'm going to keep doing this.
I enjoy the pursuit so much.** **It doesn't matter to me what the results are.**"*

> *"**The people who love the pursuit more than the destination often beat the people who love the
> destination and don't love the pursuit.**"*

Illustrated with the **Wright brothers**, via **Simon Sinek's** *Start With Why*: the rival was well
financed but *"his pursuit was just — **I want to be the first person to invent an airplane**"*, where
the Wright brothers *"just love the idea of flying."* The kicker: *"**the other people could have
continued the pursuit to make the second airplane, or the third. They just wanted to be the first.
They didn't — because they didn't love it that much.**"*

### ★★★★ "Artificially handicapping yourself" — and his own counter-example

The decision chain: you pick the cheaper school over the best one; then, of three job offers, you
decline the one that requires moving somewhere unfamiliar. *"You see — **you continue to make
decisions consistent with someone who's artificially handicapping themselves, and you never gave
yourself the best shot to succeed** — for a lot of different reasons, **mostly comfort.** Comfort with
financial stress, comfort with familiar backgrounds or family ties."*

**Then his own, told against that pattern — and it contains a biographical detail new to the corpus:**

> *"When I decided I wanted to become a designer, I asked around: **what's the best school to go to?**
> They said go to **Art Center**. So I went to Art Center **despite it being prohibitively
> expensive.** **I didn't even consider schools two, three or four. It was not within the realm of
> possibility. I only applied to one school** — it's either get into school or don't. And once I got
> in, it was very challenging."*

> *"Then I get out of school and get a job. **My uncles would be like, 'hey, come back to San Jose.'**
> I'm like — well, **the heart of advertising is in LA or New York. Those are my two choices. None of
> them sound like San Jose. I have to be here.** And I stayed here throughout the rest of my career,
> **despite it being uncomfortable for my family.**"*

★★ **And the coda, which reverses the direction of the sacrifice:** *"so much so that at some point
**my dad and mom sell their house and move in with us** for a period of time — **and now they live in
LA**, because they don't have any more commitments to anybody and **they'd rather be around their
children and live closer to us so we can spend more time together.**"*

### ★★★★ The double-sided safety net

The generalisation, argued through ethnic enclaves — *"little Ethiopia, Japantown, Koreatown"*:

> *"**We go for comfort and safety, because our people, our culture, the shops we want to shop at, the
> foods, and our uncles and aunties and our parents feel safe. That safety comes at a cost. It's a
> safety net. The net catches you when you fall — it's awesome. But it catches you when you try to
> fly, too. The net is a double-sided net. You don't know that.**"*

He grounds it in his own extended family: cousins who live *"down the street quite literally"* and
talk constantly about how good it is to have each other nearby for babysitting. *"**And this is why
you see this happen all over the country.**"*

### ★★★ Pay your dues — Ash Thorp and Kyle Cooper

**Ash Thorp**, who designed the car for *The Batman*, decided in college that he wanted to work with
**Kyle Cooper**, *"the world's most famous main title designer."* Working for Cooper meant weekends,
very late hours, *"he expects you to give all of yourself."* So Thorp *"takes the train in from San
Diego to LA… then a taxi over to Malibu where Kyle's compound is, and he works there day and night.
**Sometimes he sleeps at the office**, then takes the reverse back home."* — with the detail Chris
clearly likes: *"**his girl was waiting for him at midnight at the train station when he came
home.**"*

> *"He did this **not for a little bit, but for as long as he needed to**, to acquire the skills he
> needed… **And that's what they mean when they say pay your dues.** Some people did not pay their
> dues. **They didn't get into the club because they weren't willing to pay through sacrifice, through
> effort and energy. That's why they're stuck.**"*

### ★★★★ The prescription — and the portfolio humiliation

He acknowledges the damage first: *"you've listened to all of this and now your confidence might have
been a six and now it's a two. **I don't want to leave you here, friends.**"*

> *"**I want you to sell all your junk. I want you to live like a monk.** Get rid of all the stuff you
> think you need but really don't. **Reduce your lifestyle down to the bare essentials. Eat ramen. Do
> what you got to do.** And then **find somebody like me — not me — who's willing to teach you, to
> work with you, that you look up to, that you admire — and give them your best. Find them. They
> exist. And there's not just one of them.**"*

**Why proximity beats schooling, from his own experience:**

> *"When you go to school — and I did, I went four years to learn design — **within the first couple
> of months working with the right people, my portfolio looked like trash. A portfolio that I thought
> was really good now looks like garbage**, because of how much I learned. At school we're used to
> working at a specific pace, and the expectations are mostly from you. **But when you work with
> someone who's brilliant, they may not be the easiest person to work for. They'll have seemingly
> unrealistic expectations and standards that you cannot even understand at this point. But that's the
> beauty of it** — when they show you what's possible, **the bar at which it's raised, and you expand
> to fill that gap**, you too will look back and say: **that was pretty garbage. I now know what the
> standard is.** And I've elevated myself **from division three to the pro leagues, to the
> all-star.**"*

**And the durable asset:** *"the person you wind up working with and for — **just by mentioning their
name, that you used to work there or with them, is going to be a door opener for you.** … **There's
always a top 10 in the industry doing exactly what you hope to do one day. Find a way to get there,
and get there.**"*
