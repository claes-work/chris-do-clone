# Why You're Still Invisible (VidCon Personal Branding Talk) — 2025-07-29

> **Attribution:** a **solo keynote** at VidCon with light audience participation (audience lines are
> marked `>>` in the captions and are paraphrased here, never quoted as his). One named third party —
> an Art Center professor — is quoted with her own words attributed to her.

## Summary

★★★ **LANDMARK — the most complete single statement of his personal-branding argument in the corpus.**
Where earlier sources give pieces, this gives the whole arc as one talk: the diagnosis (**attention is
the economy**, **social is the new résumé**, **we moved from the age of character to the age of
personality**), two contrarian theses (**don't chase new**, **don't chase better**), the reframe
(**original means origin; branding is the act of remembering**), a teaching device
(**inversion — how would you hide in plain sight?**), the exercise (**the "too much" list**), and a
closing that is unusually blunt about mortality.

## The opening story — and where "I'd rather be hated" comes from

Told about **Petrula**, an Art Center professor and designer (author of *"practice safe design, use a
concept"*), after a Clubhouse call that *"got a little spicy"*:

> **[HER, quoted by Chris]** *"Chris, I think people misunderstand you… you're a little edgy. If you
> were to tone that down a little bit, I think more people would like you."*
>
> **[CHRIS]** *"**They didn't misunderstand me at all. I am edgy. I am a little bit cantankerous. And
> I think they're there in that room because I am.** … **I would rather be hated for who I am than to
> be loved for who I am not.**"*

★★★ **And the structural insight he draws from it** — that the training that makes you good in one
arena disqualifies you in another:

> *"Petrula comes from **an academic world where how you move up and down in life really depends on
> being a very intentional communicator, a very thoughtful communicator. And that kind of training is
> the very thing that you can't do online** — because then what you wind up doing is **you cut out all
> of the personality, all the nuance, the things that make you you.**"*

## The diagnosis

- ★★★★ **Character → personality.** *"We've moved from **the age of character to the age of
  personality.** It used to be **we were judged by the things that we did**, and now it's all about
  the personality… **it's 'how do you make me feel?' It's all about the feelings now.** And we can see
  everywhere we look — **character, the thing that we held in very high regard, is seemingly
  disappeared.**"*
- ★★★★ *"**It's not the attention economy that you know so much about. Attention IS the economy. This
  is where all the money is being made.**"*
- ✅ **Social is the new résumé** — third attestation, and the sharpest telling of the rejected-speaker
  story, now with the organiser named: *"I talked to my friend **Neil**, who's an event organiser…
  'Chris, I can't book the speaker.' I'm like, 'You're missing out on the genius of this person.' He
  goes, '**But they're not going to help me sell any tickets. No one knows who they are, and I can't
  work that hard to promote them. That's not my job. It's their job to get known.**'"*
- ★★★ **"There are riches in the reaches."** His own inversion of *riches in the niches*: *"brand
  sponsors will come and give you money **because they want your audience. They want your reach.
  That's what they're really paying for.** You'll get better publishing deals because you have a
  perceived audience and reach — **they don't have to market you as hard. You're more of a surefire
  thing, and that's what they're buying.**"* Evidenced with **Hailey Bieber**, whose company sold for
  ~$1B: *"that's to tell you **the power of reach.**"*
- **Warhol, updated:** *"in the future everyone will be famous for 15 minutes — **the future is now.
  And all of you can manufacture your own fame.**"* With the upside he insists on: *"**you don't need
  the gatekeepers to anoint you** as worthy of having a talk show… you don't need an editor of a
  magazine or newspaper."*

## ★★★★ Contrarian idea 1 — don't chase new

Argued with three Hollywood cases rather than asserted:

| Case | The numbers he cites | The point |
|---|---|---|
| ***The Fountain*** (Aronofsky) | 52% Rotten Tomatoes; **$16M on a $35M budget** | *"I love Darren Aronofsky. **I watched the film. I cannot remember the film. I do not know what it's about.**"* — and his filmography shows *"**he's always trying to do new.**"* |
| ***Cloud Atlas*** (the Wachowskis) | 66% RT; **$130M worldwide but only $27M domestic** | *"a lot of people don't know the Wachowskis."* |
| **Tom Cruise** | *Maverick*, a billion-dollar film | Comedian **Rich Hall**'s line that all Tom Cruise films are the same: *"**he basically makes the same thing over and over again. And we're all laughing — but he's laughing all the way to the bank**, while Aronofsky and the Wachowskis are dropping duds."* |

> ★★★★ *"**So why is it so hard to do the same thing over and over again? Because it fights our
> creative soul and our desire to do new things.**"*

**The Dip, re-explained — and this time with the restart loop, which is new:**

> *"Every time we start something new it feels really fun… and then **the joy of doing that work
> starts to wear off and it starts to become really hard.** The algorithm changes. Our audience is
> fickle — they found somebody newer, hotter, fresher. And you feel burnt out… **and so what do we do?
> 'I have a new idea. Let's start again.' Because when we start again, it becomes new and fun again.
> And we repeat this process over and over — and we're stuck in the dip.**"*

With Godin's actual point: *"**the dip is a natural barrier to competition.** When things get hard,
you've got to buckle down and keep doing it till you have that breakthrough."* And an aside about
who's already past it: *"celebrities… **they're already naturally good-looking to begin with. People
give them free stuff all the time. Like there's no justice in the world. They get the free traffic** —
because they were able to push past the dip."*

**The reframe, which is the heart of the talk:**

> *"**New is an external infinite pursuit of what's next.** And there's always something that's next,
> and **it never ends.** … **Original is an internal quest to discover yourself.** You look at **the
> root word of original — it's ORIGIN. The point or place where something begins. That's you. That's
> your story. A story that you may have forgotten.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**So branding isn't so much an act of creation** — which people like, *let's create a brand*
> — **it's not that at all.** Even for big brands, and for personal brands especially, **it's the act
> of remembering.**"*

With **Pepsi vs. Coke** as the corporate proof: *"the choice of a new generation"* against *"**the
real thing**"* — *"**the new generation aren't loyal. They're very fickle, and you're always chasing
them, spending a lot of ad dollars.**"*

## ★★★★ Contrarian idea 2 — don't chase better

Run as a call-and-response game (*"yell out: better brand, or better product"*) across McDonald's,
IKEA, Nike, Starbucks and bottled water — all of which the room calls as **brand**.

- **IKEA**, with an unusually personal read: *"it's **fast furniture. It's disposable.** I used to love
  it as a college student because **it gave me the idea I was living a European lifestyle on $89.**
  The problem with IKEA products is **you never give it to your children.** You're not going to find
  it in an antique store."*
- **Water**, as the closer: *"**you've been sold a brand. You have. I promise you.**"*
- **Seth Godin, *All Marketers Are Liars*:** *"**they lie to you, and we're complicit in the lie. We
  taste the wine before we drink it**… you're like, 'I'm going to have a good meal,' before you even
  take your first bite — because **we're seduced by our own self story.**"*
- ★★★★ *"**Products focus on features, whereas brands focus on meaning.**"* → *"if you're talking about
  what you do or what you sell **as features, you're losing the brand game.**"* (With **Liquid Death**
  named as the kind of competitor that beats you on brand alone.)
- ★★★ **"Biology"** — his own coinage for purchase-as-identity: *"**if I buy this, what does it say
  about me — my values, beliefs and preferences?**"* Anchored on **Anna Lappé**: *"**every time you
  spend money, you're casting a vote for the kind of world you want.**"* And the trend he names:
  *"**we're moving more towards identity and away from survival.**"*
- **Dell as the counter-example:** *"**there aren't really Dell people. It's just a cheap computer.** I
  buy Dells for my dad because he don't care. For everybody else, it's Apple all the way."*

## ★★★★ Different, not better — and the good/different quadrant

- ✅ **Sally Hogshead**, *Fascinate*, credited again — **the third consecutive crediting**: *"if it's
  the one thing you walk away with from today: **different is better than better.** Many people are
  pursuing being better — and **after a certain point, ROI diminishes drastically.**"*
- **James Clear's 1% math**: 1.01³⁶⁵ ≈ **37.8**, and the inverse ≈ 0.03 — *"**this number shocks me
  more than the 37.8.**"* Applied to difference rather than habit: *"**if you focus on figuring out
  what makes you different and you keep leaning in on that** — just like Tom Cruise — **do it over and
  over again.**"*
- ★★★★ **The quadrant**, with *good* demoted to a ticket price:
  > *"**Good is the price of entry. You don't get to play the game if you're not good. And good is not
  > enough.**"*

  | | **Not different** | **Different** |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Good** | **Commodity — "a me too"** (*"nothing wrong with that, especially early in your content journey"*) | **Market leader** — *"every brand you love that commands a premium price and has a waitlist of hungry customers"* |
  | **Not good** | **Knockoff and cheap** | *"That's like **wearing a clown outfit.** Okay, you're different — but how does that help anybody?"* |

## ★★★★ Inversion — "how would you hide in plain sight?"

Credited to **Charlie Munger**: *"instead of trying to do something, they look at what the opposite
thing is, to learn what they should do."* Framed as an FBI/CIA infiltration exercise:

- **In person:** *"**every industry, every community has a uniform.** So you wear the uniform of the
  people and you fit right in. You wear your hair a certain way, a certain length, a certain colour, a
  certain kind of makeup, certain shoes, you listen to a certain kind of music."*
- **Online, easiest first:** **(1) don't do anything** — *"**some of you are doing a great job of
  that**"* — and **(2) copy everyone**: *"there's this water challenge, let's do that. There's a
  pointing thing, let's do that. Let's dance in front of the camera."*
- **The full blend-in profile:** *"you participate in **groupthink**… **you take up very little
  space**… you use that **copy-and-paste content strategy. You spray and pray.** You put out the
  content, **you never engage with anybody. You say the most empty things. You straddle the fence. You
  never have a point of view. Shiny happy all the time, forever.**"*
- **Then the turn:** *"**now just take a minute and search in your mind: how did I show up today? How
  have I been showing up online?** … **Map yourself against that and see how you're doing. Give
  yourself a score.**"*

With **Paul Arden** (*Whatever You Think, Think the Opposite*): *"**safe is risky and risky is
safe.**"* And the summary line: *"**the best way to hide in plain sight is do branding. And I'm here
to talk to you about branding, not blending.**"*

## ★★★ The water argument — now with a story where it happened to him

The familiar sequence (no label → discount shelf; **Evian** and the Alps skyline; **Fiji**'s *"not
touched by man"*; *"**ionized alkaline water… overachieving H₂O — like your water is not working hard
enough**"*; *"**smart water — I don't want to drink stupid water**"*), plus a new personal coda:

> *"I was speaking on stage in **Glendale**, and they had **removed all the labels from the bottles of
> water** — they didn't want to deal with copyright or trademark issues. I'm going on stage, **I have
> this case of dry mouth. I'm looking at that bottle. I went without drinking the water.** Also,
> **they poured it into a glass** — now I'm even more scared of it, because **I'm a germ freak. Who
> drank this water? Is this for me?**"*

He then turns it: *"**if you think of yourself as the packaging for your water, what story are you
telling?** … And if you don't have any story attached, **you become questionable yourself.**"* →
**provenance** → *"**what is the source of you? Your story. Where'd you come from? Who are you when
you were a little kid, when somebody didn't corrupt you and tell you to fit in with everybody
else?**"*

## ★★★★ The repeatable story

**Michael Margolis** (*Story 10x*) again — *"a product or service without a story is a commodity"* →
*"**no story, no brand.**"* Then the addition:

> *"And what we don't want to do is **just tell any story. We want to tell a repeatable story** — **a
> story that's so powerful that someone else will do your marketing for you. I do that all the time. I
> just told you the story of six or seven different companies. I'm doing their marketing for you.** So
> they have to be **short, contained, and highly memorable and sticky.**"*

Supported by **Tansel Ali**, four-time Australian memory champion (two phone books in 24 days):
*"**silly stories is how we remember things. The sillier the story — a mnemonic device that you create
— it sticks.**"* → *"**you're telling corporate sanitised stories all the time. You're not helping
yourself to be remembered. You're unremarkable.**"*

> ⚠️ **A definition refined, not contradicted:** here he says *"branding is **meaningful**
> differentiation — how are you different **in a way that's intentional**?"* — folding **meaningful**
> into the **intentional differentiation** formula credited to **Marty Neumeier** elsewhere. Record
> both; the addition is *meaningful*.

With **Audi** as the case: *"**never follow**"*, the S-series wordplay, the swipe at Lexus auto-park
(*"Audi drivers know how to park their own cars"* — *"**it's a lot of attitude. Shots are fired**"*),
and the point: *"**where do they talk about the engine, the wheels, the wheelbase? They don't —
because that's features.**"*

## ★★★ Play against type, and the red shirt

> *"**What does the prototypical person in your space do, say, or write about?** Write a list of
> everything they do. **And everything you do must be different — or at least one of the things you do
> must be different.** Otherwise you're basically **giving people no reason to follow you.** … **You
> don't want to be the poor man's Brad Pitt.** … **That's how you unclone yourself.**"*

> ★★ *"If you don't do this, you risk becoming **a red shirt** — the generic disposable character on
> Star Trek with a very high probability of dying. **So in life, if you ever wake up and you're in a
> red shirt with a Star Trek uniform, all you have to do is change your shirt. Your chance of living
> is much higher.**"* ✅ A **second Star Trek reference** in the corpus, alongside the Spock story.

## ★★★★ The authenticity gap, Jung, and the "too much" list

- ★★★★ **The best formulation of the gap yet:** *"sometimes **if you're feeling like not yourself, it's
  because you are leaving parts of you behind. You're missing pieces.** So **a lot of us walk around
  fractured, because we're not in love with all of us.** We're so used to putting out a version of
  ourselves, a facade."*
- **The meeting-your-favourite-creator problem:** *"whoa, **they're so different than how they
  appear**… that's **the bad weird**, because they show up very specifically, very curated on camera,
  and it's very different from how they are in real life."*
- ✅ **Jung**, third attestation: the ego regulating between **the shadow** (*"the part that we feel so
  ashamed, embarrassed, nervous or anxious around showing to people"*) and **the persona** (*"who we
  show up in the world to be accepted, to be loved — at least not to be ridiculed"*). → *"**we go
  around pretending to be someone that we're not all the time. We wear a mask. And all of us do
  this.**"*
- ✅ **Normal means average**, restated: *"I don't think anybody here wants to be average… **but we
  seem to love the idea of being normal.**"*
- ★★★★ **The "too" list**, with named examples: *"**Steve Jobs is too obsessive. Oprah's too emotional.
  Elon's too weird** — that's a different weird, by the way. **Gary Vee is too intense.**"* → *"**Your
  perceived flaws aren't flaws at all. The flaws are your advantage.**"*
  > ⚠️ **Cross-clone:** Gary Vaynerchuk is named here as an example. Third-party mention — **not
  > usable as source material for that clone.**
- **The exercise:** *"**Create a 'too much' list for yourself. What do people accuse me of being too
  much of?** I'm too loud, I'm too this, I'm too that. Just go to town. **Then we reframe it and we
  flip it: who needs this? Why is this an advantage? How might this be a gift?** **This is the
  beginning process of leaning into your shadow self and healing from inside — to remember those are
  good things, not bad things. And you double down on them.**"*
- **Brené Brown**, credited: *"**trust is a byproduct of vulnerability**"* and *"**relationships travel
  at the speed of your vulnerability** — your willingness to reveal who you really are."* With: *"your
  favourite creators… **not only tell you about the high highs, but also the low lows. It's how we know
  we can trust them.**"*

## ★★★ Yin and yang — done live

*"There's no such thing as something that's purely good or purely bad."* Worked with the room:

- **Too tall** → can't fit in the car, hitting your head, misery in economy (*"it sucks to travel with
  tall friends. **It's great for me. Economy feels like first class sometimes**"*).
- **Too rich** → *"you don't know who to trust. You have tax problems… **and you never know if somebody
  really likes you for you.**"*
- **Too smart** → *"**smart people overthink everything. And they're annoying to be around most of the
  time.**"*
- **Too short** → *"you're fun size… **we consume less resources, everybody. Y'all are killing the
  environment.**"*
- **Broke** → *"**you get really inventive**… and **you know who your friends are, 'cause nobody's
  hanging out with poor broke people for no reason. You never have to question that.**"*

> ★★★ **And the asymmetry he names:** *"**it's easy to see what might be bad in the good. But what's
> the good in the bad? That's a lot harder skill to develop.**"*

**Then the corporate versions of exactly that move:** **Montana tourism** (*"there's nothing here to
do"* → *"nothing here"* as the campaign), **Southwest** (*"fares"*), and **Mini Cooper** — *"instead of
trying to compete with the SUVs and the gas-guzzlers, **they just lean into being really small**…
**they take their weakness and they make it a strength**"* (*"packs a punch"*, *"float like a
butterfly"*, *"less motor"*, *"you've met your match"*).

## ★★★★ The close

> *"The things that have been **taught out of you** — your individuality, your creativity — that is
> the thing that makes you you. **I want you to be the youest you you can be, because the more you you
> are, the more you are.** … And mostly, **you're going to be able to look in the mirror and love that
> person who shows up every single day.**"*

> *"**Life is really short. We're closer to death than we are to living.** We really are. I know
> that's a macabre thing to say, but **I'm a realist. I'm grounded in this fact. I'm rooted by this.
> So please — do not spend another day doing work that you don't like, for companies that you don't
> love, for causes you don't believe in.**"*
