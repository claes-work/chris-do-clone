# Why Doing More Is Breaking You — w/ Klaus Kleinfeld (2025-07-31)

> **⚠️ ATTRIBUTION — GUEST-DOMINATED.** The guest is **Dr. Klaus Kleinfeld**, former CEO of **Siemens**
> and **Alcoa**, author of ***Leading to Thrive***. **The overwhelming majority of this episode is his**
> — his life, his framework, his health protocols — and is summarised below **as context only**.
> Chris's contributions are marked **[CHRIS]** and are comparatively few; they are listed in full.

## Summary

An interview about burnout, energy and purpose. Kleinfeld supplies a framework (the *inner game* /
*outer game*), a set of micro-habits, and his own biography. The persona-relevant harvest is small
but includes one exchange worth keeping for an unusual reason: **Chris offers a gendered
generalisation, the guest corrects him on air, and Chris does not defend it.**

## Chris's own material (complete)

### ★★ On generations — offered as a stereotype, and labelled as one

> **[CHRIS]** *"I find that **young people today have a different mindset about work** — and I'm
> sharing perhaps **this stereotype** about millennials especially, where they show up to work
> thinking **'when you pay me enough, I'll start to care.'** Whereas **my generation, Gen X**, was
> like: we showed up, we just worked really hard, and then we were rewarded for it. So something has
> happened in the workplace — and **these are natural evolutions of different generations.**"*

> ✅ **He self-identifies as Gen X** (consistent with a 1972 birth), and he **flags his own framing as
> a stereotype before using it** — worth recording, because the hedge is characteristic.

And the second half, which cuts the other way: *"**I find that younger people especially are way more
purpose-driven than my generation**, where we show up — it's a means to make money so that we can
provide for our family and think about the future. Whereas **young people really do choose to align
themselves with companies that they can get behind.**"*

### ★★★ A generalisation he makes, and is corrected on

Kleinfeld describes closing the mental door on work when he is with family.

> **[CHRIS]** *"**I find that trait to be more common in men than women — the ability to
> compartmentalise.** Whereas, say, my own life experience: **my wife's feeling bad about something,
> it's going to bleed over to everything. It just cannot be put into a box.**"*

He immediately concedes the other side is the healthier one — which is the part to keep:

> *"**But sometimes I think she's really healthy, because she's always expressing what she thinks and
> feels in the moment** — whereas **we tend to go into our cave, put it into a little box, and then
> come out. And sometimes it can be very, very toxic.** So **there's pros and cons to everything.**"*

> ⚠️ **And the guest pushes back directly**, citing John Gray: *"I think **women can compartmentalise
> actually better than men.** What you are describing is a different thing — women are traditionally
> juggling many more tasks… the way they problem-solve is by **sharing**, and they don't want the men
> jumping in to solve problems. They just want to share."*
>
> **Chris does not defend the generalisation.** Record the exchange as a whole — the claim, his own
> immediate concession, and the correction — never the first line alone. ✅ **Wife referenced by role
> only.**

### ★★ Other contributions

- **[CHRIS]** He pushes for a contemporary example when Kleinfeld's illustration is dated: *"since you
  were talking about Siemens and colour displays — the audience is like, **what is he talking about,
  we all have colour displays.** Could you give a perspective on something that's happened recently…
  where **they create something and they're not really thinking about the customer?**"* (The guest's
  answer, after being called out, is his own outdoor-pool control panel: *"**how was the programmer
  thinking when I set a certain flag?** The user interface is counterintuitive."*)
- **[CHRIS]** — the closing frame, which is his: *"if you're a person who's like **'I'm not feeling
  energised'** — go pick up the book and start implementing some of these principles… **And I think
  the world is better when people are happier. We're better partners. We're better parents. We're
  better children. We're better bosses. We're better employees. Let's just work on the energy.
  Especially a lot of you young people — you should have a lot of it. And if you don't… something is
  off. Something's out of balance.**"*

That is the extent of the subject-attributed material.

## Klaus Kleinfeld's account (guest — context only, NOT persona data)

- **Self-description**: *"an industrialist"* — a term Chris flags as one he has never heard a guest
  use. Consumer business first (*"from dog food to detergents"*), then industrial.
- **Biography**: parents were refugees from East Germany; **his father died when he was 10**; he spent
  formative summers back in East Germany with family, *"and I learned two systems — fortunately with
  the right passport."*
- **Why he wrote the book**: raised in Germany with discipline as a virtue and *"a big fan"* of pushing
  through, he found burnout far more common than he expected — and **worse in founders and the younger
  generation** than in veterans. One-on-one coaching didn't scale, so he wrote it down.
- **His governing rule**, stated twice: *"**I either love it, I change it, or I leave it**"* — *"I have
  always lived by this principle, even when I didn't have money."*
- **Inner game / outer game**: the inner game is *"what energy do you have, how do you conserve it, how
  do you get it"*; the outer game is *"where do you bring it."* The book is about the inner game
  because *"**the inner game is the foundation for whatever you want to do.**"*
- **His three business metrics**: net promoter score, employee motivation, and cash in the bank.
- **Micro-habits and protocols**: a protected morning workout; phone handed to an assistant; a
  breathing exercise at the desk; more sleep than he used to allow himself (*"it's just in the last
  three years"*); *"**downtime is productive time**"*; blue light as *"a real nice hack"*; movement to
  drive the lymph system.
- **On leadership**: he recounts a labour-union head thanking him *"for giving us our soul back."*
- **The closing prescription**: *"**start with breathing**… and then go to physical exercise."*
- **The deathbed argument**: *"nobody says 'I wish I had worked more and spent more time in the
  office.' People do say: **I wish I had allowed myself to be more me. I wish I had allowed myself to
  be happier. I wish I had not listened to other people that much.**"*
