# STOP Blanding In! How to Create a PERSONAL BRAND That Stands Out (2025-08-12)

> **Attribution:** a **live keynote** with audience call-and-response (audience lines marked `>>` in
> the captions; paraphrased here, never quoted as his). **Family members appear in the talk and are
> referenced by role only** — see the policy note below.

## Summary

★★★ **LANDMARK, and the most biographically rich talk in the corpus.** It is a longer, warmer cousin
of the [[2025-07-29-yt-hDlWbmH96hw|VidCon keynote]] — same two contrarian theses, but here he prefaces
them with **his own story told in full for once** ("which I normally never do"): the Blind credit
list, the *"no way, Jose"* origin, his wife's verdict on the first videos, the son who travelled with
him, the son who invited him to speak, and the arrival in the US in 1975. It also adds two things the
VidCon version doesn't have: **world-building** as the positive form of *don't chase new*, and
**"personal bland."**

## ★★★★ The wound that opens it — "who are you?"

> *"I was at **VidCon** recently… and I'm thinking, okay, **we have 2.7 million followers.** I do my
> presentation, and there was a little line of people, and I'm like — great, they're going to ask me
> all these questions about how I make content, what's the team like. **Their question was: who are
> you?** … **I was hurt a little bit.** Like — well, I don't know why you're here, then."*

> ★★★★ *"**So my number one rule is: never assume anyone knows who you are.**"*

## ★★★★ Biography — the fullest career account in the corpus

Given as the reason he is about to break his own habit: *"I'm going to ask for your indulgence,
because **I'm going to tell my story, which I normally never do.**"*

- **Self-description**: *"my name is Chris Do… and **I'm a recovering graphic designer.**"*
- **The company**: a design and production company making **commercials and music videos for
  advertising agencies** of *"some of the largest brands in the world."*
- ★★★ **Stacy Peralta** — main titles and in-film graphics for ***Dogtown and Z-Boys***, ***Riding

  hero**… he's the OG."*
- **Video game key art.**
- ★★★ **Gnarls Barkley — "Crazy"**, the music video — *"if you were alive around that time, that song
  was everywhere. **And it won an MTV Video Music Award.**"*
- ★★★ **Audi** — helping launch the **A7**, *"the top secret project"* — with *"some parallels between
  the Crazy music video and what they wanted us to do."*
- **UFC, Spike TV and FX** — *"talking to mixed martial art fighters that I've watched on TV from
  afar."*
- **Xbox** — building IP and helping with a launch.
- ★★★ **Coldplay** — a music video. *"**Chris Martin's a cool guy. He is a really cool guy.**"*
- **An award**, received with visible discomfort: *"I even get to win an award, **which I'm super
  awkward to get and receive.**"*

## ★★★★ "No way, Jose" — the origin, fullest telling

> *"A former classmate of mine, his name is **Jose — Jose Caballer. He's Puerto Rican.** And he has
> this idea: '**Chris, let's start a YouTube channel together.**' And I'm like — **no. Adamant. I'm
> behind-the-scenes talent. I'm not in-front-of-camera talent.** And he told me, '**I'm not going to
> do this without you.** So what do you want to do?' I'm like — **'No way, Jose.' And that's not
> racist. That is his name.** … I was like, 'over my dead [body].'"*

> ★★★★ *"So **in 2014, we made our first video. I'm 42 years old.** This is literally a frame grab
> from that very first video. **And now we're going to be YouTube famous. Or so I thought. And then
> nobody shows up. Nobody watches.**"*

✅ **Fifth corroboration of the 2014 / age-42 hinge.**

### ★★★★ His wife's verdict — and the lesson he takes from it

> *"Reality set in, because **my wife at the time — she's like, 'What are you doing? Are you making
> YouTube videos now? You're a YouTuber?'** I'm like, 'Honey, watch the videos,' I'm so excited. And
> she watched some of them and she told me something. **'Your videos are unwatchable.'** … **And the
> worst part is she was right** — because Jose and I, we didn't really prepare. **We just showed
> up.**"*

> ★★★ *"**If you don't make that effort and you just barely show up, why would anyone give you their
> time and attention — their most valuable thing that they have?**"*

And the second lesson: *"**show up and give value. Don't try and sell people anything.**"* With the
timeline: *"it's about **a year in — I'm a slow learner** — that we start to do this, and the channel
starts to take off. **Not crazy take off, but take off by our standards.** Back then, instead of
getting **25 views**, we'd get **300 views**. That was pretty cool, in a single day."*

### ★★★ The video that changed it

> *"The channel really takes off with a video that most people know me for now. I think it has **over
> 4 million views**. In short form, it has **a combined over 90 million views. Just this one video.
> And it just changes everything. It completely changes my life.** And **it doesn't just change the
> business — it gives me a tremendous amount of purpose.**"*

✅ Explained through **ikigai**, named again: *"there's this intersection… where you get to do the
things that you love, the things that you're good at, the thing the world pays you for, and the thing
the world needs. **I found my sweet spot. Prior to that, I could only pick two of the four, and not
live a completely unified life.** In that moment it was **total synchronicity**, and it's changed me
forever."*

## The observations

- ✅ **The age of character → the age of personality**, second attestation (cf.
  [[2025-07-29-yt-hDlWbmH96hw|VidCon]]).
- ★★★ **NEW — the dematerialised culture**: *"all the stuff that we used to hold on to — analog or
  legacy or obsolete media — **has all been put into the cloud.** … **So that means we're paying more
  to have less.**"* Which is why *"biology"* matters: *"**it's less about having things. It's more
  about identity and meaning. Who am I if I buy this?**"*
- ★★★ **A current, personal example — and a disclosure**: *"**Elon's Tesla is not doing well, because
  he's telling people: this is what you're buying, this is what you're supporting.** So **even though
  I own a Tesla**, there's a lot of hate out there because of what he's saying."*
  ⚠️ **New biographical fact (self-reported): he owns a Tesla.** Dated August 2025.
- **Anna Lappé** again (*"every time you spend money you're casting a vote"*) → *"you have to tell them
  **what else they're buying.** It's not the tangible thing — **they're buying their identity**…
  because **we as humans are meaning-making machines. Everything that we do tells a story about who we
  are.**"*
- ✅ **Social is the new résumé** and **riches in the reaches**, both restated.
- **The billionaire case studies**: **Hailey Bieber** (55.2M Instagram followers; sold **Rhode** for
  **$1B** to e.l.f.; *"**she's only 28**"*) and **Poppy** (founded by Allison and Stephen Ellsworth,
  built on TikTok and influencers from 2018, sold for *"$1.9 billion"* — *"**in seven years**"*).
  → *"**Don't sleep on this.** Everybody's been telling you about making content, getting over your
  fears. **Well, if you don't do anything, we'll have the same conversation next year. New slides,
  same conversation.**"* → *"**If they can do that, then why not you? What's holding you back?**"*
- **Warhol**, again: *"**the future is now**… **you can manufacture your own fame. You don't need a
  publicist. You don't need media professionals. You don't need gatekeepers.**"* → *"it doesn't matter
  if you're **24, 42, or 60. Just numbers. Add up to six.**"*

## ★★★★ "Personal bland"

> *"**But not if you have a personal *bland*.** And I think a lot of you — that's what you're building
> right now. **It's deliberately beige for a reason.** It's very boring."*

The talk's own title: *"**the unsubtle art of standing out in a noisy world.**"*

**The two governing ideas he names up front:**

1. ★★★ *"**You have to have the courage to be disliked**"* — credited to the book of that name.
2. *"You have to figure out **how you're meaningfully different.** There's bad different, there's
   unintentional different — **but you want to be meaningfully different.**"*

## ★★★★ Principle 1 — don't chase new (and its positive form: world-building)

The Hollywood cases return (*The Fountain*: 52% RT, $16M on $35M — *"**it is a work of art. I could
not get to the end of the movie. And I love art**"*; **Tom Cruise** via **Rich Hall**), plus two
commercial cases: the **Porsche 911** (*"they're all the same, and they're very successful at doing
this"*) and the **iPhone** (*"they just rotate the camera lens… **making the same phone again and
again. And for some reason, they still dominate**"*).

> *"So what can we learn from that? **Find something to do and do it really well. Do it again and
> again until it becomes perfect.**"* With **Bruce Lee**: *"**I fear not the man who has practiced
> 10,000 kicks once, but I fear the man who has practiced one kick 10,000 times.**"* And a line he
> clearly enjoys: *"**repetition is the mother of learning. It's the father of action — which makes it
> the architect of accomplishment.**"*

### ★★★★ The Alien franchise, and the reframe

*Alien* (*"in space, no one can hear you scream"*) → *Aliens* (James Cameron: *"how about we have
**aliens**?"* — *"this time it's war"*) → *Alien 3* (*"**on Earth** everyone can hear you scream"*) →
*Alien Resurrection* (*"they were supposed to end the trilogy, but **Hollywood likes to make a lot of
movie money**"*) → *Prometheus* (*"**we're still out of ideas — we'll just go to the beginning and
start the whole thing again**"*).

> ★★★★ *"So in Hollywood, **this is not called a lack of imagination. It's called world-building.**
> And this is an important concept. I'm sort of making fun of it, but I'm also trying to help you
> understand something: **when you create more and more of the same of what people like, you get to
> expand the universe.** … So many of us have **this addiction to creating new things, because we're
> chasing what's next. Chasing new is an external pursuit. What you should be doing is looking inward
> — what you can unearth and find out in yourself, and share that with the world.**"*

**Brand assets, reduced to four:** *"as far as I can see, I've reduced it down to four things: **a
name**… **a voice** — it speaks to you in a specific way, sometimes uplifting, sometimes it drops a
lot of f-bombs, **but that needs to be consistent, because we need to know who we're talking to** —
**an identity**, and **packaging**."*

## ★★★ Principle 2 — different is better than better

✅ **Sally Hogshead** credited again. The brand-or-product game returns (McDonald's, IKEA, Nike,
Starbucks, **Liquid Death**), and this time the room **fights him on Nike** — which he takes:

> *"Most runners I talk to would prefer a brand that most people don't wear — like **Asics or New
> Balance or Hoka** — one of these things that are really **designed for runners**, and they could
> care less what it looks like. **It's all about performance.**"*

Two audience moments worth keeping: he calls out a half-hearted show of hands (*"**you guys are not
being honest with me. I thought we said we're gonna be honest with each other**"*), and when someone
undercuts a runner in the room, *"**oh, shots fired. Hey, we already said this is a safe space for us
to be honest and direct.**"*

> ✅ **Products focus on features; brands focus on meaning** — restated.

**The car taglines as case studies:** **Mercedes-Benz** — *"the best or nothing… **we build to a
standard, not to a price point**"*; **BMW** — the ultimate driving machine; **Audi** — *"**never
follow**"*, the S-series campaign, and the Lexus dig (*"our drivers know how to park their own cars"*)
→ *"instead of just showing sheet metal or talking about horsepower, **they create attitude, a tone of
voice**… **and who here is going to say 'I'm a follower'? No one says that.**"*

> *"**Don't be afraid of being different. Be afraid of being the same as everyone else.**"*

## ★★★ Principle 3 — good is the price of entry

> *"For a period of time, **being good was a barrier to entry. But now good is expected. It's what it
> costs to play the game. So you cannot hang your hat on being good — if you're not good, you don't
> get to play.**"*

✅ The quadrant is credited here to **Marty Neumeier** (*The Designful Company*): start **not good and
not different** — *"we have a name for that: **knockoff, or cheap imitation**"* — because *"**you copy
others. It's how we learn to do anything in life: dancing, singing, math, art**"* — then get good but
stay undifferentiated, *"which means **we're a *me too*. We're a commodity.**"*

## ★★ The frame, the stress, and the AI aside

- **The opening**: *"I just want to check in with everybody — **if this is a safe space for us to be
  honest and direct with each other. Is that okay?**"* ⚠️ Worth noting against the corpus's open
  tension between *"create safe space"* (coaching) and *"I create unsafe space"* (teaching); here he
  is explicitly in the first mode.
- **The stress, admitted**: *"**I'm a little stressed out of my mind right now.** I have like **347
  slides. No exaggeration.** I was going to edit it down to 245… **but then I heard Neil say this
  morning: 'people just give 80%.' Well, I'm going to give 100%. So I'm back to 347.**"*
- ★★★ **A dated, cheerful AI position (Aug 2025)**: *"the elephant in the room is AI… this is what's
  going to happen all over the internet: **more and more faceless content.** **I'm so thrilled to tell
  you that I saw YouTube announced they're going to demonetise faceless AI-generated content.** That
  means all those people sending the robots to make content **are not going to make any money. That's
  an opportunity for all of us in this room.**"*
- ★★ **1,100 members** acknowledged on camera — up from *"1,000-plus"* a week earlier. A clip was cut
  for copyright and linked off-platform.
