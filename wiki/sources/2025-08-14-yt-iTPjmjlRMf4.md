# How to Beat the Competition Without Lowering Your Prices (2025-08-14)

> **Attribution:** a short workshop excerpt. One or two audience interjections are marked; everything
> else is Chris.

## Summary

★★★ Eight minutes, one argument, well built. He takes **Ronald Baker's** claim that *"there is no such
thing as a commodity — you just lack imagination"*, walks it up an escalating ladder of examples
(lettuce → money → stock certificates), then supplies the mechanism underneath premium pricing — **time
to result** and **certainty of outcome** — and closes with a rule about **when to use a guarantee**
that is more precise than the usual advice.

## Key claims (2025-08-14)

### The death spiral he opens with

> *"A lot of you have been taught, or have taught yourself, some crazy rule in the universe where **if
> the market gets tricky, the way you win clients is** — to do what? … So when we lower our price,
> what does the other person think if they lose the business to you? **They lower their price. And
> what do you do? Lower your price. And then before you know it, you pay the client to do the work.**
> **How long can you run a company when you pay the client to do the work?** However much cash you
> have at the bank — and when that's done, your credit card — **and then you're done done.**"*

### ★★★★ "There is no such thing as a commodity. You just lack imagination."

Credited plainly and repeatedly to **Ronald Baker**, ***Implementing Value Pricing*** — *"highly
recommend you read this book if you want to be a master of pricing."*

**The lettuce ladder**, told as an escalation:

| Step | The move | The price |
|---|---|---|
| A head of lettuce | none — *"one head is the same as another"* | ~79¢ |
| **Bagged, pre-washed, triple-rinsed** | *"applying a little creativity and labour — **you make it more convenient**"* | more than the head; *"you can sell three bags for four times as much money"* |
| **A salad kit** — croutons, cheese, a dressing packet | *"**why do people even want lettuce in the first place?**"* | ~$12 |
| **A licensed name on it** (*"Puck Express"*) | borrowed brand | ~$14 |

*"And it doesn't end. **It can just go into infinity if you want.**"* — with the Japanese **$150
cantaloupe** as the outer limit: *"I understand these are **lovingly cared for** by Japanese
farmers… **they're talking to it at night, telling bedtime stories**, I don't know. But it is a $150
cantaloupe. **I don't get it.** So **there is no limit to what you can charge.**"*

**Then the harder examples, both Baker's:**

- **Money itself.** *"**Can anything be more of a commodity than money?** Who here would buy $1 from me
  for $1.10? Even $1.01?"* — *"**you lack imagination**"* — and when someone in the room says *"you
  got to sign it,"* Chris: *"**I could sign it. You're right. So now you're using a little
  creativity.**"*
- **A stock certificate.** A company sells blue-chip stock **above market price** — *"the stock might
  be $99 that day, but they're going to sell it to you for **between $150 and $250, depending on the
  quality of the frame**"* — because *"there are **grandparents who want to give their grandchildren
  something** for their birthday, bar mitzvah, Christmas, and they want to be able to **print the
  certificate and frame it.**"*

> ★★★★ **The turn on the audience:** *"Now, this is the part that's going to hurt your soul a little
> bit. **Ask yourself: do you lack imagination?**"*

### ★★★★ The two variables under premium pricing

> *"Why would people pay more for something than they need to? … **It's because if it's immediate.**
> We pay more for things if we can get the results immediately. I bought a salad — if I've got to
> handle the leaf, wash it, spin it dry, that takes time. **Time to purchase, to my mouth. Make it
> faster, I'll pay you more.**"*

Credited to **Alex Hormozi** (*$100M Offers*) — with an unusually explicit crediting norm attached:

> *"**If you like the concepts I'm sharing with you today, I highly encourage you to support the
> authors. I'm not trying to take money from people. Please buy the books and support them. So as
> often as I can, I will mention who they are, and we'll repeat it.**"*

**The worked comparison:** meditation versus Prozac. *"If you're a meditation teacher… **maybe that
entire industry is worth $100 million, where Prozac is a multi-billion-dollar industry** — because
**the time to result is short and it's guaranteed.** … If I meditate, I get distracted. I'm not sure —
does this work? It takes a really long time to master."*

> ★★★★ *"So there are **two variables**: **time delay**, which will influence price, and **certainty
> of outcome**. … **When you think about your product or service, try to reduce the time to
> satisfaction and completion, and try to increase the certainty of the outcome happening.**"*

### ★★★ Guarantees — a precise rule, from his own practice

> *"Every time somebody gives you a money-back guarantee, you're more likely to buy, **because the
> certainty of outcome is there.** But **if you're going to do a guarantee, there has to be a way for
> you to do it so you don't get killed.**"*

**And the disclosure that makes it usable:**

> *"Every time I've had a client who's going to spend a couple hundred thousand with us sit on the
> fence — '**oh, I'm not sure, Chris**' — and we've done everything we can to convince them, **the
> last thing I do is I throw in the guarantee. And every time I've done that — I've done it twice —
> they close.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**So use it wisely. Use it for the right moment — not to get the job, but to secure it. And
> there's a difference.**"*
