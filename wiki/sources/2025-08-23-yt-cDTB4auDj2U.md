# Your Dream Life Is Waiting. You're Just Avoiding It (2025-08-23)

> **Attribution:** a **one-on-one mentorship conversation** with a member of his team who recently
> moved to LA. The interlocutor asks questions and describes his own situation; his lines are marked
> and are not persona data. **Family is referenced by role only** (see the fishing story).

## Summary

★★★ **LANDMARK.** A rare format: Chris coaching one person on camera, at length, with the mentee
pushing back. It produces **two origin stories the corpus did not have** — where his inner dialogue
came from, and where his analytical eye was trained — plus his most complete account of **why he
thinks he is a better speaker than his peers**, a full diagnosis of **what he thinks holds people
back**, and the fishing story with his son.

## ★★★★ Two origin stories

### Where the inner dialogue came from — grade school

> *"I'll tell you the first part of it, which is **much younger than you would think — probably in
> grade school somewhere. Being an introvert, being quite isolated and feeling very much apart. The
> only person I could talk to is myself.** And so **I develop a pretty high level of self-awareness
> and inner dialogue that's quite clear** — thinking things, and thinking *why do I think that*, then
> asking myself: **is that a helpful thought to have? Is this going to build me up, or is it going to
> tear me down?** **I don't have an identity yet, but I have that inner dialogue, and it's pretty
> strong.**"*

And what it gives him now: *"if I act really weird, **I know it's because I'm coming from a negative
emotive state** — I'm jealous, I'm feeling abandoned, I'm feeling unsure of myself, I'm feeling really
insecure — and so I'm going to say and do weird things. **And I'm aware of it. I'm very conscious that
I'm doing this, and I've chosen to do it**, even though I know it's not a good thing."*

> ★★★★ **The completion of the self:** *"**it's not until I find myself in a skill — in terms of
> graphic design — that I start to build an identity, coupled with a self-awareness.**"* → *"**So many
> people don't have the self-awareness, because they don't like to think about what they think about.
> And then other people don't have a skill** — they faked it through life, they go through the
> motions, they're in middle management. **They fool a lot of people, but they're not really fooling
> themselves. So they don't really have a strong core. And I think it's very important for everyone to
> have a strong core.**"*

> *"**My core comes from seeking out design as a profession, throwing 10,000% of myself into it,
> soaking up information like a sponge, and being very disciplined by applying exactly what my
> teachers tell me to do. It's a theme.**"*

### ★★★★ Where the analytical eye came from — a film class at Art Center

> *"During one of my academic terms… I took a class I thought was going to be really fun. It was
> called **Critiquing Current Movies**. It was taught by **Jay Chapman** — he's a film nut. It was
> mostly film students, and **I was probably one of two designers in that room.**"*

The format: the class picks blockbusters one week, the professor picks the next; *"it's a
**discussion-based class. There's no essays, nothing to write. So you have to be really good.** And
I'm surrounded by film majors who understand lensing, directors, genres, film theory, film history —
**and I don't know any of this stuff.**"*

**What he actually did about it:**

> *"I go watch the movie and then I leave the theatre. **I buy another ticket, I go back in.** The
> first time was just to watch it as an audience member. **The second time was to dissect it.** … And
> I realised **it's not a good use of my money, which I didn't have a lot of**, and really not a good
> use of my time. **So I'm going to have to force myself to watch twice in one sitting** — watch as
> the participant, **and** watch as the person who's going to have to talk about this film."*

With the notepad — *"more like **a safety blanket**, because it's almost impossible to write in the
dark"* — and the cost: *"my mind was just **jello** at that point, because **it's using all of my
brain** to remember what's happening in an hour-and-a-half film."* The scope: *"narrative arc,
character development, lighting, art direction, camera movement. **There was nothing that was off
limits.** So you kind of have to remember it all."*

The intimidation, and the fix: *"the film students would just talk about things. I'm like — **what?
How did you get that? What are you watching?** … they have language I was unfamiliar with. **But
you're a student, so you learn.**"* By mid-semester he could hold his own.

> ★★★★ *"**I credit that class, and the structure of that class, for helping me to train my eye.**"*

**And the exercise he gives for people without the class:** decide **eight or nine questions you must
be able to answer before you watch** — narrative arc, the three acts, protagonist and antagonist and
*"what is the lesson they both need to learn"*, art direction, camera direction (*"how it's lensed and
how it moves, and why they do certain things to tell you the audience something"*), sound design
(*"how you're being manipulated emotionally, and why it works or why it doesn't"*), colour grading,
subtext, secondary and tertiary characters, the metaphor, the main titles and end credits, and
foreshadowing. Then watch twice. Secondly: *"**watch film geeks talk about films**"* — Tarantino and
the like.

## ★★★★ The mentorship diagnosis

- **The anchor and the compass:** *"you're very good about being grateful for the things you have —
  it's one of your best traits… **and I never want you to lose that part. But that is like an anchor,
  and we need a compass. The anchor keeps us grounded, but it doesn't allow us to find direction,
  because we're usually content and happy with where we're at.**"*
- **On being the strongest in the room:** *"you were doing better than most of your friends… **you're
  at the tip of the spear, and that's always a sign that you're in the wrong circles of people.**"*
- ★★★★ **Sell the outcome, not the activity:** *"**I keep encouraging you not to describe yourself as
  the things you do, but the outcomes that you achieve for people.** That small shift will drastically
  change how you talk to yourself — but more importantly, **the frame in which people look at you.**"*
  → *"**Video production is what you do, but it's not what you sell.**"* → *"the least profitable and
  hardest part of your business is the production. **It's all the other stuff, the intangibles** — I
  want to make sure you feel comfortable on camera, I want to make sure you're in your zone of genius…
  **and I try to make the rest of it transparent or invisible to you.**"*
- ★★★★ **Vision, defined literally:** *"**the whole point of the word *vision* is to see what no one
  can see. That requires faith. It requires imagination.**"* — against the pattern he names in the
  mentee: *"**until you're there and seeing someone doing it, you can't see it — and you hold yourself
  back.**"*
- **The house moment**, which he treats as the turning point: *"you said — **what do I need to do? Who
  do I need to become so that this is my reality?** Not just to rent this house, **but to own this
  house.** And **that was the first spark I've seen in you where you allow yourself, give yourself
  permission to dream so big.** … **The first part is the want, and the clarity of the vision of the
  future.**"*
- **The immigrant mindset on money:** *"**that's just too much money. I don't need that. That's all
  hype. I'll find a cheaper alternative.** … But more important, **the story you tell yourself is: I
  can't afford this.**"*
- ★★★★ **The advice he calls his secret:** *"**find a couple of people — not a lot. When I say a
  couple, I mean two or less. One. And just do whatever they tell you to do.** If they are where you
  want to be, if they have that clarity, that insight, the business acumen, or the style or taste that
  you want — **just do it. Just follow it. Don't even trust yourself.** Because you're saying quite
  loudly *I don't know what I'm doing* — **but when I'm in the presence of someone who does know what
  they're doing, I'm not going to listen to them, because I trust myself more. Makes no freaking sense
  whatsoever. But that's what people do.**"*

### ★★★★ The gift people sabotage

> *"Oftentimes when I meet somebody, **they have a gift and they're doing everything in their life to
> sabotage their gift.**"*

The unnamed example (an SEO specialist who keeps switching fields — law, tech, photography, SEO):
*"**this person's curse is that they're smart and they can learn lots of things. That's a strength.
Their weakness is they're smart and they can do lots of different things. So they won't pick one
thing.**"* ⚠️ He half-realises he has identified them (*"now I think I've revealed who that person is,
but whatever"*) and adds *"we love you."*

### ★★★★ Sacrifice, not burnout

The mentee objects: you can be good at something and not enjoy it — that's burnout.

> *"**No, we call that sacrifice. You sacrifice some momentary joy for a bigger joy, which is
> freedom.**"*

And the scarcity argument that follows: *"**everything worth having is not going to be easy. It's
going to be hard. If it weren't hard, everyone would do it and it wouldn't be valuable. Difficult
things create scarcity. Scarce things are valuable.**"* With his stated payoff — *"my favourite
expression"* — *"**sleeping very comfortably on a mattress of cash.**"*

### ★★★★ The framing question of the whole episode

> *"**Everybody needs to do something in their life that they're not doing, but they won't do. What is
> it you need to do? And then we'll figure out why you're not doing it, and then we'll figure it
> out.** Because **you can't get where I want you to go if you're the same person.**"*

> *"**You can't make 10 times more money being the same person. You have to be 10 times the person you
> are today to make 10 times as much money. That's why the game of self-development is so important.
> That's the game I'm in.**"*

The reasons people don't: *"**fear of change**, or because **they don't think it's fun**, or **it just
sounds like a lot of work**"* — and, to the mentee specifically, *"**you're addicted to the
routine.**"*

## ★★★★ Why he thinks he is better than other speakers

Asked directly, he gives a structured answer rather than a modest one:

1. **Repertoire.** *"A lot of speakers **only have one talk topic and one way of delivering it.** Much
   of it is **autobiographical** — here's what I've done, here's my life. **There's not too many ways
   you can tell that story.** And that's the easiest thing to do."*
2. **Identity.** *"**My identity is an educator. Their identity might not be** — so they don't want to
   do that work. **They want to have one hit song.** … **Very few people have multiple hit songs. The
   Beatles, the Rolling Stones, Elton John** — that's why they're so rare. Arguably Coldplay, The
   Weeknd, Twenty One Pilots."*
3. ★★★★ **The asymmetry with music.** *"**Unlike music, public speaking doesn't move your soul that
   way** — you and I can listen to the same song a thousand times. [But] **people can't really book
   you for the same thing multiple times to the same audience.**"*
4. **Craft.** *"**How are your slides? If you're going to have slides, make them good.** Unless they're
   designers or want to invest in it, their slides are usually terrible. Some have no personality —
   very even tone or monotone. **They can deliver the message, but there's no movement. You're not
   moving me.**"*
5. ★★★★ **And the failure mode at the other extreme:** *"then there are **very moving speakers** — the
   people who will get you to cry, to laugh, to sing with them. **And then you walk away thinking:
   what did I learn? What can I apply? Is this theoretical, or is this real?**"*
6. **The composite, and the practice:** *"it's this combination of **charisma, personality, tone,
   performance, design, intentionality, self-improvement** that I strive to work on at all times. It's
   not that I think my last talk was not good — but **I reflect on it and say: what could be better?
   What can I get rid of? And what can I try that's new?** So it could be **80% what I've already done
   and 20% something new. Because at some point there's nothing left for me to change — then I know
   that's one hit song. And I'm good with that.**"*

> ★★ **An outside verdict**, from the mentee: *"**he is 50-some years old and he will outwork me.**"* —
> with the example: *"you're up to four o'clock in the morning like, *I just had to redo it.*"*

## ★★★★ The access critique — and the questions he wishes he were asked

> *"**I don't think you're taking full advantage of the opportunity access that you have right now.**
> I've never travelled with my mentor. I've never gotten access to my mentor the way that you do. **I
> saw my former coach and mentor an hour, an hour and a half each week.**"*

> *"**Every time I get off stage, I ask you: what is it that you want to know?** Or I'll ask my other
> protégé, Uma. **And nobody's asking me real questions.** So — okay, I'm good. **I'm not going to
> force it on you. I have other things to do.**"*

**The questions he would ask, in his place:** *"last time you did this, **you removed that, you changed
this — I like this better. Why? Why is that working better? Did you feel that?** Or, **when this
person asked you this thing and messed you up, how did you come back with this?** … **What are you
thinking about in between the beats — before, during, and after?** … **Those are very important things
to know, and you can shortcut your whole process.**"*

### ★★★★ Attachment to the old self

> *"**It's the attachment to the old self that creates resistance to you becoming the person you
> should become.** And if you can just **divorce yourself from the old self** — those old
> relationships, the familiarity — then you would have been here much faster."*

And he includes himself: *"**I have the same problem. I have attachment to things, to ideas. And I've
learned in my life some things I can stay attached to that won't harm me, and some things will totally
ruin me. So I need to be able to move on. And you can see I move pretty quickly relative to other
people — and I just need people to keep pace with me, because my team needs to move at that speed.**"*

### ★★★★ Good vs. excellent

> *"Use your clients **as a lab for me to test ideas.** If we change a thumbnail 300 times, or run an
> ad campaign for thumbnails beforehand — **what is it we're learning?** And eventually **you do it
> intuitively.** … **Be a student of the game.** I'm pretty sure **you're not reading the reports from
> YouTube**… not reverse-engineering other people's content. **So what happens is: we're showing up to
> do a good job, but we're not showing up to be excellent. That's the difference.**"*

## ★★★ Recording in high fidelity — the mechanism

> *"When we watch movies, or a concert, or we go to an event — **you're going to see my brain work in
> hyperdrive.** I'm like: that's not good, that's good. … **What I'm doing is I'm recording in very
> high fidelity what is and isn't working, so I can make a catalogue of it in my mind so I can access
> it later.** So **most of us go through life and we sleepwalk through life. We're not paying attention
> to anything.**"*

✅ **Second attestation of *record in HD*** (first: [[2025-05-13-yt-Anr8YYbt2ws|2025-05-13]]) — and the
first time with the *catalogue* mechanism attached. The worked example is a shoe: *"the geometric
shapes in the front of your shoe and the faceted edge — a nice contrast, rectilinear versus
curvilinear… **that's why that shoe's working.** … **It shouldn't follow the curve of the foot.**"*

### ★★★★ Silencing the competing voices

> *"How am I able to see things? **Because I try very much to silence all the little naysaying voices
> in my head and find my one true voice, and let that voice do most of the talking.** You have
> conflicting voices — most people do. **What will people think? Is this cool? What do I really think?
> And will people judge me for that? And what's going to be popular this month?** Those are all voices
> in competition… **so you're listening to all the voices while not listening to any of them. And it
> comes out really mushy. That's what we're trying to avoid.**"*

## ★★★ The fishing story

Four days in the Canadian wilderness with his son (21, **name-free by policy**). The son goes rogue on
technique — *"**I think this is a sign of developing your own personality and stepping into manhood.
So I respect that**"* — and then hooks a big fish and **the line breaks.**

- The offer refused: *"if there's a big fish he's like, '**Dad, you take it**.' I'm like, '**No, son.
  This is for you. You have to learn these things in your life.**'"* — and the honesty about the
  wanting: *"**I wanted it for him, but I needed him to catch it.**"*
- The blame, and how he handled it: *"'Dad, **you told me to keep the line up. That's why it
  broke.**' … I said, '**There's a difference between keeping your rod up and keeping tension on the
  line. And I've told you some things — you don't listen, so that's okay. I'm not blaming you.**'"*
- The diagnosis: *"**the problem has been we've caught two small fish, so you can be sloppy in your
  technique. It's created bad habits.**"*
- The result, which is why he tells it: at dinner that night the son volunteers, unprompted, *"**I've
  had bad habits and dad set me straight.**"* → *"**a 20-minute span between the lesson learned and him
  saying to people: bad habits have been formed, I need to break them.** … **For some people it would
  take two, three years. For him, 10, 20 minutes.**"*

## ★★ Other

- ★★★ **Why he keeps the brain working**: *"**it's like a muscle. I want my brain muscle to be strong.
  I want the ability to recall things. I don't know if Alzheimer's or dementia is in my future. It can
  be. I'm going to give myself the best shot by engaging as much of my brain as possible** — all the
  different lobes and hemispheres. **Because if you don't use it, I think you lose it.**"*
- **On rest**: *"**I rest hard** — when I go to sleep, I sleep. When I play games, I play games. And
  when I'm in active learning mode, I'm as active as possible."*
- **An epithet he repeats without objecting to it**: *"they call you **the charming razor blade.**"*
- ★★ **The LA argument**: Miami is *"always hot, always humid, you're always sweating in a state of
  greasiness"*; Southern California has *"350 or 360 days out of the year"* and, more importantly,
  *"**a whole ecosystem of people who think of themselves as world-breakers, as doers and makers. And
  when you're in that ecosystem, they don't care about money. They care about their dream.**"*
- ★★ **A long, enthusiastic digression on *Foundation* and Isaac Asimov** — the three laws of robotics,
  and **psychohistory**: *"**his theory is we can predict society in general, but not individual
  action.** … It requires at least **three generations** and a population of **at least a billion
  people.** So **it's a science applied to human dynamics.**"* Useful as a sample of his register when
  genuinely enthusiastic rather than teaching.
