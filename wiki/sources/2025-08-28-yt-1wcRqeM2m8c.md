# 10 Tips I Follow To Have An Authentic Millionaire Personal Brand (2025-08-28)

> **Attribution:** solo, scripted, to camera. All Chris.

## Summary

A tight scripted consolidation of the personal-branding argument — *"I'm going to pull back the
curtain and save you from over 10 years of trial and error."* Most of it restates material already
recorded from the VidCon and "Blanding In" talks, **but four things here are new or better**: the
**"non-fungible human"** line with the racial-stereotype self-analysis behind it, the **three
story-excavation questions**, the **Colonel Sanders** case, and — importantly — the **repeatable
story credited to Kier McLaren**, which earlier tellings left unattributed.

## What's new

### ★★★★ "Don't be a fungible human" — and the stereotype he is working against

> *"When you think of an Asian man, what do you think of? You might think **hacker, nerdy, good with
> math, quiet, demure** — somebody who **doesn't take up a lot of space.** And so when I think about
> that, **that makes me upset, because I'm none of those things. I'm loud. I'm opinionated. I love
> design.**"*

> *"And I think **I can borrow parts of different cultures to make it a little confusing for people as
> to who they think I am.** I borrow elements from **sartorial sources like Esquire and GQ**, but I
> also **borrow from the streets and go hard on the hip-hop influences.** By combining those things,
> **I make it difficult for people to put me into a category of everyone else. Therefore, I become a
> one of one.**"*

> ★★★★ *"My line to you is: **don't be a fungible human. Be a non-fungible human.**"*

This is the clearest statement in the corpus of **why he dresses the way he does** — not taste, but a
deliberate refusal of a category — and it names the stereotype explicitly.

### ★★★★ Three questions to excavate your story

1. **Backstory** — *"who were my parents? What did they do? **What birth order am I in?** What country
   or city was I born in? And where do I live now?"*
2. **The challenging moments** — *"the ones with **peak emotional height.** … **There's a reason why
   those pop up to your memory first. It's because they resonate in here.**"*
3. **The transformative people** — *"coaches, teachers, parents **who said something to you that
   changed the way you looked at the world, who reframed it for you.**"*

### ★★★★ The repeatable story — credited, at last

> *"**I learned this concept from my business coach, Kier McLaren**, who told me: **you need to tell a
> story that's repeatable.** To which I asked him: what is a repeatable story? **It's a story that is
> self-contained and easy enough to remember that other people do the heavy lifting for you** and tell
> other people about you."*

✅ **This attributes a concept the [[2025-07-29-yt-hDlWbmH96hw|VidCon talk]] presented without a
source.** Correct the attribution wherever the repeatable story is cited.

**And the case study, which is better than the abstract version:** **Harland Sanders**, who founded
KFC in his 70s — *"**growing up, I thought Colonel Sanders was a mascot, a fictitious character.** And
what's interesting is over time the advertising industry figured out his story and wanted to make sure
he was at the forefront… **so we knew it wasn't a fictitious made-up thing by an advertising
department. He's a real person. And for some reason, when I know that, I have a deeper connection to
deep-fried wholesome chicken.**"* → *"**dig deep into your story bucket and find your 11 secret herbs
and spices.**"*

### ★★★★ The Dip, reframed through scarcity

> *"**As the expression goes: people don't fail, they quit.** And here's how I want you to reframe this
> for yourself. **When things get really hard, just remember: they're supposed to be.** Because
> **things that are difficult are a natural obstacle** for people to achieve the same thing. **And
> things that are rarely achieved, things that are scarce, are valuable. So if everyone was doing it,
> if it was easy for everyone to achieve, it wouldn't be worth much.**"*

✅ Better than the earlier Dip tellings, which stopped at *push through*. Same argument he makes about
sacrifice on [[2025-08-23-yt-cDTB4auDj2U|2025-08-23]].

### ★★★ His own catchphrases, listed together

Teaching the device via **Game of Thrones** (*"a Lannister always pays their debts"*), **Nike**
(*just do it*), **Apple** (*think different*) and **Simon Sinek** (*"people don't buy what you do,
they buy why you do it"*; *"leaders eat last"*) — *"**he's using alliteration. He's using repetition.
He's using rhyme. This makes your catchphrase easy to remember.**"*

Then his own, which he has never listed in one place before:

> *"**Inch by inch, it's a cinch. And yard by yard, it's hard.** And **if it's not on paper, it's
> vapor.** And **the more you you are, the more you are** — **that's yours truly.**"*

### ★★★ The Serengeti argument, and one degree of separation

> *"One thing you need to realise about how we evolved as humans is **we can spot differences.** When
> we're staring out in the Serengeti and the blades of grass are there, **if something is moving, if
> something stands out, we notice it right away, and we have to assess whether or not it's
> dangerous.**"*

> *"In modern society we wear a corporate uniform… **it's called a uniform because it means the
> same.** … **It doesn't take a lot. It takes one degree of separation for you to stand out. If
> everyone's wearing a blue suit, wear a red suit.**"*

With the guard he attaches himself: *"before you get mad at me and think *well Chris, you just want us
to be a clown and do different for different's sake* — **be meaningfully different.**"*

### ★★ Tribes, and Dunkin' vs. Starbucks

> *"The brand with the strongest tribe wins, because **consumers are choosing which team they're on.**
> They'll **cross the street. They'll pay a premium.** And **in a weird way, we're secretly hoping that
> the other tribe loses, because we want ours to win.**"*

The case: Dunkin's *"**friends don't let friends drink Starbucks**"* — *"**that is how you draw a line
in the sand and delineate yourself from the competition. You're either on team us, or you're getting
on the bus.**"*
