# What You Say Is Who You Are (short, 2025-09-06)

> **Attribution:** ✅ Chris. ⚠️ **Truncated at both ends** — it opens mid-sentence and ends mid-example.
> Nothing is inferred beyond what is spoken.

## Summary

★★★★ **An identity claim the non-violent-language thread does not make.** That thread is about the
*effect* of your words on others; **this says your words ARE your character**, as far as anyone can
ever know it.

## Key claims (2025-09-06)

> ★★★★★ *"[You] **can't be a kind person if you don't use kind words.** It doesn't happen that way.
> **WHAT YOU SAY IS WHO YOU ARE.**"*

★★★★★ **The argument, and it is an epistemic one rather than a moral one:** *"**People only experience
you based on what comes out of your mouth.** Rarely do they have this full interaction, **this whole
display of your entire life.** They base their entire perception of you — **your entire reputation is
simply what you say.**"*

✅ **This is the sharpest version in the corpus of why words are not a surface.** Not *choose kind words
because it works*, but: **others have no access to anything else.**

★★★★ **And the reduction, deliberately blunt:** *"**And it's this easy. You say nice things —
congrats, you're a nice person. If you say things that aren't nice — well, congratulations, you're now
seen as somebody that's not liked.**"*

★★★★ **The reframe of dislike:** *"**When you say 'I don't like this person', what you really mean is:
THEY SAY THINGS I DON'T LIKE.**"* ⚠️ The following example is **cut off mid-sentence** and is not
reproduced.
