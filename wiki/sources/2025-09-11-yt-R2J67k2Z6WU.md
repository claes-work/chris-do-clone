> **⚠️ ATTRIBUTION — GUEST-DOMINATED, and the longest transcript in this ingest run (131 KB).** The
> guest is **Jasmin Alić** (see [[2025-07-08-yt-1z1lsaQJ8K4|2025-07-08]]); **the LinkedIn tactics are

> marked **[CHRIS]**.

## Summary

A long live masterclass. Alić teaches commenting strategy, profile mechanics and posting cadence;
Chris hosts, argues with him in two places, and **coaches audience members directly** — which is where
the value is. The harvest is bigger than the 2025-07-08 Alić episode because the format gives Chris
room: **"writing is thinking made visual"**, his definition of **bad work**, **the heuristics
argument**, **the three stages of awareness**, and a complete **live offer-design correction** on a
participant.

## Chris's own material

### ★★★★ "Writing is thinking made visual"

Derived on the spot from a definition he already carries:

> **[CHRIS]** *"There's this definition that goes for — **what is typography? Typography is thinking
> made visual.** But **we can easily just borrow that and say: what is writing? Writing is thinking
> made visual.** So **when you're leaving a comment, it's your opportunity to know what it is that
> you're really thinking, what your ideas are.** And that's really important. If you treat it like
> that, you're going to do much, much better — because **we all need practice to articulate what it is
> that we know.**"*

### ★★★★ What makes work terrible

> **[CHRIS]** *"**I don't have an aversion to work.** I know why sometimes, for people, work is
> terrible. **When you do something and you put your time, energy and resources towards it and you
> don't get results — that's horrible work. That's painful work.**"*

A definition of bad work by **outcome**, not by content or effort.

### ★★★★ Heuristics in an overloaded world

> **[CHRIS]** *"We have to remember it's an **increasingly complicated world where we have thousands of
> marketing messages sent to us every single day, on every single platform. Our capacity to process is
> very low. So we're looking for heuristics — little shortcuts that let us know: this is an honest
> person.** It could be a profile picture. **This is a trustworthy person, because they have the
> pedigree, the educational certificates — or because a million people…**"*

✅ The mechanism underneath *social is the new résumé*: not that followers are merit, but that
**overload forces proxies.**

### ★★★★ The three stages of awareness

> **[CHRIS]** *"People go through three stages… **problem-unaware, then solution-unaware, then
> product-unaware.** So you're moving them through different stages of awareness. **People think: oh,
> you already know what the problem is, you already know what the solution is, so I have the product
> for you. That's the problem.** … **What we have to do is use social to make them aware of the
> problem. The way we make them aware of the problem is we have to talk about the problem. And almost
> all problems come from interpersonal relationships and emotional [ones]. That's what you have to
> focus in on.**"*

With the diagnosis: *"**people treat their content like it's bottom of funnel.**"*

> ⚠️ **Recorded as stated.** The ladder he names compresses the standard awareness sequence; the
> teaching point (**move people through stages; don't open at product**) is unaffected, but the
> labels are his and are not tidied here.

### ★★★ He refuses the guest's escape hatch

Alić carves out *"the 1%"* who succeed with tiny audiences. Chris will not let it stand:

> **[CHRIS]** *"**I don't think that 1% is watching this. I don't think that 1% cares. I don't think
> that 1% is really posting.** They're so top of the food chain, high ticket price, that **only a
> hundred people could afford what they do anyway. So they're not even involved in this equation.**
> … They do **exclusive invite-only retreats. There's not even marketing — and it makes their offer
> even better that you don't get to know about it unless you know somebody who knows somebody.** So
> **for the rest of us who are watching this piece of content, I'm saying the 100%. This is real.**"*

**And the empirical test he offers:** *"**when was the last time you actually went on some kind of
search — where you can see the results and the social proof — and you went to the bottom first?
Probably not that often.** And if you're one of those people who create videos that get 10 views
versus 3 million views — **are you getting more conversions? I've never heard of a case like this
before.**"* With the aside: *"just because **I'm a glutton for punishment, maybe because I'm a
masochist**, sometimes I do go to the one that had 15 views. I'm like — **oh, maybe somebody missed a
sleeper. It's actually terrible.**"*

### ★★★★ A live offer-design correction

Coaching a participant who wanted to sell to designers:

> **[CHRIS]** *"You may have an entire **business design problem** — that, maybe buy me lunch, I'll
> tell you what it is. But the challenge for you is: **you do not want to sell a product to a group
> who can't afford to buy it.** Every time you tell me you want to — **and we make the mistake** —
> **you sell to broke designers. They might want the solution; they can't afford you.**"*

The redirect: sell to **HR, senior management, VPs** — and then the content that reaches them:
*"you have to start making content that [makes them think] '**we need her help. She clearly
understands what we're going through, and she's been through it.**' … You can say: '**I survived 10
years in the tech space. Super toxic male bro culture. I know how to get you out of it.**' **And then
you have to be able to teach people.**"*

Two moves inside that exchange worth keeping on their own:

- ★★★★ **The listening redirect**, when the participant looks to her husband: *"**What are you
  hearing? It doesn't matter what I'm saying. It doesn't matter what your husband is saying. What are
  you hearing?**"* ✅ Full-value listening, practised rather than described.
- ★★★★ **The close:** *"I have to try. And once I try, then I will know."* → *"**So, there's no
  trying. There's just doing.** … **Just do it.**"*
- ★★★★ *"**I think the problem with really smart, educated, successful people is they think too
  much.**"* — the guest calls it *"a backhanded compliment"*; he doesn't deny it.
- ★★ And the blunt observation that opens the coaching: *"I don't think you write about this at all.
  **Don't even give me a look.**"*

### ★★★ On timing, and on planting seeds

- *"I don't know if you've noticed, **the LinkedIn reach is not as generous as it used to be.**
  People are like, oh my god, it's hard now. And **I told y'all: get on it while the gravy was hot and
  fresh. And you waited, and you waited, until everybody got on it — and that's the problem.**"*
- *"**Everybody thinks that the minute you need social to work for you, then you begin the work. But
  that was like three years ago. You have to be planting the seeds** — because otherwise **all your
  posts wind up being super transactional.**"*
- ★★ **The scale question he keeps asking:** *"**How many clients do you really need to actually be
  booked out** — to feel like, oh my god, I can't take it any more, I need a waitlist? **You don't
  need a hundred clients at once. You need three, four, five, ten, depending on your team.**"*
- ★★ On the guest's own numbers: *"sometimes I look at your post and **there are more comments than
  likes, which is ridiculous to me. How does that even happen?**"* — to which Alić answers, *"because
  **you've never let your audience down.**"*

## Jasmin Alić's system (guest — context only, NOT persona data)

- **Comment before you post.** *"If you want to get through to people, **you first got to get in front
  of people.** And **commenting makes it so much easier, because it gives you that added visibility
  without depending on an algorithm.**"* Low stakes: *"you don't care if a comment goes viral."*
- **Go broader to find your ICP** — *"if you want to grow your audience, you've got to target broader,
  because otherwise **you can't find your ICP elsewhere. It's mathematically impossible.**"* — with the
  1% carve-out Chris rejects.
- Plus profile mechanics, posting cadence, and the pinned-comment strategy carried over from the
  earlier episode.
- **Biography, his**: a former rapper (*"Giants Records"*), now a university teacher.
