# Ditch Networking. Build An Authentic Personal Brand Instead. — w/ Matt Essam (2025-09-14)

> ## ⚠️⚠️ **THE EXCEPTION: this is the FIRST episode in which Chris and Matt Essam appear together.**
> All **thirteen** prior Essam instances on this channel are **Chris-absent** and fenced do-not-train
> (see [[../entities/matt-essam|matt-essam]]). **This one is a genuine two-way conversation**, framed
> by Chris as *"we're going to hopefully persuade him or nudge him towards why he would want to develop
> a personal brand. **I have a different take on it.**"* Essam's own positions are marked **[MATT]**
> and remain **his** — they are recorded here as the other side of a debate, not as teaching. Chris's
> material is marked **[CHRIS]** and is persona data.

## Summary

★★★ **LANDMARK**, for two reasons. First, it resolves a long-standing anomaly in the corpus: Essam and
Chris finally appear together, arguing. Second, the format is **Chris running a Socratic audit on a
skeptic** — he barely argues; he interviews Essam about how he got work *before* social media and lets
the answers make the case. Along the way it produces **the most complete account of Blind's
pre-social-media marketing stack** anywhere in the corpus, a **sixth attestation of the
non-violent-language thread** (this time aimed at a colleague, on camera, and conceded), and an
unusually candid passage on **his own boundaries and how they read to other people.**

## The setup — a dinner in Dubai

Both were at the **1 Billion Followers Summit** in January; Essam lives part-time in Dubai. On a
restaurant terrace, over wine:

> **[MATT]** *"Chris, this personal branding stuff is nonsense, isn't it?"* — which he then sharpens:
> *"**attention for the sake of attention is worthless**… **I'd rather have no money and be a starving
> artist than be on camera in front of a million people doing stupid stuff for the sake of being
> famous.**"*

★★★ **Chris grants the premise before disputing the application** — which is the whole reason the
episode works: *"Matt has just voiced, I think, **the opinion of so many creative people I know out
there**… the pursuit of fame or notoriety for the sake of it being a pretty hollow pursuit. **And I
would agree with that general statement — but that's not what we're doing here.**"*

He also sets the terms explicitly: *"I'm going to ask you to listen with **an open heart, a curious
mind**, and allow the conversation to go wherever it goes. **I'm not only saying this to our listener,
I'm also saying this to Matthew.**"* And the sequencing: *"**today the focus is all on mindset and our
belief systems, because getting into tactics won't really matter if you're not really bought in.**"*

## ★★★★ The Socratic audit — he lets the skeptic make his case for him

Rather than argue, Chris interviews Essam about pre-social-media lead generation. The answers:
**in-person networking events** (~£10, 100–150 people, 1–2 hours weekly, *"I need to lie down after
those events"*), **literal door-knocking cold outreach** (*"I'd literally go into people's
offices"*), and **recruitment agencies**.

Then he closes it:

> **[CHRIS]** *"So they've stopped serving the utility for you… **so what's replaced it?**"* —
> **[MATT]** *"My online presence, I guess. Building a bit of a LinkedIn following."* — **[CHRIS]**
> *"**You may be making your own argument for me.**"*

And he narrates the method out loud: *"**all I'm doing — for people who are listening — is I'm just
asking them a series of questions.**"* Earlier: *"I think the people already see **the trap being laid
out.**"*

★★ He also notices what Essam's answers reveal about **Essam**, not just the argument: *"you are
already now demonstrating **a second set of unique skills, because most creative people would never do
that.** It's too anxiety-ridden."*

## ★★★ Reputation, social proof, and the friction ladder

- **Social proof, defined without the platform:** *"when I say social, **I don't mean social media. I
  mean how other people see evidence of competence or expertise**"* — founder, agency, headcount, years
  in business, a body of work — *"**these are markers, shorthand, heuristics for something else.**"*
  ✅ Pairs with the heuristics argument on [[2025-09-11-yt-R2J67k2Z6WU|2025-09-11]].
- **The example that carries it:** a then-unknown Steven Bartlett, whom Essam saw speak — *"**his
  reputation preceded him.** And this is the business that we're in."*
- ★★★★ *"**Trust is hard to build and easy to lose. It takes a long time for you to earn trust. It only
  takes one f-up for you to lose it all.**"*
- **The ladder:** *"people hire who they **know, like and trust**… **before anybody can hire you, they
  must know that you exist.** You can do it in person, which is **high friction, high effort.** You can
  do it online, which is **low friction, lower effort — but still effort.**"*
- ★★★★ **And the distinction inside "consistency":** *"both require a level of intentionality, effort
  and **consistency in how you show up. Not consistency in the frequency of posting, but how you show
  up.**"* — because the failure is recognisability: *"**I can't recognise you** — not physically, but
  because **you're showing a personality trait I've never seen in you before.** Trust is hard to build.
  **You've lost my trust.**"*
- **Austin Kleon**, *Show Your Work*, credited: *"**to be found, you must be findable.**"* → *"**people
  are making it very hard to find them.** You could be really talented, very earnest, full of ethics,
  charging a very reasonable rate — but *how come my inbox is empty?* **It's because we have to master
  the game of being known.**"*

## ★★★★ The non-violent-language thread — sixth attestation, and the sharpest

Essam, on creators with large followings: *"they have very little influence… because **they're selling
their soul to entertain people.**"*

> **[CHRIS]** *"**You have a lot of judging language in this.**"*
> **[MATT]** *"I'm just being honest."*
> ★★★★ **[CHRIS]** *"**Well, I always worry about when people say they're being honest. That's almost
> like a blanket statement to excuse being very violent with their choice of words.**"*
> **[MATT]** *"Okay. I'm being violent with my choice of words."*
> **[CHRIS]** *"**I think you are.** … '**Selling your soul**' — I don't think anybody's selling their
> soul."*

✅ The sixth attestation of the thread, and the first delivered **to a colleague, on camera, with the
concession secured on the recording.**

★★★★ **And he immediately names his own rhetorical move as a mirror** — which is rare: *"when I said
*you think social media is stupid*, **I think I was being reductive — but I was trying to mirror how
reductive your thing is.**"*

## ★★★ Followers ≠ influence — with evidence

> *"**The test of a relationship, and the stickiness of a relationship, is when you ask somebody to do
> something, if they'll do it.**"*

**The VidCon example:** post-COVID, VidCon invited some of the biggest TikTok creators and prepared
large rooms — *"they saw a tiny percent of people. **There were more empty chairs than there were
people in the room, by like a ten-to-one ratio.** … **I think it dawned on everybody that number of
followers doesn't equal level of influence. It's not always a causal thing.**"*

He also declines the easy dismissal of fame-seekers: young people *"trying to figure out who they are
in this world… **and I think they can do whatever they want**"* — and notes TikTok's demographics
(*"they don't have a lot of discretionary income"*) as a structural, not moral, explanation.

## ★★★★ "I've given up on that" — the introversion strategy, restated to someone who finds it worse

**[MATT]** *"When people were coming up to you at the 1 Billion Followers Summit, part of me was like:
**I'm glad that's not me**, because… **that makes me more uncomfortable than just going into a room and
nobody knowing who I am.**"*

> **[CHRIS]** *"**This is wonderful. This is not a one-strategy-fits-all**, because we're very
> different people… **My whole thing is: I'm very socially awkward. I do not know how to work a room.
> Period. And instead of learning how, I've given up on that** — and I've just done something
> different, which is **to become so well known that people will just walk up to me, so I don't have
> to develop social skills.** This works for me, and it's working really well for me now."*

> ★★★★ *"**So we all are, in a way, inventing solutions to our own problems.** So if you don't like
> social media, then you work the in-person networking events where you literally knock on doors — **and
> that works too.**"*

⚠️ Note the escalation: earlier tellings say the strategy was *"to create content so good that I didn't
need to develop social skills"*; here he says plainly **"instead of learning how, I've given up on
that."** With the permission he attaches: *"**you don't have to do the whole thing from A to Z. You
can just pick parts that work for you.**"*

## ★★★★ Blind's pre-social marketing stack — the fullest account in the corpus

*"All the things you did, I never did."* Over roughly the company's whole life:

| Channel | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Reps** | Signed **exclusively**, *"very particular who they worked with and represented"* — *"**you're as good as the company you keep**"* — representing a handful of non-competing agencies, knocking on ad-agency doors with the body of work. *"**We existed with reps for like 25-ish years, almost the entire length of our company.**"* |
| **PR firms** | *"literally PR firms who would get us press and send us copies of everything they got for us."* |
| **An executive producer** | Outbound sales plus in-person networking — *"attend parties, go to functions, opening screenings, **do the wine-and-dine stuff. So I outsourced all that, because I didn't want to do it.**"* |
| **Advertorials** | Inserted into magazines, *"several thousand dollars"* a placement. |
| **Directories** | Ads in **Alt Pick** and **the Blackbook** — *"where advertising agency folks would look through to find who's who."* |
| **Competitions** | *"Won a lot of awards, were published in design annuals."* |
| **Trade press** | Answering questions from **Shoot**, **Ad Age**, **Creativity** — *"the periodicals that advertising agencies, aka our clients, would read."* |

> ★★★★ **And the grievance that explains why he prefers what replaced it:** *"that was a lot of work,
> as you can imagine. **It was quite expensive, and there was a layer of opaqueness that I could never
> tell — because we did this, we got that.** So the general consensus from my executive producer was:
> **spend as much as you can, do as much as you can, always** — because all those things were
> opportunities that would somehow manifest somehow. **And as an agency owner, the person signing the
> cheque, that was wearing thin on me — because I think, since it's not your money, you want me to
> spend as much as possible. But you can't actually trace any of this stuff, because it was
> untraceable. And this is how it existed for decades.**"*

**The conclusion he draws:** *"whether you're in Matt's shoes as a solopreneur or freelancer, or
running an agency — **getting work, getting known, requires a lot of work. There is no way around
this. There really isn't.**"*

★★★ **With one exception, described with visible irony:** *"maybe there's one cheat code. **If you're
extremely good-looking, extremely charismatic, from a great family, living in the epicentre of whatever
it is that you do, and your work is bar none** — you may not have to do anything, because people will
just seek you out… **And those are unicorns. They do exist, and they're like: well, this marketing
stuff is nonsense. And I would agree with them, because they don't need it. But if you're listening to
this and you're curious, there's a high probability that you need it.**"*

## ★★★★ Why he said yes to a nobody — and what changed

**[MATT]** names a second cheat code — *"borrowing the attention and the trust of somebody that has
already built it"* — and asks directly: *"I was basically a nobody. **What made you give me the
opportunity?**"*

> **[CHRIS]** *"This is going to sound really weird… but **I don't look at whatever accomplishments I
> have, or social following, as a way of defining myself. I just look at myself as an entrepreneur
> who's trying to make it in this world. And you're an entrepreneur trying to make it in this world —
> we're at different levels on our journey. So I am no more important than you, and you're no less
> important than me. That's just my general perspective.**"*

> *"So **this answer is dependent on time and place. I'm at a time and place where I have time to talk
> to you.** Today it's become a lot harder — **not because I have a higher self-image**, but because
> there are lots and lots of people now asking for that same amount of time."*

### ★★★★ The podcast criteria, the offence taken, and his boundaries

Someone invited him on their show; he asked **"how many downloads do you get per episode?"** — and the
invitation was rescinded in offence. *"**Never mind. Offer rescinded.** If that's all it's about for
you, I'm not interested in having you."*

**His stated criteria:**

1. *"**I don't want to be episode one.** You barely even know what you're doing. You might not even
   turn on the mic properly — and oftentimes that's the case."*
2. *"Okay, **you've done the reps — but have you grown at all?** Because just because you keep doing
   pods doesn't mean anything to me."*
3. ★★★★ *"**What you bring to the equation is an audience. So I give you my time, which I define to be
   very valuable. If I'm to give you my time — which I have a non-renewable supply of — then you must
   bring an audience. Because otherwise, why am I on here? Because the likelihood of you asking me
   something so profound that I see into soul crevices I've not seen before is quite low.**"*

**His read of the offence:** *"if I'm to be a little judgy here, **that person is looking at the world
through their eyes only — to have really low empathy for other humans**, to say: because I ask you,
you therefore have to say yes."*

> ★★★★ **And the self-awareness, which is the best part:** *"**I'm pretty sure that person's walking
> away thinking: what a jerk. What a prick. This guy is full of himself** — because I asked the
> question. Okay. **I don't want anybody to be angry — but I can't control someone's reaction, and I
> also have to respect my own boundaries.**"*

Closing on: *"**everybody's the most important person in their universe, and I don't think that's a
horrible thing — but let's not use that to treat other people that way.**"*
