# Personal Branding Is Not What You Think (2025-09-16)

> **Attribution:** Chris is **the interviewee** in front of a live room; an unnamed host asks the
> questions and a member of the audience asks the first Q&A question. Host and audience lines are
> marked and are not persona data. **Everything substantive here is Chris.**

## Summary

★★★ **LANDMARK.** Chris interviewed at length about his own method, which produces the most careful
version of several positions the corpus holds in blunter form — plus three things it did not have:
**the three ingredients of magnetism** (a framework he says he has tested on a couple of hundred
people), **the 9-1-1 content formula**, and an unusual pair of self-assessed weaknesses (*"I'm
terrible at cash flow"*; *"I'm not so good at answering that question"*). It also contains **the
fullest telling of the Aaron Draplin moment**, and the sharpest statement of the authenticity gap —
including the concession that closing it entirely is impossible.

## ★★★★ The Draplin moment — fullest telling

The trigger was a small humiliation. In a theatre watching other designers speak, someone asked him
*"you don't know who Aaron Draplin is?"* — *"and she gave me this **'you're so pretentious'** [look],
like you don't know other celebrity designers."*

> *"I went home that night and checked him out, and I was watching this TED talk he gave in Portland,
> and **his delivery was beautiful.** He's a really large man, trucker cap, jeans, and he just gave
> this presentation. I said: **man, this guy is so charismatic.** And **the way he built his brand
> isn't that he's the world's best designer. There are many people who are better designers. He's not
> bad — but he got there because of the way he was so transparent and authentic about how he told his
> story.**"*

The opening he quotes: *"'**My name is Aaron James Draplin. I'm 39 years old, and I'm doing everything
I can to make it in the little leagues.**' And I was like — **who does that? Who tells you how old
they are, and who tells you your middle name?** And he's like, 'you know, it's an important day, so
**I washed the 501 jeans for this event.**' And **it's so endearing.**"* Then: *"I was just really
studying him **in the classic Asian way** — let me study success first and figure out the blueprint."*

> ★★★★ **And the question that turned him:** *"in trying to share this with other people, I started to
> think — **why have I been hiding all this time? Why aren't I as transparent to the outside world
> that I am with my friends, with my staff, with my team? Why don't I do that?** And so I started to
> go on this journey **for about two-ish years**, and then I started to figure this thing out, and
> here we are."*

He prefaces the whole answer with: *"**Full confession here. I'm a graphic designer. I'm an introvert.
Being in front of people like you would scare the Jesus out of me. I've done a lot of personal work to
be here.** So the introverts in this room [thinking] *dude, I can't do this* — **yes, you can. If you
want to.**"*

## ★★★★ The authenticity gap — the most honest version

He grounds it in **Jung**, with a characteristic hedge: *"for the doctors in the room, forgive me,
**this is the graphic designer version of this.**"* — shadow self, persona, and the ego regulating
between them.

**And he defends social norms before critiquing them**, which the shorter tellings drop: *"this happens
from a really early age — 'that person's ugly' and your parents say **don't stare, don't talk.** …
we don't put our feet on the table… **you start to learn all the rules and we're socialised to become
this other person, to be accepted. Now I think to be a society we have to have social norms that we
abide by to show respect for each other. But then somewhere along the way we kind of lose a sense of
who we really are.**"*

> ★★★★ **The definition, and the concession:** *"I describe authenticity as **the gap that exists
> between these two points**… **today I didn't just show up in my sweatpants and my t-shirt. That's my
> authentic self in my hotel room.** I took a shower, I brushed my teeth. **So I'm showing up a little
> bit — but I'm trying my best to close these gaps so that the gap between the two is the smallest
> possible. I don't think it's possible to be 1,000% authentically you.** How do you know this?
> Because **when you're home eating Cheetos and your face is messy, that's the real you. But we're not
> all going to show up that way.**"*

**And the risk, admitted rather than waved away:** *"**that's the risk. And I can't tell you it's not
going to go away.** The more you you are, the more you're going to draw a line in the sand… **I'm not
for everybody, and it's okay.** … **If you're still up and coming, if your business is volatile, it's
not stable enough, and you're afraid of doing this — it might mess it up a little bit.**"*

⚠️ **A dated update on Tesla** (cf. [[2025-08-12-yt-XdNQC_zDCW0|2025-08-12]], where he says he owns
one): *"I know this is not going to be popular, but **I used to love Tesla as a company. I don't love
it as much as I used to, because Elon's been saying a lot of stuff.** So that's the risk — **but he
also brings a lot of new people to that conversation**… **the more you say, the more you're going to
draw people to you, and you're going to repel others. And that's the whole point.**"*

## ★★★★ "Go to therapy" — the first step

> *"A gentleman, his name is **Omar**, asked me: '**Chris, I'm really interested in personal branding.
> What's the first thing I should do?**' I said: '**Go to therapy. Learn about who you are.**' Because
> **my viewpoint on personal branding is about understanding who you are, accepting who you are, and
> expressing your truest self. And we can't do that in any genuine way if we don't know who we
> are.**"* — with the aside: *"I didn't realise what I said would hit some people."*

**The case that convinced him**, about a friend: *"they have a lot of inner conflict. They don't know
who they are. So **every time they post, I could read between the lines: you're really conflicted
right now.** And she's like, '**why don't people like this?**' I'm like, '**because you're saying one
thing and you're meaning something else.**' **She's constantly putting the inner struggle out for
people to read.** And **it would be wonderful if she could just come to peace with that first, and
then express that.**"*

> With the limit he sets on his own advice: *"**it's not for everybody. I'm not saying everybody go out
> and do it today. You kind of have to do the inner work.**"*

★★★ **On his own groundedness**, when the host names it: *"**I've done some therapy. I've got a lot of
coaching. My wife helps out with some of the shots to the face.** So I'm like — okay, let me figure
that out."*

## ★★★★ The three ingredients of magnetism

A framework he presents as **empirically tested**: *"**I've done this with a couple hundred people.
It's almost always the same thing.**"* The prompt: think of someone you admire, living or dead, with
something magnetic about them.

1. ★★★★ **Self-acceptance — not self-awareness.** *"**I used to say it was self-awareness, but I don't
   think it's self-awareness. It's self-acceptance.** It's one thing to be aware, but it's another,
   more powerful thing **to actually accept who you are.** … They're comfortable in their own skin.
   They're not there trying to prove anything. **They love themselves so much that they leave room for
   you to be you** — which is an incredible thing. A guru, a teacher, a lover, a partner, a spouse; it
   doesn't matter."*
2. ★★★★ **Self-confidence, defined.** *"**They believe they have the tools and resources to solve any
   problem. That is the definition of self-confidence.** It's not the braggadocio — *I bought this
   house, I drive that car, I have these watches.* **People often mistake arrogance with
   self-confidence. They're totally different animals. Don't get me started.**"* ✅ Consistent with
   [[2025-08-21-yt-0eBYcYbsas0|2025-08-21]].
3. ★★★★ **True vulnerability.** *"**In order for you to be really strong, you have to be able to be
   comfortable with what makes you really weak.** To really put it out there: **this is what's ugly
   about me, or what I've done. And I've come to accept that part. And if you don't love that part —
   full disclosure, that is just who I am. And I'm not going to apologise for it.**"*

## ★★★★ The 9-1-1 formula

Named, and set up as an emergency: *"there's a simple formula. **It's 911. You're going to call 911.
It's an emergency.**"* Given to a room of real-estate and mortgage people whose content *"nobody looks
at"* — *"**it sucks, doesn't it?** When you put out something into the world and you think it's
valuable, you put your heart and soul into it, **and it just gets nothing. That sucks.** … Your content
is on life support."*

| Part | What it is | The rule |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | **Pure value, no selling at all** | *"**Don't mix up the waters.** Don't do 90% content and value and then 10% buy my stuff — **because as soon as you do that, we say: oh, your whole intent of giving me value was to serve this other thing. So you're robbing yourself of the goodwill you've just created.**" |
| **1** | **Sell, unapologetically** | *"**When you sell, sell. Don't try to be shy about it.** 'I've got a course. I've got a workshop. I have a new book. Be there. Buy a ticket.' **And you can do this unabashedly, without reservation, because you've already given the value.**" |
| **1** | **You being you** | *"**Where are you in all of this stuff?**" |

★★★★ **And the third slot exists because of a mistake he made:** *"it's a mistake that I made — because
**I identify as a teacher. I want to teach people. And I was like: nobody wants to hear from me. Who
cares about me?** And then **I heard from a friend of a friend who said: 'yeah, his stuff is cool, but
I don't know who this guy is. I don't trust him.'** So there's **trustability**, and — I think more
important — **relatability.**"*

**The tolerance and the hard limit:** *"it doesn't need to be exactly that. **It could be 7-1-1.** But
**don't flip the equation. Don't do nine pieces of selling and one of value. Don't do nine pieces of
personal content. The ratio should be value-led.**"* And the payoff: *"when you say 'buy my stuff',
**people will be thrilled. They're like: gosh, I've been wondering how to give you money.** … And
**don't make them work so hard to crawl through the internet and find it. Just tell them.**"*

> ✅ **Complementary to, not in conflict with, the sequence rule** on
> [[2025-09-20-yt-hEOFX0pYha8|2025-09-20]] (*load the ask up front*). Both solve the same problem —
> the ask that retroactively contaminates the value — by different means: **separate the posts**, or
> **front-load the ask.**

## ★★★★ Performance marketing vs. brand building — with a weakness admitted

He draws it as two columns, and apologises in advance: *"**I'm sorry if you are a coach and you teach
this. I apologise in advance. I'm going to offend you right now.**"*

| **Performance marketing** | **Brand building** |
|---|---|
| conversions, **short-term cash flow** | *"**no short-term value, but long-term potential value**"* |
| **what you do** | **who you are** |
| **outward** exploration | **inward** — *"who am I? What makes me me? What are my values and beliefs?"* |
| **manufacturing** | **discovery** |
| *"this will make you probably a lot of money"* | *"**this will help you make a difference**"* |

> ★★★★ **The admission:** *"**And I'm not good at that. I just came from a mastermind on Friday where
> everybody there's really good at that. I'm terrible at that. Cash flow.**"*

**The mechanism:** *"everybody who's trying to sell you something — you might buy from them, you might
even feel good about the product. But **I don't think you think in your mind *I owe anything*, because
the transaction has been completed.** … The thing about brand building is **you're giving value without
expectation of outcome.** And **you can't afford to do this unless you can play this game for a really
long time.**"*

### ★★★★ And the warning he issues about his own business

> *"In the age of AI — and AI-created content — **information has become really cheap. The value of
> information is really low. And if you're in the information business, which I am, I'm telling you
> from the front lines: people who do what we do, we've got a limited number of days left, because
> information is really cheap.**"*

> *"**We can all teach from the same source.** So why does someone choose [one teacher] or anybody else
> in this room? **This is the most important part. It's because you have a point of view. We're not
> buying the information so much as we're looking for you to be the trusted person to lead us to
> whatever it is we want.** And it's hard to get to this point **unless you're able to tell your story
> and point out why you're different.**"*

## ★★★★ On responsibility of speech — and being attacked

Asked how he balances free speech against **responsibility** of speech, he begins by conceding:

> *"**That's a really great question. I'm not so good at answering that question**, because I think
> **in us trying to be so responsible and thoughtful and intentional, we start to edit ourselves a
> little bit too much.** I would rather have all of you practise **a little bit more radical candour**
> than to be so careful — because **we've skewed so far on one side.** When we're reading things on
> LinkedIn, on Twitter — **what is your point of view? I don't even know where you are in this piece
> at all.**"*

**The incident he tells against himself:** *"sometimes I put out something on Twitter or X… I wrote it
and I walked away, and then I turn around a couple of hours later [and there] was all this negative
feedback. **Not in a million years, through my lens, did I ever read that thing the way some people
are reading it.** So I have to say — **friend, I didn't see it like that. Let's have a conversation
about it.** And **they're not really that happy to have a conversation. They just want to let you know
how they feel. And I think that's okay.**"*

> *"**I was really kind of hurt, because I thought this person was my friend**… **and she just went on
> the attack. I thought you knew me better than that.** So in my mind **I had to put her in a different
> category** — we're not as close as I thought. **You don't have my back the way I thought.**"*

★★★★ **The argument he makes from it** — against a saying he names as the enemy: *"**it's a dumb thing
that creatives say all the time, and it really bothers me: 'you're only as good as your last piece of
work.' No, you're not — because you're saying I must prove myself from now until I'm in the grave.
That doesn't make sense to me.** If you've shown up with genuine values, taking care of people, being
honest and sincere — maybe that one thing, in context, we understand. **But we kind of live in this
two-minute cycle of news.**"*

> ★★★★ **And then he gives the claim up, which is the interesting part:** *"**You don't have to take me
> at all.** You can say whatever you want, because **whatever you think and feel, it's not really my
> business.** I just want to see how you look at the world, and that's okay. So if you're into two
> minutes of history, I'm okay with that. **You don't have to look at the 3,000 other things I've
> done.** … **I'll try to improve — but I don't want to do that at the cost of me losing myself.**"*

## ★★★ Branding, simplified — and Thales

> *"**Branding doesn't need to be super complicated. Branding simply is meaningful differentiation.
> And if you want to make that simpler: how are you different? That's it.**"* → *"on the surface this
> would be really hard to solve… **and it's a lot easier than you think, because you just need to be
> you** — your story, your ethnicity, the city you live in, your experiences, your challenges, your
> struggle. **These are all the things that make you you.** … **So it's you to the power of you.**"*

> ★★★★ *"The great philosopher **Thales** was asked by students: **what's the most difficult thing in
> the world?** And he says: **to know thyself.** And I think **from now until you're dead, we're still
> searching for who we are.** The joke is, they asked him: **what is the easiest thing to do?** He
> said: **give advice.**"*

## ★★★★ The AI answer

> *"**I don't think AI is making thought leadership go away. I think it's making generic thought
> leadership go away.** There's a big differentiation there. … There are people here who know how to
> teach you marketing, or how to buy properties, or to invest — **but I could choose from a lot of
> different people. I'd rather choose somebody that I connect with on a human-to-human level.** And so
> **now more than ever**, surfacing those stories and showing up authentically as yourself, saying
> *yeah, I'm not for everybody* — **I think you're going to get more clients than you can handle
> without even selling anything.**"*

Plus the line from the cold open: *"**I'm an evolving human being. I've got other passions and
interests. Should I be able to talk about them? Hell yes. You just talk about them.**"*
