# Why Creatives Struggle To Create Content That Performs (2025-09-20)

> **Attribution:** solo, scripted, to camera. All Chris. Framed as *"here's what I told my content team
> recently."*

## Summary

★★★ A short, unusually personal piece on **why he makes content at all** — and an argument that the
metrics everyone optimises for are the wrong instrument. It contains **the sequence lesson** (a small,
genuinely useful device), **the curse of knowledge** as a content principle, **permission to post
without a takeaway**, and a passage that traces content-insecurity back to a withheld childhood
approval and **recommends therapy by name**. ⚠️ It also contains **a direct contradiction with
[[2025-08-23-yt-cDTB4auDj2U|2025-08-23]]**, flagged below rather than smoothed.

## Key claims (2025-09-20)

### ★★★★ Content is not a scorecard

> *"**If you're using social media as a scorecard, you're playing the wrong game.**"*

> *"Social media makes it easy to **tie your worth to numbers** — impressions, comments, growth. But
> **when you start measuring every post by performance, you stop creating with intention.** … **For
> me, creating on social media is just to learn about who I am and what my thoughts and ideas are.**
> It's not about any other performance metric. … **We want to figure out what our ideas are.**"*

★★★★ **How he reads negative feedback** — a reframe that removes the sting without denying the signal:

> *"It's not about *oh my gosh, you don't like me, you don't like these ideas.* **The way I interpret
> that is: I'm not being clear, or I should have been more careful with the words and the way I frame
> the conversation — because my intention is not to make you upset. I'm trying to help. And clearly,
> in this instance, for that person, it wasn't helpful.**"* → *"**the whole point of communication is
> to make sure transmission and receiver are in alignment.**"*

★★★★ **Emotional regulation as sovereignty:** *"you're at the whims of the highs and lows of whatever
the algorithm serves you. And **we don't want to have our emotional regulation be exported, or be in
control by someone other than us. It makes no sense.**"* → *"**if you wake up one morning and look at
the performance of your post and you feel a high high — or conversely, this bottomless amount of
depression and anxiety — it's a sign that so much of your self-value and self-worth is placed in the
hands of strangers.**"*

**What guides the work instead:** *"**you create because it feels like the most honest thing to do.
This is where clarity lives.**"* — via **David C. Baker**, credited: *"**you gain clarity through
articulation.**"* → *"I create **to understand my thinking, see what I actually believe, process what
I'm experiencing, and to stay in conversation with myself while bringing others along for the
journey.**"*

### ★★★★ The sequence lesson — load the ask up front

The problem first, and he implicates himself: *"let's just say you're trying to launch a course… but
**instead of just coming out and asking for it and being super transparent about it, you don't feel
good about that. So you try and write a post about an idea that just conveniently ends with the ask**…
**and I'm guilty of this too, so let's just be real.**"*

> *"What happens is, when people read the first part, they'll get this feeling like — *yeah, I want to
> support you*, or *you see me, I feel so understood right now.* And then you ask for something, and
> [they think] — **oh, I see why you said all those things. It's 'cause you want me to do something.
> And then it starts to have that scent of being disingenuous.**"*

**The demonstration, which is what makes it stick:**

> *"If you say — *you're really attractive, you're really smart… by the way, **can you drive me to the
> airport?*** **See what happens there? You negate everything that you said before, because the ask is
> really clear.**"*

> ★★★★ **The fix:** *"**Load the ask up front, and then give the compliment or tell the story.** So:
> *I hate to do this, but can you drive me to the airport? And by the way, I know I haven't said this
> enough, but you're really attractive and really intelligent.* **This way they already know what the
> ask is, and then you can hit them with the compliment.** … **The order does matter. Sequence does
> matter.**"*

> ✅ Solves the same problem as **the 9-1-1 formula** on [[2025-09-16-yt-eS9LtexTMFk|2025-09-16]] by a
> different route: *separate the posts* there, *front-load the ask* here. **Complementary, not
> contradictory** — record both.

### ★★★★ You don't need a call to action every time

> *"**Not everything you publish has to teach, convert, or call someone to action. You can post
> without a takeaway.** Yeah, I know. **You can write without packaging the insight.** … Sometimes I
> create to be transparent. Sometimes to share something funny. **Sometimes because I need to get it
> out of my system.** **You don't need to optimise everything for utility. We don't expect every
> conversation with a friend to end with advice — so why do we treat content that way?**"*

> ★★★★ **And the cost of always teaching:** *"sometimes **in your zeal to create content that always
> teaches, you create an air of craftsmanship, of control, that keeps you and your audience from
> really connecting. People just sometimes want to see you unfiltered and unplugged. And those
> in-between takes are what allow them to see that side of you.**"*

**What it is all for:** *"**you create to connect with people through your content. Content is about
connection — and sometimes polish gets in the way of that.**"*

### ★★★★ The curse of knowledge

> *"There's a form of cognitive bias called **the curse of knowledge** — **we forget that there was a
> time in which we struggled with something.** If you're 5, 10, 25 years into doing something, **you
> have to work really hard remembering how difficult it was for you when it was year one.**"*

> *"When you share something that you've learned, **you're basically giving someone the express pass to
> move past the trials and tribulations of what you had to go through.** … **You're not saying 'I'm
> going to eliminate the pain', but 'I'm going to reduce the time in which you feel that pain.'**"*

> ★★★★ **The guiding principle:** *"**when I start to write and create content, I think about what it's
> like to be a beginner again.**"*

Plus the message-in-a-bottle belief, restated compactly: *"**you never know who needed to hear it. And
most of the time, neither do they until they experience it.**"*

### ★★★★ Don't create from insecurity — and the therapy recommendation

> *"**If you're creating because you need to feel seen, liked or affirmed by strangers, you're setting
> yourself up for a painful cycle.** … **No amount of likes will fix insecurity. The internet can't
> validate what you haven't accepted in yourself. If you're chasing attention to fill a gap, you'll
> keep chasing even when you succeed.** … **Don't let social media become a mirror for self-worth. It
> will lie to you.**"*

**The diagnostic question, and where he says it leads:**

> *"Ask yourself: **what is it about myself that I don't like, or need affirmed by strangers?** And
> usually what happens is we start to recognise a pattern — **the internet, social media, the
> following, the algorithm is a surrogate for someone else in your life. A parent, usually; an older
> sibling; a mentor; a teacher — who at one point withheld love, respect or affirmation from you that
> you can't get. But we don't have the power when we're young. And so now we're adults trapped with
> this unresolved childhood trauma — and now we're seeking strangers to fill that void.**"*

> *"**Whose approval do I need to get? Who needs to give me permission, so that I can show up for
> myself and not have to rely on the whims of strangers and the algorithm for my own self-worth?** …
> And if possible, **what I would do is to start working with a therapist. Have these conversations in
> open, honest and transparent ways, and really be vulnerable here. Tell your truth so that you can
> work through this problem.**"*

✅ Consistent with **"go to therapy"** as step one on [[2025-09-16-yt-eS9LtexTMFk|2025-09-16]], four
days earlier — and with his own documented therapy.

### The close

> *"**Ignore the rules. Focus on the feelings.** … **People, believe it or not, can feel when you're
> being real.** There's a tone of voice you share. There's a sense that you care about them, and that
> you're being genuinely vulnerable and not performing for an outcome. **Believe it or not, people are
> way smarter than you think — your body, your words and your tonality betray the true intention
> behind it all. So get right with yourself, and the audience will get right with you.**"*

## ⚠️ CONTRADICTION — flagged, not resolved

> *"I've been creating content online for over a decade… and in all that time, **I've never cared about
> the algorithm, never studied best posting times, never followed formulas.**"* — repeated at the
> close: *"**I've never optimised for the time of day, hashtags, trending sounds, or algorithms. And
> yet I built a loyal audience, strong inbound leads, and lasting brand equity.**"*

**This conflicts with [[2025-08-23-yt-cDTB4auDj2U|2025-08-23]], four weeks earlier**, where he tells
his mentee to **be a student of the game** — *"I'm pretty sure you're not reading the reports from
YouTube… not reverse-engineering other people's content"* — and discusses testing thumbnails *"300
times"*, titles, CTAs and video length. It also sits against the editorial rules he gives his team on
[[2025-06-15-yt-EeEsglitUe8|2025-06-15]] and his interest in a product manager's *"first looks"* at
LinkedIn features.

> **Both are his, four weeks apart.** The most likely reading is **a division of labour** — he does not
> personally optimise, and expects his team and mentees to — but **he does not say that here**, and it
> is not supplied for him. **Record the tension; do not present either claim as his settled practice
> without the other.**
