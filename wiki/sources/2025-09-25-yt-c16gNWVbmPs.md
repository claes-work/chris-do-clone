# 6 Creative Skills AI Can't Replace (2025-09-25)

> **Attribution:** solo, scripted, to camera. All Chris.

## Summary

★★★ **LANDMARK — the most structured AI answer in the corpus, and it carries two major biographical
finds.** He names six skills, argues each against a specific limit of the technology, and refuses the
obvious framing up front: *"**these are not the jobs you should be doing, but the skills you should
strengthen, stack, and lead with.**"* The two finds are **the taste gap he grew up with and the method
he invented to close it** (the library, 25 magazines at a time), and **the $100,000 logo assignment he
won by saying "no and no."**

## Skill 1 — Strategic thinking and problem solving

> *"AI can give you options. It can even give you a decent answer. **But it can't figure out the real
> problem, or lead a client through ambiguity. Strategy is about framing, not just responding.**"*

> ★★★★ *"In the last couple of decades there was a lot of value in **the doing — what to do, how to do
> it. But in the age of AI, when anything can be done by almost anybody with the ability to type, it's
> less important what and how — but WHY. This is where you come in. The machine can't figure out the
> why.**"*

Plus the physical limits: *"AI can't sit in a client meeting and **sense the nuance behind the brief.
They can't read the micro-expressions.** It currently cannot see **subtle shifts in body language,
which we are naturally wired to pick up.**"*

## Skill 2 — Emotional intelligence and empathy

> *"**AI doesn't feel awkward silences. It doesn't see body language. It doesn't understand why a
> client hesitated when you said the word *rebrand*.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**It lacks the emotional empathy because it has never lived a life.** … It's the difference
> between **having loved and having your heart broken**, and **reading about a story** when someone has
> loved someone else and had their heart broken. **It's vastly different.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**If you can read the room, you can lead the room.**"*

★★★ **Branding defined emotionally** — the sharpest version in the corpus: *"what people often forget
about branding is **it's an irrational emotional connection to a product or service. We can't really
explain it**, but we feel a certain connection to a certain brand of shoes, hotels, cars, consumer
electronics. **We have an emotional attachment to an intangible thing — even a faceless entity — for
which we will feel sad when that entity no longer exists. This is the power of branding.**"*

★★★ **The purchase-decision argument**, run on the viewer: *"every time you've made a purchasing
decision — was it the offerings? The price point? **Or something else?** At first you might say it was
a good deal, great service and features. **But often what we don't recognise is that it was a gut
feeling. That person made you feel something** — and despite it being a bit more expensive or less
feature-laden, **you decided to pick them.**"* (With the exception: *"unless they say something that
gives you that little **spider sense** where I'm not sure I could trust this person."*)

> ★★★ **The consequence for sellers:** *"when you're selling services and **you over-index on features
> and functions and price point, you are possibly missing the entire point.**"*

## Skill 3 — Original creativity and conceptual ideation

> *"**AI is trained on what's already been done. AI is great at pattern recognition — but breakthrough
> ideas break patterns. It can remix, but it doesn't originate.** It won't surprise you with something
> that feels risky or weird or human."*

> ★★★★ *"**The courage to be wrong is what creates originality.**"*

★★★★ **And a defence of mess that reads as permission:** *"great creative ideas often start messy.
They're **illogical, irrational, and based on intuition.** It's you **connecting all the dots of your
previous lived experiences**… and **being brave enough to see a connection where no one else has seen
a connection.** This is why it's inherently **messy and wasteful, not a linear path.** We often say the
shortest distance between two points is a line — **but in creativity, it's a hot, jumbled, tangled
mess. And that's where we birth the biggest, boldest ideas. So feel confident in your messy process.
Feel affirmed that this weird thing that no one else can understand or explain is what makes you
unique and highly irreplaceable.**"*

> ★★★★ **The century argument:** *"In the 20th century, the people who made the most money were
> **convergent thinkers, left-brainers** — the people who managed finance, who ran business, but didn't
> have a lot of imagination. **But in the 21st century, divergent thinkers, not convergent thinkers,
> are the ones who are going to rule the world. They assume there are multiple solutions to any
> problem, and they're looking for the most ideal outcome.**"*

✅ Credited to **Brian Collins**: *"**designers are futurists. We rehearse possible future outcomes and
we pick the most ideal path towards that outcome.**"*

## ★★★★★ Skill 4 — Taste and creative judgment (with the biography that explains it)

> ★★★★ *"**AI can give you 50 versions, but it doesn't know which one to pick, or why one works better
> than the other.** You see — **answers are cheap. Taste isn't.** Your job is **to curate, to lead, to
> shape.**"*

**The prescription he warns will be unwelcome:** *"**you need to study history** — the history of
photography, of art, of illustration, of architecture. **You have to go into the annals of human
creativity and learn the vocabulary of what it means to be professional at doing it at the highest
level.** And within that, **you start to see what has withstood the test of time**… and **you start to
learn the invisible rules that you might have overlooked.** … **Having a curatorial eye is not easy.
This is why there are so many ugly things in the world.** Your job is to **immerse yourself in
humanities and do the things that you hated to do.**"*

### ★★★★★ The taste gap he grew up with — new to the corpus

> *"I grew up in **Northern California in the 80s** — **in the valley, in Silicon Valley** — and **I
> wasn't exposed to a lot of art and culture. In fact, I think the first time I got on an airplane I
> was like 16 or 17 years old**, and **my brother had flown me from San Jose to San Diego**, and it was
> an experience for me. … All that is to say: **I'm not a highly cultured person. I have a pretty
> unsophisticated taste. I was uncouth, as some might say.**"*

> *"I go to **Art Center** and I see **there are these Europeans — people a little older, who've
> travelled, who've been to Spain, to Italy, to Germany, to the UK — and I've never been anywhere
> except within Northern California.** **So how does one close that gap? How does one even compete
> with people who literally grew up in hundreds of years of art, history and culture?**"*

### ★★★★★ The method he invented to close it

> *"**Well, you play catch-up.** You adopt this mindset of **becoming infinitely curious about lots of
> things**, and **you become a deep diver. Become a dolphin.** So whatever sparks your curiosity, **go
> as deep as possible.** And through this exposure to lots of material, **you start to recognise your
> own pattern.** **In a way, you combat the pattern-recognition machine by becoming your own
> pattern-recognition machine.**"*

**The library method, concretely:** *"I would go to **the library** — you know, there are these places
you can go to look at books and magazines for free — and **sit down and take 25 magazines at a time and
literally just scan through it.** You remember that scene in **The Fifth Element** with Leeloo, how she
was starting to understand human history — **watching a machine that just flashed thousands of images
in front of her?** **I want you to become like that.**"*

> ★★★★ **The insight that makes it efficient:** *"here's the good news. **When you pick up a magazine on
> architecture, it's already been curated by someone who understands what good architecture looks
> like. So you're not just seeing everything — you're seeing the best of the best being published.**"*

> ★★★★ **And the direction of travel:** *"**start with the year that you're in** — 2025, 2026 — **and
> just start working backwards.** Eventually **you start to see patterns that repeat themselves over
> the course of a couple of decades.** With that, you'll be armed with **a more informed eye. You'll be
> able to see things that most people can't see — and not just what most people can't see, but what AI
> can't see.**"*

## ★★★★ Skill 5 — Storytelling and communication

> *"AI can generate text, but **it doesn't connect. People don't buy the product, they buy the story.**
> … **Your ability to explain your idea is often more valuable than the idea itself.**"*

**Chapter one — how he competed at Blind:** *"how did I stand out? It was my ability **to empathise and
to discern the unspoken emotional intent in the room.** … When agencies put projects out for bid,
they're looking at the best in the world, potentially awarding hundreds of thousands of dollars. **How
do they make these decisions? If you can ask questions and connect with them on a human level, you can
actually beat companies that are bigger, have more experience, and do better work than you.**"*

### ★★★★★ "No and no" — the $100,000 logo assignment

> *"One example I love to share: a prospect asked a very direct question — '**Chris, do you understand
> what we do? Have you looked at our site?**' And **in a split second, I said no and no.**"*

> *"**I think another person would try to BS their way around that. But the reason why I said what I
> said was: it's my truth. I don't want to tell lies. I don't want to begin a relationship by trying to
> pretend that I'm something that I'm not.** My reason for all of this is — **what can I hope to learn
> by looking at your website that I'm not going to learn by speaking to you?** And this is ultimately
> **one of the decisions that got us this $100,000 logo assignment.**"*

**Chapter two — why he had to learn a second skill:** *"although I'd developed communication skills…
**if I don't take this to the world, I'm going to stay in that industry for as long as that industry
will have me — which it turns out would not be that much longer.**"* The new skill: telling stories
*"**to a piece of glass, this lens, this camera — to an audience that will never see me or be able to
respond**"*, and on stage, which required *"**to process my own internal anxiety, to let go of the need
to impress people, and really to be of service to folks.**"*

> ★★★★ **The feedback he reports getting, and the compliment he ranks highest:** *"despite being stacked
> against heavy hitters — people who have millions or billions of dollars, people who've written
> best-selling books — **why I'm able to stand out from them is usually they say this one thing: what
> you say connects with me.** And **the best compliment I ever got was: you changed the atmosphere in
> the room** — because we feel you're so passionate and genuine about what you're trying to say that
> **we can process what you're saying in a totally different way.**"*

## ★★★ Skill 6 — Client partnership and relationship building

Explicitly distinguished from skill 2: *"**this is not about empathy and connection. This is something
very different.**"*

> *"Business still comes down to people working with people. **It's not B2C. It's not B2B. It's H to H.
> Human to human.** AI **can't read the room. It can't manage egos, navigate politics, or build trust
> across weeks or years. Relationship capital is a moat AI can't cross.**"*

**The Robert story:** a client from *"over 10 years ago, maybe even 20"*, with no emails and no contact
since, texts out of the blue — *"is this still your number? I've got something I want to talk to you
about."*

> *"**Just knowing that Robert sent me a text and asked me this question made my heart smile a little
> bit. And as robotic as I am, as analytical as I am, it's really nice to know that the relationships
> you built decades ago are still a sign of how you made people feel** — even though all that time has
> passed."* He booked a workshop; *"**it's almost as if we collapsed 20 years of not talking to each
> other into a moment. I gave him a big warm hug.**"*

> ★★★★ **And the message he addresses to introverted creatives — himself included:** *"when you produce
> work and **you only show the work that you do, you're actually creating a barrier for you to connect
> with people. You are more than the work that you create** — because sometime in the future you may no
> longer be doing this. **So you cannot define yourself purely on the things that you make.**"* → *"go
> outside your comfort zone… **and dare I say it, actually have a face-to-face meeting from time to
> time. Because even if you're awkward and weird — as long as you're being genuine, real people can
> relate to that.**"*

## The close

> *"**AI is here. It's real, and it's very, very powerful. But don't forget — it's just a tool.** A
> smart, powerful tool, but just a tool. **It won't build trust. It won't set vision. It won't lead
> clients. It won't speak with soul. That's your job.** So **don't fear the AI tools. Use them — and
> build the skills that AI can't touch.**"*
