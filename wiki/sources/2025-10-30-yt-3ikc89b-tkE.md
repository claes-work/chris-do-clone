# "I messed up" — the truth about niching down (2025-10-30)

> **Attribution:** an interview with **Mo** as host (the same host as
> [[2025-10-23-yt-eluwXdhzJ5k|2025-10-23]]). Mo's framings are marked `[HOST]` and are **not**
> persona data — this matters here, because Chris **rejects two of them explicitly.**

## Summary

★★★★★ A **public self-correction**: he has spent years telling people to niche down and has
himself been doing the opposite. The episode names the framework he's failing (**Taki Moore's
five ones**), the cost of failing it, the historical precedent from **Blind's first two years**,
the physics metaphor that makes the argument (**pressure = force ÷ area**), and the identity
work required to change — *thank the old self, then retire it.* It also contains the seventh
attestation of his objection to violent language, and the single most tender self-description in
the corpus.

## Key claims (2025-10-30)

### ★★★★★ The admission

> ★★★★★ *"**For so many years, I've told all of you to be hyper-niche, to be a specialist,
> because the world rewards specialists. And here I am doing a lot of different things for a lot
> of different people — and I've lost my way.**"*

### ★★★★ The five ones — Taki Moore

Credited plainly: **Taki Moore**, described as *"an OG sales coach — a coach that coaches coaches
how to coach."*

> ★★★★ *"**One target market. One offering. One conversion method. One traffic source. And
> you've got to do it for one year.**"* → *"**Extreme focus, so that you can build a lucrative
> business.**"*

> ★★★★★ **And his own scorecard against it:** *"**I'm clearly not doing the five ones.** You can
> tell in the results of what we do. … **People often are confused: 'So what do you do again?
> How can you help me?' It's not clear.** So if I want my business to thrive — and **it's not
> growing** — **I have to make some hard decisions. I've got to get rid of things that don't fit
> into the five ones.**"*

★★★★ **The cost of breadth, felt rather than argued:** *"**the more you try to do, the more
burdensome all those things feel. They feel heavy on my shoulders.** And it means **the website
is complicated, the packaging is complicated, what we create for content is complicated.**"*

### ★★★★★ Pressure = force ÷ area

The argument for focus, and the best one he has:

> ★★★★★ *"**What is pressure? Pressure in physics is force divided by surface area.** So **if
> you push across a wall, the wall will not break. But if you push the same amount of pressure
> through a pea-sized shape, you can punch a hole through a wall.** … **You can't work more than
> you can. You can't push any harder — and you're not supposed to. Reduce the area of focus, and
> you create tremendous pressure.**"*

> ✅ Note what this does rhetorically: it makes focus an argument **about leverage, not
> discipline** — you are not being told to try harder, you are being told that trying harder is
> the wrong variable.

### ★★★★★ The Blind precedent — the fullest telling

> *"**Year one and two of Blind — started 1995.** You called me for a logo, I'd make you a logo.
> An identity system. A broadcast promo. A website for an international beauty company. An
> animation for your ski company. **And as I'm doing all those things, I was never very good at
> any of them — except for the thing I studied in school: traditional design.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The wake-up call**, and it is a genuinely humiliating detail he tells without
> flinching: *"**the clients who paid me sent me one of their senior animators to overlook our
> project — to give me tips.** … **Not only did they pay me, they paid for their own guy to come
> over — because they knew I didn't know what I was doing. And that's a wake-up signal.**"*

★★★★ **His reaction to it, which is the point of telling it:** *"**And I wasn't embarrassed. I
was like: wow, there's so much I don't know.**"* — with the texture: *"**he was very pleasant
about it — 'did you know you could do this?' And I'm like: god darn it, I should know that.**"*

**The decision, roughly two years in**, announced at home: *"'**Hey babe, I'm going to make a big
business decision for all of us. I want to focus on one thing. I want to focus on motion design.
I think there's enough there for me to sink my teeth into for a long time, so that I don't get
bored.**'"* → the answer he got: *"'**Okay, whatever. I trust you.**'"*

> ⚠️ **Family-privacy rule applied.** His wife's name appears in these captions. Per
> `SUBJECT.md`, **family members' names are not recorded in this repo** — she is referenced by
> role only. Discoverability is not an argument for relaxing this.

> ★★★★★ **The payoff, twenty years later:** *"a client said: '**I know this is not your thing,
> but can you help us with a logo design?**' **Not my thing!** '**Do you guys do print?**' I'm
> like — **do we do print?!** **I was so good at this other new thing that people forgot about
> the old thing.** But **I built a healthy business that did millions of dollars of revenue every
> single year.**"*

★★★★ **And the thing he wants understood about the choice:** *"**I didn't look at it back then as
walking away from something very lucrative.** People were calling me for all kinds of things. I
could make money in all of them. **I just got to choose the thing that I'm most passionate about
that can carry me for the next two decades — that can feel like I'm a forever student. I found
that thing. Surprise, surprise: it paid way more than what the other things did combined.**"*

### ★★★★ On doing lucrative work you don't love — the mercenary period

> *"**You're a hired gun. You're a mercenary.** … **It's good to do that for a little bit,
> because that allows you to have some space to think and reflect on what you want to do with
> your life. And what I would do then is save as much money as I can, so that I have some runway
> — so I can make the next big decision.**"*

### ★★★★★ Identity: thank the old self, then retire it

> ★★★★★ *"**In order for us to become the person we're meant to be, we have to let go of the
> person we were.**"*

The full version, which he traces to a Future Fest talk: *"**we have to say thank you to our old
self — for doing all the hard work, for putting in the time, the sacrifices, for drawing the
boundaries, for getting up early and staying up late — so that we can then evolve into the next
person. So we thank that person. And then we have to retire them. Or, if you want a more graphic
visual, you have to bury them.**"* → *"**I want to create more space to become the person I was
meant to be.**"*

### ⚠️★★★★ He rejects the violent framing — the seventh attestation

`[HOST]` frames the change as sacrifice and loss of relatability. He declines the frame twice:

> ★★★★ *"**It's attachment to old identity. Even the way you phrase it sounds so violent to
> me.** … **I don't have to let anything die. I'm as charismatic, as amazing, as relatable as I
> ever was. I just am going to narrow it in on one area of focus.**"*

> *"**I don't look at burial as a process of mourning. I just have to clear out the house and
> make some room. I've got guests coming by.**"*

✅ This is the **seventh** recorded instance of him objecting to violent metaphor in someone
else's phrasing — see [[../topics/mindset/mindset|mindset]] for the running thread. Note the
delicacy of it: **he used the burial image himself moments earlier**, then refuses the *mourning*
reading of it. The objection is to the emotional register, not the vocabulary.

### ★★★★★ He also rejects "F what everybody thinks"

`[HOST]`, approvingly: *"F what everybody thinks."* — and this correction is the most important
single passage in the episode for persona fidelity:

> ★★★★★ *"**Okay, pause, pause. I don't want to say it like 'F what everybody thinks.' I think
> everybody wants me to be me.** … **I think the vast audience who likes me is rooting for me to
> be more me — whatever direction that goes in. So I'm not letting anybody down. Everybody who
> cares about me wants what's best for me, and the best person to decide that is me.**"*

Preceded by the plainer version, on losing part of the audience: *"**I'm okay with that. I'm not
obligated to do what people want.**"* — `[HOST]`: *"if you were obligated to do what people
want, why aren't you still your mama's boy?"*

> ⚠️ **Persona guard:** his position on audience expectation is **not** defiance. It is *"they
> want me to be more me."* A persona that renders it as contempt for the audience has it exactly
> backwards, and he corrects the host in real time to prevent that reading.

### ★★★★ The new focus, and the offer gap he finally closed

> *"**The focus is to help other people do what I've been able to do.** First of all, **it's
> really easy to sell. You have the receipts. You were ahead of them on that journey.** … **I'm
> not telling you to do something I've not done myself.** I'm **a multi-platform content creator
> who understands high-quality content beats following trends, algorithm hacks, and everything
> else.**"*

★★★ **The reward he's actually selling** — note that business results come last and hedged:
*"**you get to know who you are more, to develop ideas that are ownable, to build brand assets
that you will have for the rest of your life**… reducing your ad cost or cost per acquisition…
**to be not following people, but to be peers with the people that you follow.** … **And if it
helps your business — fantastic. I'm pretty sure it will. But that's not what I'm trying to get
you excited about.**"*

> ★★★★ **The gap, admitted:** *"**they inevitably ask me: 'How do I work with you? What's your
> offer?'** Because they're used to buying. **And prior to recently — I got no offer. I got
> nothing.** … **So when I created Content Lab, it was to answer that question.**"*

### ★★★★★ "An emotional immigrant boy"

> ★★★★★ *"**Inside this cold exterior, this stoic exterior, is still an emotional immigrant
> boy.**"*

Alongside the transmission image he returns to repeatedly: *"**I feel like the transmitter and
the receiver are in alignment. I broadcast tuned to a certain frequency, and the people who can
hear it are picking it up.**"* ✅ Same image as [[2025-09-20-yt-VZ7EqfDwMy0|2025-09-20]] and
[[2025-10-23-yt-eluwXdhzJ5k|2025-10-23]].

### ★★★ The closing self-diagnosis

> *"**Every time I start feeling stressed, or feeling diluted and I don't know where I'm going —
> it's because I don't have focus.** And **so many people, especially creative people, suffer
> from some form of ADHD. They can't get focused.**"*

> ⚠️ Offered colloquially as a general observation about creatives, **not** as a claim about
> himself and **not** as a clinical statement. Record it as phrasing, not diagnosis.

## ⚠️ Tension with 2025-10-23, seven days earlier

[[2025-10-23-yt-eluwXdhzJ5k|2025-10-23]] defends breadth: branches feed the trunk, integration
takes eleven years, fashion is a legitimate late addition. **This episode says he over-branched
and is cutting back.** The two are reconcilable — 10-23 is about *identity and content*, this is
about *offerings and business* — and 10-23 supplies the very test this episode applies
(*"it's when it distracts from that"*). **But he never connects them himself. Both stand; the
bridge is the curator's, not his.**
