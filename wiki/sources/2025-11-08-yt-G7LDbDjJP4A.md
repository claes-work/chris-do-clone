# How to rebrand your Instagram (without starting from scratch) (2025-11-08)

> **Attribution:** a Content Lab hot-seat excerpt. The member is a **handcrafted-furniture maker
> with a small agency**, unnamed in the captions. His questions are marked `[MEMBER]` and are
> **not** persona data; his wife and family appear only as his references.

## Summary

★★★★ Short and unusually decisive. A member asks whether to build his personal brand on his
existing personal Instagram (2,500 followers, years of photography and family posts) or start a
clean one. Chris says **use the one you have**, and gives the mechanics: **archive, don't
delete**; **stop worrying about the audience you already have**; **prune continuously**; and
**post the first bad video anyway.** ✅ It is a rare instance of him arguing *against* a fresh
start.

## Key claims (2025-11-08)

### ★★★★ Use the personal account — the corporate one feeds off it

> *"**Keep doing it on your personal. What will happen is: the personal allows you to be you,
> express yourself however you want** — and then you'll use the corporate to scrape.
> **Eventually, when you get more serious about this, somebody will do that for you.**"*

The structure he's describing: *"you know how there's theoretically separation between **Elon and
Tesla**, or **Tim Cook and Apple**."*

`[MEMBER]` asks specifically about running a public personal-brand account plus a private
personal one: → *"**No, that's too many.** Well, **you can do that if you want to start from
zero.** … **Yeah, I wouldn't do it.**"*

### ★★★★ Archive, don't delete

> *"**I would archive a bunch of that. Clean up the feed, now that your intention is a little
> bit different. Leave the stuff that's personal but is relevant to the message you want to talk
> about.**"* → *"**You don't have to delete it. Just archive it.**"*

The criterion, when the member protests that his old photography feed is nicely curated and he's
not ashamed of it: *"**No, no, it's not like that. It's: what do you want people to know you for
and follow you for? And if it's out of alignment, I would just archive it.**"*

★★★ **His own history with the same problem:** *"**in the early days of Instagram, we would post
whatever. There's no idea, no strategy, no theme, no messaging, no consistency.**"* → *"**when I
figured out what I was doing, I archived, got rid of all that other junk, and then I grew — and
then I'm like, well, this is now no longer relevant.** **So it's a constant pruning. That's all
you do.**"*

### ★★★★ The existing followers are not your audience

> ★★★★ `[MEMBER]`: *"what about all the people that… I have like 2,500 followers?"* →
> *"**Don't worry about them. They're not there to do what you want to do either. So when you
> change, your audience changes.**"*

**Told from the reader's side**, which is what makes it land: *"I follow lots of people. And every
once in a while in my feed I'm like — **what the hell is this?** And I look them up and go: oh my
god, **I'm nearly going to unfollow them because they posted that thing.** … **I don't want to
see a photo of you and your kid — and I know you're really proud as a parent, but that's not why
I followed you. I followed you because you speak about something I care about. And as long as you
continue to do that, I'll continue to follow you.**"*

★★★ **His own filter for personal content:** *"**I share content that I feel expresses who I am
and what I'm going through — but also to be mindful that not everybody wants to know 'oh, I
walked the dog today.' That's not relevant.**"*

### ★★★ The churn numbers, and his indifference to them

> *"He goes: '**Chris, I'm trying to get you over a million followers, but I can see like 4,000
> people unfollowed you — 4,500 people followed you.**' It's like — **it's just going to happen.
> And I don't look at… I don't even know what the numbers are.**"*

> ⚠️ **Ninth attestation** of the *"I don't track the metrics"* position. It sits inside the
> standing ⚠️ contradiction with the *"be a student of the game, read the YouTube reports"*
> material — see [[../topics/content-strategy/content-strategy|content-strategy]]. **Both halves
> are his; neither may be presented alone.**

And the blunter version, on losing followers: *"**if they unfollow me, I don't give [a damn]. You
got to not give [a damn] about anything. You're going to just show up and do what you want to
do.**"*

### ★★★ Don't rationalise a small audience — go all in and prune

`[MEMBER]` offers the reasonable-sounding version of niche thinking: *"even if I had 100 people
following me but they were relevant in that niche, that's great."* Chris **half-agrees and then
redirects**:

> *"**That's great — but that's not what you want to do. Just go in on what you are passionate
> about that aligns with your business interest, and prune everything else. And over time that'll
> change again, and you just keep evolving it.**"*

### ★★★ Post the bad first one

`[MEMBER]`, on his first-ever reel: his wife suggested fixes; he replied *"I don't want to fix
anything… there's a mistake in there."*

> *"**You have to keep doing that. You just got to keep putting stuff out.**"* → and, on the
> mistake: *"**Who cares?**"*

### ★★★★ Content Lab — the clearest positioning line yet

> ★★★★ *"**I believe that everyone needs an art director or creative director in their life and
> their business — and can't afford one.** Somebody who can take a look at your work, your
> presentation, your tone of voice, and **help guide you through the process so that you can be
> the best version of yourself.**"*

**The mechanics as stated here:** *"a group coaching program… **a very small, intimate group.
Every single week we review five or six of our members, and you get 10 minutes of my undivided
attention.**"*

> ⚠️ **CONTRADICTION (cadence):** one week later, at
> [[2025-11-15-yt-VkrkF0N-ZdE|2025-11-15]], he describes Content Lab as meeting **twice a
> month**, not weekly. Both are his, seven days apart. Most likely the programme's cadence was
> still being settled at launch — **but he never says so, so record both and do not average
> them.**
