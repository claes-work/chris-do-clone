# What actually makes people buy? (2025-11-13)

> **Attribution:** a workshop excerpt with a live room. Audience one-word answers are marked
> `[ROOM]`. All teaching is Chris's.

## Summary

★★★★★ The clearest statement in the corpus of **why people pay more** — built by stacking
**Marty Neumeier's brand commitment ladder** onto **Maslow's hierarchy of needs** and arguing
they are the same shape. He flags the synthesis as his own speculation up front. It resolves into
a pricing claim (*sell into the top of the pyramid and you command more money*), is demonstrated
with an **Apple-versus-Dell price-page comparison** that is fair to both, and closes with **"success
leaves clues"** — delivered, characteristically, as a reason his paying room may not need him.

## Key claims (2025-11-13)

### ★★★★★ The mapping — and his own hedge on it

> *"You know **Maslow's hierarchy of needs**. This we need to have; this is what we want to have.
> **I find that there's a strange correlation with this and lots of things in our life. And I'm
> going to be like a conspiracy theorist here.**"*

✅ **Record the hedge with the framework.** He does not present the mapping as established
theory; he presents it as a pattern he noticed: *"**and then he starts doing this — I'm like:
wait a minute, isn't that this?**"*

**Maslow, as he uses it:** at the bottom *"food, water, shelter, clothing — we have to have"*; at
the top *"**self-actualisation, like having meaning and purpose in our life. It's the very top.
It's the hardest to achieve. Many of us will not achieve it, but we desire that, to realise our
full potential.**"* And *"**somewhere in here is status.**"*

**The ladder, credited:** *"**Marty Neumeier has this thing in his book *The Brand Flip*. It's
called the brand commitment ladder, and it tells you what kind of relationship you have with your
customers.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The thesis:** *"**if we start to understand why people buy things — the psychological,
> emotional needs that we all have — and we address those things, we'll start to unlock value and
> pricing.**"*

### ★★★★★ The four rungs

| Rung | What it is, in his words | What it earns you |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Satisfaction** | *"**you said you're going to do this, and you did what you said.** You delivered the juice on time. You built the website."* | *"**I start to feel like I might try you again.**"* |
| **2. Delight** | *"**when you've surprised them.** You got same-day shipping from Amazon — damn, this is good."* → *"**there's a peak experience**"* | *"**you might start to recommend it.** … you jump on social, you tell people about it. **It's free advertising, word of mouth.**"* |
| **3. Engagement** | *"**this is where you start to feel like you belong as part of a community or a tribe.**"* | *"**you've developed customer loyalty and you will automatically repurchase.** Any brand that you love — something else comes up, you'll buy another version."* |
| **4. Empowerment** | *"**even more than tribal enrolment.** If you read the book, it says: **personal growth.**"* | the top — *"**what brand helps you to grow?**"* |

`[ROOM]`, asked what brand helps them grow: *"CrossFit."* → *"Good answer."* / *"Anthony
Robbins."* → *"Yes."* — and his own interjection, played for laughs: *"**The Futur. Thank you.
Thank you.**"*

★★★★ **The example he's surprised nobody offers** — and note the ending: *"**Nike** has a program
where you run virtually against friends and they try to keep you in shape. **You are literally
growing stronger, building a stronger cardiovascular system, you're going to hopefully live
longer, look better, feel more confident. That's a lot of personal growth. And so when they tap
into that, they will take all your money — and you'll be happy to give it to them.**"*

★★★ Above growth he places **business success** (*"they help you to grow your business"*), and
then *"**right there, number four: social status. It's right up there.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The pricing conclusion:** *"**the higher you go up in this ladder, the greater the
> commitment from the customer.** … **what you want to do is help people with feeling love and
> belonging, self-esteem, and help them to actualise their own meaning and purpose. And it turns
> out: when you sell a product or service that helps them achieve this, you will command more
> money.**"*

→ **The question he leaves the room with:** *"**Now I want you to think about what it is that you
do. Where are you selling in this?**"* — framed as *"**the debate about the wants and the needs.
Are needs or wants more valuable?**"*

### ★★★★ What you buy says something about you

> *"**We buy things because when we buy things, it gives us a sense of our identity. What you buy
> says something about you.**"* — and, when the room doesn't all nod: *"**Even when you don't
> want to buy, that says something about you too.**"*

★★★ **With his father as the example**, affectionately: *"**my dad just wants to buy the cheapest
version of everything. Costco pants are fine for my dad.** … **The $1.50 hot dog — boom, he's
good.**"* → *"**Then I know who your dad is right now.**"*

★★★ **The ethical extension, which he flags as a digression:** *"**there's a statement: we vote
with our money. Do you want to see more of this in the world, or less?** That's why I think many
people are against **fast fashion** — because **it's killing the planet. It's cheap, it's poorly
made, and they basically steal ideas that other people toil over and develop and innovate.**
Sorry — off soapbox now."*

### ★★★★★ Apple vs. Dell — where the price lives

The demonstration, and it is the best version of this argument in the corpus because **he is fair
to both companies**:

> *"I'm an **Apple ride-or-die** kind of person. And every time I want to buy a new Apple
> product — **do you ever pay attention to Apple sales pages? I study it up and down.** **Do you
> know how hard it is to find out how much something costs from Apple?** … As soon as I say
> that: **where's the price? What are the specs?** **So Apple took this whole model of selling
> and turned it upside down.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**Go on the Dell website. Where's the price? It's right there on the front page —
> because they got nothing. They're not selling you a story. They're not helping you achieve
> status.**"*

**The tell:** *"**anybody rock a Dell t-shirt? Put a Dell bumper sticker on, like ride or die?
Nobody does that** — cuz **I just want it to perform, I want it to be cheap, and I want it to be
here tomorrow. Dell does a good job with that.**"* ✅ Note the concession: Dell is **good at
serving needs**; it simply isn't operating on the upper rungs.

★★★ **The brand-extension test**, put to the room: *"**when Dell slaps their logo on something
that's not computer related, does it go up in value?**"* → `[ROOM]`: *"Goes down."* → *"**Ouch.
There goes the Dell sponsorship, guys.**"*

★★★★ **How Apple got there:** *"at the top [of the sales page] **they try to get you to buy into
the whole freaking store. You've got to keep scrolling and scrolling.** They're not going to
start with **this much RAM, that many kilohertz.** **They actually rewrote the rules about how to
market computer products — because they couldn't compete with Intel. So they just changed it
all.**"*

### ★★★★ Think Different

> *"the campaign that put him back on the map — **that was the rebirth of Apple.** What was it
> called?"* → `[ROOM]`: *"Think Different."* → *"**I know the proper English is *think
> differently*. But *think different*.**"*

**The question he asks of it, which is the whole lesson:** *"**Where in Maslow's hierarchy of
needs is *think different*? What is it doing to you? Why did they come up with that?**"*

**The credits he gives:** *"first of all, **Steve Jobs, brilliant marketer**, works with **one of
the best agencies in the world, Chiat\\Day, with Lee Clow leading.**"*

> ⚠️ **CONTESTED ATTRIBUTION — flag, do not repeat as fact.** On *"Here's to the crazy ones"* he
> says: *"**which is, I believe, a Jack Kerouac poem that they snaked brilliantly.**"* He hedges
> it himself (*"I believe"*). The *Think Different* text is generally credited to the Chiat\\Day
> team; the Kerouac resemblance is a widely-noted echo, not an established source. **Record it as
> a claim he makes with a hedge — never as an established fact, and never without the hedge.**

### ★★★★ Success leaves clues — and the offer to make himself unnecessary

> ★★★★ *"There are brilliant products and services in the world that are **giving you the
> blueprint on how to be super successful — and they give it to you for free, every single
> day.** **Jim Rohn: success leaves clues.** **Look at successful companies and brands. See what
> they do and pick up the clues.**"*

> ★★★★★ **And what he does with that, said to a room that paid to be there:** *"**so you can
> teach yourself and be an autodidact for the rest of your life. Never have to pay another dollar
> in tuition, or courses, or master classes, or boot camps like this — and just pick up the
> clues. They're all there.**"*

> ✅ **Behavioural data, not just content.** This is the same move as the Molly exchange and the
> refusal to be a permission-giver at [[2025-10-23-yt-eluwXdhzJ5k|2025-10-23]]: **given the
> chance to make himself necessary, he consistently does the opposite.** It recurs often enough
> to be a persona trait rather than a rhetorical flourish.
