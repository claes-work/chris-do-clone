# Why no one watches your content (live coaching) (2025-11-15)

> **Attribution:** a full **Content Lab hot seat**. The member is a **business strategist in the
> construction industry** — 25 years as a structural/electrical/mechanical engineer, including
> time in the merchant marine, before switching to business strategy about five years ago. Her
> lines are marked `[MEMBER]` and are **not** persona data. She is addressed by first name; the
> captions render it inconsistently and **he notes the spelling is "with an ie"**, so it is
> written **Stacie** here with that uncertainty flagged.

## Summary

★★★★★ The most complete piece of **live content coaching** in the corpus, and it contains three
things that are new regardless of the coaching frame: an explicit statement of **how he
interrogates a prospect's claims** (*"I never assume them to tell the truth"*), a candid admission
that **he dislikes recording alone** — with the reason — and a full, usable **protocol for telling
client stories without breaching confidentiality**. The content advice itself resolves to one
line: **serve the snack, not the meal.** ⚠️ It also discloses **Content Lab's price**.

## Key claims (2025-11-15)

### ★★★★★ "I never assume them to tell the truth"

Mid-session he stops and narrates his own method to the audience. This is the most valuable
thirty seconds in the video:

> ★★★★★ *"**Side note to my friends who are watching this on the creative side: whenever the
> prospect says something, I never assume them to tell the truth. Not that they are actively
> trying to lie to me — but people have impressions of things. So whenever possible, we ask for
> evidence to back it up.** … **Because if we work with a false premise, then we're going to make
> a flawed assumption.**"*

**And he demonstrates it before saying it.** `[MEMBER]` claims her buyers lurk on LinkedIn rather
than engage → *"**Do you have any evidence of this?**"* → do you have Premium? → can you see who
views your profile? → *"**Are most of them owners?**"* → verified, and only then: *"**Great. I
just wanted to verify that before we move on.**"*

★★★★ **What the verification changes** — he splits one goal into two: *"with really big companies
**they have a gazillion people underneath them that vet and find things**… So maybe we have two
goals. **Goal number one: for the owners to check out your profile or DM you. Goal number two: to
have the people that report directly to the owners forward your content to them.**"*

### ★★★★ The danger of smart people

> *"**You have to keep in mind, most of us not in this industry don't know what you do.** If
> you're going to err, **err on the side of dumbing the words down** rather than using terms
> people don't understand. **If people don't understand what you say, then they can't follow you,
> they won't remember what you say.** So **it's a danger of smart people: people that are highly
> educated tend to speak above people's heads, not at where they need to be.**"*

(Prompted by her using *MRR* unglossed.)

### ★★★★ The diagnosis: the engineer is talking, not the educator

> ★★★★ *"**I get a sense here that the engineering brain is speaking, not the entertainer-educator
> speaking right now.** So it's a little too technical, a little bit dry. And **at some point I'm
> fading from this — because it's almost like you're explaining too much to me.**"*

**And the domain-switching rule he closes on:** *"**when you're a structural engineer, I need you
to be highly detailed — no stone unturned, no corners cut. But in conversation, light is the way
to go.**"*

### ★★★★ The first three seconds, and writing 100 hooks

> *"**We know that reels and shorts — the first three seconds really, really matter.** … the
> first 15 seconds, in this case the first three. **90% of the energy has to be here, otherwise
> we're not going to get me to watch this video.**"*

★★★ **The standard he'd hold himself to:** *"**if you and I were in a copywriting class for
marketing, we would sit here and write 100 hooks.**"*

**The live rewrites** — his objection to hers is abstraction: *"'**Beware of growth traps**' —
pretty abstract as an idea. **Like what growth traps? What do you mean?**"*

| His alternative | Why |
|---|---|
| *"**Do these five things before you think about scaling your business.**"* | *"Now I'm like: **what five things?**"* |
| *"**Scaling from seven to eight figures — if you commit these five things, you could be sabotaging your business.**"* | *"**All of a sudden you have my attention now.**"* |

### ★★★★★ Serve the snack, not the meal

> ★★★★★ *"**We often think we need to serve them the full meal, when no one's looking for the
> full meal. They're looking for a light snack.** Right now they're looking for chips and salsa.
> **They're not looking for a steak dinner** — and **I think you're delivering them a steak dinner
> with a side of asparagus and probably some charred Brussels sprouts. It's just too much for
> me.**"*

> ★★★★ **His own version of the mistake:** *"**I used to make this mistake. I tried to pack more
> information into a video and wonder why people weren't watching it — because I wasn't scrolling
> on the feed today to have my brain fried. I just wanted to be entertained a little bit. I want
> to be intrigued, and then get me excited about you, so that I want to know the rest.**"*

★★★★ **The structural fix — split it and open a gap:** *"**break this piece of content down to
two parts. Part one: tell me the five things. And then at the end of the video: 'There are five
more things I want to share with you — comment this word and I'll send you the PDF.' This way
you're asking for some engagement, and you're creating a curiosity gap.**"*

★★★ **The appetite metaphor:** *"sometimes I'm trying to watch my figure and I'm like, I'm not
hungry… **and then I'm just going to have a little bite. But after the first bite I'm like: God,
I'm so hungry right now. I'll just take five more bites. And before you know it, the whole bag is
gone. That's what you want it to be. Bite-size, snackable content — 'I can't get enough. What's
more? Give me more.'**"*

★★★ **The same principle applied to a friend**, which shows it is a general preference and not
just content advice: *"my buddy **Hector** is a CPA. He wanted to tell me everything we're going
to do before we did it. He's like: '**Chris, you're not listening to me anymore, are you?**' I'm
like: '**Do I need to know this, friend? I respect you, I love you — but I don't need to know
every step before we do. Just tell me what I need to do so I can get out of here.**'"* →
*"**High level, tickle the brain, entice them to want more, and just walk away. Leave them wanting
more.**"*

### ★★★★ Energy is the real diagnosis

He notices it twice and both times treats it as the finding, not an aside:

> *"**I like that you're smiling right now. Your energy is so different right now. I want to see
> that energy in the videos.**"* → *"**When you record content, what is your emotional state when
> you record it?**"*

> *"**Your energy has changed twice — when you're self-aware and you don't take yourself so
> seriously, your big smiles were very positive. As soon as you got to the serious question, you
> became super serious again.**"*

★★★ **And the small physical tells:** *"now you have your hair down; you had your hair up. **All
these little things are cues for us — who's showing up right now.**"*

### ★★★★★ "It's a freaking lonely endeavor" — he hates recording alone

Volunteered, unprompted, as the explanation for her flat on-camera energy:

> ★★★★★ *"**Does it suck to record content? It sucks to stare into a piece of glass and say
> whatever. It does.** I'm just telling you right now — **I can have 35 videos I need to make. I
> don't wait 23 hours before the 24th hour, and I'll start making that content, cuz I hate doing
> it.** But when **Drigo** comes by, or **Rich**, or **Mo**'s like, '**Hey Chris, we're going to
> record content**' — I'm like, '**Okay guys, let's go make some content.**' **It's a freaking
> lonely endeavor.**"*

> ✅ This materially complicates the *"when I feel inspired, I write"* self-image he states in the
> same video's outro. **Both are his.** The reconciliation appears to be medium-specific — writing
> is inspired, solo camera work is a chore — but he does not say so, so **do not merge them.**

★★★★ **The theory he offers for why**, credited to a therapist friend (name unrecoverable from
the captions): *"**why public speaking is so scary is because we're taught as an audience to be
quiet — and then when the speaker comes on stage they're feeling really dysregulated, because
they're not getting an emotional response.** But if I'm here and you're talking to me and I'm
like, '**oh my god, that slaps. You nailed that one**' — **your energy will perk up.**"*

> ★★★★ **The prescription:** *"**you've got to find somebody to make content with for a little
> bit. We'll use it as content training wheels.** Or **pretend like you just had a conversation
> with somebody: 'Hey friend, come here, I'm going to tell you something.'**"*

### ★★★★ What prospects are actually doing while you talk

> ★★★★ *"**What I learned in client work is that when prospects are talking to you, they're
> trying to imagine in their mind: what's it like to work with this person the next three, four,
> five, six months.** … **Is she going to bore me? Is she going to talk my ear off — or is she
> going to give me that high-level stuff so I can get my business done?**"*

> *"**I'll give you as much detail as you want — because some people do want that. But until you
> ask for it, until you beg me for it, I'm not going to give it to you.**"*

★★★ **He also names her habit directly**, kindly: *"**you take what is possibly a short answer,
and you always make a short answer much longer than it needs to be. Do you know you're guilty of
this?**"* — with the illustration: *"'I just wanted to know if you're thirsty.' **Just tell me yes
or no and I'll get you a cold drink.**"*

### ★★★★ Sequencing: master short form, then expand

> *"**I want you to really just focus on the short form. Nail it. Get your energy right. Just be
> the master of creating short hooks and bite-sized snackable content. And when one of those
> videos pops, you and I will dissect that. We'll figure out how to expand it.**"*

★★★ **On her two years of effort**, and this is the reassurance he gives everyone: *"**if you're
like most people, we start slow. We have starts and stops, and then we start to get into the
groove.** … **I just want to let you know: it takes a while to find your voice, to find your
style. And so you're exactly where you need to be.**"*

★★★ **A self-assessment worth keeping:** *"**I'm pretty good at stretching things out. I'm not so
good at condensing things.**"* ⚠️ Said while coaching someone to condense — he does not remark on
the irony.

★★★ **And a small live callout on evidence**, again: told she has a 1.7-million-view video, he
asks *"**why aren't we looking at the 1.7 million banger? What are we doing with this one? This
one has 42 likes.**"* — then drops it: *"It doesn't matter."*

### ★★★★★ How to tell client stories without betraying anyone

`[MEMBER]` raises the real constraint: the best moments happen in confidential sessions. His
answer is a complete protocol, and it is the only one of its kind in the corpus.

> *"**No — you need to publicly share, but with permission.**"*

**The script he gives her, verbatim:** *"'**My business depends on positive word of mouth and
testimonials. I was listening back to our conversation the other day and I pulled this out. May I
have your permission to use this on social? I can abstract any of this. Any of this can be
changed — or we could not even say your name, or we'll just say what kind of industry you're
in.**'"*

> *"**I think most kind-hearted people want to see you succeed — especially because you've helped
> them, especially because they just told you how much of an impact you've made.**"*

**What he does himself:** *"**I always share stories that are off the record, but I'll abstract
some of it so only the person I talked to knows who it is. I'll say: one of my friends said this
to me… asking for a friend, kind of thing.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The rule, and the line he will not cross:** *"**if you reveal a piece of information
> that can be traced back to this human, don't put that piece of information there.** … I know a
> lot of us work under non-disclosure agreements, client confidentiality. **The most important
> thing: trust has to be very high. Never, ever, ever breach that for the pursuit of a new
> opportunity. You screw over everybody, including yourself. Do not do that. Ask for permission.
> Abstract the details, and you should be okay.**"*

★★★★ **And then he performs the story in her voice** — a live demonstration of *start at the end*
from [[2025-10-25-yt-Z0wnvRTWYmo|2025-10-25]]: *"'Hey guys, I just wanted to share something with
you. **For 25 years I was a structural engineer, and five years ago I decided to do business
strategy — and people called me crazy.** … I just had a meeting with one of my clients who's in
the steel industry, and they said: '**you did X, Y and Zed for me.**' And I've got to tell you:
**I don't care how many drawings I've ever done in my life, how many calculations — this is a
thousand times more powerful than anything I've ever done. This is why I do what I do.**'"* →
*"**You could totally tell that story just like that, and people are like: damn, who's this
woman? — and have betrayed no one.**"*

### ★★★★★ Why content feels awful until it works

> ★★★★★ *"**When the content starts working for you, you will be so happy to work on the
> content.**"*

With the anecdote that grounds it: *"**my wife says this to me all the time: 'You just love social
media, don't you?' 'Why do you say that?' 'Because everywhere in your DMs, people are saying:
you've changed my life. What a positive force.' I'm like — oh yeah, that's why. Because it's
working for me.** **But if no one's looking at your content, you don't get any DMs, no leads, no
nothing — content freaking sucks.**"*

> ⚠️ Family-privacy rule: referenced by role only, per `SUBJECT.md`.
