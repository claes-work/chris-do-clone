# How to attract higher-paying clients (it's not your work) (2025-11-20)

> **Attribution:** a workshop excerpt with a live room. One audience member — an agency person
> the captions call **Steve** — supplies the numbers for the live calculation and is marked
> `[ROOM]`. All teaching is Chris's.

## Summary

★★★★★ Six minutes that contain the tightest chain of pricing logic in the corpus:
**sell money, not services** → **size of problem (in their mind) determines size of budget** →
so **become a problem seeker, not a problem solver** → and find the pain with one question:
**"why not do nothing?"** It opens by telling a room of creatives that their favourite client
segment is a euphemism.

## Key claims (2025-11-20)

### ★★★★ The premise

> *"**Your client desires something beyond the thing that you make. But what we almost always do
> is sell the thing that we make.** 'I make websites. I make shoes. I make whatever it is.' And
> **that's why we're not tapping into the highest potential value of what we do.**"*

### ★★★★ "Startups and early-stage founders" is a euphemism

> ★★★★ *"Every time I ask creative people '**who do you serve**', they almost always say
> **startups, early-stage founders**. And that is **code word** for… **broke.** **Everybody wants
> to work with broke clients.** And [then they] go, '**Chris, I can't make any money.**' —
> **I wonder why.**"*

> *"**I've never dreamt once in my life. I never said: 'Amy, please help me find a broke client.
> Help me find an early-stage startup.' They have no money.**"*

★★★ **The one exception he grants:** *"**there's one area in which startups have money… VC.** If
you help them do a **pitch deck**, you will get lots of money. **I have a friend who does that.
She loves startups. She helps companies put together decks so they can get \$200 million.**"*

### ★★★★★ The live calculation

`[ROOM]` — an agency person helping a company raise **\$10 million**, charging *"up to 5%."*

> *"**So what is 5% of 10 million?**"* → **\$500k** → *"That's pretty good."* → *"**So a company
> who needs help getting \$10 million of investment, would they be happy to pay 5%?**"*

★★★★ **And when someone in the room hesitates** (*"I don't know, it's not my world"*), the
reframe that removes the domain from the question entirely:

> ★★★★★ *"**I can help you get \$10 million to buy more cars. And I charge you 5%. You would do
> that deal every day of the week, would you not?**"* → `[ROOM]`: *"**Oh yeah. It's such a small
> percentage.**"*

### ★★★★★ Sell money

> ★★★★★ *"**Kevin Daley says: what you need to do is you need to sell money. You guys are selling
> the wrong thing. You need to sell money. You're selling services. You're selling time.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**Every time you can dollarise the value of what it is that you do — to put it in real
> terms — it's an easy sell.**"* → *"**If you can measure it, and it matters to the client, it's
> an easy sell. It's just a matter of asking the right questions.**"*

### ★★★★★ Size of problem → size of budget

> ★★★★★ *"**There is a direct correlation to size of problem — in the client's mind, not yours —
> to size of budget.**"*

### ★★★★★ Problem seekers, not problem solvers

> ★★★★★ *"**We're taught in school to be problem solvers. We need to be problem seekers. We need
> to find a problem before we solve it. And you have to decouple your knowledge and your desire
> to solve a specific problem, so you can find the right problem.**"*

**The worked version, which is the sharpest statement of positioning-versus-craft in the
corpus:**

> ★★★★★ *"**If I make logos, and all I do is look for logo problems — I'm already in trouble.
> I'm looking for a person with a business problem that sometimes a logo's the answer. There's a
> big shift there.**"* — with the proverb attached: *"**if the only tool you have is a hammer, all
> the problems look like nails.**"*

### ★★★★ Pain is the qualifier

> *"**We need to find someone out there who has a burning, urgent desire to solve a problem that
> they feel they've felt massive pain around.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**Anytime you can't sell something and it's not worth a lot, it's because they don't
> feel the pain.**"* → *"**when you talk to the client… you should help them define the pain.**"*

### ★★★★★ The motivation formula — *Socratic Selling*

> ★★★★★ **motivation = degree of pain felt + how clear the outcome is**

> *"**Oftentimes we try to sell the clear outcome — but more often than not, if we just help them
> to understand the degree of pain that they're feeling, the combination of the two are very
> powerful to motivate people to take action.**"*

**The everyday example:** *"some of you might say 'I want to go to the gym, I want to go on a
diet.' **The problem is you don't feel the pain enough.** So if I'm selling you gym memberships
or meal plans, **I need to help you understand the cost of not taking action, so that you can
really feel this pain.**"* → **cost of inaction.**

### ★★★★★ "Why not do nothing?"

> ★★★★★ *"**One thing you should do when you're talking to your clients: you should ask them this
> one question. Why not do nothing?** **That'll help them tell you how much pain they're having
> around not taking action.**"*

Credited: *"**Jonathan Stark** is like: '**Why not do nothing? Just wait.**' And then the client
will tell you: '**Well, we can't wait. We have a real problem. We need this fixed now.**'"*

### ★★★★ The closing diagnosis — the discount is the last symptom, not the disease

> ★★★★★ *"**We're looking for the wrong problem, we're serving the wrong client — and when we
> find the wrong client and sell them the wrong service, we say: 'we'll just do it for less.'
> Do you see the problem?**"*
