## Summary

★★★★ **A causal claim the corpus states in pieces and not in one line**: price follows felt pain, so an
unsellable or cheap offer is a **pain-detection failure**, not a persuasion failure.

## Key claims (2025-11-24)

★★★★ **The diagnosis, via a borrowed adage:** *"There's an expression: **if the only tool you have is a
hammer, all the problems look like nails.** That's the problem."*

⚠️ **The adage is proverbial** (commonly attributed to Maslow) and **he does not attribute it** — he
introduces it as *"there's an expression"*, which is the correct hedge and is preserved here.

> ★★★★★ **The requirement it sets up:** *"**We need to find someone out there who has A BURNING URGENT
> DESIRE to solve a problem they've felt MASSIVE PAIN AROUND.**"*
>
> ★★★★★ **And the causal claim, which is the useful part:** *"**Anytime you can't sell something and
> it's not worth a lot, IT'S BECAUSE THEY DON'T FEEL THE PAIN.**"*

✅ **This reframes two failures as one.** *Can't sell it* and *can't charge much for it* are treated
throughout the corpus as separate problems — of positioning, of confidence, of pricing method. **Here
they are the same symptom with one cause: the buyer does not feel the pain.**
