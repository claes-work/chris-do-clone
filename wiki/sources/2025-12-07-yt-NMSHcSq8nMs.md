# How to market your services correctly in 2026 (2025-12-07)

> **Attribution:** a live workshop. Several audience members participate — one builds an ideal
> customer profile with him, one is cross-examined about a client who grew, one describes a
> friend's businesses. Their lines are marked `[ROOM]` and are **not** persona data. First names
> as heard (**Gabby**, **Greg**, **Ron**) are recorded only as workshop references.

## Summary

★★★★★ A working session on the **ideal customer profile**, built around one uncomfortable
question: *why do creatives keep choosing clients who cannot pay them?* His answer is that it is
a mirror. Along the way it produces the corpus's clearest **client-qualification arithmetic**,
its best statement of **why profiling is an empathy exercise rather than a targeting exercise**,
and ★★★★★ **"the tiers"** — the business lesson he names as the most important one his coach ever
gave him, with the concrete \$100k → \$200k → \$400k → \$1M ladder from Blind's own history.

## Key claims (2025-12-07)

### ★★★★★ The opening diagnosis

> ★★★★★ *"**What successful people do is they spend money to save time. Unsuccessful people spend
> time to save money. You are all focusing on people who have a lot of free time. That's the
> problem.**"*

### ★★★★ Psychographics, not demographics — and what it did for him

> *"**We think we're designing for someone, but we're not really doing it for anyone.** So the way
> I like to do customer profiles is to really go deep — **not just the demographics, which most
> people stop at, but psychographics: the way they think, they feel, their values and belief
> systems.**"*

> ★★★★ **The autobiographical claim:** *"**for me, it was just a creative springboard. From doing
> this, I started to learn and figure out how to do brand strategy. And that was just
> everything — and then my prices just went through the roof.**"*

### ★★★★ The trifecta — how to find your best client

Look at **the last ten clients or projects** and ask three things:

1. **Which gave you the most joy?** — *"if I could just do one more project like that… **I would
   have paid that person to do the work. That's how happy I was.**"*
2. **Was it financially good for you? Did you make money on that?**
3. **Are you aligned in their mission?**

> *"Creatively really cool, cool people, I made a lot of money — **that's a trifecta.**"*

And the non-negotiable: *"**Remember, they have to have money.** Some of you like to target broke
people and you're like, 'well, they can't afford me.' I'm like — **I wonder why. Cuz they broke is
why.** **They've got to be able to afford you. Actually afford a lot of you — because you want to
keep charging more.**"*

### ★★★★★ Why creatives choose clients who can't pay — the mirror

`[ROOM]` (Gabby) offers the honest answer: we like working with people aligned with us in taste
and creativity.

> ★★★★★ *"**Those same people don't have money. Cuz you know why? Cuz WE don't have money. Cuz
> we're just aligned. We're perfectly aligned.** So what are we going to do? **Chicken and
> egg.**"* — with the aside that lands it: *"**Is it like looking in a mirror? I see you.**"*

★★★ **And he is careful with the room's motives**, not dismissive: *"**you all are good-natured,
creative human beings who want to better the world. Yes, most of you. Some of you just want the
money — talk to me later, that's fine.**"*

### ★★★★★ "They trade up" — the client who grows away from you

`[ROOM]` reports the success story: a \$3,000 3D animation for a pharma startup that later grew
into \$40k projects and \$10k/month. Chris accepts it — *"I'm going to question you now. I'm a
hostile witness"* — and then gives the general case, which is the opposite:

> ★★★★★ *"**When you're first dating your high-school sweetheart, and then you guys went to the
> big city and went separate ways — all of a sudden she got a big job, or he got a big job.
> You're not the same to them anymore. They trade up. Most people just trade up.**"*

> *"**When they have the money, [they say] 'we want to work with Chris and his team — they're
> award-winning, look who they worked with.' And most times they just leave you in the dust. And
> you would do the same.** You know how you accepted this low-level intern and then you got real
> big? **They don't have the skills to grow with you. Not everyone is capable of going to the next
> level.**"*

### ★★★★★ The tiers — "the most important business lesson I've learned from my coach"

> ★★★★★ *"**There are tiers. You don't know it, cuz it's all invisible to you. When you hit this
> level, you think you're at the top of the mountain. You're actually just at the bottom of the
> next mountain.**"*

**The ladder, from Blind's own history:**

> ★★★★★ *"**When we would win \$100,000 jobs, we could not win \$200,000 jobs. When we win
> \$200,000 jobs, I could not win \$400,000 jobs — because they're very different animals,
> different levels of competition. And then you get to the million-dollar range, and it becomes
> cutthroat at that level, cuz everyone is world class. You've read them in your design books.
> They're winning all the awards. That's who you're competing with.**"*

### ★★★★★ The arithmetic that ends the argument

`[ROOM]` describes a friend running six or seven businesses totalling ~\$1.2M/year.

**First, the focus verdict** — and note how consistent it is with
[[2025-10-30-yt-3ikc89b-tkE|2025-10-30]]: *"if you take six and you take 1.2, **each business on
average is about \$200k. Each one of them is a hobby lifestyle business.** With all respect to
your friend, **I don't consider him an entrepreneur. He's doing too many weird things. Why don't
you just focus in on the one business that's making all the money and just get rid of the other
ones?**"*

**Then the client-qualification math, in four steps:**

> ★★★★★ *"**\$200K is not a lot of money for your client** — because **they're only going to spend
> some percentage of that on you.** As a rule of thumb, **most companies spend about 10% on
> marketing annually.** So their annual budget for marketing, **including people, is \$20K.** And
> **you're not going to take their whole budget — you're going to take a tenth of their budget,
> which would be \$2K.** **Is that roughly the size of your project?** … **That's the problem.
> Just math doesn't lie.**"*

> ✅ **The resulting criterion:** *"we need somebody who knows their client, **who's doing decent
> numbers, that they would be able to afford people like you — and happy to pay you. Happy to pay
> you. That's a criterion here for an ideal customer profile.**"*

### ★★★★ You design them; you don't chase them

`[ROOM]`: *"Do you wait for these clients to find you, or do you go chase them?"*

> *"**No. You design this person — and then you become so attracted to this person.**"*

★★★★ **With his brother's dating advice as the model** — a small biographical item: *"**My brother
told me this dating advice when I was a little dude. He goes: 'You know who it is that you would
be most attracted to? Sit down and write on a piece of paper the ideal person for you.'** You did
that and then you forgot about it. **And this is how we design our ideal partner — business
partner.**"*

### ★★★★ The live profile build, and the logic check

`[ROOM]` sells tile and furniture online; the profile they build together: a **property
development / business ops director at a hotel group**, ~45, first marriage, two children (8 and
14), a condo in New York, Aspen, business administration and marketing, **~\$400k plus bonuses.**

> ★★★★ **The discipline he applies throughout:** *"**every step that we do is predicated on the
> first part being right. So when I do this for clients, I do the little logic check: does the
> logic logic? If it doesn't, we've got to fix something.**"* — e.g. *"if you said 36, **the math
> don't work.**"*

### ★★★★★ The real reason to build the profile: empathy

> ★★★★★ *"**When you understand the kind of crap they've had to deal with, you hate them a little
> less.** … **This is why it's really important to build a really good profile — because we
> develop empathy for them.**"*

**Worked through:** *"'My god, I just walked in the door. **I'm a trustworthy person. I would
never overpromise and underdeliver. But every other person who has been in front of [him] has done
that to him.**' Can you understand? **So I'm going to be extra sensitive about this, so that they
understand: I'm not here just to get money from you.**"*

### ★★★★ Why your writing isn't working

> ★★★★ *"**Most of you don't know how to write, or create content, or create offers — cuz this is
> blurry. I don't know who I'm talking to.**"*

★★★ **And a practical trick he notices in the room:** *"even Greg was trying to change the profile
on me. But here's something really interesting: **when we gave him a name, he started to stick to
it. This is the power of being hyper-focused in serving a specific type of client.**"*

> *"**Would you rather, for the rest of your life, chase people who are barely scratching at
> this — or get three or four of these types of clients? You would have more business than you
> know what to do with.**"*

### ★★★★★ Measure a hundred times

`[ROOM]` names the resistance honestly: we rush into the work because thinking work leaves
nothing to show for the day.

> ★★★★★ *"There's that expression: **I will measure a hundred times before I cut one piece of
> wood. This is the measuring part. You guys are busy chopping down trees. You're killing earth.**
> … **You just chop away. You don't know what you're looking for.**"*

★★★★ **The response he says this work produces in clients:** *"'**I've never felt so seen. I've
never felt like I've understood our clients before. How'd you do that?**' Or they'll say:
'**you've done in 30 minutes what we've been unable to do in the last year**' — that's a real
statement somebody has told me. **Your value in their mind has gone through the roof. Now when you
see the bill, you'll understand why.**"*

★★★ **And the licence to invent responsibly:** *"I don't know this person, but **I know enough
people like this that once I get enough information, I can build the rest of the profile. And all
of you can do that too — because you have this beautiful thing, no matter what discipline you're
in: imagination. Once we have enough, we can project forward.**"*

### ★★★ The Hormozi content-budget example

> ⚠️ **Third-party recollection, hedged by him.** Recorded because it is *his* argument about
> content spend, **not** as a fact about the people named — and per the cross-clone rule it is
> **never** source material for their own clones.

> *"**I saw Alex a couple years ago. He went up on stage and he goes: 'I spend \$50,000 a month
> myself.' And then with my wife, it's 100k. And then I heard there was like 100k each now.**"* —
> the *"and then I heard"* is his own hedge and travels with the numbers.

**His point in citing it:** *"**the more they spend on their content, the bigger their business
gets, the bigger the opportunities. From the content that they generate they get so many qualified
leads, they don't know what to do with themselves.** So **you're busy talking to the
choreographers of the world, when they don't see the value in what you do.**"*
