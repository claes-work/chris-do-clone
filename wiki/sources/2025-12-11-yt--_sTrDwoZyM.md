# The secret to handling any sales objection (2025-12-11)

> **Attribution:** a workshop Q&A excerpt. One audience question opens it; everything else is
> Chris's. A coaching client's words are quoted inside his story and are marked as hers.

## Summary

★★★★ Five minutes with a single instruction — **raise the objections yourself, before they do** —
supported by the best sales call he says he has ever been on (told against himself), a coaching
story with a **price-anchoring script** he dictates verbatim, and a closing rule that is the
sharpest version of it in the corpus: **do not explain.**

## Key claims (2025-12-11)

### ★★★★ The answer to "how do I get ahead of objections?"

> *"**Do you know what the objections are? The biggest tip is to know what the objections are.**"*

### ★★★★★ The best sales call he's ever been on

Told, pointedly, as something done *to* him: *"one of the best sales calls I've ever been a
**victim** to."*

**The structure, step by step:**

1. **Ask permission first** — *"'So Chris, are you aware of what we do and how we do what we do?'
   'No — this is a sales call.' '**Do you mind if I take five to ten minutes to tell you how we do
   what we do?**' '**I would love for you to.**'"*
2. **Then say every objection out loud, before he can:**

> ★★★★★ *"'**Let me guess. You've hired sales reps in the past and it costs a lot of money. You're
> paying every single month and it's three to four to five months before you even see the results.
> And whatever results you get, there's no transparency about where those leads came from, whether
> they did anything or nothing. Am I right?**'"*

> ★★★★★ **His reaction:** *"**At that point, I was already in. Let me just take the money —
> because you already know everything I think about you right now.** I think it's going to take
> too long, I think it's going to cost too much money, and then **I'm not even sure what you do
> works, because it's kind of all magic.** **When they said that, I knew they knew who we
> were.**"*

> ⚠️★★★★ **And the coda, which he does not soften:** *"**Their problem was they could not deliver
> on the results. So we did hire them for a period of time — and then we fired them. But it was
> the best sales call I've ever been on.**"* ✅ He deliberately separates **the quality of the
> sale** from **the quality of the delivery**, and keeps his praise for the first while stating
> the second.

★★★ **He kept the artefact:** *"**the call was recorded. I had the transcript somewhere.**"*

### ★★★★★ Why pre-empting works

> ★★★★★ *"**Does knowing what the objections are beforehand give you the upper hand? 100% yes.
> Because it communicates to the other person: this is not the first time you've done this. In
> fact, you've done it so many times, you already know what I'm going to say. How's that for
> building rapport and trust?**"*

> ★★★★ **The diagnosis:** *"**The problem is everybody here doesn't think about what those
> objections are — and is too scared to bring them up.**"*

### ★★★★★ The coaching story — anchoring the price before they shop it

**The situation:** a client losing every job to a cheaper competitor who *"steals my language,
steals how I talk about my process, steals everything."*

**His first question is diagnostic, not tactical:** *"'**Do you believe this person can do what
you do?**' She goes: '**Absolutely not. They just steal the language. They cannot deliver.**'"* →
*"**Okay. So what have you done?**" → "I don't know, what am I supposed to do?"* →

> ★★★★★ *"**You know whose problem this is? It's your problem.**"*

**The script he dictates, verbatim:**

> ★★★★★ *"'Oh, you want to do brand design, strategy, web thing? Okay, great. **I just want to let
> you know something: I'm going to be two to three times what anybody else is going to charge you.
> In fact, there's going to be another person who you're most likely going to be talking to, who's
> going to give you a lower price. And if you get what you need from this person, we don't need to
> have a conversation. You don't need to call me. Only call me if you don't feel confident that
> they're going to do a good job for you.**'"*

**Then the second move — arm the buyer:**

> ★★★★★ *"'**Here's what you need to do as an expert. I want you to ask anybody that you talk to
> these three questions, so that you're an informed buyer: what evidence do you have that what
> you're saying has worked, and that you've done this before?**' … **She's teaching the client how
> to ask for proof.**"*

★★★★ **And the beat that makes the story worth keeping** — it fails first: *"she does exactly what
I tell her to do. **Clients go away.** And she's like: '**Chris, I just said goodbye to another
client. Thanks a lot.**' I said: '**Okay, I'm sorry. It didn't work. Keep trying it and see what
happens.**' **Three days later, that same client calls back** and she said: '**You're right. I
didn't have a good feeling.**'"*

> *"**Now she doesn't have to lower her price, and she's going to get the client — because she
> just brought up the objections.**"*

### ★★★★★ Do not explain

The closing rule, and the most emphatic statement of it anywhere in the corpus:

> ★★★★★ *"**Speak confidently. Hold yourself high. You don't need to explain. Whenever possible,
> do not explain anything. I don't get into the proof. Try not to prove anything.**"*

> ★★★★ **When proof is allowed:** *"**the time in which you prove is when they ask you: 'tell me
> the three clients you've worked with.' Don't tell them ahead of time. Save that. That's your
> ammunition.**"*

**The anti-example, in the voice of the person doing it wrong:** *"'Why does it cost this much?'
'**Well, we have four team members, I have two offices — you know how rates are in Sydney, so
expensive.**' **That's what you do. You've just proved yourself. Don't do that.**"*

**The replacements, three of them:**

| Their question | His answer |
|---|---|
| *"Why does it cost that much?"* | ★★★★★ *"**That's what it costs.**"* |
| *(pressed)* | *"**That's what it costs to work with people like me. That's why my customers pay me.**"* |
| *"Why is it like that?"* | *"**Policy. We don't do that. Just policy.**"* |

> *"**Don't explain.**"*
