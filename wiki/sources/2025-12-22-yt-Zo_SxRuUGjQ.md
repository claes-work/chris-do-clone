### ★★★★★ Digital-first publishing, and his stated reason

`digital.?first` returned **0**.

> ★★★★★ *"**I thought of a different way of doing a book.** I'll do a **digital workbook** first —
> it's a little bit different than a book because **you can actually write in it, work through a
> problem**… I kind of think of it as **a workshop in a book format.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"The reason why it's **digital first** is because you can download it, you can work on it,
> but **that gives me the opportunity to update it as I go to see if I can't make it better. So once
> I feel like it's perfect, then I will go to press — and that way we don't waste a lot of paper.**"*

> ★★★★ **The constraint he is designing around, named explicitly:** *"some of you who have published
> a book before, I understand the stress that goes into making a book, and that **you can't unpublish
> a book.**"*

✅ **This is his own iterate-before-committing doctrine applied to himself**, and it is the rare case
where the corpus can watch him take his own advice on a product of his own — with the irreversibility
of print named as the reason.

|---|---|

| Print pre-credit | **$20 voucher** toward the print edition for launch-day digital buyers |

✅ **Worth keeping because he teaches pricing.** This is him **anchoring against a stated retail

### ★★★★ The version and edition policy — stated as a transparency point

> ★★★★ *"If you buy the digital copy, **you'll get all VERSION ONE upgrades for free.** And I have to
> say this because **it's not always that forever you will get the updates forever**… because **I plan
> on releasing edition two and three of this book.** This is a **foundational level**, and there's
> probably more advanced components… **so there will be book two and three as a part of the series,
> not just as an update. I just want to be fully transparent.**"*

⚠️ **Note the distinction he draws**: *upgrades within v1* are free; *editions 2 and 3* are separate
products in a **series**. He raises this unprompted and calls it transparency — a **product-ladder
decision**, and evidence of the [[../topics/pricing/pricing|pricing]] doctrine applied to his own
catalogue.
