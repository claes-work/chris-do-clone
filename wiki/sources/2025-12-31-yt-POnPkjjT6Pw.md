# The only content you need to post if you want clients (2025-12-31)

> **Attribution:** a live workshop, recorded at **Adobe MAX** (he refers to the venue twice).
> Several audience members participate; their lines are marked `[ROOM]` and are **not** persona
> data. First names as heard are used only where needed to follow the exchange.
>
> ⚠️ **Publication date ≠ recording date.** The file is dated by publication per repo convention;
> the room is an Adobe MAX audience, so the recording is earlier.

## Summary

★★★★★ A complete, named system — **the Content-to-Clients Blueprint** — taught end to end:
**ICP → a diagnostic quiz that acts as a filter → a Goldilocks sort into yes/no/maybe → and
content built from the buyer's stuck moment**, tested against one criterion: **do they say
"that's me"?** It ends, at a conference for visual tools, with an argument for **writing**.

## Key claims (2025-12-31)

### ★★★★ The problem it solves

> *"**Most of us will make content with no strategy. We just spray and pray. Most of you guys
> are guilty of that.** So **you don't know what their pain points or challenges are.** You're
> making content that might be valuable to someone, **but it's not with a good strategy.**"*

**The target:** *"content that's **aligned with what you do**, that **establishes your
expertise**, and also **creates opportunities for you.**"*

### ★★★ Step one — the ICP, and an AI shortcut

> *"**We struggled mightily through two sessions to come up with your ICP.**"* → and for anyone
> without one: *"**just ask ChatGPT how to create an ICP — and then ask it to ask you questions.**
> And then fill it out and see if it comes up with something good."*

✅ Consistent with his stated AI position elsewhere: **use it to interrogate you**, not to produce
the answer.

### ★★★★★ Step two — the diagnostic quiz is a filter

**The headlines he models, live:** *"Find out what's really holding your business back"* ·
*"Why your Facebook ads are failing"* · *"Discover your biggest growth blocker… and how to fix
it"* · *"Take this 60-second assessment to see what stage you're in and what to do next."*

> ★★★★★ **The purpose, which is not lead generation:** *"**Not everyone who consumes our content
> is qualified as needing our services. We have to separate the potential people versus just
> general interest.** … **Love you for following me, love you for engaging — but you're not my
> customer. And that's totally cool. When I do something, I want it to be for my customer.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**The quiz is a filter.**"*

★★★ **Why people take them:** *"**people love taking diagnostic quizzes because it's all about
you**"* — with the Myers-Briggs aside, which is also a self-disclosure: *"at some point it becomes
really painful. Like — **god dang it, how many ways you want to ask me if I don't like people or
not? I do not like people. Stay away from people. Clearly, I'm an I.**"*

★★★★ **Specificity is the whole game.** Coaching a participant's headline: *"**that's not bad.
They can use stronger language.** … If I was trying to attract [her] into my program — **which I
don't have one** — I would say: '**Discover your biggest growth blocker attracting the dream
advertising clients… and how to fix it.**' And you're like — **what?**"* → *"**the more specific I
can get the language, where you say 'that's me' — it works.**"*

### ★★★★★ Step three — Goldilocks: sort into yes, no, maybe

> *"This is like Goldilocks and the three bears. The porridge is too hot, too cold, and just
> right."*

| | What it means | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes** | qualified now | *"**Let's get on a call. Jump on a webinar. I want to invite you to this other program.**"* |
| **No** | *"**you can't afford me; what you want I can't deliver; your timeline is too long**"* | *"**you send them a resource — what to do so that eventually you can become a yes.**" If they're stuck on business basics and you teach advanced strategies, **teach them the business basics.**"* |
| **Maybe** | ★★★★★ *"**The maybes in sales waste all your time.**"* | **not a call** — *"a **multi-day email sequence** to slowly nurture them, or a multi-week or multi-month sequence… '**We're not a perfect fit yet. I'm going to give you some resources over time.**'"* |

> ★★★★ **The constraint that makes it work:** *"**this is repeatable, scalable infinitely. So it's
> got to be a digital thing. It could be a Loom recording, a PDF, a template, a resource** —
> something they'd find valuable **that would get them back into the cycle when they take the test
> again in three, six, nine months.**"*

> ⚠️★★★★ **And the honest limit he volunteers:** *"**do nos always become maybes? No. There's a
> reason why they're no. They're just dead end. They're stuck. They'll never get out of their own
> way. We know people like this. Some of them are related to us.**"*

### ★★★★★ The test: "that's me"

> ★★★★★ *"**We want to create content that gets your potential buyer to say: 'That's me. You're
> speaking my language. You're pointing out the things that I would think.'** … **The test is
> always: if they say 'that's me.'**"*

★★★★ **The challenge he puts to the room, with teeth:** *"**Travel in your mind to the last three
to five posts you've done. Be honest with yourself here. Have you ever ONCE created a piece of
content that would elicit a 'that's me' from your core buyer?** … Cuz **if you say yes, I'm going
to look at your feed.**"* — and he does.

### ★★★★★ The stuck-moment worksheet

The structure he fills in live with the room. **The running example is his own:** *"**it's 11
o'clock at night and they're rewriting the same email.**"*

| Prompt | What the room produced |
|---|---|
| **Their stuck moment** | *"11 p.m., rewriting the same email"* |
| **What they say out loud** | *"I've tried everything"* · *"Damn, you're talented — you just need to find someone to pay you for one of those talents"* |
| **What they think privately** | *"Maybe I'm not good enough"* · *"Maybe I should give up and start from scratch"* · *"Maybe I should go back to school"* |
| **What they think the problem is** | *"I just need more followers"* · *"there aren't enough jobs"* · *"people don't get it"* · *"the market is too saturated"* · *"AI will take my job"* |
| **What the real problem actually is** | *"**they don't know how to communicate their value**"* |
| **The emotion** | frustration → self-doubt — and pushed harder: confusion → **despair** → **hopeless** |
| **If nothing changes** | *"they burn out"* → *"I stay at this job I don't like and I'm unfulfilled"* → ★★★★★ *"**you hate what you used to love**"* → *"you might start **phoning it in**"* |
| **What they wish someone told them** | ★★★★★ *"**You're not crazy. It's just your strategy that's off.**"* |

★★★★ **How he pushes for the real emotion**, twice: *"**You feel confused. Is there a stronger
emotion?**"* → *"despair"* → *"**That's a good word. Do you feel bitter?**"* → *"not yet"* →
*"**Okay, not yet. You're so young.**"* And on consequences: *"**What's more extreme? Keep going
down the logic.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The recap, in his words:** *"**create content that highlights a problem in the voice and
> attitude and opinions of the people we're trying to attract — such that by consuming the
> content, they say to themselves, out loud or in their own minds: 'that's me. I feel so seen
> right now.' If you can do that, you can move them towards the solution.**"*

### ★★★★★ The empathy-based hook

> ★★★★ *"**You know that moment when…**"* · *"**If you've ever experienced this thing…**"* ·
> *"**Most of my clients — [this type of person] — think this…**"* → **then write your line.**

### ★★★★★ "It's not you, it's the packaging"

A participant identifies as a *"slashie"* — a multi-hyphenate — and Chris builds her offer in
front of the room:

> ★★★★★ *"**I think if you just package all of your greatness into something easier for people to
> understand, you have better opportunities.**"* → *"**It's not you, it's the packaging. Or
> positioning.**"* → and the fake offer to demonstrate the shape: *"'**And that's why I have a
> seven-step program to teach you how to package yourself, so they're chasing you down.**'"*

> ★★★★★ **What everyone actually wants, in one line:** *"**We want to be understood, appreciated,
> and a plan to get us out from A to B.**"*

★★★★★ **And an extraordinary piece of listening.** When she says *"that resonates with me"*, he
reads her voice rather than her words:

> *"**See the way she said that — she didn't go high pitch on me. She went lower register.**"* →
> *"**We're not going to ask her to change. We're just going to package it differently. I think
> she can hear that.**"*

★★★★ **The reveal, which he does not hide:** *"**You know what the irony of it is? I didn't mean
to trick you — I just do it all the time naturally.**"*

> ⚠️★★★★ **He holds his position while making room for hers.** He states plainly: *"**they are a
> generalist. Isn't the solution to be a specialist? That would be my answer — and I can argue
> for that all day long. But maybe you and the slashies have a different point of view.**"* Then
> lands on: *"**The solution is for you to pretend to be a specialist. So all of you slashies,
> there's nothing wrong with you — you just have to pretend to be a specialist.**"* — and, drily:
> *"**all the specialists are just counting their money anyway. They're like: this is not for
> me.**"*

### ★★★★★ The closing — words at a visual conference

> ★★★★★ *"I know it's really weird for me to say this at Adobe MAX — this is a visual audience,
> they make visual tools. **An overlooked skill of the 21st century is writing. Because words
> shape worlds.** **Your ability to craft language will determine how people understand you — or
> if they think you understand them.** But it also opens doors in your mind, or closes doors.
> **So language is very, very powerful. More powerful than we think. All of your experiences, all
> your memories, are locked away with words.**"*

> *"**Your words really do matter. That's why I do battle with people about their words.**"*
