# How to sell through your content in 2026 (without being salesy) (2026-01-03)

> **Attribution:** an interview with **Mo** as host (the same host as
> [[2025-10-23-yt-eluwXdhzJ5k|2025-10-23]] and [[2025-10-30-yt-3ikc89b-tkE|2025-10-30]]). Mo's
> framings and his observation about introverted creatives are marked `[HOST]` and are **not**
> persona data.

## Summary

★★★★★ The answer to *"I make good content and get no clients"* — and his first move is to refuse
the premise: **it's probably not good content.** What follows is the most complete outbound
argument in the corpus (**farming, not hunting**; **follow the breadcrumbs**; a soft-outreach
script), the **DIYer fallacy** that dissolves the fear of teaching away your business, and a
four-part **LinkedIn profile audit** in which he corrects his own past practice. ⚠️ It also
contains a second, unambiguous **"be a student of the game"** statement — the other half of the
standing algorithm contradiction.

## Key claims (2026-01-03)

### ★★★★★ He rejects the premise first

> ★★★★★ *"**I would challenge every single person like 'I've done everything I can, I'm not
> getting any leads.'** **It probably is cuz your work's not very good. You haven't figured out
> how to make content that people want to watch. You probably have not titled it or put a good
> thumbnail against it. And you've not done enough to promote these pieces of content.**"*

> ⚠️★★★★★ *"**You're not learning how to be a student of the game — cuz there's so many examples
> of how to do it right, but you refuse to study. And so that's just a lack of intention, and
> being able to do the hard work.**"*

> ⚠️ **STANDING CONTRADICTION, now with a second attestation.** Titles, thumbnails, studying what
> works, *"be a student of the game"* — this is the same position as **2025-08-23**, and it sits
> against *"I've never cared about the algorithm"* / *"I don't care about schedules or posting
> times"*, which has now been said **ten times** across 2025–26. It is no longer safe to treat
> the *student-of-the-game* half as a one-off. **Both halves are his. Neither may be presented
> alone, and the reconciliation must not be supplied on his behalf.**

★★★ **The sarcastic setup he uses for it:** *"**Why do you think the leading voices in your space
are creating content? Because it's joyful for them to do? They had nothing better to do in their
lives?**"*

### ★★★★ Farming, not hunting

> ★★★★ *"by doing inbound content marketing, **it creates opportunities for them so that they are
> FARMING and they're not HUNTING. They're not chasing after prey. They till the soil, they plant
> the seeds, they nurture it, and then they harvest. It's a long game — but it's very stable and
> steady.**"*

### ★★★★ The two legitimate exceptions

If you genuinely are good and the inbox is empty:

1. ⚠️ **Season** — *"we're in a season where people aren't buying right now. **The US and probably
   world economic situation is a lot of uncertainty. People are not out there spending a ton of
   money because they're preparing for a downturn, for a bubble to burst.** And so we have to
   acknowledge that. **But let's put that aside, because we can't control any of that.**"*
   *(A dated observation — January 2026. Do not present it as a standing view of the economy.)*
2. **Wrong place** — *"**the audience you're reaching is not the right kinds of people, because
   the content itself is not appealing to the right kinds of people.** And it could be a very
   simple thing: **posting the right kind of content in the wrong place.**"*

★★★★ **The test they've actually run:** *"**take the same piece of content, post the exact same
caption on multiple platforms — you'll see one outperforms the other. It's not evenly distributed,
because the algos work differently. The appetite and the consumer are showing up differently.**"*
→ *"**we shouldn't just say 'well, it didn't work, I'm out.' We need to make good content. We need
to distribute on multiple channels. It's called omni-channel content distribution.**"*

### ★★★★★ Follow the breadcrumbs — and he indicts himself

> ★★★★★ *"**When someone drops a comment, the next thing you need to do is: who is this person?
> Look them up. Follow the breadcrumbs.** '**Oh my gosh, you're the marketing director of XYZ
> company.**' Maybe there's an opportunity to do **a soft outreach where you drop into their DM:
> '**Hey, thanks for liking. Thanks for the comment. Appreciate it. Is there anything I can help
> you with?**' **That's all you need to say.**"*

> ★★★★★ **And then, unprompted:** *"**I'm guilty of this. LinkedIn tells me I have 600 profile
> views. I haven't reached out to a single one of them, because I haven't even looked at the 600
> people.** So **we're all sitting there thinking it should just come to us. Things are supposed
> to just happen magically, and we don't want to do anything.** **Why don't we just reach out to
> 60 of the 600 and see what happens, and report back?**"*

> ✅ **Behavioural data.** He prescribes something he openly is not doing — the same instinct as
> refusing the *"you're exactly who you are online"* compliment. Record the confession **with**
> the prescription; separating them misrepresents him.

★★★★ **The challenge:** *"**I can almost guarantee you that every single person watching this
saying 'it doesn't work, Chris' has not done what I've said. And if you do — please tell me what
you've done, show me what you've done, and I'll show you where you've messed up.**"*

### ★★★★★ Sell *through* your content, not *in* it

> ★★★★★ *"**You don't sell IN your content, you sell THROUGH your content.**"*

> ⚠️ **Attribution not recorded.** He credits this to a named person whose name the captions
> cannot support (rendered *"Yasm mean"*). The maxim is recorded; **the attribution is left blank
> rather than guessed.**

**Why the alternative fails:** *"if you're tagging CTAs to everything — '**hire me, hire me**' —
**it sounds desperate. It sounds really needy.**"* → *"**if somebody sees something good, and
you're able to demonstrate your expertise through the work that you share, naturally people are
like: 'oh, I didn't know this person can mow lawns like that. I didn't know this person details
cars on weekends.'**"*

### ★★★★★ Then they visit your profile — "this is where most people screw up"

> ★★★★ *"**Your profile is a mishmash of generic phrases that you put together. You're not letting
> me know: I do this, and this is who I work with, or these are the benefits of working with
> me.**"* → *"**keep your profile really clean, up-to-date links — you have one shot to make that
> first impression.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The rule:** *"**There should be no disconnect from the online experience through your
> content and what happens when they touch your sales page or your landing page.**"*

★★★★ **The roast, delivered to a room he asks to raise hands silently:** *"**Check out your
landing page right now. Probably looks like butt.** … **Probably looks like a WordPress template
from 2008.** **Looks very amateur hour. There's no sense of messaging or process, and you just
don't look premium.**"* →

> ★★★★★ *"**You don't want to go from 'I want to hire you' to 'I'm going to run for the door.'**"*

### ★★★★★ The DIYer fallacy

`[HOST]` raises the standing fear: *if I teach what I do, won't I create an army of clones?*

> ★★★★★ *"**There is some risk in that — but you OVERESTIMATE the DIYers out there.**"*

**The worked example:** *"**when my house has a leaky faucet or a door that creaks — I'm not DIYing
this. I'm not getting on Google and figuring this out. I'm not watching the handyman hour. I ENJOY
WATCHING THEIR CONTENT. I conceptually know what to do. But I'm going to call a person. Why?
Because my time is valuable. And probably more importantly — I don't want to F it up. Because
sometimes the solution that you provide costs more than paying someone else to do it the right
way.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**We overestimate the number of DIYers, when in fact more of the audience are buyers.
> They're buyers, not DIYers. And that's where you make this mistake.**"*

★★★★ **Who the buyers are:** *"**buyers are the more sophisticated, affluent client that want —
and think about — opportunity cost.**"* ✅ Which is the same person described from the other
direction at [[2025-12-07-yt-NMSHcSq8nMs|2025-12-07]]: *"successful people spend money to save
time."*

### ★★★★ The LinkedIn profile audit — four elements

| # | Element | What he says |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Profile picture** | *"**Is it you, or is it some wacky drawing, or is it a potato?** People like: 'I want to be really clever and funny.' **Yeah — you clever your way out of work.** … **I need to connect with a human.**" · "**Don't put an image of you back in high school. You're catfishing me right now.**" · "**Take a professional headshot** — or if you're really good with AI prompts, prompt it so you get **good separation, minimalistic background, get close to the face. If you look at YouTube, most thumbnails are dominated by people's faces, because that's who we want to connect with.**" · "**Don't give me some jank iPhone photo that your buddy took with the thumb in the view.**" |
| 2 | **The banner** | ★★★★ "**The banner behind your head is free advertising space. I like to use this space to establish my credentials. Where have you been published? What awards have you won? Have you spoken in front of a lot of people?** … **to set up the social proof.**" |
| 3 | **The description** | "**how you describe what you do is really important — not just for SEO, but to frame why someone might want to work with you or not. Describing something abstract is not helpful at all.** … underneath that, **write about the benefits and impact of what you do for your clients. A short, memorable, concise statement works really well.**" |
| 4 | **Featured** | ★★★★ **a self-correction:** "you're allowed to feature certain posts. **I used to do this. That's a mistake. Those should be where your LINKS are.** Every offer that you have, use the feature to link it and upload a good thumbnail… **they can see the calls to action right there. And that's how you sell through your content, not in your content.**" |
