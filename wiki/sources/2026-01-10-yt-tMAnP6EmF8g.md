# The worst way to tell any story (and everyone does it) (2026-01-10)

> **Attribution:** a **Content Lab hot seat** with a professional violinist. Her lines are marked
> `[MEMBER]` and are **not** persona data.
>
> ⚠️ **Third-party privacy.** The member's story involves a childhood connected to a religious
> movement her parents belonged to. She has published that material herself and it is the subject
> of the coaching, so the **one line Chris uses as a teaching example** is retained. Everything
> else about her family and origins is **deliberately not reproduced here** — this page records
> *his craft instruction*, not her biography.

## Summary

★★★★★ The clearest statement of his **story structure** teaching in the corpus, and it names the
error in the title: **linear narration.** The fixes are specific — **begin at the point of
greatest conflict**, replace *"and then"* with *"but"*, keep telling people **what's at stake**,
and **cut everything that doesn't land the plane.** It closes with a named, complete
experimentation framework — **the 30-30-30 plan** — that appears nowhere else.

## Key claims (2026-01-10)

### ★★★★ Two opening moves worth noting on their own

**He slows her down with her own vocabulary:** *"**Whoa, hold on. Now you're going too fast. It's
like we have everybody from staccato to now speed demon.**"*

★★★★ **And he refuses to let her hedge her own level:** *"**What calibre of musician are you?**"*
→ she qualifies → *"**God, just say it.**"* → *"I'm world-class."* → *"**World-class. That's all I
want to hear.**"*

★★★ **Then he finds the hook in the raw material immediately**, turning her own most arresting
detail into a positioning line — *"the [X] of world-class musicians — **that's your intro.**"*

### ★★★★★ The diagnosis: you're telling it in a straight line

> ★★★★★ *"**You have a wild, crazy, interesting story — but you're telling the story in a LINEAR
> way**, which is mostly what people do in a narration style."*

### ★★★★★ The Moth, and what's actually inside it

> *"**Do you listen to The Moth Radio Hour?**"* → *"**there's a reason why [people] are telling
> you to listen to it. Cuz they know how to tell stories.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The observation that makes the recommendation useful:** *"here's the thing that
> surprised me but doesn't surprise me. **Almost all the ones that have me in tears — listening
> in the driveway while I'm still sitting at home — it's because they're WRITERS. Duh. Writers
> know how to craft a story.**"*

→ **The prescription:** *"**when you're doing top of dome, it can be a little sloppy and
unstructured. So I would love for you to sit down and try and WRITE your story.**"* ✅ The same
instruction as the writing workshop at [[2025-10-25-yt-Z0wnvRTWYmo|2025-10-25]], arrived at from
a different direction.

### ★★★★★ Begin at the point of greatest conflict

> ★★★★★ *"**We don't begin the story at the beginning. We begin it at the point of greatest
> conflict.**"*

**His live example, using her own material:** *"'**Some of you don't know this, but I was labelled
as the Antichrist.**' Let's just say. They're like — **what?** **Okay. And then you reverse
engineer, and you tell just parts of the story.**"*

### ★★★★★ "And then, and then" is not a story

> ★★★★★ *"**You have to keep telling people what's at stake. Cuz your story is 'and then, and
> then, and then' — which is not that interesting. Instead, if it's 'BUT' or 'INSTEAD OF' —
> there's twists and turns.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**We have to give them enough to figure out what's going to happen, and then create
> tension around: no — if this didn't happen, this happened instead. It's those little diversions,
> the turns in the road, the FORKS IN THE ROAD, that make a story really interesting. And you have
> to set up an expectation, and then DISRUPT it.**"*

★★★★ **What a told story needs beyond structure:** *"**great stories have other senses, sensory
detail. It's rich in dialogue, and you're painting the scene. You need to ACT OUT those
moments.**"* → *"**when you're just saying it to camera like that — although it could be the
world's most interesting story — you're not giving it the best chance to succeed.**"*

### ★★★★★ The exercise, and the editing rule

> ★★★★★ *"**What are the top three turning points in your life?** … you should be able to quickly
> scan your memory bank and say: there's one, there's two, there's three. And then write about it
> like: **what was it that flipped the switch for me?**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**And if you know how the story ends, you'll know how to begin — and you'll know what
> parts to include and what parts to exclude.**"* ✅ **A second attestation of "start at the end"**
> ([[2025-10-25-yt-Z0wnvRTWYmo|2025-10-25]]), now applied to spoken story rather than written.

> ★★★★ *"You could tell your story in multiple parts. **Act one — how does this story end? And
> then only tell the parts that build up to that ending. Otherwise it's just extra
> information.**"*

**His verdict on her draft, and note the metaphor:** *"there's a lot for me to figure out, and
**not all of that is relevant to LANDING THE PLANE.**"* → *"**That's my feedback for your story.
It's just too much for me to consume, and it's too much information for me to remember.**"*

### ★★★★★ How comedians do it

> *"**You'll see that master storytellers and comedians usually will tell you something — but they
> only tell you the PARTS.**"*

He recounts a bit — flagging that he cannot recall the comedian's name — about someone concealing
lactose intolerance on a first date, and draws the lesson from the omissions:

> ★★★★★ *"**He's setting up only the parts that make sense. He didn't tell you what she looked
> like, where they were, what they ate.** … **So a lot of editing. So you think: where does this
> story end, and how do I begin it with a maximum amount of tension? And once you have them
> hooked, they can't get off.**"*

**The assembly instruction:** *"**figure out how many parts of the story you want to tell, and
break it into bite-size pieces. Make each part work — and then when you glue them all together,
they should work really well.**"*

### ★★★★★ Pattern interrupt, and the production hierarchy

**Why her fight-venue performance video worked**, analysed cold: *"**I do not expect a
half-Black, half-Asian woman to come in with a violin right before a guy… to punch each other in
the face. That is such an unusual PATTERN INTERRUPT — no wonder it has so many views.**"*

> ★★★★★ **And the hierarchy, stated compactly:** *"the other part is **it was produced well** —
> good camera, good exposure, good movement. **All things being equal, better production
> outperforms sloppy production. BETTER CONCEPT BEATS BETTER PRODUCTION — but all things being
> equal.**"*

★★★★ **The craft note for a musician**, which is really an attention note: *"the one where I
watched you performing on stage — **the camera's not even on you. It's low quality, and I hear
the other instruments sometimes more than I hear your instrument.** … **I want you to perform when
you're the focus of attention, when your audio is the cleanest and clearest, so that I can just
hear you.**"* — with the exception granted: *"**unless you're busking — where the environment IS
the story, not the instrument itself.**"*

★★★ **Why genre-smashing works:** *"**those are things that get a lot of attention because we've
not heard it that way before. Your interpretation of it is the thing that excites people.**"*

### ★★★★★ Finding the bio in a throwaway line

`[MEMBER]`, in passing: *"I play drums terribly."*

> ★★★★★ *"**That's probably your bio. 'I play drums terribly. You should listen to me on the
> violin.' That's a pattern interrupt, by the way.**"* — and then the video: *"**you could
> literally just play it out… you're like: 'You did terrible.' 'Hold on, hold on, let me do this
> other thing.'**"*

★★★ **With the disclaimer he attaches to his own idea:** *"**I don't know if this is a good idea…
just don't hire me to do this.**"*

### ★★★★ "You need a good editor" — and constraint

> ★★★★ *"**You sometimes may have an abundance of ideas, and it's overwhelming for people. You
> need a good editor** — and I don't mean a person cutting your videos, but **a person who says:
> you need to just focus in on this. Make it about that.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**You have to show constraint. It's like drinking from a fire hydrant — it's just too
> much.**"*

★★★ **And the encouragement he pairs with it:** *"**you're kind of cooking weird, and I like that
about you. So you've got to lean into that.**"*

### ★★★★★ The 30-30-30 plan

> *"When I do content workshops, I tell people to do **the 30-30-30 plan. It's a 90-day plan.**"*

| Phase | Instruction |
|---|---|
| **Days 1–30** | ★★★★★ *"**No rules. Do whatever the hell you want. No results — who even cares? Just experiment, have fun. Do 30 experiments. Every day do a weird thing.** And **don't even look at the results. FORBID yourself from looking at the results, because they'll be disheartening sometimes. Do it as a form of exercise.**"* |
| **Days 31–60** | *"**look at the first 30 days and see: what are the two or three things that worked the best AND gave me the most joy? And see if you can repeat that.**"* → *"**now we're having an informed decision, and we're narrowing it down.**"* — *"you don't have to create 30 days then. You probably create one every three or four days."* |
| **Days 61–90** | *"**I think I found something. Refine, refine, refine.**"* |

> ★★★★ **The stated goal:** *"**that usually gets you to something that's going to both give you
> joy but also build an audience for you.**"*

> ⚠️ **Note for the algorithm contradiction.** *"Forbid yourself from looking at the results"* is
> the **eleventh** metrics-indifference statement — but here it is **tactical and reasoned**
> (results contaminate the experiments), not a blanket indifference, and it is time-boxed to
> thirty days. That is a materially better articulation than *"I've never cared about the
> algorithm"*, and it sits days after the *"student of the game"* statement at
> [[2026-01-03-yt-meVstiZhvqA|2026-01-03]]. **Any bridge between the two halves is the curator's
> reading, not his — he never connects them.**

### ★★★ Warmth, in closing

> *"**I wish you well. Continue being the violent violinist — just not towards me.**"*
