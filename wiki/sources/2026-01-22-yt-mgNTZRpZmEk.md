# Why your customers don't buy from you (2026-01-22)

> **Attribution:** a live workshop. One participant builds a profile with him throughout (he
> addresses him as *"son"*, colloquially); others contribute. Their lines are marked `[ROOM]` and
> are **not** persona data. Participant names as heard are used only to follow the exchange.

## Summary

★★★★★ A working session that produces **three named frameworks the corpus does not have** —
**"the market is bigger than your target"**, **TGO** (tasks → obstacles → opportunity), and
**AIDA + C**, his customer-journey hack. Between them sits the practical move for anyone with no
ICP at all: **steal someone else's customers**, and a distinction that does most of the work —
**what they need is external; what they want is emotional.**

## Key claims (2026-01-22)

### ★★★★★ The market is bigger than your target

> ★★★★★ *"**This is your target. And this is the market. And what you need to understand is: the
> market is bigger than your target. The market is bigger than your target. You're going to have
> to say that a thousand times.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**But when we aim out here, we hit zero people. Because when you aim for her, you'll get
> people a little older, a little younger. You'll get guys. You get all kinds of people.** I'm
> telling you right now — **if we can just focus on one specific target, your business will
> 2x.**"*

✅ Restated at the end via the funnel: *"**this has to appeal to lots of people — because if you
don't appeal to a lot of people, just through attrition, this gets really, really small really
fast.**"* — i.e. **aim narrow precisely because the spill is wide.**

### ★★★★ The three questions on every call

> ★★★★ *"**Everybody who reaches out to you has a problem. No one's super happy with everything and
> then they call you for some strange reason.** The question on that call is: **① Do you have a
> problem I can solve? ② Are you cool enough that I want to work with you? ③ And do you have enough
> money to respect the value I'm going to generate for you?** **Those are just the big three
> questions.**"*

★★★ **The rule underneath it:** *"**the number one rule in the client service business: know thy
customer.**"*

### ★★★★★ No ICP? Steal someone else's customers

> *"**If you don't know who your ideal customer profile [is], it's a first sign of a problem.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**But what we can do is look at someone who you're like: 'I'd like to steal their
> customers.' Who the hell are their customers?** And that works really well. **So if you're a
> startup, you have no customers — we understand that.** So you look at: **I would really love to
> take some market share away from them.** … **Where do they buy from? Let's try and understand
> that. And then we reverse engineer.**"*

> ★★★★ **And the warning that goes with all ICP work:** *"**I want to warn you all — when you build
> your ideal customer profile, sometimes you just make them who you WANT them to be, and they're
> not even real.**"*

### ★★★★ The live build — and two things he does while building it

They construct a New York realtor: ~\$450–500k a year, luxury brand affinities, a Porsche Cayenne,
Pilates and yoga, meditation and astrology, running, fine dining and travel, Instagram plus
Facebook (*"the older demo. Facebook. I don't get it — but you know, I'm saying I'm not old"*).

★★★★ **He corrects the room's contempt in real time.** A participant describes her interests as
*"this new-agey, like meditation"* →

> ★★★★ *"**Just take the derogatory part out of it.**"*

✅ The same instinct as *"when you understand the kind of crap they've had to deal with, you hate
them a little less"* at [[2025-12-07-yt-NMSHcSq8nMs|2025-12-07]]. **Contempt for the avatar is
treated as a defect in the work, not a private attitude.**

★★★★ **And he insists on a name, for a stated reason:** *"**Now we're going to give her a name. The
name matters, because it tells us her ethnicity a little bit.**"* → they land on a first name, a
surname, and a nickname → *"**You guys get a clear visual in your mind. Once we get to this part,
everything else becomes a lot easier.**"*

### ★★★★★ Needs are external; wants are emotional

> ★★★★★ *"**What does she need? This is usually externally — she's usually asking for something:
> 'can you help me with this?'**"* → *"grow IG followers."*
>
> *"**So what are their internal WANTS? People say 'I want to grow my account.' What's the internal
> EMOTIONAL want? What's the feeling? What do they want?**"* → `[ROOM]`: *"**they want to feel
> important. They want to feel significant, like they mean something in this world — and that they
> have a legacy they can leave behind.**"*

Then the business layer beneath the feeling: *higher-quality leads · 2x profit · scale the team.*

> ★★★★★ **The question most people skip:** *"**When you build your own customer profile, you have
> to be realistic about: what do they FEEL ABOUT PEOPLE LIKE US? When you can understand that,
> you'll sell to them differently. You'll talk to them differently.**"*

★★★★ **And the fears**, which turn out to be about the relationship, not the work: *"being burned,
wasting money, wasting time"* · *"**or maybe not vibing with you — there's a vibe check**"* ·
*"**there's a fear of: they're going to make me do something I don't want to do. They're going to
make me dance on TikTok.**"*

### ★★★★★ TGO — tasks, obstacles, opportunity

> **Credited:** *"**I learned this from one of my friends, Nicole Johnson, who works in user
> experience design. She told me about TGO. I'm like — I love TGO.**"*

> ★★★ **What a framework is, in his terms:** *"**frameworks are tools you can use — mental models
> you can use to solve problems.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**Tasks are the jobs that she has to do from morning to night — hence the sun and the
> moon.** And then we look at **the obstacles that are preventing them from doing that.** And then
> **the opportunity is: what could we do to make their lives easier? It's really that simple.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**Every obstacle is an opportunity wrapped in disguise.**"*

**The live run** produced ordinary obstacles (commute, what to wear, exhaustion, meal prep, waking
the kids) and two useful ones:

- **Email:** *"**log jam. Too many emails to go through. Sorting emails — what's important?**"* —
  with a personal aside: *"**I'm almost scared to open email. For me it's like a psychological
  obstacle.**"*
- **Social:** *"looking for ideas, getting inspired — **probably a little jealousy or envy of
  people who are just crushing. So there's an emotion there.**"*

> ★★★★ **The scoping discipline, which is the real lesson:** *"you're going to give her a strategy
> on how to sort and get through those emails — **you would do that if you were a virtual
> assistant company. But he's trying to sell personal branding. So he's not going to want to get
> into her emails.**"* ✅ **Pick the obstacle that is in your lane.**

**What he lands on instead:** a curated-inspiration email with breakdowns — *"**this is good, this
is why it went viral, and this is how you can do this in under 60 seconds a day**"* — plus a
**personalised audit gated behind a quiz**: *"**if you take this quiz, if you qualify, I'll audit
your account and tell you the four things you need to do.**"* ✅ The quiz-as-filter from
[[2025-12-31-yt-POnPkjjT6Pw|2025-12-31]], reused.

> ★★★★ *"**Now we're looking for a problem — because when we find a good problem, we have a good
> solution.**"*

### ★★★★ Retention beats acquisition

> *"**It's really hard to get a customer. It's easier to keep a customer buying more stuff from
> you. And that's the thing most of us don't figure out. We do one job with one customer, they pay
> us, and they're gone. We don't have a strong relationship with them. When someone else figures
> that out, they'll no longer come to you.**"*

### ★★★★★ AIDA + C — the customer journey he can recall without notes

> *"I just want to share with you **the easiest way I know how to do it. And the way I know how to
> do it, it's so easy because I can remember it without any notes.**"*

> *"If you're familiar with copywriting, there's a rule we use to write headlines. **It's called
> AIDA — awareness, interest, desire, action.** It's quite famous. **We don't know exactly who
> invented it**, but most copywriters and direct-marketing people use it. **All we do is add one
> extra letter: C. And the C stands for CONVERSION.**"*

**The resulting sequence, in his prompts:**

| Stage | The question |
|---|---|
| **A — Awareness** | *"How do they become aware?"* |
| **I — Interest** | *"What makes them interested?"* |
| **D — Desire** | *"Why do they desire?"* |
| **C — Conversion** | *"Why do they convert — meaning, why do they buy?"* |
| **A — Advocacy** | ★★★★★ *"**And then how do they become ADVOCATES for what we do?**"* |

> ★★★★★ *"**Most of us stop here. We get the conversion, we never see them again — it's like: see
> you later.** But **if we can keep them as a long-term customer, our business will grow, the
> relationship would strengthen, and at some point you'll be IRREPLACEABLE. Which is what we all
> want.**"*

### ★★★★★ The awareness ladder, and the content for each rung

> ★★★★★ *"**They're in problem-unaware. Then they become solution-unaware. Then product-unaware.**
> So now: what is the solution to this? **And then YOU are the product.**"*

**Rung 1 — problem-unaware.** *"**Miranda doesn't quite fully understand she has a problem. She has
an inkling of it — but we will sell more if we just talk about the problem in our marketing.**"*
The room supplies it in her voice: *"she's not aware her content is boring"* · *"**nobody cares
about her content**"* · *"I am boring"* · *"not special."* → *"**Now that you're agitating the
problem — why do you become interested?**"*

**The content that moves her:** *"if you're doing these three things, **you're just wasting your
time**"* → *"they see that piece of content, they go check out more content that drives them down
the funnel."*

**Rung 2 — solution-unaware.** The format the participant supplies and Chris endorses:

> ★★★★ **MYTH → EXAMPLE → TRUTH** — *"**here's one thing you believe to be true, which is not.
> Here's an example. And then here's what you need to do instead.**"* → *"**you do enough of those
> pieces of content and they go: who is this smart guy? I need to figure this thing out from
> them.**"*

**Rung 3 — product-unaware.** *"**Now I want to buy something from someone. Why you versus someone
else? Between you and three other people, why would they choose you? What makes you and your
program so unique or different?**"*

★★★★ **And the conversion mechanics:** a webinar or a five-day challenge → *"**what do they
experience such that they're like: I'm ready to buy from you now?**"* → *"**you have to make them
feel EMPOWERED that they can do it**"* → *"**lay out the plan so they don't have to do too much
thinking: 'oh, this guy knows what he's doing. When you join the program, one, two and three will
happen'**"* → *"**we're almost dovetailing into irresistible offer**"* — money-back guarantee, try
before you buy, *"**establish a quick win.**"*
