# How to package your service correctly (2026-01-30)

> **Attribution:** a live offer-design workshop. He builds an offer with a designer who sells
> identity systems, with a second participant playing the buyer. Their lines are marked `[ROOM]`
> and are **not** persona data.

## Summary

★★★★★ An **irresistible offer built live, from nothing**, in four moves: **the dream outcome**,
**perceived certainty**, **time to result**, and **friction removal.** What makes it the best
packaging source in the corpus is that the language work is done in public — *mood board* becomes
**alignment**, *checklist* becomes **road map**, *kickoff* becomes **discovery** — and he ends by
refusing to call the result finished.

## Key claims (2026-01-30)

### ★★★★★ The opening challenge

> ★★★★★ *"**Why do y'all think you need to make something affordable? Who told you you need to do
> that? You want to charge less — but for some reason you want to pay more. There's a problem with
> that, cuz you'll be broke.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**There's a reason why I like to work with very successful people: cuz they think like I
> think. So you have to change your mindset, your outlook, your pricing, your positioning — if you
> want to attract the buyers who also see the same value as you.**"*

### ★★★★ The definition, and the prerequisite

> *"**An irresistible offer is an offer so good people would be silly to say no. But most of you
> can't talk about what you do, [can't] package it in a way that's compelling to people.**"*

**It runs on the ICP:** *"we know what their wants, needs, hopes and fears are. We know their
demographics and their psychographics. **What we need to do is figure out what they want in life.
Cuz if we give them what they want — and they have the means to give us this thing that we care
about, so we can continue doing this for more people — then it starts to march towards becoming an
irresistible offer.**"*

★★★ **A pricing reality check first:** *"anybody here do roughly half a million dollars a year?
**Would you consider spending \$6,000 on an identity system?** … **I talked to attorneys who do at
least this. They start choking around this amount.**"* → `[ROOM]`: *"they're not making enough
money yet — I'd say 750 and up."* → *"**It's kind of arbitrary, but let's just say that.**"* ✅
Consistent with the 10%-of-10% arithmetic at [[2025-12-07-yt-NMSHcSq8nMs|2025-12-07]].

### ★★★★★ Move one — the dream outcome

He starts from the deliverables — *"logo, type, colours, applications, social posts, secondary
elements, patterns, textures"* — and refuses to stop there:

> ★★★★★ *"**Why does anybody want that? What is the result of them having that?**"*

`[ROOM]`: *"Style. Coolness."* → *"**That's real. We understand.**"* → and then he digs past it to
what people will actually admit to wanting:

> ★★★★★ *"**Something you don't have to be embarrassed by, ashamed of. Have an identity that you
> don't feel ashamed to show people.**"* → *"**That's a real result, by the way.**"*

**The final formulation, which is the offer:**

> ★★★★★ *"**An identity system that's reflective of your taste, and values, and identity — how you
> see yourself.**"* — grounded in the observation that makes it sellable: *"**very few people
> actually have an identity that they feel is reflective of their values and their taste.**"*

> *"**Matches your taste. Reflects your values. That's worth something. How many people would want
> that?** … **Proud to show.**"*

★★★★ **The language rule he states while doing it:** *"**Notice I didn't use 'continuing education'
or any weird 'ed-tech integration implementation vision planning mapping strategy' — no.**"*
⚠️ *He labels those words in a way the captions render as "Anglo-Saxon", which does not fit the
vocabulary he is describing; **the label is recorded as spoken and flagged, the rule is clear
regardless**: reject the abstract corporate register in favour of plain, concrete, felt words.*

★★★ Two smaller corrections in passing: *"**Don't use the word branding**"* (when listing
deliverables), and *"**sometimes a feeling can be a very powerful thing that you're selling.**"*

### ★★★★★ Move two — increase the perceived certainty of outcome

> *"**How can you increase the perceived certainty of outcome? Those are a lot of complex
> words.**"*

**First, list the fears** — and note he makes the room say them:

- *"**the identity is just not going to match**"* · *"it's not who we are"*
- *"**waste time and money**"*
- *"**maybe you feel really frustrated. They just don't get me.**"*
- *"it's going to take too long, too many rounds, I don't have time for this"* · *"**I don't know
  what I want.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**First we can identify what they're afraid of, and then we can try to address them.** …
> **Well, you need to VOICE those things.**"* ✅ The pre-empt-the-objections rule from
> [[2025-12-11-yt--_sTrDwoZyM|2025-12-11]], applied to offer design rather than a sales call.

★★★★ **And when the participant dismisses one fear as a cop-out** (*"that one's just cuz it's too
expensive"*), Chris refuses the dismissal:

> ★★★★★ *"**No, no, no. You need to understand: the person who you're selling to has not bought a
> lot of identity systems before. In fact, this might be the one and only — or the second one —
> they've done.**"*

> ★★★★★ **Then the question that produces the rest:** *"**How have they been screwed over before?
> You got to know your customer.**"* → `[ROOM]`: *"didn't work out" · "didn't get the vision
> right" · "**sold a bill of goods**" · "**they talked a good game, did not deliver**" ·
> "non-responsive" · "**ghosted the project**" · "not what I expected."*

**The solutions are the process itself**, renamed — see the language section below — plus:

> ★★★★★ **The exit clause:** *"**I would insert an exit clause. If we do the mood board and you feel
> we're totally off — we'll exit. I'll refund your money.** So that's MBG, money-back guarantee:
> **that you can leave at that point.**"*

> ★★★★ **And why he likes it for the seller, not just the buyer:** *"**I like this even better,
> because if they bail on this part, you're done — and that's happening really fast, so your whole
> pipeline isn't tied up.**"*

### ★★★★★ The language work — three renames, done in public

> ★★★★★ **"Discovery", not "intro" or "kickoff".** *"**Why do we use the word 'discovery' versus
> 'initial call' or 'kickoff'?**"* → `[ROOM]`: *"it feels collaborative"* · *"you're getting them
> to tell you what they want and feel — **this is result-based. The other's just a description.**"*
>
> → ★★★★★ *"**Same meeting, different feeling about it. Who wants to get on an intro call? Who
> wants to get on a kickoff call? Who wants to get on a discovery call? … I want to discover
> something. LANGUAGE MATTERS.**"*

> ★★★★★ **"Alignment", not "mood board".** *"**Mood board is what you DO. What they GET is
> ALIGNMENT.** So call two is about alignment."* → *"**Don't talk about what you do. Talk about the
> OUTCOME of what you do. This is very important.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The press-on nails test:** *"**People don't buy press-on nails. They want to show off to
> their girlfriends. It's a date night.** **Give them the result, not what you do.** Otherwise we
> run the risk of talking about **the foam insert, the leather overlays, and how comfortable our
> shoes are**"* — versus what the shoe is actually sold as: *"**Style. Affordable style. Urban.
> Affordable urban style.**"*

★★★★ He then applies it to the next line item unprompted: *"'design concepts' — **that's still what
you do, not the result of what you do.**"*

> ★★★★★ **"Road map", not "checklist".** `[ROOM]` proposes *"a checklist with a timeline"* →
> *"**We'll call that a road map.**"* → *"**Are you playing the game I'm playing?**"* → and the
> demonstration: *"**Who wants a checklist? No one. Who wants a road map? I do.**"*

### ★★★★★ Move three — reduce the time to result

> ★★★★★ *"**You only need 24 hours to bang out something good. The rest of it is all this
> garbage.** So **what can we do to optimise the garbage such that we can do this faster? Pick any
> part of the process that's high friction, let's talk about it, we'll design something — and you
> can shrink this time.**"*

★★★★ **The paradox of choice, applied:** *"How many mood boards do you present?"* → *"Four."* →
*"**They should do three. The paradox of choice.** **One is too few. Four is probably too many —
cuz all of a sudden they're like: oh, I like too many things, I'm confused.**"*

★★★★ **The response-time condition:** *"**And we don't move forward until you identify a direction
that you like. My process works IF you can respond in 24 hours. You have one business day to
respond.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The two-option speed premium — a complete, usable mechanic:**
>
> *"'**I have two options for you. Option one, we can do this in 30 days. Option two, we can do
> this in 14 days. Are you interested in option two?**' **What would they say? Yes — almost
> everybody's going to say yes.** '**Okay, here's how option two works. You must make decisions,
> we cannot go beyond two revisions, and we're done. If not, you pay me more. Are you cool?**'
> **Anything that takes more than 14 days — now you [bill] another 2K.**"*

★★★★ **And the permission move that makes enforcement painless:** *"**ask for the permission to
remind them that they're breaking the covenant — that way you won't feel like the bad person.**"*

> ★★★★ **The synthesis:** *"**You see, now you're ANTICIPATING all of what they're thinking, and
> you design through the whole thing.**"*

### ★★★★★ He refuses to declare it finished

> ★★★★★ *"**So this is not an irresistible offer yet. But you keep working on this, and eventually
> it'll become really, really tight. That's the idea.**"*

✅ Worth keeping as behaviour: he has just spent a session building something impressive in front of
a room, and his closing move is to **withhold the verdict.**

## L3 candidate

★★★★★ — **promote, high priority.** Three items: ★★★★★ **the four-move offer construction** —
dream outcome, perceived certainty, time to result, friction removal — worked end to end on a real
service, which the corpus has never had in one place; ★★★★★ **the three renames** (*discovery* /
*alignment* / *road map*) with *"same meeting, different feeling about it"* and **press-on

you can watch the value change without the work changing; and ★★★★★ **the two-option speed
premium**, a complete mechanic (14 vs. 30 days, decisions required, two revisions, otherwise the
price goes up) that converts client slowness from a grievance into a priced option. ✅ Also worth
promoting as behaviour: **he ends by refusing to call it done.**
