# The sales strategy to beat the competition even if you're outmatched (2026-02-10)

> **Attribution:** a **film breakdown** of *Air* (2023), the Nike/Michael Jordan story. Dialogue
> quoted from the film is **screenwriters' dialogue performed by actors** — it is **never** persona
> data. Only Chris's commentary is. Film lines are marked `[FILM]`; everything else is his.

## Summary

★★★★★ The strongest negotiation teaching in the corpus, built on a scene-by-scene reading of how
an outmatched bidder wins: **cast legitimate doubt**, **turn the competitor's strength into the
weakness**, **arm the buyer with questions**, and **identify who actually decides.** He then tells
a real client case that mirrors it — **the Carrie story**, which ends with a client paying **twice**
the competing quote — and closes on a second scene whose lesson is **concentration**: one athlete,
the entire budget.

## Key claims (2026-02-10)

### ★★★★★ The underdog strategy

> ★★★★★ *"**If you find yourself in a position where you're going to go up against bigger players —
> people who have bigger teams, more experience, better quality of work — you don't really stand
> much of a chance unless you can CAST SOME DOUBT from the client side. And the best thing you can
> do is think about their GREATEST WEAKNESS and exploit that — and have your potential client
> focus all their energies on your competitor's weaknesses rather than their strengths.**"*

★★★★ **The opening gambit is a no-deal.** Of the scout offering not to be met at all:
*"**He's offering no deal. They're not even interested in taking a meeting with Nike at this point.
'You don't need to meet with us if everyone else is cool. Only meet with us if they both suck.'**"*
✅ Structurally identical to the price-anchoring script at
[[2025-12-11-yt--_sTrDwoZyM|2025-12-11]] — *"only call me if you don't feel confident."*

### ★★★★★ Turning a strength into the weakness

Of the competitor whose roster held the three biggest players in the league — and whose pitch was
exactly that:

> ★★★★★ *"**They know they have the three biggest basketball players of the time, and they think
> that's a strength. And so he uses the strength AGAINST them — and teaches her how to ask the
> question.**"*

`[FILM]` — the line she is armed with: *"another way of looking at this is that **the best Michael
will ever be with you is number four.**"*

> ★★★★ *"**It's going to catch them flat-footed. They're not going to expect that. So they won't
> know how to respond. It's going to make them look embarrassed.** And so he's helping to create a
> friction point — that **he's just one of many.**"*

★★★ **And the read on the other competitor**, whose predictability is the attack: *"**he tells them
how they're going to dress, and it's almost predictable and laughable. And if this were to play
out to be true, it almost seems like they're very cookie-cutter and formulaic. And the last thing a
mom wants for her son is to feel as if she's just part of an assembly line.**"*

### ★★★★ When the competitor has no obvious weakness

Of the front-runner — cool, culturally embedded, better product:

> *"**There aren't many areas in which they're weak. They have the cool factor. They make
> all-leather shoes, so they have superior shoe craftsmanship.**"*

His reading of the two levers the character finds: a **cultural distance** between the buyer and a
foreign company, and — the substantive one —

> ★★★★ *"**the fact that they're run by a board. Four people who have a stake in the game, four
> opinions. They don't agree. FOUR HEADS ARE NOT GREATER THAN ONE.**"* → the question she is
> given: *"**who's really running this?**"* → *"**she's going to be extremely sensitive and
> observant to the fact the four of them are not always speaking the same language. And he's
> saying: it's going to be a headache for you.**"*

> ⚠️ **Framing note.** This is **analysis of a character's tactics in a film**, and the cultural
> lever in particular is described, not endorsed. Do not render it as advice he gives in his own
> voice.

### ★★★★★ If you arm the buyer, be ready for the same question

> ★★★★★ *"**Naturally, if you're going to create a lot of doubt and give your prospective client
> questions to ask each competitor, you have to be prepared that they're going to ask you: 'Well,
> what should I be asking YOU? How are YOU weak?'**"* → *"**when she asked that question, she's
> fallen squarely into his trap — because he set it up for this.**"*

★★★ **On the answer the film gives** (*"because I believe in your son… and I believe you might be
the only person on earth who knows it"*): *"**he's paying homage to the mom — which is very
important, because he's identified her as the decision maker.**"*

### ★★★★★ Isolate the decision-maker

> ★★★★★ *"**Notice the father's not in the story at all. It's all about the mom. And so when you're
> in a room full of decision makers, try to isolate who really has the power. And you need to make
> sure that not only do you hear everybody, but you pay careful attention to the one that's going
> to make the decision.**"*

### ★★★★★ The Carrie story — the same play, in his own practice

> *"I'll tell you a real story. **There's a friend of mine, her name is Carrie**, and she called me
> one day, frustrated with life."*

**The problem:** *"'**I am tired of losing the pitches to this girl. There's a girl who does what I
do, says what I say — and she's doing it for less than half of what I'm charging. So every time I
get on a call with a client, I'll mention my price and it's too much, and I just lose the client
time and time again.**'"*

> ★★★★★ **His diagnosis, and it is not sympathetic:** *"**Well, Carrie — if this happens to you
> once, that sucks. But if it happens to you MORE THAN ONCE from the same person: whose fault is it
> really? You've not adequately addressed what's happening.**"*

**His questions:** *"**Does this person deliver on the work that you can do?**"* → *"No. She uses
the language so it can fool clients."* → *"**Well, what kind of questions would you ask the client
to prove to them that you know what you're talking about versus them?**"* → **she had no answer.**

> ★★★★★ **The script — he flags it as recalled** (*"here's the exact thing I told her, as best as
> my memory can recall"*):
>
> *"'**Out of curiosity, I know you have lots of options to consider. What call am I for you today
> in terms of who you're considering? And if I'm not the last person, I would prefer to be last.
> And the reason why I say this is because I'm going to give you a price that's going to be twice
> as much as somebody else is going to say — and I don't want to start there. But I would love for
> you to ask whoever you're talking to this one question: when they tell you they can do branding,
> design and strategy — ask them to show you examples, for you to feel confident that they can do
> this work for you. Because lots of people say things, but they can't back them up.**'"*

**The outcome:** *"**A couple days later Carrie calls me back: 'Chris, I said everything you told
me to say.' And guess what? They called me back. They signed for the amount that was twice as much
as what the other person was offering — because that person could not answer those basic
questions.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The two mechanisms, named:**
>
> **① Casting doubt legitimately** — *"**and by the way, it's not just throwing shade, because
> people say things that they can't back up with real evidence.**"*
>
> **② Priming the price** — *"**she's planting the seed that she's going to be twice as much. So
> that when she says the price this time, they have no excuse to say 'wow Carrie, that was a lot of
> money' — cuz she could say: I TOLD you it was a lot of money.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**This is a great way to prime your prospective buyer so that they don't see price as a
> barrier anymore. And they see it as just a cost of doing business.**"*

### ★★★★★ Where innovation comes from

On the second scene — the mother demanding a revenue share on the shoes bearing her son's name,
against an industry that had never done it:

> ★★★★★ *"**This is where most innovations come from: somebody who is fairly new in an industry,
> who isn't bought into the doctrine or dogma of how things are done — or someone who's completely
> an amateur and knows nothing about this. That's where the innovation comes from, because she has
> the boldness to think differently. Not because she's a bold person per se — but because SHE
> DOESN'T KNOW HOW THINGS ARE DONE.**"*

★★★ *"**Ultimately he relents, because she's persistent and she's not taking no for an answer,
which is beautiful.**"* — and the affectionate aside: *"**I think we'd all be blessed if we had a
tough Black mom who negotiated on our behalf, who believed 100% in us and wasn't taking no guff
from any person.**"*

> ★★★★ **The paradox he draws:** *"**It's the richest deal that any sports company's ever made. And
> at the same time, it's the cheapest deal they've ever made in terms of what they've gotten.**"*

### ★★★★★ Concentration — the lesson he actually wants

> ★★★★★ *"**I love this strategy so much, because a lot of us commit this mistake. Rather than
> concentrating our effort into ONE thing — one niche, one offer, one problem we solve, one
> promotion — we have so many different offers to so many different avatars that we DILUTE OUR
> POWER.** … **If you take that half a million dollars and you give \$50,000 here and there, you can
> have 10 athletes that are all going to be mediocre. But there's only one Michael Jordan.**"*

✅ **This is the five ones and *pressure = force ÷ area*** ([[2025-10-30-yt-3ikc89b-tkE|2025-10-30]])
**with a case study attached** — the same argument he applied to himself in October, now
demonstrated on someone else's decision.

> ⚠️★★★ **The figures, with his own hedges intact** (*"let me give you some stats. I looked this
> up"*): a deal paying **\$500,000 a year for five years — a \$2.5M contract**; *"more than what
> they paid any other athlete from any other company by 3x"*; *"**they took their entire marketing
> budget for all basketball athlete endorsements and just gave it all to Michael**"*; a **5%
> royalty** on the signature line only; a first-year projection of 3 million shoes against
> *"over a hundred million dollars worth of shoes"* actually sold; and *"**Forbes magazine has
> estimated** Michael Jordan's endorsement deal with Nike has garnered him **over \$1.3 billion**"*.
> **Record as claims he makes with those hedges — never as verified figures.**
