# Is your pitch so simple a kid gets it? (2026-02-24)

> **Attribution:** an excerpt from a **business competition television format** in which Chris
> appears as a **mentor to the contestants**, with a separate panel of judges. Contestants' pitches
> and founder stories are marked `[CONTESTANT]` and are **not** persona data. The show's host and
> format announcements are marked `[SHOW]`. Only Chris's teaching is persona data.
>
> ⚠️ **The show is not named in the captions and is not guessed here.**

## Summary

★★★★ Short, and unusually well-tested: he teaches **the repeatable story** to a room of founders,
and the format then puts it under the hardest possible test — each contestant must brief **a total
stranger**, who then pitches on their behalf. It also contains the corpus's clearest technique for
rescuing a statistic: **make a fact visual.**

## Key claims (2026-02-24)

### ★★★ How he introduces himself

> *"**My name is Chris Do. I'm a loud introvert. I'm a serial dreamer and entrepreneur.**"*

✅ *Loud introvert* is his standing self-description; *serial dreamer* is a less common pairing and
worth keeping.

★★★ **The framing he opens on:** *"**I do not envy you in your positions.**"* → *"**For the rest of
your business life — until you're a billionaire — you're going to have the same challenge over and
over again: to communicate your message. To an investor, to a partner, to your team, to enrol them
in your mission, your vision. And also to communicate across social channels through
marketing.**"*

### ★★★★★ The repeatable story — credited to his business coach

> ★★★★★ *"**This is a concept I learned from my business coach: you have to tell a story that's so
> easily packaged that WHEN YOU'RE NOT IN THE ROOM, they can take that and share it with someone
> else.**"*

✅ Consistent with the corpus attribution of this idea to **Kier McLaren**, who is named as the
business coach at [[2026-03-10-yt-Zys3dt503Q4|2026-03-10]].

> ★★★★★ **Why it matters, and what gets in the way:** *"**It's a lot of work for you to be telling
> every single person what it is that you do. The goal is to tell a repeatable story. And what gets
> in the way is you feel compelled to tell the WHOLE story — not just the part that's memorable,
> different, and unique.**"*

★★★★ **The test he applies:** *"**The first problem with telling your story is: is it too
complicated? If you make something too complicated, I won't be able to tell that story.**"* → the
room supplies the opposite: *easy, simple, direct* → *"**very direct. Not a lot of adjectives.**"*

### ★★★★ Character, want, obstacle

> *"**As we talk about story, there's a character — that's your audience, that's the customer. You
> need to be very clear with who the character is. So with each one of you, think about the
> character: what is it that they want, and what is the obstacle?**"*

★★★★ **And a live compression.** `[CONTESTANT]`: *"I help people who are tired of their socks
sliding off… and I give them a sock that does not slide off your feet."* →

> *"**Can we refine that? Make it clearer and punchier: 'I make socks that don't slide off your
> feet.'**"* — plus *"and add style."*

### ★★★★★ Facts versus stories — and how to rescue a fact

A contestant asks whether to lead with facts or story, since their category is one everyone
already participates in.

> ★★★★★ *"**The problem with that is: if you bombard me with facts, it's overwhelming. STORIES ARE
> THE MOST NATURAL WAY THAT WE COMMUNICATE. FACTS ARE NOT.**"*

Then, given the fact — *over half the plastic produced in the world goes to packaging* — he does
not discard it. He converts it:

> ★★★★★ *"**How much is that in volume?**"* → *"Good question."* → *"**That's the more interesting
> fact. You could say: which is the equivalent to three Empire States.**"*
>
> → ★★★★★ *"**You make a fact more interesting by making it VISUAL. Make it something I can relate
> to.**"*

### ★★★★★ The closing charge

> ★★★★★ *"**Let's just be real. You have a challenge today. And the one thing that you're going to
> do is you're going to try and tell them TOO MUCH. Resist that instinct, please.** **It's much
> more effective for you to say LESS and for them to REMEMBER — with a really strong hook. Make it
> easy to understand. Make your story repeatable.**"*

### ✅ The format tests the lesson

`[SHOW]` — the twist announced after his talk: contestants will **not** pitch the judges. They will
brief **a total stranger**, who then presents on their behalf, and the judges score **how well the
stranger communicates it.**

> ✅ **Worth recording as validation, not as his claim.** The format independently operationalises
> exactly what he taught minutes earlier — *a story so easily packaged that when you're not in the
> room, they can share it.* The stranger **is** the absent room.
