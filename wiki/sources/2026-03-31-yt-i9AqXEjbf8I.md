# Your insecurity is your brand (2026-03-31)

> **Attribution:** a talk and fireside Q&A at a **Vietnamese-American community event**. Audience
> questions are marked `[ROOM]` and are **not** persona data.
>
> ⚠️ **Family-privacy rule applied throughout.** He speaks at length about his wife, his two sons
> and his parents. **No family member is named here**, and identifying details about his sons
> (institutions, courses, exact ages) are **deliberately omitted** — only the parenting philosophy,
> which is his own belief material, is recorded. Per `SUBJECT.md`, discoverability is never an
> argument for relaxing this.

## Summary

★★★★★ **The most personal source in the corpus, and a biographical landmark.** It contains the
origin of his self-concept — arriving in America at three, wanting to be invisible, the Art Center
graduation that broke the self-hatred — the **first record that his business coach Kier McLaren has
died**, the **full Daywalker origin in Kier's own words**, the family structure that explains the
hybrid, and a parenting philosophy so far from the stereotype he grew up under that he has *tried to
bribe his children to quit school.*

## Key claims (2026-03-31)

### ★★★★★ The wound at the start

> ★★★★★ *"There was a comedian who said this: **the least desirable type of man in America is an
> Asian man.** … **because Asian men are seen as effeminate, bad drivers, not good lovers, and not
> very attentive to the other person.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**And so for a long time, the self-narrative — my own identity — was: I WISH I WASN'T
> LIKE THIS. Because all I wanted to do is BE INVISIBLE my entire life. Just leave me alone.**"*

★★★★ **The three stereotypes he was choosing between:** the gangster · the nail salon owner or
worker (*"there's a whole history why that has happened in America"*) · and *"the most dominant
one — **the one that works in tech. Silicon Valley. You must be a good engineer.**"* →

> *"**I'm not a gangster. I'm too soft to be a gangster. I don't care about doing nails. And I'm
> terrible at math.** … **Luckily, my older brother fulfilled that promise. The chosen one — he
> became an engineer.**"*

### ★★★★★ Biography — arrival and assimilation

> ★★★★★ *"**I was born in Vietnam, came to America when I was 3 years old.**"* — in **1975**.

> ★★★★ *"**My parents were different than their siblings — they really wanted us to ASSIMILATE
> QUICKLY. So though they spoke to us in Vietnamese at home, it wasn't forbidden to speak English,
> and they wanted us to fit in fast. And so my Vietnamese is terrible.**"*

★★★★★ **Junior high, mid-70s to early 80s:** *"**there weren't a lot of Asian people.** … there was
one other Asian girl, and when we had music and dance, **all the white kids were like: you two
should get together.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**Throughout my life I was like: I must think that I'm white or something. Because being
> Vietnamese meant I was a source of someone to be picked on, to be bullied.**"*

★★★★★ **The advice that shaped him**, from the older brother — four years his senior:

> ★★★★★ *"**The best thing that you can do for a bully is to STAND UP.** So I'm a small guy. I'm not
> a big person. I'm not strong. I'm not athletic. But he always told me: **stand up to people and
> they'll leave you alone, because they'll just move on to someone who was easier.** And **though I
> was scared out of my mind to get into fights — and I would get into some of them — after their
> first fight they would just leave you alone.**"*

✅ **This is the root of *"I love my bullies"*** at [[2026-03-24-yt-GaDK_zVy1Cc|2026-03-24]], stated
a week earlier: the adult version of the same instruction.

> ★★★★★ **And the loneliest detail in the corpus:** *"**My biggest concern in school was: I wonder
> who I'm going to eat lunch with today. Because I just would not like to eat alone.**"*

### ★★★★★ The Art Center graduation — the turning point

> *"I get into Art Center and it's graduation. **I have my own struggles there, because I was
> thinking: I don't want to be here anymore. I'm done with school.** But I do go finish all the work
> and go to graduation."*

> ★★★★★ *"**And I see something for the very first time: a lot of Asian people — Korean, Chinese,
> Japanese, and even some Vietnamese who were not born here — PROUDLY WEARING THE DRESS OF THEIR
> COUNTRY. And I had never seen them like this before.** … I would have been like: **I'm not
> wearing that. That's not for me. That looks very feminine. I cannot wear that.** And I saw them do
> this. **AND SOMETHING CHANGED INSIDE OF ME.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**I had not seen Asian people at the height of excellence and to that level of pride. IT
> MADE ME REALLY ASHAMED OF MYSELF FOR THE SELF-HATE THAT I HAD FOR BEING VIETNAMESE AMERICAN.**"*

★★★★★ **And the analysis he immediately applies to it** — unusually sociological, and unusually
unsparing about his own position:

> *"**The reason why they were so proud of their heritage and their culture was because THEY GREW UP
> IN THEIR DOMINANT CULTURE, where they were the man or the woman.** And in order for them to go to
> private art school, which is very expensive, **they had to have a lot of money. So not only were
> they part of the dominant culture — they were the top 10%, 1% of their society.** So naturally,
> when they came to America, oftentimes **they had more money than the Americans. They were driving
> the fancier cars, they were living in the nicer places.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**And me — even though we weren't poor, MY PARENTS MADE SURE WE FELT POOR, because I never
> saw any of that money. So I was just living by credit card.**"*

→ *"there were a lot of things going right for them — that started **a multi-year-long journey
within myself to celebrate and to love myself 100%.**"*

### ★★★★★ Why identity never came up at the agency

> ★★★★★ *"We're located in **Santa Monica** and we're working in advertising — **which is a
> predominantly white industry, white man industry. Okay, nothing wrong with that, that's just the
> culture.** … And throughout that whole time, **because it was mostly just conference calls — you
> win or die by the phone call. THEY DON'T SEE YOU. You show your work and you pitch, and WE LIVE
> IN THE REALM OF IDEAS. And so identity and who I was NEVER CAME INTO PLAY. It just meant we had
> to be the best. And we're pitching on \$100,000 jobs, \$500,000 jobs — and we were winning.**"*

### ★★★★★ 2014 — representation arrives unbidden

> *"**It's not until 2014, when I started making YouTube videos with my then partner Jose, that
> people start DMing me** something I had not asked myself in a while: **the issue of
> REPRESENTATION.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**I didn't realise this — but being an Asian man, Vietnamese American, IT GAVE PERMISSION
> to lots of people out there who wanted to pursue the arts, and to see what excellence looks
> like.**"*

★★★★ **On the medium:** *"**YouTube is a great place, because anybody with a camera and an internet
connection can make content — and we see a lot more diverse programming on YouTube, unlike in
traditional media.**"*

> ★★★★★ **And the honesty about the burden:** *"**that is when it starts to hit me that EVEN THOUGH
> IT WASN'T IMPORTANT TO ME to represent other people — I DIDN'T WANT TO CARRY THAT BURDEN — it was
> important for PEOPLE TO SEE PEOPLE LIKE THEM DOING GREAT.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The resulting mission, which reframes his personal style entirely:** *"**I want to do
> everything in my power to break down those stereotypes — to be a strong, masculine, fashionable
> person that understands money and can talk about different things and swim in a lot of different
> circles — SO THAT PEOPLE CAN SEE IT'S SAFE TO DO THAT.**"*

✅ The fashion interest catalogued as a *"branch"* at [[2026-... |2025-10-23]] is here given a
second, prior motive: **counter-stereotype work.**

> ★★★★★ *"**I've worked with a business coach for 13 years… His name is Kier McLaren. HE'S PASSED
> AWAY.** He's a big Irish-American man, very big, rides a motorcycle. **And I would meet with him
> every single week for 13 years, for about an hour and a half to two hours.**"*

> ⚠️ **No date is given.** **This is not the first record of his death** — that is

> on [[../entities/kier-mclaren|his entity page]]. **What this source adds is the fullest account
> of the relationship**: the 13-year weekly cadence, his other clients, and the Daywalker verdict
> in his own words.

★★★ **On Kier's other clients:** *"he consulted and coached many people in our industry. **Some of
them are world-famous title designers who worked on big blockbuster films. Some of them work with
world-class musicians, people who play in rock bands you've heard of. So I'm just one of many people
he consults.**"*

### ★★★★★ The Daywalker — in Kier's own words

> ★★★★★ *"He goes: '**Chris, of all my clients, you're not the most creative. Clearly there are more
> creative people.**' — **and I know, because I know his clients. I admire them. They're my
> heroes.** And then: '**And you're not the best business person either, because clearly they're
> much better than you. They're doing gazillion dollars.**' **And I'm thinking: is this going
> somewhere, coach?** And he goes: '**BUT NO ONE IS AS GOOD AT DOING CREATIVE AND RUNNING A BUSINESS
> AS YOU.**'"*

> ★★★★ **The two failure modes Kier was contrasting:** *"**the creative person is the pursuit and the
> purity of artistic expression — and they're running their business into the ground.** And **the
> people who are really good business operators are making a lot of money, but for him they have no
> creative soul. They know how to hire and fire and leverage and negotiate and arbitrage.** And so
> he's like: **you're the best HYBRID.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The Blade line, with its origin now attached:** *"**he's half vampire and half human, so
> they call him the DAYWALKER. He has all the strengths of a vampire and none of the weaknesses. He
> can walk in the sunlight.** So I like to tell people **I'm a Daywalker. I have all the strengths
> and artistic imagination of a designer and creative thinker, and the business acumen of a business
> person — and none of the weaknesses between the two.**"*

### ★★★★★ Why he is the hybrid — the family structure

> ★★★★★ *"**My father and my entire father's family — there's like NINE SIBLINGS. THEY'RE ALL
> ENGINEERS. Silicon Valley. They're all doing really well. They own multiple homes, living in San
> Jose.** **My mom's side — they're all ARTISTIC. They're singers, poets, artists, writers. My mom is
> one too. THEY'RE DREAMERS. And they're the most loving, beautiful people. And they love life. And
> many of them are financially just not doing well.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**So you can see now: I'M THE EXACT CHILD BETWEEN MY MOM AND MY DAD. And maybe it's
> coincidental — but I'M ALSO THE MIDDLE CHILD.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The three brothers, resolved for the first time:**
> - **Older brother** — *"a Silicon Valley engineer. **He's exited on multiple companies. He's a
>   founder.**"*
> - **Chris** — the middle.
> - **Younger brother** — *"**never went to college**, artist, freelancer who does **visual effects
>   for TV shows.**"* ✅ The same brother who *"learned typography from me"* at
>   [[2026-03-10-yt-Zys3dt503Q4|2026-03-10]].

### ★★★★★ His parents — and the origin of his questioning style

★★★★ **Latchkey childhood:** *"**both parents worked all the time and we were LATCHKEY KIDS.** They
go to work, you have a key, you go home and you don't leave the house."* → *"**for a long time I
felt really bad, because I was thinking we should do more stuff together as a family** — this
experience I see in my Chinese friends, my American friends. **But my parents were just busting
their butt just trying to make sure we can pay the mortgage.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The reframe:** *"**What I realised many years later was that IT GAVE ME A LOT OF FREEDOM to
> learn about who I was going to be in this world — versus following a blueprint that they wanted
> to lay out for me.**"*

> ★★★★★ **His father's method — and this is where his entire coaching style comes from:** *"**instead
> of telling me what to do, MY DAD WOULD JUST ASK ME LOTS OF QUESTIONS. When I got into trouble, it
> wasn't a long lecture. IT WAS JUST A LOT OF QUESTIONS — getting me to think. And it allowed me the
> space to not fill in what he wanted me to become, to fit in a mould.**"*

★★★★★ **And the story about his mother, which he plainly did not know at the time:**

> ★★★★★ *"**When I decided to go to art school — I didn't know this at the time — MY DAD WAS REALLY
> AGAINST IT. And I didn't know this because HE NEVER TOLD ME. And the reason he never told me was
> because my mom — she's like that tall, and she never talks back to my dad, traditional family —
> SHE STOOD UP AND SAID: 'YOU'RE NOT ALLOWED TO TELL HIM YOUR THOUGHTS.'**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**I thank my dad a lot for shaping who I am. I didn't know this part of the story. AND SO
> NOW MY MOM HAS BECOME MY HERO.** I'd like to think I'm strong enough to pursue a career in art and
> design even if they didn't approve — **but I NEVER HAVE TO KNOW, BECAUSE I NEVER HEARD. She
> shielded me from that.**"*

★★★★★ **The cousins, and the ledger he draws:** *"**of all my cousins who went a very traditional
path, who did what their parents told them to do to stay tight to the family unit: number one, THEY
NEVER MOVED MORE THAN 15 MILES AWAY FROM HOME.** They're all still in San Jose. And number two,
**they're pharmacists, dentists, doctors, nurses, engineers, lawyers, accountants. Very respectable
things. THEY'RE POORER THAN I AM. And now I'm seen as the one who breaks the mould and is
successful.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The message he draws from it:** *"**at one point in your life, in your adulthood, I hope
> that you find the courage to do what you want to do — and the people who really care about you,
> who really love you, will be patient enough to allow you to do that. AND EVEN IF THEY DIDN'T — I
> have to say: I don't care.**"*

★★★★ **The proximity reversal:** *"**we moved to Los Angeles, and then eventually they moved to Los
Angeles — because when we had kids, the grandparents want to be around the kids. So they sold their
house. Now THEY live close to US, versus us living close to them.**"*

> ★★★★★ **And the cost he attributes to the traditional path:** *"**I look at all my cousins who spent
> all this quality time with their parents — they're still, to this day, in their 40s, concerned
> about what their parents are doing. They never got away from that relationship.** … **I know a lot
> of you have those traditional values, which I think are good — BUT EVERY POSITIVE THING HAS A
> NEGATIVE THING.** The negative is **they never fully realised their own identity and their own
> potential. And I think that's just a shame.**"*

### ★★★★★ His parenting philosophy

> ★★★★★ **The inheritance theory:** *"**we inherit the problems that our parents gave us, because they
> couldn't solve them.** Parents want you to do the thing that they couldn't do in life. **If they
> didn't go to college — guess what, you're going to college. If they were blue collar, they want
> you to be white collar. If they married this kind of person, they want you to marry another
> kind — because they're not happy.**"*

**Citing a yogi:** *"**children are born perfect. It's parents who mess them up — because we're
trying to correct the sins of our own past in our own children. WE DO NOT HAVE THE COURAGE TO ACT;
WHAT WE WANT IS TO HAVE THEM ACT FOR US.**"* — with the example: *"**how many moms don't know how to
play an instrument, but their children must play the violin and the piano?**"*

> ★★★★★ **His conclusion:** *"**And so if I believe that — and I do — my children are born perfect.
> I'M TRYING TO DO AS LITTLE AS POSSIBLE TO HARM THEM. THAT'S THE GOAL OF BEING A PARENT.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The inversion, stated plainly:** *"**In our family it's the opposite of most families. Most
> families want you to go to college, go to a good school, have great grades. WE'RE LIKE: JUST DO
> WHATEVER YOU WANT. WHO CARES ABOUT SCHOOL?** In fact, **we saved up money for them for college
> since they were little kids — and I'VE TRIED TO BRIBE MY CHILDREN TO QUIT SCHOOL. Every semester I
> ask: do you still want to be here? Because YOU CAN HAVE ALL THE MONEY, NO STRINGS ATTACHED. Do
> whatever you want in your life. No expectations.** And people are like: **what kind of hippie
> family is this?**"*

★★★★ **The rules he does keep** — *"**strict guidelines on EDGES, but not a lot in the middle**"*:
no physical violence between the brothers; **respect** — *"respect your parents, respect other
people, try not to hurt people. And the rest of it, you do whatever you want."*

★★★★★ **The homework exchange**, when a relative asks how he gets his sons to do it:

> ★★★★★ *"'**I don't know. Never cared that they've done their homework.**' And then I remembered:
> **I'VE NEVER EVEN LOOKED AT THE REPORT CARD.** And I know some of you are shocked — **what kind of
> Asian dad are you? I'm dad. I don't care.** But you know what? **They've always done well in
> school. How do I know? Cuz my wife tells me. Cuz she cares.**"*

★★★★ **How he stays connected:** *"**I'm doing the things that they're doing — and I'm purposely
learning Gen Z slang so I can embarrass them all the time. That's my connection.**"*

★★★★★ **The travelling years**, and the best line in the passage: one son was homeschooled online for
two years — *"**so I had none of the work and all the benefit. He literally went to work with me
every single day. Little man would bring his backpack, his computer**… He'd come into the office and
say: '**Hey boys, how's it going?**' I said: '**YOU CAN'T CALL THEM BOYS. THEY'RE MY EMPLOYEES, AND
YOU'RE JUST MY SON. DON'T ABUSE THE POWER.**'"*

→ *"we got to travel to Germany, to the Philippines, stayed on a private island, flew in a
helicopter, swam with whale sharks — **and he got to do an opening keynote for me. This is an
experience I'll cherish for the rest of my life.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The closing formulation:** *"**the seeds that we planted with our children while they were
> young, allowing them to be their fullest self, their higher self — that's how I stay connected to
> them. And LISTENING to my parents is how I stay connected to my parents.**"*

★★★★★ **And the line he ends the talk on:** *"**It's great to be able to meet your heroes. IT'S EVEN
BETTER TO BECOME ONE TO YOUR OWN CHILDREN.**"*

### ★★★★ "Confessions of a Recovering Graphic Designer"

Two of his named talks: ***Unbland Yourself*** and ***Confessions of a Recovering Graphic
Designer*** — *"**my life story and all the twists and turns and the things that go unplanned…
the slide should have said: A SERIES OF UNFORTUNATE EVENTS LED ME TO EXACTLY HERE.**"*

> ★★★★★ **Why "recovering":** *"**my identity for a very long time WAS graphic design. But I realised:
> being a graphic designer means YOU MUST DESIGN GRAPHICS.** Like a fashion designer designs
> fashion. An interior designer designs interiors. An experience designer designs experiences.
> **And I felt, after I outgrew that title — although it served me really well — it's time for me to
> move on. So I still have graphic design tendencies, but I've evolved beyond that. Now I help
> people with relationships, with their business, with negotiations, with public speaking, with
> their own personal brand.**"*

★★★ **On himself, in passing:** *"**I love Q&A. I love fireside chats. The happiest that I ever am is
with a large piece of paper and some markers. And that was just how it was when I was 5 years old.
So they continue on into my 50s.**"*
