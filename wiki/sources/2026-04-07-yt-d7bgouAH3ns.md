# How to build a brand people actually remember (2026-04-07)

> **Attribution:** a podcast interview built around the **Unbland Yourself** workbook. The host —
> a video producer who works with The Futur — pushes back repeatedly and productively; his lines
> are marked `[HOST]` and are **not** persona data. Third parties named in passing are recorded as
> context only.
>
> ⚠️ **Family-privacy rule applied**: his wife appears in a business disclosure and is referenced
> **by role only.**

## Summary

★★★★★ **A landmark, and the most complete statement of his branding system anywhere.** It contains
the **Unbland Yourself** premise, the **three-part essence** (one thing · enemy · rallying cry) with
two worked examples, the **be-number-one rule** and its arithmetic, **three principles of personal
branding**, **clothes as tools**, and — unusually — a candid **business disclosure**: staff
departures that took a revenue line with them, a wife telling him they are \$20,000 a month short,
and his decision to come out of *"semi-retirement mode."*

## Key claims (2026-04-07)

### ★★★★★ Unbland Yourself — the premise

> ★★★★★ *"**We have been socialised to blend in, to get along** — and depending on what culture you
> come from, it's stronger in some than others. But we're wearing a **professional mask**. You show
> up, you say certain things, you play your cards, you don't try to upset anybody. **And if you do
> this for long enough, YOU FORGET ABOUT WHO YOU ARE, what your core beliefs are — or you're afraid
> to go against the grain. AND THIS IS AMPLIFIED ACROSS SOCIAL MEDIA. We are so fearful of saying
> our thoughts, expressing our truest self, dressing the way we want to — because we're afraid of
> the backlash.**"*

★★★★ **Its provenance:** *"**a culmination of the last 3 years of research, testing, experimenting**
— in virtual and in-person workshops, where I'm testing concepts."* The question it asks: *"**who
were they meant to be in this lifetime? Who were they when they thought to themselves at 7 years
old: this is what I want to be when I grow up — and something happened to that plan.**"*

> ★★★★★ **Why a workbook rather than a course** — a clear statement of his scaling logic:
> *"**I realised that my ability to teach as many people as I want is FINITE if I do it as an
> in-person experience, or even through the price barrier.** … I want something **standalone, at
> your own pace, in the privacy and comfort of your own home**, where you ask yourself these deeply
> personal questions that require a contemplative, reflective moment — **and it's not always
> conducive to do that around people on a certain timetable.**"* → *"**the goal is not to finish it
> as quickly as possible, but to learn about things you buried way deep inside of you — and to have
> some honest conversations with yourself.**"*

### ★★★★★ Self-imposed rules — the collar and the chain

> ★★★★★ *"The human mind, once expanded, does not shrink back to its original size. … **When you find
> out that these SELF-IMPOSED RULES that you live by are actually just that — self-imposed, not real
> rules, not laws — you start to be more free.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"It's the classic story of **a dog who wears an electric shock collar** — when they approach
> the perimeter it sends a signal. After a while they learn there's a perimeter, **and you can just
> take the collar off and they never cross it.** Same with **the elephant held by a chain since
> birth — eventually it's just a string, and they never push past.** **WE ARE LIKE THAT. We learn
> really fast what we can and cannot do according to societal norms — and then we PLAY SMALL for
> the rest of our lives.**"*

→ *"**and we never realise that once we leave the city we grew up in, or the group of friends we
thought we must always need, or the job we've always had — WE LEARN THAT THE WORLD'S MUCH BIGGER.
There's a lot more to us to give. And that's one of the most liberating things.**"*

### ★★★★★ The fear he had to get past, and the two lessons

> ★★★★★ *"**The biggest hurdle I had to get through was: IF I AM MYSELF, what kind of negative impact
> is it going to have on my business?** Because at that point I'm still running a service design
> company doing really big projects — **and if I say this, does it mean that I lose that?**"*

> ★★★★★ **Lesson one:** *"**NOBODY'S REALLY PAYING ATTENTION TO YOU.** In our own little bubble we
> falsely believe we're the most important thing in the world, because we have no other thing to
> reference. … **Clients are busy. They're thinking about themselves. They're not sitting there
> looking at every social post you make** — unless you do something super sensational in a way
> that's not good for you."*

> ★★★★★ **Lesson two:** *"**There's a WIDER LEVEL OF ACCEPTANCE for you than you think.** … **People
> are so afraid of alienating everyone that they wind up saying nothing to everyone — and they're
> just BLANDING THEMSELVES into oblivion, into irrelevancy.**"*

> ★★★★★ **Compel and repel:** *"when you say something based on your convictions — **not to rile
> people up; I'm not saying to be a contrarian, to be divisive, but to say things that have meaning
> to you** — **IT SHOULD BOTH COMPEL AND REPEL.** It should compel people to say '**I get your vibe,
> I want to be around you**' — and **it should repel the people who are like: I don't like the way
> you think.**"*

★★★★★ **His two worked examples of what repels — and the second is the more revealing:**

- **Clothes:** *"sometimes **I wear fashion that is unpalatable for some people.** If I wear some kind
  of weird skirt-short thing… or some crazy colour or floral print."*
- ★★★★★ **Therapy:** *"if I talk about **therapy, and how that's been ONE OF THE BIGGEST RETURNS ON
  INVESTMENT I'VE EVER HAD**, they're like: 'don't bring your therapy agenda to this conversation,
  everybody needs it.' **I NEVER SAID EVERYBODY NEEDS IT.** But if you're searching and you're lost,
  it's worked really well for me. I recommend you give it a try. **That's all it is. I'm not trying
  to impose anything on anybody. For the people who feel called, try it. If not, no worries.**"*

### ★★★★★ The comments, the hat, and the man bag

> ★★★★★ *"**Everyone has an a-hole and everyone has an opinion. That doesn't mean I want to see
> either.**"* ✅ Second attestation (see [[2025-10-23-yt-eluwXdhzJ5k|2025-10-23]]).

**The comments he gets:** *"your hat"* · *"who does he think he is, he's trying too hard"* ·
*"every time I tune in there's another necklace and it's getting out of control"* · *"you can tell
he's poor cuz he's dressed like he's poor."*

> ★★★★★ **His answer to the hiring one:** *"'no one would take you seriously — if you walked in on
> me, I would never hire you.' **Bro, trust me — YOU DON'T HAVE ENOUGH MONEY TO HIRE ME. So let's
> start there. You probably have never hired anybody.**"*

> ★★★★★ **And the principle underneath it:** *"**These ideas — asking people to meet you where you
> are, to be able to have the pleasure of your company — is ridiculous. We all come in different
> shapes and sizes and have different preferences about how we carry ourselves. To each their
> own.** … **But to project your values and your aesthetic onto people is just ridiculous to me.
> NOBODY ASKED YOU. NOBODY CARES.**"*

★★★ **On the net-worth jibes:** *"let's bring those people on the show… **take it down any dimension
you want, and chances are you probably have me beat in a couple of categories** — but **you project
that energy into the world, and I think it just consumes you. So most of the time I look at that
and laugh.**"*

★★★★★ **THE MAN BAG STORY**, at a mortgage-and-real-estate industry event. A friend — *"as a
straight white guy, giving me a hard time, but with love"* — says he could never pull off the bag
Chris is carrying:

> ★★★★★ *"And I said: '**ARE YOU SURE? Why don't you try it on?**' And he tries it on — **and all the
> women around, part of the staff, were like: 'Oh, you look so good.' HE GOES: 'DO I?'**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**So that's a presumption of reception — of how people would read you — and also A LIMITING
> BELIEF about what you can and can't pull off in this life.**"*

★★★★ **The outcome:** *"**his game has changed. He's dressing differently, and he's sending me DMs
like: 'hey, is this a good fit or not?'**"*

> ★★★★★ **And the generalisation, which lifts it out of fashion entirely:** *"**it doesn't have to be
> about fashion. It could be: you never thought you could drive that kind of car, or live in that
> neighbourhood, or date that kind of person. WE HAVE SO MANY LIMITING BELIEFS.**"*

★★★★★ **"Out of my league"** — a small tirade worth keeping whole: *"one of the things that would
really bother me about some of my friends is they'd say '**she's so out of my league**.' I'm like:
**WITH THAT ATTITUDE, FOR SURE SHE IS.** … You mean some people are born more asymmetrically
beautiful, therefore you're less than? **SHE DID NOTHING TO EARN THAT — NOR DID YOU** if you're a
super-stud athlete; that's mostly genetic. **I respect the game — that you worked out and you're
focusing on skills. That's cool, I can look up to that. But the rest of it — why do we believe
someone's in or out of our league? That's a value statement full of judgment that really bothers
me.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"'I can't wear that. You shouldn't wear that.' **It's really A PROJECTION OF YOUR OWN
> INSECURITY AND YOUR OWN PREJUDICE. And I think people need to check themselves.**"*

### ★★★★★ Why people talk about what they do instead of who they are

> ★★★★★ *"There are a lot of templates out there. We don't know what to talk about. **We're told from
> a very young age: DON'T TALK ABOUT YOURSELF. Don't think about yourself so much. So what do we do?
> WE DEFLECT.** 'Nobody's going to complain if I tell people I'm a plumber, or a roofing contractor,
> or I build websites.' **But so does everybody else who does that exact same thing. WHY SHOULD I
> CARE ABOUT YOU?**"*

★★★★ **The paradox of choice as a conversion killer:** *"**the more options we have, the harder it
is to make a decision. And the reason some of your clients don't choose to follow you or reach out
is because THEY CAN'T TELL WHY YOU'RE DIFFERENT than anyone else — and so they shut down.**"*

### ★★★★★ The two doors — Steven Bartlett

> ★★★★★ *"**Door one: you go in and it locks behind you, you can never leave — the only path is
> forward. Door two: you go in and it's not locked, you can leave if you want. And choices are like
> that. Sometimes we mistakenly believe THAT ALL DOORS ARE DOOR ONE.**"*

The example — someone weighing whether to quit a job: *"**what's the consequence of you leaving?
Are you able to come back or not?**"* → she could → *"**so she was thinking too long about a decision
that could be easily undone.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**The greatest problem in modern entrepreneurial circles is we think everything's a
> door-one situation. SO IT'S NOT THAT WE MAKE THE WRONG DECISION — IT'S THAT WE MAKE NO
> DECISION.**"*

### ★★★★★ We are the worst judge of our own value

> ★★★★★ *"**Historically speaking, WE ARE THE WORST JUDGE OF OUR OWN VALUE.** I could do something
> and you're like: 'dude, how did you do that?' And I'm like: **'well, doesn't everybody know
> that?'** … And then I explain it — and depending on your reaction, I think: **maybe I'm on to
> something.** And that begins the process of understanding: **WOW, SOMETHING I TAKE FOR GRANTED IS
> VALUABLE TO OTHER PEOPLE.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The method, and the video's epigraph:** *"**if we want to learn more about ourselves, WE
> NEED TO PUT OURSELVES IN A POSITION WHERE WE'RE EXPLAINING TO PEOPLE what it is that we know,
> think and believe — and judging by their reaction. And if you get enough feedback from different
> types of people, YOU MIGHT DISCOVER SOMETHING YOU WEREN'T EVEN AWARE ABOUT YOURSELF.**"*

### ★★★★★ The robot-voice test

`[HOST]` says he builds his brand by talking about video production.

> ★★★★★ *"**Talking about what you do is NOT how you build a personal brand. You're basically doing
> TUTORIALS. Nobody has any sense of who you are, what you stand for.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**If we put a ROBOT VOICE on you — the exact same script — and we BLURRED YOUR FACE: would
> anybody be able to tell it's you?**"*

★★★★ **The opposite failure mode:** *"contrast that against people who have very strong personal
brands but maybe **not put a lot of value into the world**. In the OG days, **all you had to do was
turn on the camera and be a personality**… they grew gigantic accounts and have massive fandoms.
**But to the average person it's like: WHAT VALUE DID YOU GIVE ME?**"*

> ★★★★★ **The synthesis:** *"**the solution is somewhere in between: I create a lot of value, I give
> you utility, I'm useful, I'm helpful to you creating transformation — BUT I'M ALSO WEAVING IN MY
> STORY ABOUT WHO I AM. So you have a choice: do I want YOUR brand of video production, or someone
> else's?**"*

### ★★★★★ Water, provenance, and meaning-making machines

He flags it as his stock example (*"I'm sorry if people are bored of this"*): water is free and
abundant, yet one bottle is \$2 and another \$25.

> ★★★★★ *"**The answer is THE STORY that each bottle brings. And there's a fancy word — PROVENANCE,
> where something comes from.** This came from the Swiss Alps. The Amazon River. The Canadian
> glacier. Never touched by man. Vapour distilled. **They're all stories — because marketers have
> learned that we need a story.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**HUMANS ARE MEANING-MAKING MACHINES**, and we need meaning in everything we do. **So if
> you tell the story, I allow myself to accept your meaning. And when you don't tell your story,
> there's no meaning. There's no connection.**"*

### ★★★★★ The shadow self is the interesting part

> *"'What's my first post?' — **you're talking about the TACTICS. Before we create something, we
> need to know: WHO THE HECK ARE YOU? And there's a good chance you don't even know.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**What people tend to do is want to talk about all that they're good at — the strengths and
> the beautiful things.** … **This is why people appear too perfect on TV or on social media: because
> they're presenting their very best self on their very best day. THAT'S REALLY BORING TO ME. And it
> REEKS OF INAUTHENTICITY.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**So I say to people: LOOK INTO YOUR SHADOW SELF — the things you feel great vulnerability
> around, things that bring you anxiety or make you nervous. Let's figure out how you can
> incorporate that, BECAUSE THAT MAKES YOU MUCH MORE INTERESTING.**"*

★★★★★ **Why quirks work:** *"**those little quirks and the weird things allow people to say: 'You're
not a perfect person. I'm not a perfect person. WE MIGHT HAVE SOMETHING IN COMMON.'**"* → *"**when I
see perfect people — which don't exist, but they present themselves that way — it feels like THEY'RE
DRIVING A WEDGE BETWEEN ME AND THEM. My ability to connect with them is not going to be great.**"*

### ★★★★★ Winging it versus doing the work

★★★★ **He refuses the "only way" framing first:** *"**You can make whatever you want. All over the
internet there are people making whatever they want, and it hasn't stopped them. Some will figure it
out and some won't. But if you want to be more INTENTIONAL before you make the work — spend a
minute.**"*

Then the standard, using storytelling as the example: *"**a learned person, somebody much more
intellectually curious, would say: what are the top five books on storytelling? And watch movies,
read stories, watch plays — and start to understand THE INVISIBLE STRUCTURE THAT BINDS THEM
TOGETHER. What is the thing that makes the top 100 films of all time great?**"* → then test the
ideas in conversations and small workshops → *"**and then you start to see what ideas stick. Now you
have something.**"*

> ★★★★★ **What "learn" means to him:** *"**When I say learn, I don't mean READ, I mean LEARN — that
> you can internalise it, you can share ideas.** … **What happens inevitably is: once you learn
> something, IT GETS MUTATED BY YOUR OWN BRAIN IN A GOOD WAY. You say it a little differently. You
> embellish this and remove that — and all of a sudden IT STARTS TO BECOME MORE YOURS AND LESS
> THEIRS.**"*

> ★★★★★ **Mechanical teaching versus demonstrated teaching:** *"**one way is mechanically saying:
> here's how you tell a story. The other, more interesting way is YOU TELL AN AMAZING STORY AND SAY:
> 'do you want to know why I did that? Here's what happened.' THAT becomes part of your brand.**"*

★★★★ **And a clean positioning distinction:** *"**what is the underlying layer that you're
selling?**"* → views, reach → *"**if I'm a strategist to help people get views, conversions, audience
building — I DON'T TALK ABOUT VIDEO PRODUCTION AT ALL. I talk about VIDEO STRATEGY. That's very
different.**"*

### ★★★★★ The three-part essence — the second workbook

**Origin:** *"**Unbland Yourself is a dense book, 100-plus pages. It's deep inner work, and people's
heads are melting. So I was thinking: HOW DO I PROVIDE A QUICK WIN?**"*

★★★★ **The advice that produced it**, from a friend on a trip to Japan: *"**what I find to be
effective is when there's HIGH FREQUENCY OF EXERCISES BUT LOW INTELLECTUAL LIFT.** Instead of giving
us a big thing to figure out that we don't talk about for 3 days, **give us lots of little things,
quick check-ins, so we feel like we're making progress.**"*

> ★★★★ **And his own self-diagnosis in accepting it:** *"**it's very different from how I think. I'M A
> DEEP-DIVING KIND OF GUY, and these seemingly complex problems are not that complex for me — but
> for other people it's very complicated. So I took that to heart.**"* → the brief: *"**how do we
> lower the mental lift, and make it fast, fun and easy — something somebody can do in 30 minutes
> and feel like they have some success?**"* → *"**dare I say, I might actually even like it more than
> the 100-page book.**"*

> ★★★★★ **THE THREE THINGS** — a triangle or Venn diagram, *"where they meet in the middle is where
> you are in your personal brand"*:
>
> | # | The question |
> |---|---|
> | **1** | **What is the one thing you want to be known for?** — *"I'd like to reduce it down to a WORD if possible."* |
> | **2** | **Who's the enemy?** — *"**What are you on a mission to rid the world of? What do you want to eradicate?**"* |
> | **3** | **Your rallying cry / call to arms** — *"the bridge between the two… **the phrase that when you say it, ACTIVATES your audience and the community.**"* |
>
> ★★★★ *"**The cool thing is you only need to know ONE of these things to figure out the other
> two.**"*

> ★★★★★ **Why an enemy at all:** *"**WE ARE MORE UNITED IN A COMMON ENEMY THAN WE ARE IN A COMMON
> FRIEND. And all the best brands in the world, as far as I know, THEY HAVE AN ENEMY.** … **If we
> can't identify that, there's a big part of the triangle that's missing.**"*

★★★★★ **The worked example that lands it** — a longevity figure: **one thing = longevity · enemy =
death** (*"another \$6 word for death is ENTROPY, where systems start to decay"*) **· rallying cry =
"Don't die."**

> ★★★★★ *"**And it's perfect.** So: I want to be known for longevity, my enemy's death, my rallying
> cry is 'don't die.' **So he has a conference — the Don't Die Conference. He might have a package
> of supplements called Don't Die. SO NOW IT BECOMES REALLY CLEAR, AND HE CAN BUILD BRAND EXTENSIONS
> FROM THIS.**"*

★★★★ **A second, brand-side example** — a luxury house that insists *"**we're not a fashion company,
we're a trunks company**"* — *"**and you know why they say that? CUZ THAT'S WHAT THEY SELL THE MOST
OF.**"* With the attention analysis attached: appointing high-profile non-designers as creative
directors *"**directs attention**"* to the runway — *"**this is THEATER**"* — while the bags, which
are the actual business, barely change.

> ★★★★★ **The halo effect, stated:** *"**we are going to direct your attention to something, then you
> check out the rest of the things — and the likelihood of you buying the rest is great. SO THEY CAN
> MAKE FIVE FUZZY BOOTS AND NOT WORRY THAT ANYBODY'S GOING TO BUY THEM — because it's being talked
> about. THAT'S WHAT MATTERS.**"*

### ★★★★★ Be number one, or pick another lane

> ★★★★★ *"**Can you own this space? BECAUSE THE WORLD REWARDS NUMBER ONE — not number two, three or
> four. If you cannot own this space within a relatively short amount of time — and when I say
> short, ONE TO TWO YEARS — then you should not try.**"*

**Citing Seth Godin's *The Dip*:** *"**we should PREDETERMINE if we're going to be successful — and
once we determine we can be, WE NEVER QUIT. What people do is they don't think; they try things and
quit, try things and quit. They never get anywhere.**"*

★★★★ **The practical test:** *"**type into Google or Gemini: who are the world's foremost thought
leaders in the [X] space? Get a list, double-check your homework, and ask yourself: CAN I, WITH
INTENTIONAL EFFORT, OVERTAKE THEM IN A YEAR'S TIME? If you realistically can, I'd suggest you go for
it.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The arithmetic:** *"**The number one leader in any vertical is THREE TIMES BIGGER than
> number two. Number two is three times bigger than number three. So the three top players control
> the entire market — and THE REMAINING 97% FIGHT FOR CRUMBS. IF YOU CAN'T BE NUMBER ONE, DON'T EVEN
> PLAY. That's my opinion.**"*

**Proved by memory test:** who was first on the moon — and who was second? Who ran the first
four-minute mile — and who was second? *Why* → Simon Sinek, and number two? *Vulnerability* → Brené
Brown, and number two?

> ★★★★★ **And he applies it to himself, which is the reason the book has its title:** *"you say:
> 'Chris, can you be number one in personal branding?' **MAYBE. I'M NOT SURE. BUT I CAN DEFINITELY
> BE NUMBER ONE IN UNBLAND.**"* → *"**See — this is just words. AND WORDS DO MATTER.**"*

★★★★ **The same logic applied honestly to a competitor:** *"**if I cannot out-research and
out-storytell and do TED Talks that are among the 10 most viewed in the world, and compete with
Brené — who's written so many books, who's been on Oprah — I'M NOT GOING TO TRY TO WIN THAT GAME.
I'll try to win a different game. Maybe I use the word SHADOW SELF.**"* — with a writer who has
built a modern identity around Stoicism as the model: *"**do I want to out-Stoic that boy? Probably
not. I'm not going to write five books.**"*

★★★★★ **When `[HOST]` calls this discouraging:**

> ★★★★★ *"**Why? YOU HAVE A SYSTEM OF THINKING DESIGNED FOR FAILURE, MY FRIEND.**"* → *"**the coin has
> two sides. You only look at the side of 'that's not cool.' But the good side is: ONCE YOU OWN IT,
> IT'S KIND OF HARD TO UNDO. Because once something occupies a space in your mind, it's very hard to
> undo that.**"*

★★★★ **And the escape hatch he offers:** *"**it only takes ONE DIMENSION for you to change the game.
One level of specificity.** 'I want to be the YouTube strategist **for Latin America**.' Boom. **You
can own that.**"*

### ★★★★★ Three principles of personal branding

> *"I'm a little afraid to tell you, but I will — **because the principles are philosophies and
> ideas. They're not actionable.**"*

1. ★★★★★ **Be intentionally or meaningfully different.** *"**Because if you're the same as everybody
   else, then you might as well not have a brand. YOU HAVE A BLAND.**"*
2. ★★★★★ **Have the courage to be disliked.** *"**If you're different — different gets smashed.
   Different gets ridiculed. Different is misunderstood. So as soon as you have the courage to stand
   up, people are coming after you. Then you have to have the courage to be disliked. I'm different,
   and I'm cool with that.**"* (He notes the phrase comes from a book title he likes.)
3. ★★★★★ **Be aesthetically discerning.** *"**More than 50% of our brain is dedicated to visual
   processing** … **the way I present myself visually signals to other people really quickly, BEFORE
   WORDS ARE FORMED, a feeling.**"*

### ★★★★★ Clothes are tools

> ★★★★★ *"**Your clothes, your packaging, is a TOOL. I want to reframe it: your clothes are not
> clothes, they're TOOLS — and tools are designed to solve a certain job.** A wrench is good for
> turning nuts. A screwdriver for screws. A hammer for driving nails. **You probably shouldn't use a
> hammer to turn a nut.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The question:** *"**The tools that you have — WHAT JOB ARE YOU TRYING TO SOLVE?** If you're
> working in the kitchen, an apron works really well. In a car, a jumpsuit. In a hospital, scrubs.
> **So when you're dressed and you're going to step on stage — what job are you trying to solve?**"*

★★★★★ **The corporate-uniform trap** — the sharpest part of the argument. If the job is *"be taken
seriously,"* the tools are the sport coat, trousers, loafers, maybe a tie. *"**And that would be a
pretty reasonable thing.**"* But:

> ★★★★★ *"**The problem is, you're also telling them: I'M LIKE EVERY OTHER PERSON ON THIS STAGE. I'm
> actually not that special. Maybe you guys should ignore me. Because what I'm going to say is going
> to be like what everyone else is saying.** You know how they start with this? I'll start with that.
> You see their tone? I will adopt that tone. **So you're inadvertently signalling to everybody by
> adopting the corporate uniform: I'M LIKE EVERYONE ELSE. PLEASE DON'T PAY ATTENTION TO ME.** This
> hurts, cuz a lot of people dress this way."*

> ★★★★★ **His own job, stated:** *"**what job I'm trying to solve is: I'm going to be different.**
> Whatever you heard before and after may or may not matter, **but this will not be the same
> presentation. I'm not the same person.** … **I'm going to tell you why it's an advantage to be
> different BY PROVING IT TO YOU, not just talking about it. Imagine if I came on stage and said
> 'dare to be different' — and I looked like every single other person. THERE WOULD BE A MISMATCH
> BETWEEN MESSAGE AND DELIVERY.**"*

★★★★ **On the comments it will generate:** *"**are they going to light me up in the comments? For
sure — not probably.** But you know what they don't pay attention to? **How many people say positive
things before, during and after, that make the negative comments seem tiny.**"* →

> ★★★★★ *"**I know that if I do something that doesn't repel people, I'M NOT DOING ANYTHING OF NOTE.
> If you play it really safe, if you drive down the middle lane always at 55 mph, you're not going
> to offend nobody — and you're not going to break a speed record. I'M HERE TO BREAK THINGS.**"*

### ★★★★★ The answer he gave at dinner — the provocateur

Someone at a dinner tested him: *you're not on stage now — what job are you solving?* He calls it a
question that caught him off guard (*"I like being tested… you don't turn the lens back on me"*):

> ★★★★★ *"'**Here's something you don't know about me. I HATE SOCIAL EVENTS. It took a lot of energy
> for me even to decide to come to this — even to say yes, I'll be there. That was a lot of effort
> for me. And my biggest problem AS A SOCIALLY AWKWARD INTROVERT is I don't know how to start
> conversations with people — but I enjoy the company of other people. So when I walk into a room
> and there's 18 people who all know each other, chopping it up like they go way back, I FEEL LIKE
> I'M BROUGHT BACK TO JUNIOR HIGH AGAIN. Like I'm the new kid who just transferred in, and I look
> like nobody, and I feel very much the outsider.**'"*

> ★★★★★ *"'**What I do is I am A PROVOCATEUR. I want to wear things that INVITE PEOPLE TO HAVE A
> CONVERSATION WITH ME.** So they can say: hey, what's up with your hat? Why did you wear this today?
> **That solves most of my problem. So in social circles the job, unlike on stage, is: COME TALK TO
> ME, PLEASE. I'd like to have a conversation with you, cuz I think you're a person worth getting to
> know — but I don't have those skills.** **You just don't know that's what I'm doing to you.**'"*

> ✅ **This is the single most useful passage in the corpus for understanding his personal style.**
> It is not self-expression and it is not status — it is **a social prosthetic for an introvert**,
> and he says so. It also connects directly to *"I wonder who I'm going to eat lunch with today"* at

> ★★★★ *"**Now I'm like: I've got to cut some of this stuff out. I've got to be more efficient.** …
> **And if you think I've been cooking before — you ain't seen nothing yet.**"*
