# Stop Charging Local Rates (short, 2026-04-24)

> **Attribution:** ✅ Chris throughout. A short, but a dense one — **four separate arguments**, and the
> corpus carries the first only as a label.

## Summary

★★★★ **"Geographic arbitrage" made concrete**, plus the sharpest statement of **why the price belongs
on the call**, and a rule about which cheap work is worth taking.

## Key claims (2026-04-24)

### ★★★★ Sell to markets that can afford you — named

*"If you want to make a hundred thousand dollars a year, you want to focus on doing design work
**probably for markets that CAN AFFORD YOU.** At least here in the United States, **it's on the coast**
— San Francisco, Los Angeles, New York, and down to Miami. **They tend to have better budgets.**"*

★★★★★ **And the instruction to everyone else:** *"**if you're from a small country, YOU DON'T HAVE TO
CHARGE A LOCAL RATE — because the people who are buying this stuff ARE ALREADY EXPECTING TO PAY THIS
KIND OF PRICE.**"*

★★★★ **He raises the obvious objection himself and answers it:** *"I know what you're thinking — **'but
Chris, they called us because they wanted a better deal.'** This is where you need to learn **to act
and to present like a professional** — and **if your work is at that calibre, and the language is
clear, and your process is super clear, THEY WILL BE FORCED TO HIRE YOU AND PAY YOU THAT RATE BECAUSE
YOU'RE THAT GOOD.**"*

### ★★★★★ Price on the call — and the reason is unusually sharp

> ★★★★★ *"**You need to tell them what it's going to cost ON THE CALL.** If you don't do this and **you
> kick this down the road in a proposal, YOU'RE GOING TO BE IN THE PROPOSAL-BUILDING BUSINESS AND NOT
> IN THE DESIGN BUSINESS.**"*

★★★★ **Then read the reaction, and name it:** *"say '**I'm five hundred dollars a day**' up front… and
**you want to GAUGE THEIR REACTION to the price.** And if they hem and haw about the project rate, you
need to ask them: '**I feel — or sense — SOME HESITATION on your part. What do we think?**' **And then
they'll tell you the budget.**"* ✅ Consistent with the be-still-and-watch discipline elsewhere in the
corpus, with a script attached.

### ★★★★ Which cheap work is worth taking

★★★★ *"I would be **very selective here**, especially if you're not being paid very much, **to pick
projects that are going to be SHOWCASE PIECES.** There is **absolutely no point, in my opinion, to do
low-budget work that ALSO LOOKS LIKE ASS.**"*

> ★★★★★ **And the principle under it:** *"**you don't always have to make money up front IF WHAT
> YOU'RE GETTING IS OF GREATER VALUE THAN THE MONEY you would have received.**"*

✅ **A test for underpriced work** the corpus does not currently state this plainly: cheap is
acceptable **only if the non-monetary return exceeds the fee forgone** — and *"low budget AND bad"* is
the case that fails both ways.
