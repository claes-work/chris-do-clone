# Stop Asking Clients for the Answer (short, 2026-05-07)

> **Attribution:** ✅ Chris throughout. ⚠️ **He credits the metaphor to *"my business coach"* — and does
> not name him**, exactly as at [[2024-10-22-yt-XOGrJDvWAbM|2024-10-22]]. **Recorded as he gave it.**

## Summary

★★★★★ **A new metaphor for a doctrine the corpus has everywhere.** *Ask, don't tell* is on dozens of
pages; **the bull's-eye-and-wall image is on none of them** — and it does something the doctrine alone
doesn't: it explains **what questions are for** and **in what order to ask them.**

## Key claims (2026-05-07)

★★★★ **The error, named concretely:** *"Some of us go into these client meetings saying: '**tell me
exactly what typeface your logo should be.**' **THAT'S ASKING FOR THE BULL'S-EYE.** That's not what
we're supposed to be asking — **because IF THE CLIENTS KNEW HOW TO TELL YOU THAT, WHAT DO THEY NEED YOU
FOR?**"*

> ★★★★★ **The metaphor, credited:** *"**My business coach used to say this to me all the time.** When
> we're talking to prospects, **it's not that we're trying to ask them 'where's the bull's-eye?' — WE
> JUST WANT TO KNOW WHAT WALL IT'S ON.** Because **if we shoot in the wrong direction, NO MATTER HOW
> GOOD WE ARE, WE HAVE 0% CHANCE OF HITTING IT.**"*

★★★★★ **And the procedure that follows from it — a halving search:** *"we need to **orient ourselves
north, south, east or west.** And then once we know what wall it is, **then we ask another question
that eliminates HALF THE WALL, then A QUARTER of the wall, then AN EIGHTH of the wall. NOW we know
where the bull's-eye is. NOW we use our creativity, we use our experience** — because **WE USE
QUESTIONS TO NARROW THE FIELD.**"*

✅ **This is the missing operational half of *ask, don't tell*.** The corpus records that you should
ask; this says **what each question is doing** — eliminating half the remaining possibility space —
and **why creativity comes last, not first.**

⚠️ **Attribution note:** *"my business coach"* is the same unnamed formulation used at 2024-10-22 for
*"we are responding to things that are not here right now."* The corpus identifies that role as
[[../entities/kier-mclaren|Kier McLaren]]; **neither source names him**, and this page follows the
same rule — **attribute as Chris did.**
