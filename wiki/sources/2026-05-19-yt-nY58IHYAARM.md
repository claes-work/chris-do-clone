## Key claims (2026-05-19)

### ★★★★★ The million-dollar reverse math

Same divide-by-ten rule as [[2026-02-17-yt-nTthfof409U|2026-02-17]], run at scale:

> \$1M ÷ 10 = **\$100k/month** ÷ number of clients you can serve — *"**the lower the better. But
> also it means the higher ticket.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The client-count heuristic:** *"**we don't want this to be 100, cuz you cannot turn over
> that many clients in a month. So the beautiful number is somewhere between zero and probably
> five. MORE THAN FIVE, I THINK YOUR OFFER IS NOT HIGH ENOUGH.**"* → pick four → **\$25k per
> engagement** → *"**now you have to design an offer where your PERCEIVED VALUE is much greater
> than 25.**"*

★★★★ **His own addition to offer design** — and note which lever he singles out: *"**one thing I
know, which I never thought about before, is: how do you make it EASIER? How do you make it
FASTER? And how do you REDUCE PERCEIVED RISK? AND IT'S PERCEPTION. And perception is great because
YOU CAN CHANGE PERCEPTION — whereas faster is not so easy to change. It's either fast or it's
not.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The smallest-win mechanic, new to the corpus:** *"**if you can take the big result —
> which sometimes takes a lot of time, especially a \$25,000 engagement — FIGURE OUT WHAT THE
> SMALLEST WIN IS AND PUT THAT UP FRONT. So they feel like progress is being made. THAT WILL
> INCREASE THE PERCEPTION OF THE RESULT.**"*

★★★ **And the framing under it:** *"**we have mistakenly thought to ourselves, if we're in the
service space, that we sell time. But what we do is we're really selling A RESULT.**"*

⚠️ He recommends **Alex Hormozi's *\$100M Offers*** by name — *"it takes you through the process in
painstaking detail."* **Third-party recommendation; never source material for that clone.**

### ★★★★ "The rules of one" — a fourth naming of the same idea

> *"for the million dollars, we're going to have to do **the rules of one: one offer, one profile,
> one promotion, one channel. That's what we got to do. HYPER HYPER FOCUS.**"*

✅ This is now the **fourth register** of one argument — alongside *the five ones* (Taki Moore),
*pressure = force ÷ area*, and *diluting your power*. See

### ★★★★★ Delegation, and the reason for it

> *"**my rule of thumb is: if you can pay someone less money than it costs you to do it yourself,
> PAY THEM. Cuz what you get is what you value the most — YOUR FREE TIME.**"*

> ★★★★★ **On why solopreneurs don't:** *"**what solopreneurs do is they're CONTROL FREAKS. And
> sometimes rightfully so — you're always the best at doing something the fastest possible way.**"*
> → *"**the expression is: YOU CAN RUN FAST BY YOURSELF, BUT YOU CAN RUN FAR WITH PEOPLE. And I
> would rather run far.**"*

★★★★ **How to pick the first hire:** *"**look at where you spend the most time doing something that
someone else can do. THAT'S YOUR FIRST HIRE.**"*

### ★★★★★ Trading up — how he went \$1M → \$5M

> ★★★★★ *"**when you work with a \$10,000 client, IT MAKES APPROACHING A \$20,000 CLIENT A LOT
> EASIER. And so you just keep TRADING UP. And you go to 40K, and you just keep trading up. I don't
> know what the limit on trading up is — but you just keep doing it. YOU LEVERAGE ONE PROJECT, ONE
> RELATIONSHIP, FOR A SLIGHTLY BETTER ONE.**"*

★★★★ **The shortcut version**, credited to a friend, with the Gordian knot as the image:
*"**basically, in every industry there's the top dog. WORK WITH THE TOP DOG, ALL THE LITTLE DOGS
FOLLOW.**"* — his own case being **Coldplay**, *"at that time the world's biggest band"*; and:
*"**if you work with Google, all the tech companies follow in line.**"*

### ★★★★★ The supply-chain side door — new, and the most useful idea here

When the top dog will not say yes:

> ★★★★★ *"**You want to work with Nike — you're not going to get them to say yes. BUT THERE ARE
> PEOPLE THAT NIKE WORKS WITH IN THEIR SUPPLY CHAIN THAT MIGHT SAY YES.**"*

He walks both directions of the chain: **distributors** (Foot Locker, Champs, Eastbay) and
**suppliers** (Vibram soles, Gore-Tex waterproofing, shoelaces, whoever designs the boxes or the
t-shirts) — *"**those companies are way more accessible than Nike is.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**Here's the cool part. You work with Vibram, you work with Gore-Tex or one of the
> designers or the agencies — YOU'RE JUST NOW A HALF STEP AWAY FROM NIKE, and it's much easier for
> you to get a call with Nike.**"*

★★★★ **The framing:** *"**you're going for the moonshot — but GO TO THE MOUNTAIN TOP. And then when
you get to the mountain top, GO TO EVEREST. And then it's not that far away to get to the
moon.**"* — plus the referral version: call existing clients and ask *"**do you know anyone that
works over here? I'd love to help those people. NOT A COMPETITION.**"*

### ★★★★★ The referral script — with the step the corpus was missing

Credited to **Phil M. Jones**, *Exactly What to Say* (*"probably the best version I've ever read
about how to ask for a referral. It's genius. It's right at the end of the book"*).

> ★★★★★ **The theory, which the earlier telling didn't include:** *"**his theory is: PEOPLE ONLY SAY
> THANK YOU WHEN THEY FEEL A GENUINE NEED TO EXPRESS APPRECIATION. You've done something great and
> they want to BALANCE THE SCALE by saying thank you. WHEN THEY SAY THANK YOU, YOU MUST ASK FOR THE
> REFERRAL RIGHT THEN AND THERE. If you don't, you've missed opportunity.**"*

**The four steps, verbatim:**

1. *"**You wouldn't happen to know one or two people who could benefit from [what you do], would
   you? Don't worry — I don't need their name and information right now.**"*
2. *"**Well, when you said yes — who were you thinking about?**"*
3. *"**Can I ask you for one more favour? So that [they] know who I am when I reach out, could you
   call them in advance and do a soft introduction for us?**"* → *"**Would it be all right with you
   if I followed up with you, say in a week, to see if he did that?**"*
4. ★★★★★ **The follow-up move — the part that makes it work.** A week later: *"'**I'm betting that
   you probably didn't have a chance to speak to James or Jenny.**'"* → *"and then they say:
   '**You're right, I'm going to call them right now.**'"*

> ★★★★★ **Why it's phrased as a defeat:** *"**the reason you do that is BECAUSE PEOPLE WILL LIE TO
> YOU. So you already give them a way out — 'I bet you're so busy, you didn't have time.' SO YOU
> REMOVE THE SHAME AND THE GUILT. But then they fix that by calling them then and there.**"*

✅ **This completes the script recorded at [[2026-03-10-yt-Zys3dt503Q4|2026-03-10]]**, which had
the first three moves but not the fourth. ★★★ And, characteristically: *"**if you can work with
that, you don't need to read the book.**"*

### ★★★★★ His own revenue history, dated

> ★★★★★ *"you have to keep time in context — **it's like in the '90s, late '90s. I THINK I BROKE A
> MILLION DOLLARS IN THE SECOND YEAR OF BUSINESS. I didn't feel like it was hard.** That's why I
> tell everybody in the world: **MAKING A MILLION DOLLARS IS NOT HARD. MAKING THE OTHER PART IS
> HARDER.** And then I got to two million, and that didn't feel that hard either."*

⚠️ With Blind founded 1995, *"the second year"* is ≈ **1996–97** — which **corroborates** *"a
couple million dollars before I turned 25"* ([[2026-03-24-yt-GaDK_zVy1Cc|2026-03-24]]) against a
birth year of ~1972. **Both self-reported; recorded as consistent, not as verified.**

### ★★★★★ The \$200k ceiling — the tiers, with their origin and the actual numbers

> ★★★★★ *"generally the budgets we were seeing were about \$200,000. **GETTING PAST \$200,000 PER
> ENGAGEMENT WAS HARD.** … Later on I find out **we can win these probably 75% of the time**, I'm
> not even trying. It's weird. **\$250k? CAN'T WIN THESE. We dropped down to a 20% close rate. And
> I'm like: WHAT IS WRONG WITH ME? I have performance anxiety. There's a ceiling I can't break
> through.**"*

> ★★★★★ **His coach's diagnosis — the minors and the majors:** *"**the game that you're playing is
> THE MINOR LEAGUES. This is the minors. This is the majors. And it's a big step up.**"* — with the
> reason: *"**when they get the budget to a certain point, THEY CAN PRETTY MUCH HIRE ANYBODY IN THE
> COUNTRY. And when they go beyond that, THEY CAN HIRE ANYBODY IN THE WORLD. So your competition
> went from REGIONAL to NATIONAL to INTERNATIONAL.**"* → *"**you bring a regional game to fight in
> a national game**… **you're pretty good for a local town person, but not in the national
> market.** **YOU HAVE TO BE A DIFFERENT PERSON.**"*

✅ **This is the origin story and the arithmetic for *the tiers*** already on
[[../topics/business/business|business]] — see [[../entities/kier-mclaren|Kier McLaren]].

> ★★★★★ **And the unlock, which was not craft:** *"**the breakthrough for me, believe it or not,
> WASN'T THE DESIGN WORK. Learn how to have a sales conversation. HE GAVE ME PERMISSION TO ASK THE
> QUESTION THAT I WAS THINKING. Prior to that, I would just never ask. I don't know why — but THAT
> WAS THE UNLOCK FOR ME.**"*

★★★★ **The reframe of what a limitation actually is:** *"**it's not a limitation on your
creativity, it's a limitation on your ability to understand what the clients want — TO MAKE THEM
FEEL SEEN, HEARD, AND UNDERSTOOD. That's your limitation.**"*

### ★★★★★ BAR — and a correction he accepted from therapists

> **B**elief → **A**ction → **R**esult.

> ★★★★★ *"**I used to try and coach people saying: 'well, your belief is X, and I'll use a tool of
> belief and talk to change X to Y.' AND MY FRIENDS WHO ARE IN THERAPY AND COACHING [said]: YOU
> CAN'T USE A BELIEF TO CHANGE A BELIEF. It's very difficult. If you're afraid of cats, [saying]
> 'don't be afraid of cats' — that don't work. BUT WHAT I CAN DO IS ASK YOU TO TAKE A DIFFERENT
> ACTION, WHICH WILL GET YOU A DIFFERENT RESULT, WHICH WILL CHANGE YOUR BELIEF.**"*

✅ **A documented change to his own coaching method, with the source of the correction named as a
category.** Worth carrying: it explains why his coaching is almost always *do this specific thing*
rather than *believe this.*

### ⚠️★★★★★ "I lost a million dollars in revenue"

Asked how he handles self-doubt after setbacks, he answers by disclosing his own — and he does it
to normalise a questioner's 20% decline:

> ★★★★★ *"**Did anybody have a decline in revenue from 2023 to 2024? Raise your hand.** Okay —
> **I'll tell you, I LOST A MILLION DOLLARS IN REVENUE. I'M NOT A LOSER. I just haven't found a way
> to fix this yet. Pretty confident I will.**"*

★★★★ **The general point:** *"**we don't have a culture that's comfortable with failure. So we
interpret the failure INTERNALLY — versus: there's lots of things I can't control. I need to accept
that part and work on the things I CAN control.**"*

★★★ **And on paying for help, including his own position:** *"**find someone who's done it before…
pay them whatever they're worth — WHICH IS THE PROBLEM HERE — to get you out of this. Or you can go
out on your own. Either is okay.** … **If you don't have any money, then you have to grind it out
on your own. I'M GOING THROUGH THAT MYSELF.**"*

⚠️ ✅ **Consistent with the 2025 disclosure** at [[2026-04-07-yt-d7bgouAH3ns|2026-04-07]]
(departures taking a revenue line; the \$20k/month shortfall). **This gives the decline a figure.**

### ★★★★★ Disrupt yourself — the Apple argument

> *"**2024 for a lot of people, especially in the service space, has been really funky. And
> something that might be driving some of this — I hate to say it cuz I'm an optimist — AI.**"*
> (prompted by an email from a friend: *"after 11 years in business, this is our last year"*)

> ★★★★★ *"**the last time there was a really big paradigm shift, it was called THE INTERNET, and it
> wiped out a lot of people. Then there's this other paradigm shift that most people don't talk
> about: IT'S CALLED APPLE.**"*

**His list of what Apple displaced:** the **Thomas Guide** (the spiral-bound Los Angeles street
atlas — *"as soon as GPS was integrated in your phone, that business was gone"*); **Garmin**
(*"their business suffered too"*); **Nokia** and the **Motorola RAZR** (*"Nokia is barely a footnote
today"*); MP3 players, CDs, laser discs; **Creative Labs** (*"they made, I think, the first MP3
player. I had one of them. Dead."*)

> ★★★★★ **The rule:** *"**Every 3 to 5 years, your industry is going to be disrupted. SO IF YOU
> HAVEN'T DISRUPTED YOURSELF, YOU WILL BE DISRUPTED.**"* — ⚠️ **with an honest live revision:**
> *"**I say years, but I got caught blindsided cuz AI is making it faster. I DO NOT KNOW NOW HOW
> LONG THE CYCLE IS.**"*

> ★★★★★ **Fight fire with fire — the prompt he recommends:** *"**if your business is being displaced
> by AI — which I guarantee most of you will be, from coding to creativity to writing music — USE
> AI TO FIGHT ITSELF. Say: 'This is my business. This is what I do. This is what I charge. This is
> who I do it for. This is the impact I hope to create. DISRUPT MY OWN BUSINESS IN ANTICIPATION OF
> THE WAY AI IS GOING TO DISRUPT ME.' USE ONE ROBOT TO FIGHT ANOTHER.**"*
