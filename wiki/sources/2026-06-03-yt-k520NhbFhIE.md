# The psychology of making people buy (2026-06-03)

> **Attribution:** a comment-response episode on the earlier *Better Call Saul* breakdown, with a
> host putting audience objections to him. The host's lines and quoted viewer comments are marked
> `[HOST]` / `[COMMENT]` and are **not** persona data.

## Summary

★★★★★ Half of this is a **category-creation masterclass** — blue ocean, the *Innovator's Dilemma*
Honda story, and three worked case studies — and half is **the most direct answer he has given to
the charge that marketing is manipulation.** The ethics half is the more valuable: he concedes the
predatory practices exist, refuses to be exempted from the system, draws **one specific line
(false scarcity)**, and then argues that *"if you have a good product you shouldn't have to sell"*
is a myth.

## Key claims (2026-06-03)

### ★★★★ The premise — what people actually buy

> *"**Whenever we make something, WHAT PEOPLE BUY ISN'T WHAT THEY PAY FOR necessarily.** You buy a
> pair of shoes — but what you pay for is **belongingness, a feeling of accomplishment, and maybe
> some inspiration.**"*

★★★ **Drucker again:** *"the purpose of a business is **to be profitable and to create a
customer** — and that **all businesses are in the business of sales and marketing.**"*

### ★★★★★ Blue ocean, and the Honda story

Citing **Clayton Christensen**'s *The Innovator's Dilemma*:

Honda's small, underpowered motorcycles could not compete with Harley-Davidson in America —
*"loud, powerful, heavy and expensive"* versus bikes built for Japanese roads where *"you just
can't drive that fast."* The launch team, failing and stressed, took the bikes **off-roading at
weekends to blow off steam** — *"this is an ideally suited situation for smaller, more agile
motorcycles. And some people notice. They're like: **hey, what are those?**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**And soon a new industry was created called dirt bikes, or off-roading with motorcycles.
> And thus Honda was able to have a footprint in America. THEY CREATED A NEW LANE, A NEW USE CASE —
> and almost by accident, they found a new use for the motorcycle that no one else had paid
> attention to. THEY MOVED INTO A LANE THAT HAD NO COMPETITION.**"*

### ★★★★★ Applied to video production — the sharpest diagnosis in the episode

> ★★★★★ *"**Most people get into video production because they aspire to become cinema directors.**
> … **This is why we love lenses and bokeh and aperture** — cuz we aspire to make a Hollywood
> feature film production. **And so what we settle for is some version that is A PROXY FOR
> THAT.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The resulting mismatch:** *"you're going to try to **oversell them 'quality'**… **but they
> don't give a rat's tail about whether or not this is quality. They want it to DO SOMETHING.** …
> **They want their phone to ring, and they want to pay as little as possible. AND YOU WANT TO SELL
> ART. THAT'S THE PROBLEM. So there's a conflict.**"*

★★★★★ **The reframing question he substitutes:** *"**who in this world would pay the most amount of
money for videos — AND I have a steady constant supply of customers for it?**"* — explicitly not
Hollywood: *"**there's not a constant steady supply of customers, and there's way more qualified
people to do it than you today. Maybe not forever, but today.**"*

**His answer:** authors, experts and authorities who want to grow influence. *"**video is the
dominant format for content because it's easiest to consume. It requires THE LOWEST AMOUNT OF
MENTAL LIFT from the end user** — plus it activates not only visuals but sound and music."* And the
buyer's motive: *"established entrepreneurs… **they're not in it to make more money, THEY'RE IN IT
TO BUILD AUTHORITY** — to rise above their peers."*

★★★★ **The worked example of someone who did it** — a friend who consults on *building* video
content teams rather than making videos: *"**I don't need to make the videos. What I want to do is
BUILD SYSTEMS for people who don't know how to run a media company.**"* → *"he has too many clients.
He has too many opportunities."*

> ⚠️ **Cross-clone rule:** he names **Simon Squibb, Alex Hormozi and Dan Martell** as examples of
> the buyer type. **Third-party characterisation; never source material for those clones.**

### ★★★★★ PAS — with the caveat that makes it hard

> *"When you write a sales page, use the **PAS system: problem, agitate, solution.**"* → but:
> ★★★★★ *"**the problem with PAS is YOU MUST FIRST IDENTIFY A NEW TYPE OF CUSTOMER — not the same
> type of customer that everyone else is going after.**"*

**His own example of getting it wrong:** *"'tired of your phone not ringing off the hook?' …
**THAT IS GOING AFTER THE SAME DEMOGRAPHIC.**"*

> ★★★★★ *"**This requires radical thinking. This is why it's not so easy to create a new customer.
> But if you can create a new customer — one who didn't realise they needed the solution which no
> one else has thought about before — THAT'S WHERE YOU MAKE A LOT OF MONEY. THAT'S WHERE YOU OWN
> AND DEFINE CATEGORIES.**"*

★★★★ **With the honest caveat:** *"**creating paradigm shifts, inventing new categories, IS NOT FOR
THE WEAK AT HEART.** This is not something you just [think of] like 'oh, I got a brilliant idea.'
**It requires a lot of brain work. But whenever we see it and we reverse-engineer it, IT BECOMES
VERY OBVIOUS TO US.**"*

### ★★★★★ Three category-creation case studies

| | The insight | The move |
|---|---|---|
| **Method Soap** | ★★★★★ *"**all the people who have hand soap are EMBARRASSED to have it on the counter.** When guests come by you want to put it away, because the market is dominated by [a brand whose] bottle is ugly, the label's ugly… **you feel shameful to have it out in your aesthetic apartment.**"* | They had budget for **one idea** from industrial designer **Karim Rashid** → the teardrop bottle. ★★★★★ *"**if they put it in the exact same packaging and the same aesthetic, I GUARANTEE YOU IT WILL FAIL. Cuz this is why [the incumbent] can't do this.**"* → a new category: *"people who want to wash their hands **but care about the environment and the interior design of their space.**"* |
| **Dude Wipes** | Adults without bidets *"feel a little gross after going to the toilet"* | *"**Literally the same product, the same ingredients** — but package it in black, change the design so it appeals to men, and call it something they can relate to."* → ★★★★★ *"**create a different market for the exact same product.**"* |
| **Liquid Death** | *"people who are moving away from alcohol… **maybe a little punk rock, or younger, or straight edge**"* — against a backdrop where *"the sales of [major soft drinks] in North America have steadily declined year after year"* | Three moves stacked: the **ironic name**, the tagline ***"Murder Your Thirst"***, and **hiring the illustrator who created the melting liquid skull** — in a **tallboy aluminium can** *"so it looked like alcohol."* → ★★★★★ *"**So you have all the taboo of alcohol and NONE OF THE HANGOVER. That's the innovation.**"* |

> ★★★★ **And the instruction to the sceptics:** *"**don't dismiss valid marketing product innovation
> ideas because you don't like the literal example. LOOK BELOW THE SURFACE**… you could very well
> be the next Dude Wipes or Method Soap or Honda motorcycles."*

### ★★★★★ The exercise

> ★★★★★ *"**First of all, FORGET ABOUT YOUR CATEGORY, because you're too close to it.** What you
> want to do is **pick any common product and look for a problem — and lots of problems — and say:
> HOW MIGHT WE SOLVE THIS AND INVENT A NEW CATEGORY OF A DIFFERENT BUYER?**"*

★★★★ **Two supporting observations:** *"**the world is ripe for disruption, because products you
use and love today were designed in a whole different decade or a different era, and things have
changed dramatically**"* · and *"**look for niche markets that are PASSIONATE BUT
UNDERSERVED.**"*

## ★★★★★ The ethics answer — the more valuable half

`[COMMENT]`: *"this is pretty much everything I hate about the marketing industry… create false
demand, creating fake scarcity, leveraging people's insecurities, misrepresenting your product."*

### ★★★★★ "You are all complicit in the lie"

> ★★★★ **He concedes the charge first:** *"**there ARE predatory practices of marketing and
> advertising. We want to set that aside**"* — then makes the harder argument.

> ★★★★★ *"**Humans are meaning-making machines. IF I BUY THIS, WHO AM I? Who do I become in the
> pursuit of this thing that I have?** … **the shoes that you wear, the house that you live in, the
> neighbourhood, the party you vote for — YOU ARE ALL COMPLICIT IN THE LIE, BECAUSE IT'S PART OF
> YOUR IDENTITY. And to deny that is not to really look at reality.**"*

**His examples:** men's grooming products sold on attraction · **Porsche** (*"you drive a Porsche,
you get a girl. It kind of comes with the car — at least that's the thing that they tell you"*) ·
and **De Beers** — *"**a gigantic marketing ploy: 'a diamond is forever', 'a diamond's a girl's best
friend' — and now we have a whole society built on A MAN'S WORTH IN RELATION TO THE SIZE OF THE
DIAMOND, the clarity, the cut, that he can provide.**"*

> ⚠️ **He extends the argument to religion**, and it should be recorded accurately and in context:
> *"if you want to rebuke all of this, **you can even get into religion. Religion is [a] giant
> marketing propaganda campaign designed to save your soul — and every religion believes it's the
> one true religion and that every other one is false.**"* → *"**these are all marketing things, and
> it's TRIBALISM. If I believe this, if I say these words, if I wear these things, if I drive this
> vehicle — I BELONG TO A CERTAIN TRIBE OF PEOPLE.**"*
> **This is offered as an argument about identity signalling, not as a theological position.** A
> persona should not extend it into one, and should note that it is his sharpest such statement in
> the corpus and sits against his usual *"this is not my lane"* reticence.

> ★★★★★ **The audit that closes it:** *"**unless you wear plain white t-shirts and you live in a
> white room with nothing branded and you participate in none of this — then you can say I AM FREE
> OF SIN. But otherwise, WE FEEL GREAT ABOUT ALL THESE THINGS AND WE'RE COMPLICIT IN THE LIE.**"* →
> recommending **Seth Godin's *All Marketers Are Liars***: *"**it might break your mind.**"*

### ★★★★★ Where he draws his own line

On why the *Better Call Saul* framing struck a nerve: *"**Saul is a con man… he does despicable
things. He has a questionable morality — but down at the core he's a really good guy. He's trying
to do right, BUT HE'S WILLING TO TAKE SHORTCUTS**, and we see the devolvement of his ethical
boundaries as the series progresses."*

> ★★★★★ *"**What I'm trying to do is EXTRACT, FREE OF THE MORALITY PART, the marketing and sales
> principles that are driving the conversation.** … **But we all do it to our own moral and ethical
> code. I'M NOT MANIPULATING ANYBODY. I DON'T BELIEVE IN LYING TO ANYONE. IF YOU CREATE FALSE
> SCARCITY, THAT'S A PROBLEM FOR ME.**"*

★★★ He also credits the show's writing: *"**Vince Gilligan must have had a background in marketing,
advertising or writing** — he writes amazing scenes where people are negotiating, objection
handling, pricing, and creating a new market."*

### ★★★★★ Real scarcity versus false scarcity

Credited to **Daniel Priestley** (*Oversubscribed*):

> ★★★★★ *"**we all have finite amounts of clients that we can take on.** [He] at any point in time
> can only work with two to three clients — **YET HE DOES NOT COMMUNICATE THAT TO HIS AUDIENCE.** To
> say 'I can only take on three clients a month' — **THAT CREATES SCARCITY ALREADY. We do not have
> unlimited capacity. You cannot infinitely scale creative businesses — but what we don't do is
> COMMUNICATE THAT TO THE OUTSIDE WORLD.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The mechanism, and it is about penalty, not hype:** *"**we want what we can't have — and
> we're aware that if we don't get in on this round, we might have to wait two, three, four, six
> months, and our problem can't wait that long. BUT IF YOU ARE ALWAYS AVAILABLE 24/7… THERE'S NO
> URGENCY FOR THEM TO MAKE A DECISION, BECAUSE THERE'S NO PENALTY FOR THEM TO WAIT. SO IF THERE'S
> NO PENALTY, THEY JUST WAIT.**"*

★★★★ **The script:** *"**at any one time, I can only process six or seven clients. I actually have
an opening in June. If you're interested in working with me, GET ON THE WAIT LIST NOW.**"* → and
where to say it: *"**every single place you could write and tell people this — BECAUSE THEY NEED TO
KNOW.**"* (restaurants, concert on-sales, sneaker drops)

★★★ ⚠️ **And his honest dislike of the consumer-facing version:** *"**for me as the end consumer, I
hate it**… **the shoe I want is never retail anymore. It's 2–3X. But I'm not willing to wait in
line and I don't want to participate in that.**"*

### ★★★★★ "Con artist" — the etymology, turned into an argument

> ★★★★★ *"**Do you know what con artist stands for? The root of that is you're a CONFIDENCE MAN. You
> make me believe this is real.** … **You're being conned all the time, and you're conning other
> people. THE ONLY DIFFERENCE BETWEEN YOU AND A CRIMINAL IS THEY'RE DOING IT WITH LIES. YOU SHOULD
> DO IT FOR REAL — cuz the same principles are at play.**"*

**Recommending Robert Cialdini's *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion***, with a hedge on his
own recall: *"**the top three things I RECALL from Dr. Cialdini's book: contrast, reciprocity, and
consistency**"* — plus **scarcity**. ⚠️ *Recorded with his hedge; the book's canonical list differs,
and he does not claim to be reciting it.*

### ★★★★★ "If you have a good product you shouldn't have to sell" — the myth

> ★★★★★ *"**I think that's a myth**… I understand the sensitivity, because **in creative communities
> we're taught to focus on our craft — and that if you're really good, you never have to sell. That
> may have worked a long time ago through reputation and referrals. BUT WE LIVE IN A MODERN ERA
> WHERE BEING GOOD IS EXPECTED. YOU HAVE TO BE BETTER THAN GOOD. And what does that mean? IT MEANS
> YOU HAVE TO LEARN HOW TO DO MARKETING AND SALES.**"*

★★★★★ **The two questions he puts to the audience** — and they are the argument: *"**how many of you
know somebody, or ARE that person, who's really good but doesn't get any work?**"* and *"**how many
have witnessed — or are the beneficiaries of — where you know you're not as good, but they seem to
get the lion's share of the opportunities?**"* →

> ★★★★★ *"**In a just world, that wouldn't happen.** … **We have to acknowledge THE REALITY OF THE
> WORLD versus the ideal world.** We want to say everything's based on meritocracy, based on merit,
> that you get the opportunities. **THIS IS NOT HOW IT WORKS. People hire who they know, like and
> trust.**"*

★★★★ **Word of mouth, fairly assessed:** *"**that works — and for many of you, that's 99% of how you
get work. BUT IT'S A VERY PASSIVE FORM OF GETTING LEADS, and that's why you have fewer
opportunities than the capacity to do the work.**"*

> ★★★★★ **The close:** *"**would you like to be the master of your own destiny — to write your own
> chapters in your book? If you do, YOU HAVE TO BE PROACTIVE, NOT REACTIVE.** … **so that you become
> a little bit better known than your competitors. How we choose to do that is up to you.**"*

★★★ **And an admission about his own business:** *"**I've been made aware that we're painfully bad
at letting people know about the programs that we have.**"* — followed by the **Anna Lappé** line
again (*"every dollar you spend is a vote for the kind of world you want to live in"*) and the
purpose he attaches to it: *"**you're saying you hope that we win, and that we're able to drown out
all the charlatans, all the people of low value, of low experience.**"*
