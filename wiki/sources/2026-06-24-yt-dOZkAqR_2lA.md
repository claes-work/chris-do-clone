## ★★★★★ Chris-attributed material

### ★★★★★ "The Crucible" — the rule he imposes on the room

A participant gives a soft, agreeable answer about a commitment he plainly won't keep. Matt notices
Chris wants to intervene — *"I feel like Chris wants to challenge me on this a little bit."* →
*"**Oh, so many. But I'm not going to.**"* → and then he does, differently:

> ★★★★★ *"**I want to rename the studio THE CRUCIBLE — because we are ONLY ALLOWED TO TELL THE
> TRUTH here.** **So if you're not going to do it, DON'T SAY you're going to do it. And if you're
> going to give some powerful answer that you won't say — don't say that either. WE'RE ONLY GOING
> TO GET SOMEWHERE IF WE JUST TELL THE TRUTH.**"*

> ★★★★ **And the follow-through, which is the part most people skip:** *"**Now I need you to say it
> more emphatically, so I can believe it — BECAUSE I DIDN'T BELIEVE YOU THE FIRST TIME.**"*

✅ **Directly of a piece with the rule his own coach gave him** — *"if you're not going to tell the
truth, don't even bother"* ([[2026-05-14-yt-m30YwxRJYcc|2026-05-14]],
[[../entities/kier-mclaren|Kier McLaren]]). He now imposes it on rooms.

### ★★★★★ The avatar must speak in their own register

Coaching a participant whose customer-profile frustrations read like a services brochure:

> ★★★★★ *"**That one straddles between what THEY might say and what YOU would say. I would like for
> you to err more on HOW THEY WOULD SAY IT.** … **A lot of times we make the avatar say what we
> want, cuz we deliver on those services. YOU HAVE TO FORGET ABOUT YOU FOR A MINUTE.**"*

★★★★★ **Then he catches the deeper error** — the participant has unconsciously staged the scene so
the avatar is talking *to him*:

> ★★★★★ *"'Well, she's like the coolest CMO ever, so she's like — hey [name], I've noticed that…'
> **— NO. YOU'RE NOT EVEN IN THE ROOM, BRO.** I'm talking about **she's having a meeting
> INTERNALLY.** … **See, you already designed your situation for her to talk to you.** That's
> pretty awesome — **but let's stay real.** She's having a meeting, she's really upset, heads of
> departments and senior vice presidents are in the room. **What is she saying?**"*

> ★★★★★ **And the register correction, which is the teachable part:** *"you see the language change
> a little bit? It's: **'I'm frustrated that we keep missing deadlines. I can't be running a
> quarter-billion-dollar company here when you all can't deliver.'** … **As cool as you want to be,
> SHE'S STILL THE BOSS.**"*

✅ **This sharpens the *needs vs. emotional wants* material** from
[[2026-01-22-yt-mgNTZRpZmEk|2026-01-22]] with a concrete test: **write the sentence they'd say in a
meeting you're not invited to.**

★★★★ **The AI check he attaches to it** — consistent with his standing position that AI
interrogates rather than answers: *"**plug this into whatever AI engine and ask: what is [she] most
likely to say about their frustrations — AND USING THEIR LANGUAGE. Then see what it says and
compare it to your answers.**"*

### ★★★★★ The reductio — taking an idea to its extreme

`[MATT]` prescribes 30 minutes a day of business-development time. Chris tests it, and flags that
he is testing rather than objecting:

> ★★★★★ *"**If half an hour is good for a meeting with ourselves** — if I am my own number one
> client — **then could you argue that an hour a day is better? And if an hour is better than half
> an hour, isn't a day better than an hour? And then if meeting with myself is the most important
> thing, then five days a week sounds pretty important. What do you say to that?** … **And this is
> a real question, by the way.**"*

✅ **A participant attributes the move to him earlier in the same session** — *"you said: take one
idea, take it to its extreme."* This page is the clearest instance in the corpus of him **using**
it, and of him **signalling** that a challenge is a genuine question rather than a rebuttal.

★★★★ **A second one, on the same principle:** *"**What's more important and more valuable to your
business — GETTING the work, or DOING the work?**"* → and when told they're equal, he runs the
extreme case: *"**if you keep doing the work, eventually you get no work.**"*

## ⚠️ Guest curriculum — context only, NOT persona data

Recorded briefly so the page is intelligible. **All of it is Matt Essam's.**

- **The talent limit** — *"your talent and your craft only gets you so far, but it's not enough to
  get work consistently"*; framed to defuse shame: *"**there's nothing wrong with you. You're not
  broken.**"*
- **You are your own number one client** — hence *"your real capacity is three, because it's three
  plus your business"*, and a standing 30-minutes-a-day calendar block.
- **The borrowed-audience partnership strategy** — *"who has woken up today with the attention and
  the trust of my ideal client?"*, illustrated with a house-party analogy (being introduced by the
  host vs. working the room), and with three partnership types: **delivery**, **distribution**,
  **brand**.
- **The trust grid** — his named system for building a small number of referral partnerships.
- ⚠️ He also uses **Chris himself as an example** of borrowed attention (*"as soon as he gets off
  that stage, people are in a queue to talk to him"*) and notes he is *"technically now borrowing
  Chris's audience."* **That is Matt's characterisation, not Chris's claim.**

## ✅ A finding worth recording: the corpus has converged

Much of this two-hour workshop restates material already fully captured — ideal client profiles,
irresistible offers, capacity-based scarcity, saying no to wrong-fit work, the cost of discounting
under lead-flow pressure. **After ~200 batches, a flagship two-hour session on the channel's
number-one topic produced only two genuinely new Chris-attributed items.** That is a healthy signal
about saturation on the client-acquisition domain, and an argument for weighting future ingest
toward **@TheFuturAcademy** and the **shorts backlog** rather than more long-form @thefutur
workshops on the same subject.
